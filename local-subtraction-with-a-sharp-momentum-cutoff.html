Local subtraction with a sharp momentum cutoff (IR​(M;μ)=log(μ2/M2)/(16π2))

The following scalar toy isolates one step in renormalization: removing a local divergence while retaining a finite dependence on a physical mass. For M,μ>0 and a sharp cutoff regularization in Euclidean space with momentum bound Λ, define the dimensionless Feynman integral
IΛ​(M)=∫∣kE​∣<Λ​(2π)4d4kE​​(kE2​+M2)21​.
(15)
This is a zero-external-momentum scalar bubble integral stripped of coupling and combinatorial factors. Using the three-sphere area 2π2 and t=kE2​ reduces the integral to
IΛ​(M)=16π21​∫0Λ2​(t+M2)2tdt​=16π21​[log(1+M2Λ2​)−Λ2+M2Λ2​].
(16)
The antiderivative follows by writing t=(t+M2)−M2. At large Λ the divergent part is logΛ2/(16π2), with the logarithm's dimensionful argument understood only within the displayed ratios. It has no external-momentum dependence. In a four-scalar-field vertex this structure can therefore be removed by a local operator proportional to ϕ4, rather than a momentum-dependent nonlocal interaction.
Choose the fixed reference mass μ and subtract IΛ​(μ). If the loop contribution to a chosen four-point vertex coefficient is cλ2IΛ​(M), choose the additive counterterm in that same coefficient as δλΛ​=−cλ2IΛ​(μ), where c includes its stated diagram factors. This defines a finite subtraction prescription:
IR​(M;μ)=limΛ→∞​[IΛ​(M)−IΛ​(μ)]=16π21​logM2μ2​.
(17)
The renormalization condition for this toy loop contribution is IR​(μ;μ)=0. The reference mass stays fixed when M is varied; finite dependence on M survives. A different finite subtraction changes the renormalization scheme, and the defined renormalized coupling must change consistently.
For M=2μ, the limiting result is 16π2IR​=−log4≈−1.38629436. At Λ=10μ, the exact regulated difference is about −1.32846343; at Λ=100μ, it is about −1.38569459. Expanding the exact expression gives the leading residual 2(M2−μ2)/Λ2 in 16π2[IΛ​(M)−IΛ​(μ)]. This explicitly separates finite-cutoff error from the chosen finite renormalization condition.
A sharp cutoff regularization is convenient for this scalar demonstration, but shifts of a divergent loop momentum can change its integration domain. A gauge theory calculation cannot assume that it preserves the Ward identities. The toy contains no interacting gauge bosons, ghost loops or overlapping ultraviolet subdivergences, so its successful subtraction does not prove Non-Abelian gauge theory renormalizability.
