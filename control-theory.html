Controllability

Words: 976 Articles: 14
Control theory OurBigBook.com
A system is controllable on a state space if every target there is reachable from every initial state using admissible controls. Time-constrained and unconstrained-time controllability are different conditions. In quantum control, conservation laws require choosing the relevant physical state space or orbit explicitly.

Quantum controllability

Words: 934 Articles: 13
Controllability OurBigBook.com
Quantum controllability concerns the transformations reachable under specified quantum control inputs. Unitary operator controllability concerns entire propagators; density operator controllability concerns conjugation on every spectral orbit; pure-state controllability concerns normalized state vectors or their physical rays. These notions need not coincide.

A finite-dimensional closed quantum control system with fixed drift and control Hermitian matrices, real control inputs, and dynamics linear in the state at fixed controls and affine in the controls at fixed state. Its Dynamical Lie algebra is generated over the reals by iH0​,iH1​,…. Reachability depends on the admissible controls, allowed duration and phase convention.

Dynamical Lie algebra (g=LieR​{iH0​,iH1​,…,iHM​})

Words: 683 Articles: 8
Quantum controllability OurBigBook.com
The dynamical Lie algebra of a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian control system is the real span of its skew-Hermitian drift and control generators and all their nested commutators. The sign choice iH or −iH gives the same algebra. Its connected group determines reachable propagators under the usual freely timed, real-control assumptions. An operator commuting with every generator also commutes with this algebra, imposing invariant sectors.
Connected nondegenerate transition chain generates special unitary control (Lie{iH0​,iH1​}⊇su(N))
Words: 98
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
Let H0​ be diagonal and H1​=∑j=1N−1​dj​(Ej,j+1​+Ej+1,j​), with every dj​=0 and the adjacent absolute energy gaps distinct and nonzero. The squared drift adjoint action has distinct eigenvalues on these coupling components, so Lagrange interpolation isolates every adjacent symmetric coupling. One more commutator gives its antisymmetric partner. Their mutual brackets produce adjacent traceless diagonals, while brackets along the connected chain give all longer-range matrix units. Thus the special unitary Lie algebra is generated. Any nonzero-trace generator also supplies the central direction, giving all of u(N). This is a sufficient condition in the finite-dimensional freely timed real-control model; connectivity alone does not remove frequency degeneracies.
Strongly regular quantum Hamiltonian (∣Ej​−Ek​∣=∣Er​−Es​∣ for distinct transitions)
Words: 57
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
A drift Quantum Hamiltonian is strongly regular when its energy levels are nondegenerate and distinct unordered transitions have distinct absolute nonzero Bohr frequencies. Sometimes regularity is required only on the graph of allowed control transitions. This lets polynomials in the squared drift adjoint action separate different coupling quadratures and permits frequency-selective resonant quantum control. This notion is unrelated to a strongly regular graph.
Codimension-one quantum dynamical algebra is special unitary (dimg=N2−1⟹g=su(N))
Words: 110
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
For N≥2, let g be a Lie subalgebra of u(N) with codimension one. With inner product −Tr(XY) on skew-Hermitian matrices, its orthogonal complement is a line RZ. Invariance of the inner product shows that [X,Z] lies in that line for every X∈g; skew-adjointness of the adjoint action makes [X,Z] orthogonal to Z, hence zero. A nonscalar skew-Hermitian Z has a centralizer of dimension at most (N−1)2+1<N2−1, by its orthogonal eigenspace decomposition, and cannot centralize all of g. Thus Z is scalar and g=su(N). This gives density operator controllability and all determinant-one gates, but not the central phase direction needed for unitary operator controllability.
Symplectic dynamical symmetry in quantum control (XTJ+JX=0⇒UTJU=J)
Words: 118 Articles: 1
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
For a nonsingular skew-symmetric unitary J, if each skew-Hermitian control generator obeys XTJ+JX=0, then so do their real linear combinations and commutators. Differentiating UTJU shows that it stays equal to J. The reachable group is therefore contained in a unitary conjugate of the compact symplectic group. This is preservation of an alternating bilinear form, not commutation with J.
Symplectic invariant of a density operator (IJ​(ρ)=Tr(ρJρTJ†))
Words: 58
Symplectic dynamical symmetry in quantum control OurBigBook.com
For UTJU=J with real orthogonal skew-symmetric J, the dual ρ​=JρTJ† transforms as UρU†​=Uρ​U†. Hence IJ​(ρ) is constant on the reachable unitary orbit of a density operator. Different values can obstruct density operator controllability even when the ordinary spectra agree. For a four-dimensional J pairing indices 1,4 and 2,3, the two diagonal arrangements (a,a,b,b) and (a,b,b,a) have invariants 4ab and 2(a2+b2).
Two adjacent transitions generate the qutrit unitary Lie algebra (LieR​{−iH0​,−iH1​}=u(3))
Words: 84
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
Let H0​=diag(−ω1​,0,−ω2​) and H1​=d1​(E12​+E21​)+d2​(E23​+E32​), with nonzero dj​, distinct positive ωj​. The squared adjoint action of −iH0​ has different eigenvalues −ω12​,−ω22​ on the two adjacent skew-Hermitian couplings. Linear combinations of −iH1​ and its double commutator with −iH0​ isolate both couplings. Further commutators give their real antisymmetric partners, two traceless diagonal generators, and both 1–3 generators, hence the special unitary Lie algebra su(3). The drift has nonzero trace; subtracting its traceless part supplies iI, giving the Lie algebra of the unitary group u(3) and unitary operator controllability under the usual freely timed real-control assumptions.
Orthogonal dynamical symmetry in quantum control (xTJ+Jx=0⇒UTJU=J)
Words: 84
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
Let J be a symmetric unitary matrix and every skew-Hermitian control generator obey xTJ+Jx=0. The condition is closed under real linear combinations and commutators, and evolution preserves UTJU=J. In a unitary basis where J becomes the identity, the generators are real antisymmetric, so the Dynamical Lie algebra is contained in so(N). The state quantity ∣ψTJψ∣ is invariant under the reachable evolution and insensitive to global phase, obstructing unrestricted pure-state controllability. This is a bilinear-form symmetry; it does not assert that J commutes with the Hamiltonians.
Identity augmentation in quantum controllability (g+RiI=u(N),Trg=0⇒g=u(N))
Words: 69
Dynamical Lie algebra OurBigBook.com
For N≥2, suppose a skew-Hermitian Lie algebra becomes all of u(N) after adding the scalar identity direction. Scalar matrices commute, so [g,g]=[u(N),u(N)]=su(N) is contained in g. If g contains any nonzero-trace generator, subtracting its traceless part supplies iI, and g was already all of u(N). This permits using identity-augmented commutator calculations without assuming an additional physical identity control.

Pure-state controllability (Uψ0​=eiχψ1​)

Words: 64
Quantum controllability OurBigBook.com
For freely chosen control duration in a finite-dimensional closed system, pure-state controllability means the reachable group acts transitively on normalized states or their rays. Up to unitary conjugation, the possible Dynamical Lie algebras are u(N), su(N), and for even N=2n the standard compact symplectic Lie algebra sp(n), with or without RiI. For N≥4, the symplectic possibilities are pure-state controllable but generally lack density operator controllability.

Density operator controllability (ρ1​=Uρ0​U†)

Words: 54
Quantum controllability OurBigBook.com
A closed system has density operator controllability when every pair of density operators with the same spectrum can be connected by its controls. Under the standard finite-dimensional Hamiltonian assumptions, this holds exactly for Dynamical Lie algebra u(N) or su(N). This definition ranges over every spectral orbit; a particular degenerate spectrum can be controllable under a smaller group.

Unitary operator controllability (g=u(N))

Words: 54
Quantum controllability OurBigBook.com
A finite-dimensional closed-system control model is unitarily operator controllable when its reachable propagators include every unitary operator. For the usual real Hamiltonian controls and freely chosen duration, this requires the Dynamical Lie algebra to be u(N). If global phase is ignored, su(N) also suffices; exact access to all of U(N) still requires the central phase direction.

Control theory OurBigBook.com
A target is reachable from an initial state if an admissible control input drives the dynamical system between them in some permitted time. The reachable set depends on the controls and any time or state constraints. This is a dynamical notion, distinct from graph reachability.

Reachable set (RT​(x0​)={x(T;x0​,u):u admissible})

Words: 42
Reachability in control theory OurBigBook.com
The reachable set collects the targets attainable from one initial state by allowed control inputs, at a specified time or over allowed durations. Controllability compares these sets with the declared state space. In closed quantum control, a density operator reachable set lies inside its unitary orbit of a density operator, since coherent evolution preserves the spectrum.

Open-loop control (u(t)=u(t;x0​))

Words: 58
Control theory OurBigBook.com
An open-loop control is prescribed as a function of time, possibly after calculation from an initial state and a dynamical model. It is executed without using observations of the actual current state to change the input. A simulated feedback control law can generate an open-loop waveform, but the resulting laboratory control does not automatically correct errors in the actual state or model.

Quantum control

Words: 1k Articles: 21
Control theory OurBigBook.com
Quantum control chooses externally adjustable interactions to steer a quantum state or implement a desired unitary operator. Its achievable transformations depend on the Hamiltonian or Lindblad equation, the available control inputs, and physical constraints such as bandwidth and amplitude. Quantum optimal control optimizes performance within those constraints.

Geometric quantum state synthesis by adjacent rotations (Rjk​(β)∣j⟩=cosβ∣j⟩+sinβ∣k⟩)

Words: 87
Quantum control OurBigBook.com
On a connected transition chain, independently selected resonant pulses realize embedded two-level rotations with tunable angle and phase. Eliminate target amplitudes from one end of the chain using complex Givens rotations, and invert those rotations to prepare the target from a basis state. The pulse angles come from amplitude ratios and the phases from relative complex arguments. Spectator components are unchanged by each embedded rotation. Work in the interaction picture and compensate the known drift phases at the chosen final time when the target is specified in the laboratory frame.

Ising coupling of two qubits (HI​=Jσz​⊗σz​)

Words: 106 Articles: 1
Quantum control OurBigBook.com
An Ising coupling generates e−iϕZ⊗Z with ϕ=∫J(t)dt in units ℏ=1. It is entangling precisely when ϕ is not an integer multiple of π/2. A π rotation on one qubit reverses its sign, permitting an ideal echo to cancel an always-on coupling.

Ising spin echo (e−iHI​τ/2Pe−iHI​τ/2P†=I)

Words: 62
Ising coupling of two qubits OurBigBook.com
A π rotation about the x-axis on one qubit conjugates Z⊗Z to its negative. With ideal instantaneous pulses, two equal free-evolution intervals separated by this pulse and its inverse cancel an Ising coupling exactly. Finite-duration pulses require accounting for the coupling during the pulses; the error is small only when ∣J∣ times the pulse duration is small, or when the control is explicitly designed to compensate it.

Coherent quantum feedback

Words: 341 Articles: 5
Quantum control OurBigBook.com
In coherent quantum feedback, a quantum controller interacts with the controlled system without first converting the control signal into a classical measurement record. Optical feedback through a beam splitter is one example. The input-output interconnection must preserve the physical quantum dynamics, including noise and commutation relations; a classical state-feedback formula does not automatically define a valid quantum controller.

Markovian reservoir engineering

Words: 47
Quantum control OurBigBook.com
Design Lindblad operators and a Quantum Hamiltonian so that desired density operators are stationary and unwanted perturbations decay. A dissipative interaction can change the spectrum and purity of a density operator, reaching targets unavailable to purely coherent Hamiltonian engineering. The engineered stationary state must be physical, and uniqueness alone must be distinguished from an explicit proof of asymptotic convergence.

Hamiltonian engineering (H(t)=H0​+∑k​fk​(t)Hk​)

Words: 443 Articles: 7
Quantum control OurBigBook.com
Hamiltonian engineering designs accessible interactions or their effective time average to implement desired quantum states or unitary operators. The available control inputs, Quantum Hamiltonian generators and laboratory constraints determine which transformations can actually be achieved. Model-based open-loop control computes a waveform before running it; adaptive experiments instead evaluate its performance on the physical system.

Dark state of a driven Hamiltonian (H1​∣D⟩=0,H0​∣D⟩=ED​∣D⟩)

Words: 50
Hamiltonian engineering OurBigBook.com
Such a quantum state remains an eigenstate of H0​+f(t)H1​ for every control input. Its projector commutes with all available generators, so its population is conserved. This obstructs full-space pure-state controllability even when the orthogonal bright subspace can be controlled. An instantaneous dark state of a slowly varying Quantum Hamiltonian instead supports adiabatic quantum control if a spectral gap and slowness condition are maintained.

Average Hamiltonian engineering (H(0)=Tc−1​∑j​τj​Pj†​H0​Pj​)

Words: 67
Hamiltonian engineering OurBigBook.com
Fast prescribed pulses change the frame in which the system evolves during successive intervals. To leading order over a short control cycle, the effective Quantum Hamiltonian is the duration-weighted mean of these conjugated generators. The Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula supplies commutator corrections. Symmetrizing the cycle can cancel unwanted interactions or retain a desired one. The approximation requires control cycles short compared with the relevant dynamical timescales and a suitable account of finite pulse duration.

Adiabatic quantum control

Words: 118 Articles: 1
Hamiltonian engineering OurBigBook.com
Design a slowly varying Quantum Hamiltonian path whose relevant initial eigenstate connects to a desired final eigenstate. Under the gap and slowness hypotheses of the quantum adiabatic theorem, the state follows that instantaneous eigenstate up to phase. The path, spectral gap and duration all matter; a level crossing or excessive speed can spoil the intended state transfer.
Stimulated Raman adiabatic passage (STIRAP)
Words: 65
Adiabatic quantum control OurBigBook.com
For a resonant three-level chain with couplings Ω1​,Ω2​, the zero-energy dark state of a driven Hamiltonian is ∣D⟩=(Ω2​∣1⟩−Ω1​∣3⟩)/Ω12​+Ω22​​. Turning on the target-to-intermediate coupling first and sweeping Ω1​/Ω2​ from zero to infinity rotates this state from ∣1⟩ to −∣3⟩. The other eigenvalues are ±Ω12​+Ω22​​, so ℏ∣θ˙∣≪Ω12​+Ω22​​ controls nonadiabatic leakage. Ideally the intermediate state remains unoccupied, suppressing spontaneous-emission loss. The gap must not close while the dark state is rotating.

Measurement-based quantum feedback

Words: 110 Articles: 1
Closed-loop control OurBigBook.com
Measurement-based quantum feedback adapts a control input from a measurement record or conditional quantum state estimate. A measurement contributes backaction and generally stochastic dynamics. Exact instantaneous expectation values cannot be read from a single system without these effects. A feedback law derived for deterministic closed-system evolution therefore needs a new conditional dynamical analysis before being applied as measurement feedback.

Qubit stabilization by measurement feedback

Words: 58
Measurement-based quantum feedback OurBigBook.com
An observed dissipative channel combined with direct feedback can create a modified Lindblad operator and a compensating Quantum Hamiltonian. Choosing those parameters can make a prescribed pure state stationary and globally attractive. At exceptional parameter values, the dissipator can instead leave an entire family of states stationary, so algebraic target stationarity does not establish stabilization. Checking the Affine Bloch equation and its transverse eigenvalues distinguishes these possibilities.

Work-then-consume optimum for a nonconcave consumption-leisure utility

A stochastic control problem chooses an admissible control input using information available in a filtration to optimize an expected objective for a random dynamical system. Dynamic programming often characterizes its value function through a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation.

Controlled Markov process

Words: 131 Articles: 1
Stochastic control OurBigBook.com
A controlled Markov process has transition probabilities or rates depending on its current state and the chosen control action. A Markov policy selects an action from the current state and possibly the time. Once such a policy is fixed, the induced state evolution is a Markov process. For general history-dependent controls, the state alone need not remain Markov. A dynamic programming operator optimizes over the available actions at each state.
Markov decision process
Words: 67

A Markov decision process specifies states, admissible actions, action-dependent transition probabilities and rewards. A policy chooses actions from observed information; a time-homogeneous problem uses transition and reward rules independent of the date. A Bellman equation optimizes current reward plus the conditional value of subsequent decisions. For nonnegative rewards, finite-horizon values increase as the horizon grows, while identification of their limit with the infinite-horizon value requires an approximation argument for admissible policies.
