Standardization endpoint and blank bias

Hold the known primary standard inventory fixed, and let V be the correct blank-subtracted titre. In the following model, any remaining endpoint offset has been removed from this reference. If a titration endpoint overshoot adds δV>0, the inferred titrant concentration is too small:
c=Vns​​,c=V+δVns​​,cc−c​=−V+δVδV​.
(20)
The denominator grows while the numerator stays fixed. For δV≪V, the relative effect is approximately −δV/V, but the displayed fraction is exact for this additive model. An unremoved positive titration blank b has the same mathematical effect: replace δV by b. If both remain, replace it by δV+b. This analogy concerns their effects on the denominator; it does not mean a blank measures endpoint overshoot.
For the chosen 24.80mL reference titre, an extra 0.05mL gives c=0.1006036217…molL−1 and relative error −0.05/24.85=−0.201207…%. Leaving both a 0.05mL blank and a 0.05mL overshoot gives denominator 24.90mL and relative error −0.10/24.90=−0.401606…%.
For comparison, the same extra 0.05mL on a 5.00mL reference titre gives −0.05/5.05=−0.990099…%. The ratio of these error magnitudes is 24.85/5.05=4.920792…, while the small-error approximation would give 24.80/5.00=4.96. A larger titre reduces the fractional effect of a fixed extra volume; it does not fix unknown purity, incomplete transfer, or an incorrect stoichiometry.
