Cross-validation (CV=n−1∑i​L(Yi​,f​(−k(i))(Xi​)))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical learning
OurBigBook.com Words: 576 Articles: 7
Cross-validation estimates predictive performance by repeatedly fitting on one part of the data and evaluating on held-out observations. In K-fold cross-validation, divide training data into disjoint folds I1​,…,IK​; k(i) identifies observation i's fold, and f​(−k) is fitted without fold Ik​. The displayed average scores each observation using a rule that did not train on it. Weight by observation count when fold sizes differ. Leave-one-out cross-validation uses one observation per fold. [ref]
For the training error example, three folds contain indices i with equal remainder modulo 3. Each fit uses eight observations. The squared-error loss averages for degrees 1, 2, and 7 are 0.724132, 0.027192, and 1.532790 respectively. Select degree 2 and refit on all twelve training data observations. The folds reuse scarce data while challenging each candidate to predict unseen responses; they do not touch the final test set.
Any learned preprocessing, including variable selection, must also be fitted within each training fold to avoid data leakage. [ref] Repeated tuning can itself overfit the cross-validation score; nested cross-validation uses inner folds for model selection and outer folds for assessment of that entire procedure. [ref]
Folds use fewer training observations than the final refit, and their scores are dependent because training observations overlap. [ref] Splitting must match deployment: for time series forecasting, rolling-origin cross-validation trains on the past and evaluates later observations, preventing future information from entering a forecast. Ordinary random folds need not answer that forecasting question. [ref]
