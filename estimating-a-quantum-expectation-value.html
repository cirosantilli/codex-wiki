Estimating a quantum expectation value (μ​N​=N−1∑i=1N​Xi​)

Prepare a fresh copy of the same quantum state for each trial and measure the same observable. If preparations and readouts are independent, the outcomes Xi​ are independent and identically distributed random variables with the Born rule probability distribution. Their sample mean is an unbiased estimator of the expectation value:
μ​N​=N1​∑i=1N​Xi​,E[μ​N​]=⟨A⟩,Var(μ​N​)=N(ΔA)2​,SE(μ​N​)=N​ΔA​.
(77)
The law of large numbers explains convergence of the sample mean, while the standard error describes its finite-sample spread. It takes four times as many independent trials to halve that standard error. This reduces estimation error; it does not reduce the quantum uncertainty of a single prepared copy.
For the spin expectation value, let n+​ and n−​ be the two outcome counts, with N=n+​+n−​. Then
μ​N​=2ℏ​Nn+​−n−​​,n+​∼Binomial(N,p+​),Pr(μ​N​=ℏ(Nk​−21​))=(kN​)p+k​(1−p+​)N−k.
(78)
The binomial distribution gives the exact sampling model. For θ=π/3, the standard error is ℏ3​/(4N​): approximately 0.1083ℏ for N=16 and 0.03827ℏ for N=128. A run can fluctuate away from the predicted mean even as its typical spread decreases.
Repeatedly measuring one surviving system is a different protocol. Under the ideal Lüders rule, outcome ar​ leaves Pr​∣ψ⟩/pr​​ when pr​>0. With no intervening change of observable or dynamics moving the state out of that eigenspace, the next projective measurement returns ar​ with certainty. Such a record is correlated and does not sample the original Born rule distribution independently. The quantum nondemolition measurement discussion describes this repeatability; fresh quantum state preparations supply the ensemble needed for the estimator above.
