Minkowski addition (A+B)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Convex geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
The Minkowski sum of subsets A,B of a vector space collects the result of vector addition for every possible pair:
A+B={a+b:a∈A, b∈B}=⋃b∈B​(A+b).
(15)
Think of fixing a point b of B, making a translation of the whole of A by b, and then taking the union of these copies over every b∈B. The points of B specify displacement vectors; they need not be the centre of a shape. Minkowski addition applies to nonconvex sets too. It is different from set union: for A=B={0,1} on the real line, A∪B={0,1} but A+B={0,1,2}.
For axis-aligned rectangles in the Euclidean plane, the coordinate intervals add separately:
([0,a]×[0,b])+([0,c]×[0,d])=[0,a+c]×[0,b+d],a,b,c,d>0.
(16)
Every coordinate between 0 and a+c can be split into one coordinate in [0,a] and another in [0,c], and likewise for the vertical coordinate. Thus a 2×1 rectangle plus a 1×2 rectangle gives a 3×3 square of area 9, although the two input areas total only 4. The Minkowski sum adds possible displacements, so its area need not be the sum of input areas.
For closed Euclidean disks Dr​={x∈R2:∥x∥≤r} centred at the origin, the triangle inequality gives Dr​+Ds​⊆Dr+s​. Conversely, if r+s>0, split any x∈Dr+s​ as x=rx/(r+s)+sx/(r+s). Therefore Dr​+Ds​=Dr+s​, including the case r=s=0. Disks of radii 1 and 2 give a disk of radius 3, with areas π,4π,9π. For disks with centres u,v, the resulting centre is u+v. These two examples lead to the root-of-area comparison in the Brunn-Minkowski theorem.
Minkowski addition is commutative and associative because vector addition is. Its identity is the singleton {0}, and A+∅=∅. For displacement vectors u,v and t>0, translations and uniform dilations satisfy
(A+u)+(B+v)=(A+B)+(u+v),t(A+B)=tA+tB,
(17)
where tA={ta:a∈A} uses scalar multiplication. A Minkowski sum of convex sets is convex: a line segment between a1​+b1​ and a2​+b2​ is obtained by adding the corresponding points of the line segments in A and B. Closure requires care; see sum of two closed sets need not be closed and sum of a compact set and a closed set.
