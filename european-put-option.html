European put option (PT​=(K−ST​)+)

 ... Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Fundamental theorem of asset pricing Contingent claim Put option
OurBigBook.com Words: 217
A European put option gives its holder the right, without an obligation, to sell one unit of an underlying financial asset at the strike price K only on the expiry date T. For one stock share, its terminal financial payoff is the positive part
PT​=(K−ST​)+={K−ST​,0,​ST​<K,ST​≥K.​
(10)
For strike price K=100, terminal stock prices 80, 100, and 120 give financial payoffs 20, 0, and 0, respectively. At ST​=80, selling for 100 rather than the market price 80 supplies the difference 20; at ST​=120, the holder can let the right expire. Equality at ST​=100 gives zero net exercise value. The European call option example plots both financial payoffs on the same axes.
Combining a European put option with the underlying stock share produces the terminal amount ST​+(K−ST​)+=max(ST​,K). Its floor at the strike price is why a European put option can describe protection against a fall in terminal stock value. The protection has an initial cost and lasts to the specified expiry; the terminal floor alone says nothing about the financial return. Put-call parity replicates this same amount with a European call option and a zero-coupon bond.
Using the put-call parity example's price P0​=10+100e−0.05−100≈5.12294245, the cost financed for one year at interest rate r=0.05 grows to P0​e0.05=100−90e0.05≈5.38560133. The terminal net amount is therefore approximately 14.61439867 when ST​=80, and −5.38560133 when ST​=100 or 120. These computations use the unrounded price and distinguish the financial payoff from the financed gain. Unlike an American put option, a European put option supplies no right to exercise before T.
