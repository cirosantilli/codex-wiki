Total correlation (TC(X1​,…,Xn​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Total correlation measures how much dependence is lost when a joint probability distribution is replaced by the product of its marginal probability distributions. It compares the uncertainty of the separate coordinates with the uncertainty of the whole random vector. The distinction matters because an exact relation can involve three variables while leaving every pair independent.
For random variables X1​,…,Xn​ with finite alphabets, define
TC(X1​,…,Xn​)=i=1∑n​H(Xi​)−H(X1​,…,Xn​)=D(PX1​,…,Xn​​​i=1⨂n​PXi​​).​
(71)
Here H is information entropy, the entropy of the full random vector is joint entropy, and D is relative entropy. Base-two logarithms give bits; natural logarithms give nats. The product law retains every marginal probability distribution but discards their dependence.[ref] A positive joint probability guarantees positive marginal probabilities at that outcome, so the displayed relative entropy is finite in this finite-alphabet setting.
For two variables, total correlation is exactly mutual information. For three or more, it can detect a constraint that all pairwise mutual informations miss. Relative entropy nonnegativity gives TC≥0, with equality exactly when the coordinates are jointly independent random variables.
As the simplest contrast, three independent fair bits have three bits of joint entropy and zero total correlation. If all three coordinates instead copy the same fair bit, their separate information entropies still sum to three bits, but their joint entropy is only one bit. Their total correlation is therefore two bits. The separate uncertainties count the same underlying choice three times.
