Order of reaction (n)

OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 7
In a power-form rate law v=k[A]α[B]β, the partial orders of reaction are α and β, and the overall order of reaction is n=α+β. For example, doubling [A] at fixed [B] multiplies v by 2α: factors of one, two, and four correspond to partial orders of reaction zero, one, and two. These exponents can also be fractional or negative. A single constant order of reaction need not describe a rate law that is not a power form. See IUPAC Gold Book: order of reaction.

For the zero-order reaction, the plotted extension X=max(1−τ,0) stops conversion at depletion. It does not continue to obey dX/dτ=−1 once X=0. A negative concentration would be unphysical. The first-order reaction and second-order reaction approach zero without reaching it at finite time in these models.
The common τ axis compares curve shapes after each model's own time rescaling, rather than equal laboratory times for an identical numerical k. In the lower panels, X, lnX, and 1/X respectively give straight lines with slopes −1, −1, and +1 for the matching order of reaction. The natural logarithm uses a dimensionless quantity. These transformed plots help test a proposed rate law; the zero-order reaction line applies only before depletion. See Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.4: integrated rate laws.
