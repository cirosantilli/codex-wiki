Citric acid cycle

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Metabolic pathway
OurBigBook.com Tags: Energy metabolism Words: 1k Articles: 4
The citric acid cycle is a metabolic pathway that accepts a two-carbon acetyl group from acetyl-CoA, releases two carbon dioxide molecules per turn, and regenerates its four-carbon acceptor, oxaloacetate. Its oxidation steps load electron carriers; oxidative phosphorylation subsequently uses their electrons to support ATP production. In mitochondria, most steps occur in the mitochondrial matrix, while succinate dehydrogenase lies in the inner mitochondrial membrane.[ref]
The economy is striking: the acceptor is rebuilt rather than supplied anew for every incoming acetyl group. Regeneration does not create extra oxaloacetate. Withdrawal of intermediates for amino acid synthesis must be balanced by replenishment if the available pool is to remain steady. Thus the citric acid cycle connects energy metabolism to synthesis as well as to breakdown.[ref]
