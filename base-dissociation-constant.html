Base dissociation constant (Kb​)

 Home Chemistry Acid-base chemistry Base (chemistry) Conjugate base
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
For the conjugate base A− accepting a proton from water,
A−+H2​O⇌HA+OH−,Kb​=aA−​aHA​aOH−​​.
(25)
This base dissociation constant uses the same water thermodynamic activity approximation and standard state conventions as the acid dissociation constant. Multiplying the two relations cancels the partner thermodynamic activities:
Ka​Kb​=aH+​aOH−​=Kw​,pKa​+pKb​=pKw​,pKb​=−log10​Kb​,pKw​=−log10​Kw​.
(26)
Here Kw​ is the aqueous ion product associated with self-ionization of water. At fixed temperature, a larger acid dissociation constant gives a smaller base dissociation constant for its own conjugate base; this reciprocal rule compares matching conjugate acid-base pairs. A weak acid does not necessarily have a conjugate base that reacts almost completely with water. For example, stipulated Ka​=10−5 and Kw​=10−14 give Kb​=10−9.[ref]
The value of Kw​ changes with temperature; 10−14 is the familiar approximate value near 25∘C, not a universal constant. If water is retained explicitly in both full equilibrium constants, their product instead equals aH3​O+​aOH−​/aH2​O2​, the full equilibrium constant for 2H2​O⇌H3​O++OH−. The acid dissociation constant, base dissociation constant and self-ionization of water convention must agree.
