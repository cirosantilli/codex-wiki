Temperature sensitivity of a free-expansion experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Free expansion Joule's free-expansion experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 273
The gas-only condition ΔU=0 requires negligible heat exchange with the apparatus. If the insulated isolated system instead includes the walls, sensors and water, the first law of thermodynamics fixes their combined energy. Once everything returns to a common final temperature, a constant apparatus heat capacity Ca​ gives
Ug​(T2​,V2​)−Ug​(T1​,V1​)+Ca​(T2​−T1​)=0.
(30)
Define A=Ug​(T1​,V2​)−Ug​(T1​,V1​), the gas's internal energy increase for the same expansion evaluated at the initial temperature. For a small temperature change, linearizing at the final volume gives
ΔT≃−CV,g​+Ca​A​.
(31)
Here CV,g​ is the whole gas sample's heat capacity at constant volume at the reference state. Compared with an isolated gas sample's −A/CV,g​, the common final signal is reduced by CV,g​/(CV,g​+Ca​). This energy accounting explains why a massive bath can conceal a real-gas effect. Fitzpatrick gives the corresponding apparatus correction for the Van der Waals equation. [ref]
For a toy calculation, stipulate A=5J, CV,g​=10JK−1 and Ca​=990JK−1, all constant in this example. The predicted cooling magnitude changes from 0.5K for the gas alone to 0.005K for the combined apparatus. An illustrative effective detection threshold of 0.01K would miss the latter. These are chosen numbers, not Joule's measurements. In this approximation a null observation only bounds ∣A∣≲(CV,g​+Ca​)ϵT​, where ϵT​ is the detectable temperature change after allowing for drift and other experimental uncertainties; a display's smallest digit is not automatically this threshold.
The historical limitation was recognized explicitly: Joule and Thomson later estimated that the common apparatus in Joule's experiment would cool by only about 0.003K, explaining why the earlier external thermal effect was undetectable. This was a later calculation about the apparatus, not a measured cooling trace. [ref]
