At fixed radial comoving distance and fixed emission scale factor, open spatial geometry has transverse radius sinh(κDC​)/κ>DC​. Hence its angular diameter distance exceeds the flat value, and a standard object of fixed proper size has
δθ=ℓ/DA​smaller in the open geometry.​
(28)
The same comparison can be expressed at fixed proper radial distance on the emission slice. A comparison at fixed redshift must additionally account for the different expansion histories in DC​(z); curvature and expansion effects must not be silently conflated.
Known-size cosmological standard rulers with measured redshifts provide DA​(z) through their angular sizes. The function depends on the spatial-curvature parameter directly and on matter, radiation or dark energy through the expansion history. For a comoving ruler such as a calibrated baryon acoustic oscillation scale, the transverse angular measurement determines (1+z)DA​. Angular acoustic scales in the cosmic microwave background similarly probe the ratio of a calibrated early-time length to a cosmological distance. A range of redshifts constrains parameter-dependent shapes and the overall distance scale, but one angular measurement cannot uniquely separate all density parameters. Ruler calibration, source evolution and lensing must be modeled, and independent expansion or distance information helps break the degeneracies.
