Using the source's rounded solar mass, each hole has m≃3×1031kg. Its Schwarzschild radius is
rs​=c22Gm​≃9×10162(6.7×10−11)(3×1031)​≃4.5×104m.
(60)
Thus rs​∼105m​ at order-of-magnitude accuracy. Using the more accurate solar mass would give about 9×104m.
The stated orbit is not a consistent separated-hole weak-field binary. Its nominal center-to-center separation is only 20km, below even the above single-hole radius. Applying two isolated Schwarzschild spacetime descriptions and a circular Newtonian orbit at that separation is not controlled. Likewise an estimate hˉ11′​∼rs​/r at r=10km is of order one or larger, contradicting the small-perturbation assumption. The intended near-source statement is therefore a strong-field scale,
∣hˉ11′​∣near​∼1,​
(61)
not a reliable prediction from linearized gravity at the specified orbit. The exact radiative component is also gauge dependent near the source.
One can still estimate the intended distant gravitational wave strain scale. A radiative amplitude decreases as 1/D in the wave zone. A compact strong-field source has a characteristic length of order rs​, giving a dimensional strain estimate hfar​∼rs​/D, up to mass, orientation and efficiency factors of order unity. This is also the strong-field dimensional scale suggested by far-zone quadrupole strain; it is not an application of the slow-motion formula to the inconsistent orbit.
