a OurBigBook.com
The strong equivalence principle extends universality of free fall to bodies with appreciable gravitational self-energy, and states that the outcome of any local experiment, including a gravitational experiment, in a freely falling laboratory is independent of its location and velocity. In a sufficiently small freely falling laboratory the external gravitational influence can be removed to first order by choosing a local inertial frame.
This does not remove tidal forces or the Riemann curvature tensor. The laboratory must be small compared with the length scale over which the external gravitational field varies. These qualifications distinguish a local equivalence statement from the incorrect assertion that gravity can be transformed away throughout an extended curved region.

In general coordinates the basis changes along the world line. Differentiating only the components of a vector omits that basis change, and does not transform as a vector under a position-dependent coordinate transformation. The appropriate rate is the covariant derivative along a curve:
dλDVα​=dλdVα​+Γαβγ​dλdxβ​Vγ.​
(1)
For the metric-compatible torsion-free connection, the Christoffel symbols are determined by the metric tensor and its first derivatives:
Γαβγ​=21​gαδ(∂β​gδγ​+∂γ​gδβ​−∂δ​gβγ​).
(2)
Thus the ship's four-acceleration is
aα=dτDuα​=dτduα​+Γαβγ​uβuγ.​
(3)
In Cartesian coordinates of an inertial frame in flat spacetime, the connection coefficients vanish and the ordinary component derivative happens to suffice. In curved spacetime it suffices at a chosen event in a local inertial frame, but not generally along an extended trajectory.

For radial motion outside the Schwarzschild event horizon, the equations needed to determine the acceleration components are
−F(u0)2+F−1(ur)2−Fu0a0+F−1urar−F(a0)2+F−1(ar)2​=−c2,=0,=g2.​​
(11)
They are respectively the four-velocity norm, velocity-acceleration orthogonality, and the maximum proper acceleration norm. Together with part d they give the stated thrust equation; no integration of the trajectory is required. The positive radial sign corresponds to outward thrust.
Let rI​=GM/g​. A first transition from inward to outward motion requires ur=0 at a minimum radius and dur/dτ≥0 there. The largest possible radial derivative at such an event is
dτdur​​ur=0​=g1−rs​/r​−r2GM​.
(12)
At r=rI​>rs​, this is
dτdur​​rI​,ur=0​=g[1−rs​/rI​​−1]<0.​
(13)
Thus the ship cannot reverse its inward motion there. It cannot reverse at any smaller exterior radius either: GM/r2 grows while 1−rs​/r​ decreases. If it first reaches rI​ while falling inward, it cannot return home using the stipulated bounded radial thrust. If rI​≤rs​, it would instead have reached the black-hole horizon before that radius, which already prevents escape.
The minimum radius for radial turnaround with bounded proper acceleration lies strictly outside the proposed radius. Define rc​>rs​ by
g=rc2​1−rs​/rc​​GM​.​
(14)
The right side is the proper acceleration required to hover, decreases monotonically from infinity to zero, and exceeds GM/r2. A genuine turnaround needs rturn​>rc​>rI​; equality gives no positive initial outward acceleration at rest. A sufficiently early burn is also needed to remove the pre-existing inward velocity. The failure at rI​ follows from the turning-point condition, not from treating the moving ship as static throughout its fall.

In coordinates (ct,r,ϕ,z) the diagonal metric tensor is diag(−1,1,f2,1), and only gϕϕ​ depends on position. Substitution into the connection formula gives
Γrϕϕ​=−ff′,Γϕrϕ​=Γϕϕr​=ff′​,​
(15)
with all other Christoffel symbols zero away from singular axes.
Use the explicit Ricci tensor formula and the Einstein equation sign printed on the source formula sheet. They give
Rrr​=ff′′​,Rϕϕ​=ff′′,R=gαβRαβ​=2ff′′​.
(16)
For example, in Rrr​ the two terms proportional to (f′/f)2 cancel. For Rϕϕ​ the derivative of −ff′ contributes (f′)2+ff′′, the product terms contribute −2(f′)2, and the determinant term contributes (f′)2.
Formula-sheet qualification. Directly contracting the separately printed Riemann curvature tensor as instructed on that sheet instead gives Rrr​=−f′′/f, the opposite sign. For the concrete example f(r)=ℓsin(r/ℓ), that contraction gives +1/ℓ2, whereas the explicit Ricci tensor formula gives −1/ℓ2. The two source formulas cannot both hold with the stated contraction. The calculation here uses the explicit Ricci tensor formula, whose pairing with the negative Einstein equation coupling yields the requested stress-energy tensor.
The desired matter tensor assumes Λ=0; otherwise the cosmological-constant contribution must also be included. With that assumption,
Tαβ​=−8πGc4​(Rαβ​−21​gαβ​R),
(17)

The static observer measures energy density T00=−c4f′′/(8πGf) and equal-magnitude negative axial pressure when that energy is positive. The axial stress is a tension. Keeping the source's curvature sign and its negative Einstein-equation coupling together is essential to this result.

Assume ϕ has period 2π, f>0 in the regular bulk, and consider a unit proper length in z. The proper transverse area element is dA=fdrdϕ, while the mass density is T00/c2. The axial boundary term in the mass of a cylindrical spacetime is therefore
μbulk​=∫0R​c2T00​2πfdr=−4Gc2​∫0R​f′′dr=4Gc2​[f′(0+)−f′(R−)].​
(19)
Choose the usual deficit branch 0≤λ<1, so fout​=(1−λ)r. Continuity of the induced metric requires f(R−)=(1−λ)R. If there is no distributional thin shell at R, continuity of the extrinsic curvature also requires f′(R−)=1−λ. A jump in f′ would add a surface-layer stress and cannot simply be discarded.
Using the printed inner condition f′(0+)=0, these assumptions yield
μbulk​=4Gc2​(λ−1).​
(20)
This is the literal smooth-bulk answer. It is negative for the ordinary deficit range, so it must not be silently replaced by the usual positive cosmic-string mass formula.
For comparison, a regular axis with proper radial coordinate and angular period 2π requires f(0)=0 and f′(0)=1, since the circumference-to-radius ratio must approach 2π. Under that different regular-axis condition the same integral gives
μregular​=4Gλc2​,δ=2πλ=c28πGμregular​​.​
(21)
The original PDF really prints the derivative limit zero. It describes either a nonregular axis or an inner geometry different from an ordinary smooth string core; any additional singular-axis matter is not included in the bulk integral above and would need its own stress specification. Standard regular-core matching can also require an offset in the exterior radial origin. Thus the regular-axis formula is a qualified physical interpretation, not a derivation from the printed zero. For a general exterior branch replace 1−λ by ∣1−λ∣ in the boundary derivative.

Outside the core, f′′=0 and the metric is locally flat: set ϕ​=(1−λ)ϕ. Static test particles have no Newtonian-like attraction because g00​ is constant and Γr00​=0. Local trajectories are straight lines in locally unwrapped coordinates, so a very thin dark core is hard to identify through ordinary orbital accelerations or local tidal forces.
The global angular range is nevertheless 2π(1−λ) rather than 2π. The missing angle δ=2πλ can give gravitational lensing with two nearly identical, essentially undistorted images of a background galaxy, and multiple such pairs aligned along the projected object. For a small deficit, a straight string and favourable geometry, their angular separation is of order δDls​/Ds​, with an additional inclination factor for a tilted string. The matching spectra and common line of image pairs distinguish this from an ordinary compact lens.
A moving cosmic string can also induce a step in the cosmic microwave background temperature across its path. That signature needs motion in addition to the static geometry supplied here. These are consequences of the exterior conical geometry; interpreting the inferred deficit as a positive mass per length requires the regular-axis/core assumptions distinguished in part b.

The cosmic distance ladder starts by calibrating a physical baseline. Radar echoes from planets or spacecraft give a round-trip light time and hence a distance d=cΔt/2, after accounting for orbital motion and propagation delays. Combining this with orbital geometry fixes the Earth-Sun orbital scale; directly timing a reflected pulse from the solar surface is not required.
That baseline supplies annual stellar parallax: for small parallax angle p, d≃b/p, or d in parsecs equals 1/p when p is in arcseconds and b is one astronomical unit. Nearby parallax distances calibrate stellar absolute magnitudes. Main-sequence fitting extends the scale to star clusters, and the calibrated Cepheid period-luminosity relation gives luminosities for Cepheids visible in nearby galaxies.
Cepheid-host galaxies then calibrate Type Ia supernovae. After light-curve and colour standardization, their high luminosities permit distances to much more remote galaxies. For a source of calibrated luminosity L and observed bolometric flux F,
dL​=4πFL​​,m−M=5log10​(10pcdL​​)​
(22)
with consistent bandpass definitions and corrections. Interstellar extinction, composition, stellar-population differences and calibration uncertainty must be propagated through the steps. Redshift can subsequently provide a distance within a fitted cosmological model; using it alone cannot independently calibrate the Hubble constant.

Use standardized Type Ia supernovae as distance indicators and measure their redshifts. The curvature of the luminosity-distance relation constrains the deceleration parameter:
dL​(z)=H0​c​[z+21−q0​​z2+O(z3)],q0​=−R(t0​)H02​R¨(t0​)​.​
(26)
Fainter supernovae at a given redshift imply larger distances, and comparison with the predicted curve tests accelerated expansion. In practice one fits the full expansion history over the observed redshift range and controls luminosity evolution, extinction and calibration, rather than relying only on a short low-z series.
The cosmological fate from local expansion data is conditional on a matter model. For the spatially flat model of part b, if the only relevant components are pressureless matter and a constant Λ, put A0​=R¨(t0​)/R(t0​). The two Friedmann equations give
ρm0​=4πGH02​−A0​​,Λc2=H02​+2A0​.​
(27)
Here Λ follows the sign of the supplied Friedmann equations, where positive Λ contributes positive acceleration. These fix the parameters governing subsequent evolution. Positive Λ yields eternal late-time accelerated expansion; zero Λ gives the flat matter solution; negative Λ leads to eventual recollapse in this dust model. If instead one assumes dust and zero Λ while allowing curvature, q0​=Ωm0​/2 determines whether the matter universe is closed and recollapsing or expands indefinitely.
Without a fixed equation of state and its future evolution, two present derivatives do not uniquely fix the future. For a concrete counterexample in units with t0​=0, the positive scale factors a+​(t)=1+t+t2/2 and a−​(t)=1+t+t2/2−t3 have the same a,a˙,a¨ now. The former expands forever; the latter turns around and later reaches zero. Different time-dependent matter stresses can support their different flat expansion histories. Thus the printed sufficiency statement needs the cosmological-model assumption.

Spatial flatness constrains the total density, not matter alone. A flat model can have
Ωm0​+ΩΛ0​+Ωr0​=1​
(31)
with a substantial dark energy component. A positive cosmological constant has pressure pΛ​=−ρΛ​c2 and does not dilute like matter; it can produce the observed accelerated expansion.
If positive Λ persists, a(t) becomes exponential and the future conformal time interval
Δηfuture​=∫t0​∞​a(t)dt​
(32)
is finite. For two comoving civilisations separated by χ, each one-way signal consumes χ/c. Thus a message completing n legs at finite time requires
ncχ​<Δηfuture​.​
(33)
Only finitely many legs, and hence finitely many full exchanges, can then occur. A reply to even the first new message requires 2χ/c<Δηfuture​, the cosmological round-trip communication horizon condition. Depending on the density parameters, the initial outward message might arrive while its reply can never reach us, as in the right-hand panel of part d.
The source gives no matter and dark-energy fractions, so it does not specify an exact count for the z=1 pair in the real Universe. Flatness and lack of matter dominance alone do not prove a finite horizon: a radiation-dominated flat universe, for example, has divergent future conformal time if that power-law expansion persists. The finite-message conclusion uses persistent accelerated expansion such as positive constant Λ.

a OurBigBook.com
For a photon gas in thermal and chemical equilibrium, photon number is not a conserved thermodynamic charge. Number-changing processes can create and absorb photons without a corresponding conserved-number constraint, so the equilibrium photon chemical potential is zero.
At T=5×109K, kB​T≃0.431MeV. The other light species are electrons, Positrons, three neutrino species and their antiparticles. Neutrinos are ultrarelativistic; electrons and Positrons are only moderately relativistic at this particular temperature because me​c2=0.511MeV. The later simple counting formulas use the intended ultrarelativistic approximation for them. Baryons, muons and heavier particles are not an abundant relativistic bath at this temperature.
Pair equilibrium implies μe−​+μe+​=2μγ​=0. Approximate charge symmetry, with the net electron excess tied to the much smaller baryon asymmetry, then makes both electron and Positron chemical potentials small compared with kB​T. For neutrinos one additionally assumes negligible lepton asymmetry. Their near-zero chemical potentials are not implied merely by their statistics. After neutrino decoupling, a distribution initially having negligible chemical potential retains a redshifted Fermi-Dirac distribution, with its own temperature.
