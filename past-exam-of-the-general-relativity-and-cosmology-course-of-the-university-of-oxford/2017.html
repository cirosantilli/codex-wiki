The covariant derivative differentiates a vector field while accounting for how its coordinate basis changes between neighboring points. For the Levi-Civita connection,
vβ;γ​=∇γ​vβ=∂γ​vβ+Γβγδ​vδ.​
(1)
The Christoffel symbol term makes this a tensor with one contravariant and one covariant index; ordinary partial derivatives of vector components do not generally have that transformation law. Equivalently, one compares the neighboring vector with the original after parallel transport.
For a curve xγ(λ) with tangent Uγ=dxγ/dλ, contraction gives the covariant derivative along a curve
dλDvβ​=Uγ∇γ​vβ=dλdvβ​+Γβγδ​Uγvδ.​
(2)
It is the rate of change measured relative to a parallel transport frame along the curve. It vanishes precisely when v undergoes parallel transport along that curve. If v=U, it is the covariant acceleration, and its vanishing is the geodesic equation with an affine parameter.

Use metric signature (−,+,+,+) and a bulk four-velocity normalized by uαuα​=−c2. A perfect fluid in general relativity is isotropic in its local rest frame, with no rest-frame energy flux or anisotropic stress. In an orthonormal frame in spacetime comoving with that fluid,
uα^=(c,0,0,0),Tα^β^​=diag(ρc2,p,p,p).
(3)
Here ρ is rest-frame energy density divided by c2, including random kinetic energy, and p is isotropic pressure. For a particle gas, p=31​∫p⋅vdn and ρc2=∫Edn, with dn the rest-frame particle distribution. Isotropy makes the off-diagonal spatial stresses and net energy flux vanish.
Introduce the spatial projector of a timelike congruence
Pαβ=gαβ+c2uαuβ​.
(4)
The only isotropic decomposition with the preceding components is
Tαβ=ρuαuβ+pPαβ.
(5)
Since both sides are tensors, equality in the comoving orthonormal frame in spacetime establishes
Tαβ=(ρ+c2p​)uαuβ+pgαβ.​
(6)
Coordinate-independent definitions of the two scalar fields are
ρ=c4Tαβuα​uβ​​,p=31​Pαβ​Tαβ.​
(7)
Thus ρ is not, in general, only the sum of the particle rest masses per volume.
The microscopic averaging notation needs a normalization qualification. The normalization of an averaged four-velocity matters because a literal average of normalized microscopic four-velocities is not normally a normalized bulk four-velocity. For example, six equally weighted streams with the same ordinary speed v, directed along the positive and negative coordinate axes, have an isotropic velocity distribution and
⟨vα⟩=(γc,0,0,0),⟨vα⟩⟨vα​⟩=−γ2c2,γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2>1.
(8)
The physical rest-frame bulk four-velocity is (c,0,0,0), not this unnormalized average. Even taking the source's schematic kinetic expression literally with prefactor ρs​ gives
T00=ρs​γ2c2,p=3ρs​γ2v2​.
(9)
Substituting u=⟨v⟩ and the same ρs​ into the claimed fluid expression would instead increase T00 by p(γ2−1). The standard result therefore requires a normalized bulk velocity and a carefully defined rest-frame total energy density; it does not follow with both microscopic identifications interpreted literally. More generally the bulk velocity can be defined as the future-directed timelike energy eigenvector of the stress-energy tensor.
The equation ∇α​Tαβ=0 is local stress-energy conservation. In a weak gravitational field and the nonrelativistic limit its leading energy component gives mass conservation,
∂t​ρ+∇⋅(ρv)=0,
(10)
while its spatial components give momentum conservation, or the inviscid Euler equations
ρ(∂t​+v⋅∇)v=−∇p−ρ∇Φ.
(11)
The next energy order gives conservation of internal plus kinetic energy, including pressure work and gravitational work. These are local conservation laws, not an assertion that arbitrary curved spacetimes possess a global conserved total energy.

Work consistently to first order in the metric perturbation, with ∂μ​ξν of the same small order. Under the passive change x′μ=xμ+ξμ(x),
∂x′μ∂xα​=δμα​−∂μ​ξα+O(h2).
(44)
Applying this to the full metric tensor and writing gμν′​=ημν​+hμν′​ with the same fixed numerical Minkowski metric gives
hμν′​(x′)=hμν​(x)−∂μ​ξν​−∂ν​ξμ​+O(h2).​
(45)
Indices on ξ are lowered with η. This is the gauge transformation of the perturbation relative to a fixed background. If the background were transformed as a tensor as well, the difference of those two fully transformed metrics would instead obey the homogeneous tensor transformation law. The inhomogeneous terms arise from retaining the fixed-background identification used in linearized gravity.
The trace changes by h′=h−2∂α​ξα, so the trace-reversed metric perturbation changes as
hˉμν′​=hˉμν​−∂μ​ξν​−∂ν​ξμ​+ημν​∂α​ξα.
(46)
Let Fν​=∂μhˉμν​. Commuting partial derivatives gives
∂μhˉμν′​=Fν​−□ξν​,□=ηαβ∂α​∂β​.
(47)
Thus choose a solution of the inhomogeneous wave equation
□ξν​=Fν​.​
(48)
Such a solution exists locally with suitable initial data, and can be selected with appropriate wave-equation boundary conditions. This imposes the Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, ∂μhˉμν′​=0. All the divergence terms in the supplied vacuum equation then vanish, leaving
□hˉμν′​=0.​
(49)
The remaining freedom □ξν​=0 is the residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity.
Using the old coordinates in these derivatives is consistent at the retained order. Indeed
∂μ′​=∂μ​−(∂μ​ξα)∂α​+O(h2).
(50)
Acting on a first-order perturbation, the correction is a product of two first-order quantities and is discarded. Similarly replacing the argument x′ by x changes h′ by O(h2) under the same perturbative ordering. This does not assert an exact equality of the two coordinate differential operators; it is the linear approximation about the fixed flat background.

As the temperature falls, electrons and protons form neutral hydrogen through cosmological recombination. The free electron fraction drops sharply, and so does the scattering rate Γγ​=ne​σT​c. When this rate becomes comparable to or smaller than the Hubble parameter, the plasma becomes transparent and photon decoupling occurs. The Thomson scattering cross-section itself does not abruptly vanish. The rapid change is in the abundance of free targets.

(75)
This is an order-one-degree estimate. Including the earlier radiation domination and late dark energy changes the coefficient; it does not make the horizon span the sky. A horizon patch diameter has twice this angle, whereas the acoustic scale uses a sound horizon and differs again. The redshift by itself, without an expansion model or normalization, is insufficient for a unique horizon distance.
The horizon problem is that the observed cosmic microwave background has nearly the same mean temperature across angular separations vastly greater than this scale. In the purely decelerating Hot Big Bang model, sufficiently separated last-scattering regions had neither time to exchange signals nor a common post-Big-Bang causal region that could establish their observed thermal uniformity. The puzzle concerns the missing causal explanation of those initial correlations; it is not an inconsistency of specifying uniform initial conditions mathematically. An earlier period of cosmic inflation can enlarge a formerly causally connected region beyond the later observable horizon.
