Curvature and conservation. The stress-energy tensor is symmetric and obeys covariant conservation of stress-energy, ∇a​Tab=0. To construct a local field equation linear in the Ricci tensor and its trace, consider Rab+αgabR. The contracted Bianchi identity and metric compatibility give
∇a​Rab=21​∇bR,∇a​(Rab+αgabR)=(21​+α)∇bR.
(10)
The divergence must vanish identically, rather than constrain every matter source to have constant scalar curvature. Therefore α=−1/2, giving the Einstein tensor
Gab=Rab−21​gabR,∇a​Gab=0​.
(11)
A constant multiple of the metric can also be added, corresponding to a cosmological constant; it is omitted in this stellar problem. Proportionality to matter by itself does not exclude arbitrary higher-curvature theories. The stated linear combination assumes the usual second-order metric field equation, linear in curvature, with its coupling fixed by the Newtonian limit.
The source's displayed curvature components use the convention Gab​=−8πGTab​/c4, opposite to another common curvature convention. Retain both that sign and those components together: replacing only the sign would give an incorrect negative stellar mass.
Fluid frame and mass function. Because the metric and the Einstein tensor are diagonal, the mixed time-space components of the stress-energy tensor vanish. For the perfect fluid in general relativity, T0i=(ρ+p/c2)u0ui. A future timelike four-velocity has u0=0, so ordinary matter with ρc2+p=0 must have ui=0. Normalizing gab​uaub=−c2 then yields
u0=ce−Φ,u0​=−ceΦ​.
(12)
The static perfect-fluid rest-frame criterion needs the nonzero enthalpy assumption. A vacuum-like component with p=−ρc2 has Tab=pgab for every normalized timelike ua, so diagonality cannot pick its rest frame. That is a concrete exception to the unqualified inference about velocity; it does not describe the ordinary stellar fluid considered next.
Lowering the indices of the static-fluid tensor gives T00​=ρc2e2Φ and T11​=pe2Λ. The time component of the field equation therefore implies
drd​[r(1−e−2Λ)]=c28πG​ρr2=c22G​drdM​.
(13)
Regularity at the centre fixes the integration constant to zero, giving
e−2Λ(r)=1−c2r2GM(r)​,Λ(r)=−21​ln(1−c2r2GM(r)​)​.
(14)
A central point mass would introduce an additional constant, but is excluded by the regular-star boundary condition. The radial field equation is
r2e2Λ−1​−r2Φ′​=−c48πG​pe2Λ.
(15)
Solving and substituting the mass function gives
Φ′=c2r2[1−2GM(r)/(c2r)]G[M(r)+4πpr3/c2]​​.
(16)
Combining this with fluid conservation gives the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation, in conventions where ρ is the rest-frame mass-equivalent energy density:
drdp​=−r2[1−2GM(r)/(c2r)]G(ρ+p/c2)[M(r)+4πpr3/c2]​,M′=4πρr2​.
(17)
The pressure contributes to inertial mass density and active gravity, while the last denominator is a relativistic curvature correction. The weak-field, low-pressure limit recovers p′≃−GρM/r2.
Constant-density integration. Set w=p/(ρc2) and α=8πGρ/(3c2). Now M(r)=4πρr3/3 and
(1+w)(1+3w)dw​=−2α​1−αr2rdr​.
(18)
The surface boundary condition is p(R)=0, with finite central pressure. Integrating from the surface gives
21​ln1+w1+3w​=41​ln1−αR21−αr2​.
(19)
Write s(r)=1−2GMr2/(c2R3)​ and sR​=1−2GM/(c2R)​. Solving for w gives the Interior Schwarzschild solution:
p(r)=ρc23sR​−s(r)s(r)−sR​​,p0​=ρc23sR​−11−sR​​​.
(20)
The printed integration hint has a missing factor for general A. The common-intercept partial fraction identity gives
(A+3x)(A+x)1​=2A1​[A+3x3​−A+x1​](A=0).
(21)
For instance, at A=2,x=0 the integrand on the left is 1/4, while the derivative of the printed right-hand side is 1/2. The normalized w calculation above uses A=1, where that missing factor happens not to matter.
Positive finite central pressure requires 3sR​>1. Consequently
c2R2GM​<98​,M<9G4c2R​​.
(22)
Equality is a singular limiting solution with divergent pressure. The constant-density model is incompressible and is not a realistic causal neutron-star equation of state. Nevertheless, Buchdahl's theorem extends this compactness bound to regular static spherical isotropic fluids with nonincreasing density, under its usual assumptions.
For an emitted surface line received by a static observer at infinity, the gravitational redshift between static Schwarzschild observers gives
1+z=(1−c2R2GM​)−1/2<3,z<2​.
(23)
A claimed purely gravitational surface redshift z=3.1 would require compactness 1−1/(4.1)2≃0.9405, exceeding 8/9. It is inconsistent with the regular isotropic static neutron-star interpretation, even though the surface would formally remain outside its Schwarzschild radius. Misidentification, additional kinematic shifts or departures from the model would need examination before treating that line as a neutron-star surface measurement.
