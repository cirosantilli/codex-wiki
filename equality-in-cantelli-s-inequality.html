Equality in Cantelli's inequality (P(X=μ+a)=σ2/(σ2+a2))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Chebyshev's inequality Cantelli inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
For a>0 and σ2>0, place probabilities
P(X=μ+a)=σ2+a2σ2​,P(X=μ−aσ2​)=σ2+a2a2​.
(8)
Their weighted deviations cancel, giving expected value μ; their weighted squared deviations give variance σ2. Only the upper point belongs to X−μ≥a, so its tail probability attains Cantelli's inequality. With a=2, σ2=1, μ=0, the locations are two and −1/2, with masses 1/5 and 4/5. The optimized shift makes the lower point zero before squaring, explaining equality in the Markov's inequality step.
The threshold convention matters: this example has P(X−μ>a)=0. For a strict threshold, placing the upper point at a+ε instead gives probabilities approaching the same bound as ε↓0, with the prescribed mean and variance retained. Extra restrictions on the support of a probability distribution can change which extremal models are allowed.
