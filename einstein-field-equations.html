Einstein field equations (Gμν​+Λgμν​=8πGTμν​/c4)

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 39
With signature (−,+,+,+) and vanishing cosmological constant, the Einstein field equations are
Rμν​−21​gμν​R=κTμν​.
(1)
In four spacetime dimensions, contraction with gμν gives the useful trace relation R=−κT.
In the usual commutator convention for the Riemann curvature tensor, κ=8πG/c4: G is the gravitational constant, whereas Gμν​ is the Einstein tensor. The Ricci tensor Rμν​ and scalar curvature R encode contractions of curvature. The stress-energy tensor Tμν​ includes energy density, momentum flow and stresses, so pressure also gravitates. A cosmological constant adds Λgμν​ to the left-hand side. See Tong's Einstein equations and matter coupling.
For a concrete consequence, set Tμν​=0 and Λ=0. The trace gives R=0, and substitution gives Rμν​=0. Yet the exterior Schwarzschild spacetime still has the tidal forces computed under geodesic deviation: vanishing Ricci tensor does not imply vanishing Riemann curvature tensor. Geometry carries gravitational effects through empty space. Einstein presented the final matter equations on 25 November 1915, including the essential trace correction, in Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, pages 844-847, especially equation (2a). His symbols and sign conventions differ from modern ones; the displayed formula uses modern notation.

Trace coefficient fixed by stress-energy conservation (Rμν​−αgμν​R=−κTμν​)

Words: 76
Einstein field equations OurBigBook.com
In four dimensions, the contracted Bianchi identity gives (1/2−α)∂μ​R=−κ∇νTμν​ for the displayed equation. Its trace is (1−4α)R=−κT. For α=1/4,
∇νTμν​=1−4α1/2−α​∂μ​T.
(2)
Compatibility with covariant stress-energy conservation for unrestricted conventional matter therefore requires α=1/2, yielding the Einstein tensor. Other coefficients impose constant trace on conserved sources. The exceptional α=1/4 equation instead requires T=0; conservation additionally makes R constant. A trace-free equation sourced by Tμν​−gμν​T/4 is a different system and does not impose vanishing matter trace.

Newtonian normalization of the Einstein field equations (Gab​+Λgab​=κTab​)

Words: 74
Einstein field equations OurBigBook.com
Assume a metric theory in four dimensions, a Levi-Civita connection, covariant stress-energy conservation and field equations linear in second metric derivatives. The contracted Bianchi identity selects Gab​=Rab​−gab​R/2. Its trace-reversed equation is Rab​=κ(Tab​−gab​T/2)+Λgab​. In the convention R00​≃−∇2φ and signature (+−−−), nonrelativistic matter gives R00​≃κρ/2. The Poisson equation ∇2φ=4πGρ fixes κ=−8πG. Reversing the curvature convention reverses this displayed coupling sign without changing the physical theory. The strong equivalence principle alone does not establish uniqueness without the stated additional assumptions.

Einstein-equation coupling under reversed curvature (Rabcd​↦−Rabcd​⟹κ↦−κ)

The same physical field equation is therefore written with the opposite coupling sign. For signature (+−−−), the convention Rabcd​=∂d​Γbca​−∂c​Γbda​−Γeca​Γbde​+Γeda​Γbce​ and Rab​=Rcacb​ gives R00​≃−ΔΦ in a static Newtonian limit. The trace-reversed equation and ΔΦ=4πGρ then require Gab​=−8πGTab​.

Planar Einstein metric with cubic radial function (ds2=z−2[−Fdt2+dx2+dy2+F−1dz2],F=1−αz3)

Words: 69
Einstein field equations OurBigBook.com
This metric has Rab​=−3gab​ and cosmological constant Λ=−3 in unit curvature-radius normalization. Its regular static coframe is real when F>0 and z=0. Setting q=αz3, its six independent curvature 2-form coefficients are −(1−q) in the 03 and 12 planes and −(1+q/2) in the other four planes. Their Ricci contractions cancel q. Lovelock's theorem

Words: 56
Einstein field equations OurBigBook.com
In four dimensions, a local natural symmetric divergence-free metric tensor involving the metric and its derivatives of at most second order has the Lovelock form aGab​+bgab​ with constant coefficients. Generic nonlinear metric f(R) gravity instead has fourth-order terms and does not meet that derivative-order hypothesis. For affine f, the metric field equation is precisely of Lovelock form.

Vacuum spacetime (Tab​=0)

Words: 44
Vacuum Einstein equations OurBigBook.com
A vacuum spacetime has vanishing matter stress-energy tensor. In D>2 dimensions, tracing the Vacuum Einstein equations gives R=2DΛ/(D−2) and hence Rab​=2Λgab​/(D−2); in four dimensions this reduces to Rab​=Λgab​. With zero cosmological constant it is Ricci flat, although its full Riemann curvature tensor can be nonzero: a vacuum Kerr black hole and vacuum gravitational waves illustrate that distinction.

Einstein-Hilbert action

Words: 718 Articles: 12
Einstein field equations OurBigBook.com
The Einstein-Hilbert action is the integral of the Ricci scalar against the metric volume form. Varying it with respect to the metric gives the vacuum Einstein field equations, up to a boundary term.

Einstein-Cartan theory ((ea,ωab))

Words: 170 Articles: 2
Einstein-Hilbert action OurBigBook.com
An independent vierbein and metric-compatible spin connection allow spin density to source a torsion tensor. The connection equation from the Einstein-Hilbert action is algebraic in torsion. In the absence of connection-dependent matter it fixes the Levi-Civita connection. For a minimally coupled Dirac field, eliminating the connection generates a local four-fermion interaction. Thus its equivalent torsion-free second-order action includes this contact term; simply dropping it defines a different matter theory.

1.5-order formalism (δS/δω=0)

Words: 62
Einstein-Cartan theory OurBigBook.com
Solve the algebraic connection equation for ω(e,χ), but perform the remaining variations as though the connection were independent. The chain-rule contribution (δS/δω)δω vanishes on that solution. The resulting equations are those of the connection-eliminated second-order action. This is especially useful for local supersymmetry calculations, where the gravitino-induced contorsion is quadratic in fermions and the omitted induced connection variation would otherwise be lengthy.

Magnetically charged dilaton black hole (V=1−r+​/r,R2=r(r−r−​))

Words: 362 Articles: 4
Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory OurBigBook.com
The unit-coupling magnetic solution of Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory has ds2=−Vdt2+V−1dr2+R2dΩ22​, magnetic potential A=Pcosθdϕ, and dilaton ϕ=−21​log(1−r−​/r), where r−​=2P2/r+​ and r+​>0. Its physical asymptotic component has r>r−​. For 0≤r−​<r+​ it has a regular Killing horizon at r+​, area 4πr+​(r+​−r−​) and surface gravity 1/(2r+​). Its asymptotic mass is r+​/2. Causal structure of the magnetic dilaton black hole
Words: 102
Magnetically charged dilaton black hole OurBigBook.com
For r−​<r+​, an ingoing chart with v=t+r∗​ has metric −Vdv2+2dvdr+R2dΩ22​. In its future interior, ∇r=∂v​+V∂r​ is future timelike, so every future causal curve decreases r and cannot reach future null infinity. The singularity r=r−​ is spacelike. A Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates extension has the same qualitative Penrose diagram as Schwarzschild spacetime, with two exterior regions, a future black-hole interior and a past white hole interior. The past interior has the same radial interval but is not a black hole region; specifying the radial interval alone does not identify the future component. For r−​>r+​, the physical exterior r>r−​ has V>0 everywhere and outgoing null rays escape from arbitrarily near a timelike naked singularity.

Tensor-vector-scalar gravity (TeVeS)

Words: 53
Modified gravity OurBigBook.com
A relativistic gravitational theory constructed by Jacob Bekenstein using tensor, vector and scalar fields, together with a specified physical metric for matter. It illustrates how the low-acceleration phenomenology of MOND can be embedded in a theory with calculable light deflection. Matching rotation curves is not sufficient to establish its lensing or cosmological predictions.[ref]

Modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND)

Words: 62
Modified gravity OurBigBook.com
A low-acceleration modification associated with Mordehai Milgrom. For an isolated spherical system, gμ(g/a0​)=gN​, with μ(x)→1 at high x and μ(x)→x at low x. An isolated point-like baryonic distribution then gives vc4​=GMb​a0​ and a flat asymptotic galaxy rotation curve. The algebraic spherical relation is not a complete relativistic field theory and should not be assumed to hold vectorially for arbitrary mass distributions.[ref]

Scalar-tensor theory

A scalar-tensor theory uses a metric tensor and an additional scalar field to mediate gravity. A scalar multiplier of the Ricci scalar makes the curvature coupling depend on that field. The Brans-Dicke theory is a standard example; the choice of scalar potential and kinetic coupling distinguishes different members of the class.

Brans-Dicke theory (Lg​=ΦR+ωΦ−1(∇Φ)2)

Words: 177 Articles: 2
Scalar-tensor theory OurBigBook.com
The Brans-Dicke theory adds a dynamical scalar field Φ to a metric tensor theory of gravity, with constant dimensionless coupling ω. In a (+−−−) signature and the reversed-curvature convention used here, the displayed Lagrangian is supplemented by matter and integrated against the metric tensor volume density with prefactor 1/(16πG). Its scalar multiplier of the Ricci scalar lets the curvature coupling vary in spacetime. Both the metric tensor and scalar are varied; the scalar equation of Brans-Dicke theory follows by eliminating curvature between the scalar equation and the trace of the metric tensor equation. The original construction is described in Brans and Dicke's 1961 article.
