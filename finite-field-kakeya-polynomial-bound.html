Finite-field Kakeya polynomial bound (∣A∣≥(nq+n−1​)≥qn/n!)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Vector space over a finite field Finite-field Kakeya set
OurBigBook.com Words: 75
For a finite-field Kakeya set A⊆Fqn​, a smaller cardinality would give a nonzero polynomial of total degree of a polynomial at most q−1 vanishing on A, by the dimension of a bounded-total-degree polynomial space. Its restriction to each complete affine line in a vector space has q roots of a polynomial and degree less than q, hence vanishes identically. The top homogeneous polynomial part then vanishes in every direction. A polynomial of degree less than q in each variable cannot vanish everywhere on Fqn​ unless it is zero, giving a contradiction. For the prime field, this is the polynomial nonvanishing below the field size.
