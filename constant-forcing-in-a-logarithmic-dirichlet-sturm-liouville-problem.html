For ℓ>0, the logarithmic change of variable in a Sturm-Liouville problem sends this equation to −Y′′−αY=1 on (0,ℓ). The normalized eigenfunctions are 2/ℓ​sin(nπt/ℓ), with eigenvalues λn​=(nπ/ℓ)2−α. If every λn​ is nonzero, the Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction expansion gives
y(x)=n≥1n odd​∑​nπ[(nπ/ℓ)2−α]4sin(nπlogx/ℓ)​.​
(33)
Indeed the constant function has sine coefficients 2[1−(−1)n]/(nπ); division by each eigenvalue solves the equation in the operator domain. The series is uniformly convergent, with coefficients O(n−3), while applying the differential operator is justified in the weighted square-integrable sense. At a zero eigenvalue the solvability condition at a Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue must instead be checked: an odd resonant index makes the constant forcing incompatible, whereas an even resonant index permits solutions with an arbitrary multiple of that zero mode.
