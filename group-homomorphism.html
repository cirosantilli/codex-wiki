Group homomorphism (φ(gh)=φ(g)φ(h))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 648 Articles: 11
A group homomorphism is a map φ:G→H preserving the group operation: φ(gh)=φ(g)φ(h). It also preserves the identity element and inverse elements. In an additive group, the same requirement reads φ(g+h)=φ(g)+φ(h). A group homomorphism preserves a way of combining inputs, even when several inputs have the same output.
Reduction modulo three from Z/6Z to Z/3Z is a group homomorphism: reducing a sum agrees with adding the reduced residues. For example, 2+5=1 modulo six, and both sides reduce to one modulo three; the outputs 2 and 2 also add to one modulo three. This map is surjective but not injective, since zero and three both map to zero.
The sign of a permutation gives another example, from the symmetric group S3​ to the multiplicative group {1,−1}. A product of two odd permutations is even, matching (−1)(−1)=1. Its group kernel is A3​, the alternating group of even permutations. This shows how a group homomorphism can retain a useful feature while forgetting other details.
