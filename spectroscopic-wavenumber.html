Spectroscopic wavenumber (ν~=1/λ0​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Wavenumber
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
A spectroscopic wavenumber commonly means the reciprocal of the vacuum wavelength λ0​ of electromagnetic radiation, often in cm−1. It is an ordinary spatial frequency, so the vacuum angular wavenumber is K0​=2πν~. Specify vacuum or in-medium wavelength: the same frequency can have different wavelengths in different media. IUPAC's “wavenumber in medium” entry defines the in-medium reciprocal 1/λ and notes that unqualified spectroscopic usage often means the vacuum reciprocal.[ref]
For example, a vacuum wavelength of 500nm=5×10−5cm corresponds to ν~=20000cm−1=2×106m−1. Multiplication by 2π gives K0​=4π×106radm−1. Converting centimetres to metres and converting cycles to radians are separate operations.
