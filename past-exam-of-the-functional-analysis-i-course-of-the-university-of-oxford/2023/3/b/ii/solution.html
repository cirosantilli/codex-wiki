Write ψ=ψx0​​; its value at x0​, if that point is in (0,1), may be assigned arbitrarily because Lp spaces identify functions equal almost everywhere. Its L-infinity norm is one.
The supplied estimate shows that ψ cannot agree almost everywhere with any continuous function q: choosing g=−q would give 0≥∣q(x0​)∣+1. Hence
V=C([0,1])⊕⟨ψ⟩

is a direct sum, so every element has a unique expression g+cψ.
For any complex a with ∣a∣≤1, define the linear functional
La​(g+cψ)=g(x0​)+ca.
(52)
If c=0, the supplied estimate, applied to g/c, and absolute homogeneity of a norm give
∥g+cψ∥∞​=∣c∣∥g/c+ψ∥∞​≥∣g(x0​)∣+∣c∣≥∣g(x0​)+ca∣.

For c=0, the same bound follows from point evaluation. Thus ∥La​∥≤1, and La​(1)=1 makes its operator norm exactly one. The complex Hahn-Banach theorem extends La​ to a norm-one linear functional on all of X, with value a at ψ.
Conversely every norm-one extension satisfies ∣ℓx0​​(ψ)∣≤∥ψ∥∞​=1. We have therefore determined all possible values:
{ℓx0​​(ψx0​​):ℓx0​​ is a norm-one extension of Ex0​​}={a∈C:∣a∣≤1}.​
(54)
In particular, taking a=0 and a=1 proves nonuniqueness of norm-preserving point evaluation extensions.
