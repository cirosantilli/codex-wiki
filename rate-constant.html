Rate constant (k)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
A rate constant is the coefficient in a specified rate law, held fixed under specified conditions. It can describe an empirical overall chemical reaction, as well as an elementary reaction. Fixed temperature matters: changing temperature can change k, even for the same chemical reaction. See IUPAC Gold Book: order of reaction and rate constants and IUPAC Gold Book: rate coefficient.
For v=k[A]n, dimensional consistency requires
units(k)=(molL−1)1−ns−1.
(51)
A zero-order reaction therefore uses molL−1s−1; a first-order reaction uses s−1; a second-order reaction uses Lmol−1s−1. Comparing bare numerical values of k across different orders of reaction is meaningless without their units and concentrations. Even for the same order of reaction, state whether k multiplies the normalized reaction rate v or a particular reactant disappearance rate: a stoichiometric coefficient can change that convention.
