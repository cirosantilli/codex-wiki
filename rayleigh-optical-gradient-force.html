Rayleigh optical gradient force (Fgrad​=41​Re(α)∇∣E0​∣2)

The geometrical optics account of an optical trap adds the momentum flux changes of refracted and reflected light rays. It applies when the bead is large compared with the surrounding medium's wavelength. When the bead is much smaller, an induced electric dipole gives a different approximation. For a homogeneous dielectric sphere of radius a, define p(t)=Re[αE0​e−iωt], with E0​ the peak complex electric field and α its SI polarizability. In a transparent, nonmagnetic, nondispersive host,
ϵm​=ϵ0​nm2​,α=4πϵm​a3np2​+2nm2​np2​−nm2​​,I=21​nm​ϵ0​c∣E0​∣2,
(23)
where np​,nm​ are particle and host refractive indices, I is cycle-averaged light intensity, ϵ0​ is the vacuum permittivity and c is the speed of light. The quasistatic electric dipole approximation requires 2πnm​a/λ0​≪1, with λ0​ the vacuum wavelength, and a field nearly uniform across the bead.
For real quasistatic α, building an induced electric dipole gives instantaneous potential energy −αE2/2. Averaging over one optical cycle gives
Ugrad​=−41​α∣E0​∣2,Fgrad​=−∇Ugrad​=2nm​ϵ0​cα​∇I.
(24)
Thus np​>nm​ gives positive polarizability and attraction toward larger light intensity; np​<nm​ reverses this contribution. The factor 1/4 uses a peak field: substituting a root mean square field without changing that factor would halve the force incorrectly. Dimensionally α∣E0​∣2 is energy, so its gradient is force.
This leading conservative term does not remove the optical scattering force. Even a nonabsorbing induced electric dipole reradiates light: at fixed wavelength and index contrast, its Rayleigh scattering cross-section scales as a6, while α scales as a3. A smaller bead can therefore improve the ratio of gradient to scattering forces while weakening the well relative to kB​T, where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and T the temperature. Optical force balance and resistance to thermal escape are separate questions. Intermediate bead sizes require a wave-scattering calculation; neither a ray calculation nor this small-dipole expression is uniformly valid there.
