The nine-point finite-difference stencil for the Laplacian has expansion D9​u=Δu+h2Δ2u/12+h4(uxxxxxx​+uyyyyyy​+5uxxxxyy​+5uxxyyyy​)/360+O(h6). If Δu=f, differentiating this equation gives uxxxxyy​+uxxyyyy​=fxxyy​ and uxxxxxx​+uyyyyyy​=Δ2f−3fxxyy​. Hence the corrected compact equation
D9​U=f+12h2​Δf+360h4​(fxxxx​+4fxxyy​+fyyyy​)
(6)
has sixth-order normalized consistency of a numerical method. Source derivatives can be supplied analytically; numerical approximation of Δf must be fourth order and of the fourth derivatives second order. With exact square-grid Dirichlet boundary data and uniformly smooth solution, the discrete maximum principle inverse bound converts this residual into sixth-order nodal convergence. The unknown stencil stays compact even if more distant known source values are used.
