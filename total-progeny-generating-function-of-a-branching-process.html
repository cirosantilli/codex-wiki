Total progeny generating function of a branching process (g(s)=sF(g(s)))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Galton-Watson process Total progeny of a branching process
OurBigBook.com Words: 93
For an almost surely finite total progeny of a branching process, let F be the offspring probability generating function and g that of the total, including one ancestor. Conditional on k children, the total is one plus the sum of k independent descendant totals. Independence and the law of total expectation give g(s)=sF(g(s)) for ∣s∣≤1, including the empty-product case k=0. If the offspring expected value is m=F′(1−)<1, differentiation for 0<s<1 gives g′(s)=F(g(s))/(1−sF′(g(s))). The denominator is positive because F′(g(s))≤m. Letting s↑1 and using the monotone convergence theorem on the derivative series proves ET=1/(1−m), establishing finiteness rather than assuming it. For Poisson branching process offspring, F(z)=em(z−1).
