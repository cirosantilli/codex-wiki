Stationary points are fixed points of Newton's method (∇f(p)=0⟹N(p)=p)

For a twice continuously differentiable real function, define the Newton map in optimization N(p)=p−H(p)−1∇f(p) wherever its Hessian matrix H is invertible. Every stationary point in this domain is a fixed point, because its gradient is zero. This includes a local maximum and a saddle point of a scalar function, as well as a local minimum. Thus Newton's method in optimization needs a separate minimum classification or a method for ensuring descent when it is used for minimization problem.
For example, f(x,y)=(x+y2)2+(x2+y)2 has a stationary point at (−1/2,−1/2), with Hessian matrix

Its eigenvalues are −1 and 7, so it is a saddle point of a scalar function, but starting Newton's method in optimization there gives a constant sequence. Its value is 1/8, whereas the global minimum zero is attained at (0,0) and (−1,−1).
