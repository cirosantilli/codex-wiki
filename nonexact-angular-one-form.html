Nonexact angular one-form (ω=(−ydx+xdy)/(x2+y2))

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential form Exterior derivative Closed and exact differential forms Closed differential form
OurBigBook.com Words: 325 Articles: 1
On the punctured plane, this one-form is closed and locally the differential of the polar angle. Its line integral around a closed curve is 2π times the winding number. A once-winding circle therefore has nonzero integral, proving that no global single-valued angle potential exists and the form is not an exact differential form.
For a counterclockwise circle of radius R>0, substitute x=Rcost, y=Rsint. The pullback of a differential form is
ω=R2R2(sin2t+cos2t)​dt=dt,∮ω=2π​.
(9)
The radius cancels. In coordinates, P=−y/(x2+y2) and Q=x/(x2+y2) satisfy ∂x​Q=∂y​P away from the origin, so dω=0 there. The nonzero integral is a global obstruction to being an exact differential form, not a nonzero local exterior derivative.
Compare this with the smooth one-form η=(−ydx+xdy)/2 on the whole plane. Here dη=dx∧dy, and its integral around a radius-R circle is πR2, the enclosed area. The Generalized Stokes theorem applies to the full disc for η. For ω, that disc crosses the excluded origin, where the one-form is undefined. On an annulus the theorem applies, but its induced inner boundary is clockwise: the two integrals 2π and −2π cancel. This explains exactly why closedness and nonzero circulation can coexist.
