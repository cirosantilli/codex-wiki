Radioactive decay (N(t)=N0​e−λt)

 Home Physics Nuclear physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 58
Radioactive decay is the spontaneous transformation of an unstable atomic nucleus or nuclear state. Its timing can be unpredictable for an individual atomic nucleus while a large population has a reproducible average behavior. A constant nuclear decay constant λ>0 gives the familiar exponential decay law. The half-life describes how quickly the parent population falls, while radioactive activity describes its rate of transformations.[ref]
This connection between randomness and a regular population law makes radioactive decay useful for measuring elapsed time and for understanding sources of radiogenic heating. It also distinguishes two questions in an experiment: what the source emits, and what an instrument records. Becquerel's uranium-radiation experiment used a photographic plate; the Curie ionization-current measurement measured an electrical consequence of radiation. Their readouts reveal different aspects of the same investigation.
