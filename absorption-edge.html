Absorption edge

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation X-ray X-ray attenuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
An absorption edge is an abrupt change in photon absorption as incident photon energy crosses an electronic excitation threshold. For inner-shell photoionization, a new vacancy-production channel opens near the shell's binding energy. This threshold concerns incident energy; a Characteristic X-ray concerns a subsequent difference of atomic energies. They are different observations. Barkla's lecture explicitly treats characteristic emission as requiring a critical excitation condition, while discussing characteristic radiation separately from scattering.[ref]
In the stipulated X-ray fluorescence model, the K threshold is 10.0keV, whereas the K lines are 8.5 and 9.7keV. An absorber made of a different material could have its own absorption edge between those line energies, preferentially removing the upper line. Actual near-edge structure and attenuation values require material data; the following deliberately chosen coefficients are not such data.
