Bounded two-dimensional capacitor field model (∂x2​ϕ+∂y2​ϕ=0)

OurBigBook.com Words: 335
The diagrams solve an explicitly bounded Dirichlet problem in a uniform charge-free medium of constant permittivity. Take a width scale W=1 and terminal electric potential difference V=1. Two zero-thickness ideal plate segments occupy ∣x∣≤W/2 at y=±d/2, with electric potentials ±V/2. Fix ϕ=0 on the entire rectangle x=±2W, y=±1.5W. This boundary is an additional enclosing equipotential, rather than a condition at infinity. The model is invariant along the omitted third direction. The connection between the electric potential, its gradient, and the charge-free Laplace equation follows from electrostatics.[ref]
On a square mesh of spacing h=W/160, the finite difference method imposes
ϕi,j​=41​(ϕi+1,j​+ϕi−1,j​+ϕi,j+1​+ϕi,j−1​)
(9)
at free nodes, while plate and outer nodes retain their prescribed electric potentials. There are 641×481 nodes. Each plotted gap aligns exactly with the mesh; the smallest gap has 16 mesh intervals. The generators solve the resulting linear system by the conjugate gradient method, stopping when the maximum dimensionless five-point residual is below 2×10−10, and use central finite differences to approximate E=−∇ϕ away from plate nodes.
The discrete maximum principle keeps the computed electric potential between −V/2 and +V/2. Reflection checks require evenness in x and oddness in y; halving h tests discretization effects, while enlarging the outer rectangle tests sensitivity to its artificial boundary conditions. These are different checks: a small residual only establishes that the discrete equations were solved. Differentiating near zero-thickness endpoints is especially sensitive to the mesh, so the plots do not report a converged edge peak. No total electric charge, exact open-domain three-dimensional capacitance, or measured material property is extracted from this model.
For a bounded numerical check, compare h=W/80 with the plotted h=W/160 at midplane positions x/W=0,0.25,0.40,0.50,0.60,0.80. At d/W=0.10, the largest change in −Ey​d/V is approximately 0.0265, at x/W=0.50; the other five sampled changes are below 0.00472. At d/W=0.25, moving the outer rectangle from ±2W×±1.5W to ±3W×±2W while retaining h=W/80 changes these samples by at most approximately 0.00622. These calculated sensitivities support the qualitative comparison and expose its limits; they are not experimental uncertainties or evidence that the artificial boundary has converged to infinity.
