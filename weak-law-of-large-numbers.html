Weak law of large numbers (Xn​P​μ)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Convergence of random variables Convergence in probability
OurBigBook.com Words: 930 Articles: 11
Let X1​,X2​,… be real independent and identically distributed random variables with E∣X1​∣<∞ and expected value μ=E[X1​]. The weak law of large numbers states that their sample mean has convergence in probability to μ: for every fixed ε>0,
P(​Xn​−μ​≥ε)⟶0,Xn​=n1​∑j=1n​Xj​.
(7)
Thus a large sample is unlikely to give an average with a specified fixed-size error. The strong law of large numbers makes the stronger statement of almost sure convergence along an entire sequence. Under the displayed assumptions both laws apply; their names distinguish the conclusions, not two competing predictions.
When the variance σ2 is finite, variance additivity for independent random variables gives Var(Xn​)=σ2/n. The Chebyshev inequality immediately supplies a useful quantitative form and a brief proof:
P(​Xn​−μ​≥ε)≤min(1,nε2σ2​).
(8)
For independent trials with a Bernoulli distribution of parameter p=0.2, the expected value is 0.2 and the variance is 0.16. At n=1000, the chance of an error of at least 0.1 is therefore at most 0.016. The exact binomial distribution gives a much smaller probability, as the figure under the strong law of large numbers shows. The bound is conservative, but it is valid for every sample size under these assumptions and does not require a normal approximation.
The weak law of large numbers addresses a fixed tolerance ε. The central limit theorem instead resolves errors on the shrinking scale σ/n​ and gives their approximate normal distribution. In particular, the weak law of large numbers alone supplies neither a bell-shaped error law nor a universal rate of approach. For example, a Pareto distribution with minimum one and shape 3/2 has expected value three but infinite variance: both the weak law of large numbers and strong law of large numbers apply, while the finite-variance statement of the central limit theorem does not.
