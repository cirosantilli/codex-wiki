Polynomial identity testing

 Home Computer science Algorithm Randomized algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 989 Articles: 3
Polynomial identity testing asks whether a polynomial represented implicitly, such as by an arithmetic circuit or a remainder computation, is the zero polynomial.
Here zero means that every coefficient vanishes after collecting equal monomials in the polynomial ring. Testing equality of two polynomials A,B amounts to testing P=A−B for zero. An arithmetic circuit can permit cheap polynomial evaluation even when expanding all monomials is expensive. The random evaluation test for a polynomial identity exploits this: one nonzero value disproves the polynomial identity, while a known total degree limits how often a nonzero polynomial can evade detection.[ref]
For example, for an integer n≥1, ∏i=1n​(1+xi​) has 2n distinct monomials, one for each subset of the n variables. Its factored expression needs only n additions and n−1 multiplications for one polynomial evaluation. Evaluating the compact expression avoids creating its expanded list of coefficients. The saving is especially attractive in many variables; it does not make a zero result into a proof of identity.
