Symmetric and alternating parts of a bilinear form (B=S+K)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Bilinear form
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
Over a field whose characteristic is not two, a bilinear form has a unique decomposition into a symmetric bilinear form S and an alternating bilinear form K:
S(v,w)=2B(v,w)+B(w,v)​,K(v,w)=2B(v,w)−B(w,v)​.
(5)
Adding and subtracting the equations B(v,w)=S(v,w)+K(v,w) and B(w,v)=S(v,w)−K(v,w) both proves uniqueness and gives these formulas. In matrix notation, S has matrix (A+AT)/2 and K has skew-symmetric matrix (A−AT)/2.[ref]
The diagonal values q(v)=B(v,v) alone do not determine a general bilinear form, because K(v,v)=0 for every vector. For the matrix of a bilinear form in the example above,
A=symmetric part(11​11​)​​+alternating part(0−1​10​)​​,q(x,y)=(x+y)2.
(6)
Thus two different bilinear forms, represented by A and its symmetric matrix part, have exactly the same quadratic form. To recover a bilinear form from its diagonal, one needs extra information such as the hypothesis that it is a symmetric bilinear form.
