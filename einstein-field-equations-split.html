Einstein field equations (Gμν​+Λgμν​=8πGTμν​/c4)

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 39
With signature (−,+,+,+) and vanishing cosmological constant, the Einstein field equations are
Rμν​−21​gμν​R=κTμν​.
(1)
In four spacetime dimensions, contraction with gμν gives the useful trace relation R=−κT.
In the usual commutator convention for the Riemann curvature tensor, κ=8πG/c4: G is the gravitational constant, whereas Gμν​ is the Einstein tensor. The Ricci tensor Rμν​ and scalar curvature R encode contractions of curvature. The stress-energy tensor Tμν​ includes energy density, momentum flow and stresses, so pressure also gravitates. A cosmological constant adds Λgμν​ to the left-hand side. See Tong's Einstein equations and matter coupling.
For a concrete consequence, set Tμν​=0 and Λ=0. The trace gives R=0, and substitution gives Rμν​=0. Yet the exterior Schwarzschild spacetime still has the tidal forces computed under geodesic deviation: vanishing Ricci tensor does not imply vanishing Riemann curvature tensor. Geometry carries gravitational effects through empty space. Einstein presented the final matter equations on 25 November 1915, including the essential trace correction, in Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, pages 844-847, especially equation (2a). His symbols and sign conventions differ from modern ones; the displayed formula uses modern notation.
