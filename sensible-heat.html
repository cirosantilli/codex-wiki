Sensible heat (Qsens​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat
OurBigBook.com Words: 362
Sensible heat is transferred heat associated with a temperature change within a single thermodynamic phase. By contrast, latent heat can change the phase fraction while the temperature stays fixed during equilibrium phase coexistence. Neither name denotes an additional stored substance: both describe how transferred heat participates in a process.
For fixed mass m and composition, use the heat capacity appropriate to the heating constraint. Under the quasistatic process assumptions in heat capacity, with only pressure-volume work and unchanged bulk kinetic energy and potential energy, fixed-pressure heating gives
Qsens​=m∫T1​T2​​cp​(T)dT,Qsens​=mcp​(T2​−T1​)if cp​ is constant.
(2)
Here cp​ is specific heat capacity at constant pressure; fixed-volume heating instead uses specific heat capacity at constant volume cV​. A cooling step has negative Qsens​ with the sign convention in heat. A temperature difference has the same numerical value in units of the kelvin or degree Celsius. The integral follows by adding the small transfers δQ=mcp​(T)dT, rather than assuming a constant heat capacity.
As a stylized ice-to-water example for a fixed-composition closed system at fixed pressure 1atm, take m=0.5kg, an initial temperature of −10∘C, melting at 0∘C, and a final temperature of 20∘C. Assume constant specific heat capacity at constant pressure values cice​=2.1kJkg−1K−1 and cwater​=4.18kJkg−1K−1, and latent heat of fusion Lf​=334kJkg−1. These are stipulated, rounded model values; ignore the container and heat losses, retain all the material, and assume only pressure-volume work under the quasistatic process conditions above. The three steps are
Qice​Qmelt​Qwater​Qtotal​​=0.5×2.1×10=10.5kJ,=0.5×334=167kJ,=0.5×4.18×20=41.8kJ,=10.5+167+41.8=219.3kJ.​
(3)
Only the first and third terms are sensible heat; the middle term is latent heat. The plateau begins after 10.5kJ and ends after 177.5kJ of total supplied heat. At 94kJ total input, half the mass is liquid water and the temperature remains 0∘C. The equilibrium phase transition temperature depends on pressure; this example stipulates a single fixed pressure. [ref]
This split makes heating budgets easy to interpret: melting consumes 167/219.3≈76.2% of the supplied heat in this model despite causing no temperature rise. Within either single thermodynamic phase, the heating curve slope is dT/dQ=1/(mcp​); a larger heat capacity makes the slope shallower. A flat interval represents changing phase fraction, so it cannot be modeled by applying Q=mcp​ΔT across the phase transition. The complementary constructed example in latent heat tracks that phase fraction explicitly.
