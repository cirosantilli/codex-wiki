Signed curvature (k=det(T,dT/ds))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Curvature
OurBigBook.com Words: 334 Articles: 1
Fix the standard orientation of a vector space on the plane. For an oriented regular curve in that plane, let T=(Tx​,Ty​) be its unit tangent vector and let NL​=(−Ty​,Tx​) be its left unit normal, obtained by an anticlockwise rotation through π/2. Its signed curvature k, also commonly written κs​, is defined by
dsdT​=kNL​,k=det(T,dsdT​),κ=∣k∣.
(8)
Here s increases with the chosen traversal and det is the determinant. Positive signed curvature means turning left; negative signed curvature means turning right. The left unit normal exists even when k=0; when k<0 it points opposite to the principal normal vector. See Ghomi's university lecture notes, section 1.10.
For an arbitrary regular parametric curve r(t)=(x(t),y(t)) traversed with increasing t, the signed curvature is
k(t)=(x′(t)2+y′(t)2)3/2x′(t)y′′(t)−y′(t)x′′(t)​.
(9)
For the anticlockwise circle r(t)=(Rcost,Rsint), the numerator is R2 and the denominator is R3, so k=1/R. For the clockwise traversal q(t)=(Rcost,−Rsint), the numerator is −R2, so k=−1/R, while unsigned curvature is 1/R in both cases. On this circle NL​ points inward for anticlockwise traversal and outward for clockwise traversal. For any simple closed regular curve traversed anticlockwise, NL​ is the inward unit normal, but signed curvature can still be negative along nonconvex portions.
To see why the formula uses the cube of the speed, write r′=vT and r′′=v′T+v2(dT/ds). The term parallel to T contributes zero to the determinant, giving det(r′,r′′)=v3det(T,dT/ds)=v3k. Under a reparametrization q(u)=r(ϕ(u)) with ϕ′=0, the numerator gains a factor (ϕ′)3 and the denominator gains ∣ϕ′∣3. Thus orientation-preserving reparametrization preserves signed curvature, while reversing traversal changes its sign at corresponding points. Unsigned curvature is preserved in either case.
Locally write the unit tangent vector as T=(cosθ,sinθ). Differentiating with respect to arc length gives k=dθ/ds: signed curvature records the direction as well as the amount of turning. Its integral therefore measures net turning, as in the turning tangent theorem; the integral of ∣k∣ is total curvature and counts both left and right turns positively.
