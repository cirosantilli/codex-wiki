Bubble-chamber track readout

Stereoscopy enables three-dimensional track reconstruction.[ref] Calibrated camera positions and optical geometry are needed before a photographed arc becomes a physical radius of curvature. Neutral particles do not directly leave the same continuous ionization trail as charged particles; an interaction or decay can instead reveal them through charged secondary tracks. A photon's inferred path between interactions is not a photographed luminous line.
In a uniform magnetic field, with negligible stopping power and elastic scattering over the fitted segment, the transverse projection is a circle. The Lorentz force turns transverse momentum at angular rate v⊥​/r, giving
rp⊥​v⊥​​=∣q∣v⊥​B,p⊥​=∣q∣Br.
(35)
Here r is measured in the plane perpendicular to the magnetic field. The relation remains valid with relativistic physical momentum; it does not require p=mv. Thus curvature determines magnetic rigidity, not mass or kinetic energy alone. For chosen ∣q∣=e, B=1.5T and r=0.40m, p⊥​≃0.180GeV/c. This is an invented example, not a fit to a historical photograph. Reconstructing a three-dimensional helix also determines the component parallel to the magnetic field; the transverse radius of curvature alone does not. Determining the sign of electric charge additionally requires the field direction and the direction of travel, as in the charge-sign ambiguity of a curved track.
