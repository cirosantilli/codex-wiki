Uniqueness of stationary distributions for an irreducible countable chain

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Stationary distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
An irreducible Markov chain on a finite or countably infinite state space has at most one stationary distribution. On a countably infinite state space it exists exactly when the chain is positive recurrent. In that case, for every state i,
πi​=Ei​Ti+​1​,
(78)
where Ti+​ is the first return time. The expression determines every component uniquely. The formula follows by dividing the one visit to i in each return cycle by the mean recurrence time; the strong law of large numbers for successive cycles makes this occupation fraction precise.
Every irreducible finite Markov chain is positive recurrent and has exactly one stationary distribution. On an infinite state space, an irreducible null recurrent Markov chain or transient Markov chain has none. For example, the simple symmetric random walk on the integers is null recurrent. Aperiodicity affects convergence of the one-time laws, but is unnecessary for existence or uniqueness of a stationary distribution.
