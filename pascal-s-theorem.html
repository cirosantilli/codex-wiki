Pascal's theorem

 ... Geometry and topology Algebraic geometry Degree of an algebraic variety Degree of a projective curve Bézout's theorem Projective plane curve
OurBigBook.com Words: 68
For six distinct points on an irreducible conic in the complex projective plane, the three intersections of opposite sides of the resulting hexagon are collinear. Form the two cubics given by the products of alternate side equations. They meet at the six conic points and the three opposite-side intersections. Applying residual intersection through a curve pencil with degrees three and two places the last three points on a line. This is the conic counterpart of Pappus's hexagon theorem for two lines.
