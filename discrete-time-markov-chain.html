Discrete-time Markov chain (P(Yn+1​=j∣Fn​)=PYn​j​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain
OurBigBook.com Words: 476 Articles: 2
A time-homogeneous Discrete-time Markov chain is a sequence of random variables Y0​,Y1​,… indexed by nonnegative integers, with the displayed conditional probabilities. Here Fn​ is its natural filtration and P is a row-stochastic transition matrix. The Markov property removes dependence on earlier observations once Yn​ is specified; the time-homogeneous Markov property makes P independent of n.
Consider states S (sunny) and R (rainy), with the constructed transition matrix
P=(0.80.4​0.20.6​).
(13)
Rows give today's state and columns tomorrow's, in order S,R. These numbers define an illustrative model, with one step interpreted as one day; they are not estimates of real weather. Let qn​=P(Yn​=S). The law of total probability gives the recurrence relation
qn+1​=0.8qn​+0.4(1−qn​)=0.4+0.4qn​.
(14)
Subtracting 2/3 makes the deviation multiply by 0.4 at each step. Thus
qn​=32​+(q0​−32​)0.4n.
(15)
Starting sunny gives q0​=1, q1​=0.8, q2​=0.72 and q3​=0.688; starting rainy gives q0​=0 and q2​=0.56. The two predictions approach the same stationary distribution while retaining a shrinking trace of the initial probability distribution.
An expected hitting time answers a different question. Starting sunny, let TR​=inf{n≥0:Yn​=R}. Every day before the first rain remains sunny, so
PS​(TR​=k)=0.8k−10.2(k≥1).
(16)
This geometric distribution has finite expected value. A first-step calculation gives hS​=1+0.8hS​+0.2hR​, with hR​=0, hence hS​=5 days. The same result follows from the geometric series ES​TR​=∑n≥0​0.8n=5. This is a mean waiting time, not a prediction that rain first occurs exactly five days later.
A probability row vector π is a stationary distribution when πP=π. The Markov chain is reversible in that law when detailed balance holds: πi​Pij​=πj​Pji​. Multiplying those equations along a finite path gives equal forward and reversed stationary path probabilities.
