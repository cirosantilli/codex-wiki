Gaussian beam with one transverse coordinate (E=q−1/2e−Az2/q)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Gaussian beam
OurBigBook.com Words: 60
For the free parabolic wave equation Ex​=iEzz​/(2k), input E(0,z)=e−Az2 with ReA>0 propagates as
E(x,z)=q(x)−1/2e−Az2/q(x),q(x)=1+2iAx/k.
(1)
The branch is continuous from q(0)−1/2=1. One-dimensional transverse Fresnel propagation or two Gaussian integrals prove the formula. With A=D−2+ik/(2F), the squared envelope is ∣q∣−1exp[−2z2/(D2∣q∣2)]. The amplitude power is −1/2 because there is only one transverse coordinate. A two-transverse-coordinate Gaussian beam instead has power −1 when both coordinates share the same q.
