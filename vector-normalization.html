Vector normalization (v=v/∥v∥,v=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Vector Unit vector
OurBigBook.com Words: 220 Articles: 1
Vector normalization converts a nonzero vector in a normed vector space into a unit vector. Absolute homogeneity of a norm gives
​∥v∥v​​=∥v∥∥v∥​=1.
(16)
The direction is unchanged by multiplying by a positive real scalar. For c>0, ∥cv∥=c∥v∥ and cv=v; for a negative real scalar, cv=−v. Thus (3,4) and (6,8) normalize to the same unit vector, while (−3,−4) normalizes to its negative. This decomposition is useful for specifying a direction independently of a force, velocity or displacement magnitude.
In a complex normed vector space, a nonzero complex scalar c instead gives cv=(c/∣c∣)v. The remaining complex argument is not removed by normalization. For a pure quantum state, vectors differing only by this overall global phase represent the same physical state; vector normalization alone does not identify them.
The zero vector cannot be normalized this way. The construction also applies in a function space: an eigenfunction of a Sturm-Liouville problem can be scaled to unit weighted norm by
ynormalized​(x)=∫ab​w(s)∣y(s)∣2ds​y(x)​.
(17)
The positive weight w specifies the weighted inner product. Multiplication by this nonzero constant preserves the eigenvalue equation and its homogeneous boundary conditions.
