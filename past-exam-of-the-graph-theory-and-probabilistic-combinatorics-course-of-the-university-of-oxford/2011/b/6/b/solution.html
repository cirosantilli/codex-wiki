Explore the family tree one individual at a time, maintaining a queue of individuals not yet explored. Initially its size is Y0​=1. Exploring individual t removes it and adds its independent offspring Zt​, so before the queue empties,
Yt​=1+∑j=1t​(Zj​−1).
(44)
One may define the independent increments indefinitely, but the tree exploration stops at T=inf{t:Yt​=0}. This hitting time is the total progeny, including the ancestor. Since each increment is at least −1, a queue cannot jump below zero before hitting zero. If it never hits zero, the family has infinitely many individuals.
Suppose kp<1. A Chernoff bound in moment-generating-function form says that for independent Wj​ and θ>0,
P(∑j=1n​Wj​≥0)≤∏j=1n​EeθWj​.
(45)
Here take Wj​=Zj​−1, with
M(θ)=Eeθ(Z−1)=e−θ(1−p+peθ)k.
(46)
We have M(0)=1 and M′(0)=kp−1<0, so some θ>0 has M(θ)<1. Let c=−logM(θ)>0. If T>n, the queue is still positive after n explorations; its integer value is at least one, giving ∑j=1n​(Zj​−1)≥0. Therefore
P(T>n)≤M(θ)n=e−cn,n≥1.​
(47)
This proves the claimed bound without appealing to a general branching-process tail theorem.
The argument proves the more general exponential total progeny tail for bounded subcritical offspring.
