First take 0<p<1 and kp>1. Part (a) gives an extinction probability η∈(0,1). Write pj​=P(Z=j). Conditional on the ancestor having j children, extinction requires extinction of all j independent descendant trees and has probability ηj. Bayes' rule therefore gives the conditional offspring law
p​j​=P(Z=j∣extinction)=pj​ηj−1.
(48)
Its sum is f(η)/η=1. Conditional on extinction of all descendant trees, those trees remain independent, each with its own extinction-conditioned law. Thus the entire conditioned process is a Galton–Watson process with offspring generating function
f​(s)=ηf(ηs)​.
(49)
For binomial offspring, using η=(1−p+pη)k gives
Z∼Bi(k,p​),p​=1−p+pηpη​.​
(50)
Its mean is kp​=f′(η)<1. Indeed, f(s)−s is strictly convex and has its two zeros at η and one, so its derivative at the first zero is strictly negative. This proves subcriticality directly from the generating function.
Let T be total progeny, taking value infinity on survival. Apply part (b) to the conditioned process. For some c>0,
1−η≤P(T>n)=(1−η)+ηP(T>n)≤1−η+ηe−cn.​
(51)
Thus the unconditional tail tends to the positive survival probability 1−η, with an exponentially small finite-extinction contribution. It does not decay to zero.
If p=1 and kp>1, extinction has probability zero and conditioning on it does not specify a probability law. In that deterministic case total progeny is infinite and P(T>n)=1 for every finite n.
The nondegenerate law above is the binomial branching process conditioned on extinction.
