Phototaxis

Words: 346 Articles: 4
Biological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
Phototaxis is directed organism motion in response to light. An ideal spherical swimmer subject to light torque p×L and rotational resistance α aligns towards the light while ambient vorticity rotates it. Its deterministic orientation satisfies p˙​=ω×p/2+[L−(p⋅L)p]/α. The right-hand side is perpendicular to p and preserves its unit length.

Bioconvection

Words: 195 Articles: 2
Biological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
Bioconvection is buoyancy-driven fluid motion generated by a nonuniform concentration of swimming microorganisms. Upward swimming can create a dense upper layer, while a disturbance redistributes the cells and drives overturning. A cell conservation equation coupled to incompressible Newtonian fluid dynamics describes its linear stability under an appropriate dilute-suspension approximation.

The exactly uniform horizontal mode κ′=0 instead changes conserved total cell number and does not select the instability threshold.

Gyrotaxis

Words: 839 Articles: 12
Biological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
Gyrotaxis is the orientation response of swimming cells to the competition between gravity-induced torque and fluid rotation. A bottom-heavy spherical cell tends to point upward in still fluid, while ambient vorticity rotates it. The response changes the mean swimming direction and can focus cells or contribute to bioconvection.

Lighthill elongated-body theory

Words: 457 Articles: 5
Biological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
In the small-amplitude slender-body theory approximation, an undulating swimmer accelerates an added mass of fluid per unit length m(x). With D=∂t​+U∂x​ and transverse displacement h, the lateral force on the body is −D(mDh). Its reaction on the fluid has the opposite sign. For a periodic stroke and the usual negligible leading-edge added-mass contribution, the mean trailing-edge thrust is m⟨ht2​−U2hx2​⟩/2. This is a reactive inertial model, distinct from viscous resistive-force theory.

Recoil correction in elongated-body theory

Words: 311 Articles: 3
Lighthill elongated-body theory OurBigBook.com
A deforming free swimmer must satisfy total linear and angular momentum balances. The unknown lateral translation and yaw are its recoil correction. If the reference translation is the true centre of mass, the prescribed deformation must first have its mass-weighted mean removed. The body mass and its hydrodynamic added mass contribute with the same sign to its effective inertial coefficient.
