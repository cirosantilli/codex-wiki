Flux-distance degeneracy ((L,d)↦(k2L,kd))

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Radiative transfer Radiation-field moment Radiative flux Observed radiative flux
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
One observed radiative flux cannot determine both luminosity and Euclidean distance: for every k>0, replacing (L,d) by (k2L,kd) leaves F unchanged. In particular, 4×1026W at 40 parsecs produces the same observed radiative flux as 1026W at 20 parsecs. This is a concrete failure of identifiability in an inverse problem.
An independent stellar parallax fixes the distance and breaks this degeneracy; alternatively, a calibrated standard candle supplies the luminosity. Interstellar extinction can further reduce the observed radiative flux, and a photometric passband measures only a selected spectral range. At cosmological distances, use luminosity distance in the bolometric relation. Each inference must specify which radiative flux was measured and what propagation assumptions connect it to luminosity.
