Conic Carathéodory theorem

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex set Convex hull Carathéodory's theorem (convex hull)
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
In an N-dimensional real vector space, each member of a conic hull has a representation using at most N generators. To prove it, take a finite positive-coefficient representation with more than N terms. Its generators are linearly dependent, say ∑j​μj​sj​=0, with some μj​>0 after reversing the relation if necessary. Subtract tμj​ from each coefficient, taking t=minμj​>0​λj​/μj​. All coefficients stay nonnegative and at least one vanishes. Iterate. The bound differs from the N+1 bound for a convex hull because the coefficient sum is unrestricted.
