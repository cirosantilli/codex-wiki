Radius of a Maclaurin series from successive derivatives (R=limn→∞​​(n+1)f(n)(0)/f(n+1)(0)​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 90
Write the Maclaurin series coefficients as an​=f(n)(0)/n!. When the successive ratios exist eventually and have a limit, the ratio test gives the displayed radius of convergence, including zero and infinity. The unconditional formula is R−1=limsupn→∞​∣an​∣1/n, from the Cauchy-Hadamard theorem. Endpoints must be checked separately.
The existence and convergence of this power series do not by themselves imply that it equals f: equality requires analyticity or control of the Taylor remainder. The smooth function f(x)=e−1/x2 for x=0, f(0)=0, has all derivatives zero at zero, so its Maclaurin series is identically zero despite the nonzero values away from zero.
