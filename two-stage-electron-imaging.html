Two-stage electron imaging (m=m1​m2​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Electron optics Electron microscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 315
Consider two ideal thin electron lenses in successive imaging stages. In each stage choose positive object and image distances ui​,vi​ for a real image, measured from its effective lens plane, and neglect the image rotation of a magnetic electron lens. The paraxial approximation gives
fi​1​=ui​1​+vi​1​,mi​=−ui​vi​​,m=m1​m2​.
(7)
The product follows directly by writing y1​=m1​y0​ and y2​=m2​y1​. Here mi​ is transverse magnification, rather than the enlargement applied later to a photograph. Both inversions cancel in this planar model; actual magnetic electron lenses can additionally rotate the image.
For an original worked example, take u1​=40mm, v1​=80mm, u2​=50mm and v2​=150mm. Then
f1​=380​mm,f2​=37.5mm,m1​=−2,m2​=−3,m=6.
(8)
A 100μm object interval becomes 600μm at the screen. The second lens sits v1​+u2​=130mm after the first. These are calculated distances, not measurements of the 1931 instrument.
To measure magnification, divide a screen interval between corresponding grid features by the independently known object interval. Repeat over several periods to reduce endpoint uncertainty. For a proposed check, record accelerating voltage, lens electric currents, screen position and specimen orientation; compare one-stage and two-stage images, refocusing as necessary. Changing a lens electric current should change focus and scale, while a blank exposure checks detector background. Keep irradiation and recording enlargement controlled, and repeat the original setting to test drift or specimen damage. These are suggested checks, not a claim that every check was performed in 1931. A grid-scale measurement establishes magnification; resolving much finer features requires a separate target and contrast criterion.
