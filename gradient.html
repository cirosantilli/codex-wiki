Gradient (∇f)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Partial derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 371 Articles: 3
In Cartesian coordinates on Euclidean space, the gradient of a scalar-valued differentiable function is the vector of its first partial derivatives. It converts a scalar field, such as temperature or a height, into a vector field describing its local change.
For a unit vector u, the directional derivative is Du​f=u⋅∇f. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∣Du​f∣≤∣∇f∣. At a point where ∇f=0, the greatest increase per unit distance occurs along u=∇f/∣∇f∣, while the opposite direction gives the greatest decrease. These are first-order statements at the current point; they do not predict how the function behaves far along a straight path.
For f(x,y)=x2+2y2 at (1,1), ∇f=(2,4). Moving in the x direction gives rate 2 and moving in the y direction gives rate 4, but the steepest direction is (1,2)/5​, with
Du​f=25​​.
(10)
The direction (−2,1)/5​ has rate zero and is tangent to the level curve through that point. More generally, differentiating f(γ(t))=constant along a regular level curve gives ∇f⋅γ′=0, so the gradient is its normal vector.
This is useful when navigating a measured scalar field or applying gradient descent: one collection of partial derivatives predicts every infinitesimal directional change. The derivative assumes a differentiable function, not merely the existence of all directional derivatives. [ref].
