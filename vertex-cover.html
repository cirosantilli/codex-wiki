Vertex cover

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 656 Articles: 3
A vertex cover chooses vertices that collectively touch every edge of a graph. Think of placing monitors at junctions: if a monitor watches every connection incident to its junction, a vertex cover watches all connections. The minimum vertex cover asks how few monitors suffice. This is a model of covering connections through their endpoints, rather than of monitoring distances in the drawing.
For a finite simple graph G=(V,E), a vertex cover is a subset C⊆V satisfying
{u,v}∈E⟹u∈C or v∈C.
(19)
Both endpoints may be selected; the condition requires at least one. An isolated vertex covers no edge, so a minimum vertex cover never needs it.[ref]
Take the cycle graph C5​ with vertices 0,1,2,3,4 and edges 01,12,23,34,40. The set C={1,3,4} is a vertex cover: vertex 1 covers 01 and 12, vertex 3 covers 23 and 34, and vertex 4 covers 40 as well as 34. In particular, the closing edge 40 is covered even though vertex 0 is unselected.
The unselected vertices I=V∖C={0,2} form an independent set. This is the key connection: an edge missed by C would have both endpoints in I, precisely what an independent set forbids. For every subset C⊆V,
C is a vertex cover⟺V∖C is an independent set.
(20)
Here V∖C is the complement of a set within V; the graph itself keeps all its edges. The complement graph is a different construction.[ref]
The endpoints of any maximal matching form a vertex cover: an edge avoiding all endpoints would enlarge that matching in a graph. Consequently a graph with no matching in a graph of size s has a vertex cover of size at most 2(s−1). This supplies a convenient vertex cover, but it need not be a minimum vertex cover.
