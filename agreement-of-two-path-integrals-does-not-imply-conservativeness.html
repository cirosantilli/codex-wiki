Agreement of two path integrals does not imply conservativeness

 ... Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Fundamental theorem for line integrals Path independence
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
Equality of line integrals on two selected paths is a necessary test for a conservative vector field, but it is not sufficient. The paths may both miss the component responsible for nonzero curl.
For an explicit example, take W(x,y,z)=(z−y,x−z,y−x)T. Its curl is the nonzero constant 2(1,1,1)T, so it is not a gradient field on R3. Nevertheless, on the plane x=y, the field equals (z−x,x−z,0)T. Every tangent vector to that plane has equal first and second components, giving zero dot product with W. Thus every line integral of W along a path confined to that plane is zero, regardless of endpoints or the path's shape.
In particular, a broken straight path and a genuinely curved path in that plane can have matching line integrals while revealing nothing about global path independence. To establish a conservative vector field, construct a vector-field potential, or verify the appropriate global criterion such as zero curl on a simply connected domain.
