Smoothing bandwidth (h)

 ... Probability and statistics Statistical inference Nonparametric statistics Nonparametric regression Local polynomial regression Kernel for nonparametric regression
OurBigBook.com Words: 433 Articles: 2
A smoothing bandwidth h>0 determines the scale over which a regression kernel averages data, or a kernel for density estimation spreads each observation. It has the same units as the observations. In kernel density estimation, changing h changes the visible detail while preserving the total integral one. For a Gaussian density kernel, each bump has standard deviation h and its individual contribution has peak height 1/(nh2π​).
If the smoothing bandwidth is too small relative to the spacings in a statistical sample, the kernel density estimator shows narrow spikes tied to individual observations: apparent peaks may be sampling accidents. If it is too large relative to features of the unknown probability density function, it blurs distinct groups together and spreads fitted probability far from the observations. An intermediate smoothing bandwidth can reveal broad structure, but visual smoothness alone cannot establish accuracy.
The first three panels use exactly (−1.8,−1.4,−1.1,−0.7,0.7,1.1,1.4,1.8) with smoothing bandwidths 0.08, 0.35 and 1.2, respectively. Every curve has area one; the two groups in the observed statistical sample become less distinct as h increases. No true probability density function is specified in this illustration, so the middle panel is illustrative, not a certified optimal fit. The fourth panel uses (0.1,0.4,0.9) and h=0.5 to illustrate boundary bias of a kernel density estimator.
For a fixed nonnegative kernel for density estimation K with finite squared integral, a probability density function f that is bounded and continuous at an interior point x with f(x)>0, and a deterministic sequence h=hn​↓0, the pointwise sampling variance obeys
Var(f​n,h​(x))∼nhf(x)​∫R​K(u)2du.
(14)
Thus decreasing the smoothing bandwidth increases the leading sampling variance while reducing interior smoothing bias of an estimator: this is a bias-variance tradeoff. The conditions hn​→0 and nhn​→∞ express two goals, increasingly fine resolution and enough observations contributing locally. Practical methods such as plug-in bandwidth selection use the data to choose h; pointwise optimal kernel bandwidth depends on local features of the unknown probability density function. The smoothing bandwidth remains a tuning choice in nonparametric statistics, and knowing the support of a probability distribution can require more than a width adjustment near a boundary.
