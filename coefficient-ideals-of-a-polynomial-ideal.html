Coefficient ideals of a polynomial ideal (An​(K))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Polynomial ring
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
For an ideal K⊆R[t], let An​(K) consist of the coefficients at tn of elements of K of polynomial degree at most n, including the zero polynomial. Each An​(K) is an ideal of R, and multiplication of representing polynomials by t shows that An​(K)⊆An+1​(K).
If R is a Noetherian ring, this chain stabilizes at some N. Choose finite generating sets of an ideal for A0​(K),…,AN​(K), and for each nonzero generator choose a representing polynomial fnj​∈K with that leading coefficient and polynomial degree n. These finitely many polynomials generate K: for any nonzero f∈K of polynomial degree d, use the chosen generators at level min(d,N), multiplied by suitable powers of t, to cancel its leading coefficient. Induction on polynomial degree finishes the argument. This proves the Hilbert basis theorem without assuming that R is an integral domain.
