Shortest cycle by repeated shortest-path trees

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Shortest path problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
In a positive-weight directed graph, run the Dijkstra algorithm from each source s and minimize ds​(u)+w(u,s) over closing edges. In an undirected graph, minimize ds​(u)+w(u,v)+ds​(v) only over edges absent from the current shortest-path tree. A tree edge would otherwise give a spurious traversal out and back. A non-tree candidate contains a genuine cycle no longer than its score, even when the two tree routes share a prefix.
For the undirected optimum, choose a shortest cycle and a source on it. If an incident cycle edge is absent from the tree, its candidate is bounded by the cycle length. Otherwise its two neighbours lie in distinct tree branches, and some edge on the remaining cycle segment crosses branches. That edge is non-tree; shortest distances to its endpoints are bounded by the two complementary cycle segments, again giving a candidate no greater than the optimum. Thus the minimum candidate equals the shortest-cycle length. Repeated Dijkstra algorithm runs take O((∣V∣2+∣V∣∣E∣)log(∣V∣+1)) running time with a binary heap.
