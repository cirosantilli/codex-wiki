The Basel problem supplies the total sum, and the integral test for convergence gives the displayed tail bound. The First Borel-Cantelli lemma makes the limsup of events have probability zero. In fact, the tail bound implies that the probability of even one alarm at or after m is at most 1/m; for m=100, it is at most 0.01. This bound remains valid for dependent events with the same individual probabilities.
For pn​=1/(n+1), the harmonic series diverges, so the Second Borel-Cantelli lemma makes the limsup of events have probability one. There is also an exact finite-horizon calculation using independent events:

For any fixed m, this finite product of numbers tends to zero as M→∞. Every infinite tail therefore contains an alarm almost surely, even though the individual probabilities tend to zero.
