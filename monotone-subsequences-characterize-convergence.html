Monotone subsequences characterize convergence (an​→L⟺every monotone subsequence tends to L)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence Subsequence Monotone subsequence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 86
For a sequence of real numbers, a common finite limit of a sequence for all monotone subsequences forces the whole sequence to have that limit of a sequence. There is no need to assume a bounded sequence if the hypothesis already includes convergence of every monotone subsequence.
If an​ fails to tend to L, select a subsequence with ∣anj​​−L∣≥ε for some fixed ε>0. The monotone subsequence theorem gives a monotone subsequence of this subsequence, which contradicts the common limit of a sequence L. Conversely, every subsequence of a convergent sequence has the original limit of a sequence.
For a bounded sequence, the bounded monotone sequence theorem already ensures that all monotone subsequences converge. Only agreement of their limits of sequences remains to be checked.
