Apply backward induction to the finite perfect-information game, accounting for the two friendship pairs in every terminal assignment. Student S4​ receives the last room and has no remaining choice. Given the first two rooms, S3​ chooses the remaining room maximizing their own room value plus 0.4 if adjacent to S2​.

For example, after S1​ takes r2​ and S2​ takes r3​, student S3​ compares r1​, giving 0, with r4​, giving 0.5+0.4=0.9. Choosing r4​ also gives S2​ the adjacency bonus, making U2​=0.3+0.4=0.7. The last room r1​ is adjacent to S1​'s r2​.
The table makes S2​ choose r3​,r3​,r2​,r2​ after first-stage choices r1​,r2​,r3​,r4​, respectively. Their resulting consequences for S1​ are 0.5,0.7,0, and either 0 or 0.4. Thus S1​ strictly prefers r2​, proving the displayed outcome.
There is an off-path tie for S3​ after (r4​,r2​), so the entire subgame perfect equilibrium strategy profile need not be unique. Either choice, or any mixture of them, gives S1​ at most 0.4 after choosing r4​, below 0.7. The equilibrium assignment on the played path is therefore unaffected by this tie. Specifying these optimal continuations at every history, including the unplayed ones, supplies a subgame perfect equilibrium rather than merely a proposed path.
