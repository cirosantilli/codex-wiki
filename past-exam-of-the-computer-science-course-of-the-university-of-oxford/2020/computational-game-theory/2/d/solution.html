To check S2​'s incentives behind these continuations, the resulting utilities for their available choices, in ascending room order, are respectively (0.50,0.70,0.02), (0.01,0.70,0.42), (0.41,0.50,0.02) and (0.41,0.90,0.70). Student S3​'s own best responses determine those figures, and S4​ again takes the remaining room. All comparisons selecting these continuations are strict.
Hence S1​ chooses r2​ and the subgame perfect equilibrium outcome is
a=(r2​,r3​,r4​,r1​),U(a)=(0.70,0.70,0.90,0.41).
(15)
But the feasible assignment
a′=(r1​,r3​,r4​,r2​),U(a′)=(0.90,0.70,0.90,0.90)
(16)
strictly improves S1​ and S4​ and leaves S2​,S3​ unchanged. Thus a is not Pareto optimal. This is compatible with equilibrium: a Pareto improvement can require several players' actions to change. If S1​ alone takes r1​, the later best responses lead to (r1​,r3​,r2​,r4​), not to a′; anticipating that reaction gives S1​ only 0.50.
