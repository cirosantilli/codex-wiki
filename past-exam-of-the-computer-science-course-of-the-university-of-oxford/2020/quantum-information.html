iii OurBigBook.com
Let R=diag(1,eiπ/12). Since the Hadamard gate maps the standard basis to ∣+⟩,∣−⟩, the given gate is Q=HRH. Because H2=I,
Q3=HR3H=HTH,T=HQ3H.​
(9)
Next, use HZH=X on the target wire of the Controlled-Z gate:
CNOT=(I⊗H)CZ(I⊗H).​
(10)
The control-zero block remains the identity, and the control-one block becomes HZH=X, proving the identity with the stated control convention. We have therefore constructed each gate in the assumed universal quantum gate set {CNOT,H,T} using only CZ,Q,H.
Replacing every gate of a circuit by its displayed finite construction reproduces that circuit exactly. Thus {CZ,Q,H} is universal under the usual arbitrary-accuracy convention: it can perform every approximation obtainable from the given universal set. The claim concerns approximation of arbitrary unitary operators; it does not assert that finitely many circuits exactly represent an uncountable continuum of gates. This is universality transfer by exact gate synthesis.

i OurBigBook.com
The controlled-NOT gate is its own inverse, so CNOT2=I. Every remaining nontrivial gate acts on one wire. By the tensor product rule, any circuit on a product input stays a product state; in particular, starting at ∣00⟩ it cannot produce a Bell state.
This also prevents arbitrary approximation, not merely exact preparation. For normalized single-qubit vectors ∣u⟩,∣v⟩,
∣⟨Φ+∣u⊗v⟩∣2=21​∣u0​v0​+u1​v1​∣2≤21​
(11)
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Hence the quantum fidelity with ∣Φ+⟩ is bounded away from one for every reachable state. This gate set is not universal. The obstruction is preservation of product states, a special case of the existing product-state obstruction to SWAP universality.

Solution
Words: 224
iii OurBigBook.com
Now T2=S=diag(1,i). The gates H,S and CNOT are Clifford gates: conjugating a Pauli matrix or a tensor product of them gives another signed Pauli tensor. On one wire,
HXH=Z,HZH=X,SXS†=Y,SZS†=Z.
(12)
For CNOT the generator images are X⊗I↦X⊗X, I⊗X↦I⊗X, Z⊗I↦Z⊗I, and I⊗Z↦Z⊗Z. These identities can be checked on the four computational vectors and generate the preservation property for all Pauli tensors.[ref]
For a fixed number n of wires there are only finitely many possible images of the 2n generators Xj​,Zj​ in the Pauli group. Their images determine a unitary up to global phase. To see this, if two unitary operators induce identical images, their quotient commutes with every Zj​, so is diagonal in the computational basis; commuting with every Xj​ then forces all diagonal entries to be equal. The quotient is a scalar phase. Thus the generated gates form a finite set modulo global phase, which cannot be dense in the continuous space of all unitary operators. This is finiteness of the projective Clifford group.
A small visible consequence is that from ∣0⟩, one-qubit Clifford circuits reach only the six states ∣0⟩,∣1⟩,∣+⟩,∣−⟩,∣+i⟩,∣−i⟩ up to global phase. For instance, T∣+⟩ is outside this finite set. The fixed-n Pauli argument also covers circuits using CNOT on several wires. Therefore {CNOT,H,T2} is not universal.

Write the product state as ∣Ψ⟩=∣a⟩⊗∣b⟩. The two partial inner products are
∣ψj′​⟩=(⟨ψj​∣a⟩)∣b⟩=cj​∣b⟩,j=1,2.
(13)
Both vectors therefore lie in the same one-dimensional vector subspace, possibly with one or both equal to zero. If (c1​,c2​)=(0,0), choose λ1​=c2​ and λ2​=c1​; then λ1​∣ψ1′​⟩=λ2​∣ψ2′​⟩, and the multipliers are not both zero. If both cj​ vanish, choose (λ1​,λ2​)=(1,0).
Thus the conditional vectors are linearly dependent, with a nontrivial pair of multipliers in every case. The printed existential condition does not explicitly exclude (0,0), which would make it vacuous for arbitrary vectors; the argument establishes the stronger intended statement. These unnormalized vectors need not themselves be valid unit state vectors, and a zero vector corresponds to a measurement outcome of probability zero. This is the forward direction of the conditional-vector product-state criterion.

Measure the first qutrit in the computational basis and the second in the orthonormal basis {∣ϕ0​⟩,∣ϕ1​⟩,∣ϕ2​⟩} from Question 1(a), with those labels as outcomes. For first-party outcome 0, the unnormalized conditional vector of the second party is ∣0⟩/3​, so this outcome occurs with probability 1/3 and leaves the normalized state ∣0⟩.
The second-party Born rule probabilities are then ∣⟨ϕ0​∣0⟩∣2=1/2, ∣⟨ϕ1​∣0⟩∣2=0, and ∣⟨ϕ2​∣0⟩∣2=1/2. Hence
Pr(B=0∣A=0)=Pr(B=2∣A=0)=21​,Pr(B=1∣A=0)=0.​

More generally, conjugate-state steering from a maximally entangled pair explains how the first local measurement determines the second party's conditional vector; the basis choice here makes that vector especially simple.

i OurBigBook.com
Fix the two subsystem dimensions. For reflexivity, ∣Ψ⟩=(Id​⊗Id′​)∣Ψ⟩, and both identity operators are unitary.
For symmetry, if ∣Ψ⟩=(U⊗V)∣Φ⟩, apply the inverse local unitary transformation:
∣Φ⟩=(U†⊗V†)∣Ψ⟩.
(17)
The conjugate transposes of unitary operators are unitary, so the reverse relation holds.
For transitivity, if additionally ∣Φ⟩=(R⊗S)∣Θ⟩, the tensor product multiplication rule gives
∣Ψ⟩=(U⊗V)(R⊗S)∣Θ⟩=(UR⊗VS)∣Θ⟩.
(18)
Both products are unitary, for example (UR)†(UR)=R†U†UR=I. Thus local-unitary equivalence is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, and hence is an equivalence relation.

Extend the normalized vector ∣ϕ⟩ to an orthonormal basis {∣fj​⟩}j=1d​ with ∣f1​⟩=∣ϕ⟩, and extend ∣ψ⟩ to an orthonormal basis {∣ej​⟩}j=1d​ with ∣e1​⟩=∣ψ⟩. The Gram-Schmidt process provides such extensions. Define

A local unitary transformation preserves a product factorization:
(U⊗V)(∣a⟩⊗∣b⟩)=(U∣a⟩)⊗(V∣b⟩).
(23)
The output is therefore another product state. If an entangled state could be locally mapped to a product state, applying the inverse U†⊗V† would make the original entangled state a product as well, a contradiction. Hence a product state and an entangled state are never local-unitary equivalent.
In terms of a tensor-product coefficient matrix, C changes to UCVT, with an ordinary transpose on the second factor. Both multiplying matrices are invertible, so the matrix rank, equivalently the Schmidt rank, is unchanged. This supplies an algebraic form of the same argument: a nonzero pure state is a product state exactly when that rank is one.

The algorithm returns a uniformly random marked input with certainty. The Born rule applied to the final state gives, for each individual basis label y,
Pr(Y=y)={4/N=1/S1​,0,​f(y)=1,f(y)=0.​​
(30)
Summing over the S1​=N/4 marked inputs gives Pr(f(Y)=1)=1. The outcome is a marked input label, not merely the bit indicating whether some marked input exists. There is one use of the supplied phase oracle, together with the specified preparation, reflection and measurement.
The exact success relies on the marked fraction r=S1​/N=1/4. More generally, the mean amplitude immediately after the oracle is (1−2r)/N​. Inversion about the mean sends an unmarked amplitude to (1−4r)/N​ and a marked amplitude to (3−4r)/N​. Thus a single iteration has marked success probability r(3−4r)2. At r=1/4 the unmarked amplitudes vanish and the marked amplitudes become 2/N​, giving the one-step exact Grover search with a quarter of the inputs marked. No claim of one-query certainty for an arbitrary marked fraction is needed.
