so exact evaluation generally needs all outcomes with positive probability. An unfavorable child does not determine the expected value: a later, sufficiently favorable child can offset it. Ordinary alpha-beta pruning therefore does not directly discard the remaining outcomes.
Random outcomes also enlarge the effective branching factor. If each player decision has b choices and each action has c chance outcomes, exploring d player decisions can require order (bc)d leaves rather than bd. Under the same budget, this gives roughly
dstochastic​≈logb+logclogn​
(19)
before additional pruning benefits available in the deterministic game are considered. Exact probability calculations add overhead as well. These effects explain the typically shallower horizon; randomness alone is not a theorem of inevitable slowdown. Known utility bounds permit some expected value bounds and specialized pruning, and chance sampling can trade exactness for speed.[ref]
