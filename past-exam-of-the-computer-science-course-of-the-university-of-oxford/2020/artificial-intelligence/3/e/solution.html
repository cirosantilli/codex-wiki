Propagate an entire utility pair from a child chosen to maximize the component of the player whose turn it is. This is the two-player case of max-n search. At a terminal node let V(s)=(UA​(s),UB​(s)). At an A-node choose
s∗∈argmaxs′∈Children(s)​VA​(s′),V(s)=V(s∗),
(20)
and at a B-node replace VA​ by VB​. Do not maximize the two coordinates independently: their separate maxima may belong to incompatible outcomes. Fix a tie rule, such as the first child in the specified order. The rule recursively gives a pure strategy subgame perfect equilibrium in a finite perfect-information game: backward induction makes the moving player's selected continuation a best response at every node.[ref]
In the original Figure 2, each of the three children of the root belongs to B. The left B-node prefers its first leaf because 1>0, the middle B-node prefers its first leaf because 2>−2, and the right B-node prefers its second leaf because 3>1. Their propagated utility pairs, from left to right, are
(1,1),(−1,2),(−1,3).
(21)
At the root A compares 1,−1,−1 and chooses the left child. Thus the root pair is (1,1), with play following the root's left branch and then that B-node's first branch. No tie affects this outcome.
