Cutset conditioning selects a cycle cutset C: deleting its vertices makes the binary constraint graph a forest. Enumerate assignments to C, reject those violating internal constraints, and filter each remaining constraint domain against the chosen cutset values. Solve the residual components by tree-structured constraint satisfaction. Stop at a successful assignment, or report failure after all cutset assignments fail. Completeness follows by considering the restriction of any full solution to C; soundness follows by checking internal, crossing and residual constraints. With maximum constraint domain size q and k cutset variables, a typical bound is O(qk(n−k)q2) for the residual forest work, with cutset and crossing checks added explicitly.[ref]
Here take C={c,e}. The residual constraint graph has vertices a,b,d and only edges ab,ad, a tree rooted at a. Choose the consistent cutset values c=3,e=1. Filtering against these assignments gives
Da​={1,2},Db​={2},Dd​={2,4}.
(13)
The upward support pass from b deletes a=2, because b can only equal 2. The pass from d retains a=1, which has support 2 or 4. Assign a=1 at the root and then supported child values b=2,d=2. Together with c=3,e=1, this gives the solution in (c).
No singleton cycle cutset exists: deleting a,b,d or e leaves, respectively, a triangle on bce,acd,abc or abc; deleting c leaves the cycle in a graph a−b−e−d−a. Thus the two-element choice is also minimum, although the algorithm only requires a valid cutset.
