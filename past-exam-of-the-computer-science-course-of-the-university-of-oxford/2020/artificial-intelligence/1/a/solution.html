For a readable instance, depth-first search follows the deepest pending branch and breadth-first search expands by search depth; neither generally minimizes unequal edge costs. Uniform-cost search orders by path cost g, whereas A-star search orders by g+h. A displayed A-star search score would identify the estimate by subtracting the accumulated path cost. Under nonnegative costs and the usual terminating-search assumptions, uniform-cost search returns a least-cost goal state when testing goals on removal from the search frontier; A-star search additionally needs an admissible heuristic with appropriate reopening, or a consistent heuristic for the usual closed-set implementation.
