A basic measurement of a finite-dimensional quantum system is specified by an orthonormal basis {∣bj​⟩}j​ of its Hilbert space. For a normalized input pure state ∣χ⟩, the Born rule gives
pj​=∣⟨bj​∣χ⟩∣2,∣χj​⟩=pj​​∣bj​⟩⟨bj​∣χ⟩​(pj​>0).​
(1)
The post-measurement state is therefore ∣bj​⟩ up to a global phase. An outcome of zero probability has no conditional post-measurement state. For a general input density matrix ρ, the same basic measurement has pj​=⟨bj​∣ρ∣bj​⟩ and conditional density matrix ∣bj​⟩⟨bj​∣ whenever pj​>0.

If Alice's basic measurement returns a normalized vector ∣u⟩A​, form Bob's unnormalized conditional vector
∣χ​u​⟩B​=(⟨u∣A​⊗IB​)∣χ⟩AB​.
(4)
The Born rule and normalization give
pu​=∥χ​u​∥2,∣χu​⟩B​=pu​​∣χ​u​⟩B​​(pu​>0).​
(5)
The joint post-measurement state is ∣u⟩A​⊗∣χu​⟩B​. For ∣u⟩A​=α∣0⟩A​+β∣1⟩A​ with real coefficients and α2+β2=1, contraction of the Bell state gives
(⟨u∣⊗I)∣ϕ+​⟩=2​α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩​,pu​=21​.
(6)
Thus Bob's normalized conditional state is α∣0⟩B​+β∣1⟩B​. For complex coefficients the contraction instead contains their complex conjugates; this is the same conjugation underlying conjugate-basis steering of a Bell pair.

Let pab​ denote the conditional winning probability in the CHSH game. With both settings zero, computational-basis measurements of the Bell state always agree, so p00​=1. When just one setting is one, Bob's conditional pure state from part (i), or the corresponding calculation with Alice and Bob exchanged, gives agreement probability cos2θ. Hence p01​=p10​=cos2θ.
For a=b=1, winning requires different output bits. Alice obtains each of her two outcomes with probability 1/2. Given her first outcome, Bob's desired overlap is
⟨f1​(θ)∣e0​(θ)⟩=2sinθcosθ=sin2θ;
(7)
given her second outcome, the other desired overlap is ⟨f0​(θ)∣e1​(θ)⟩=−sin2θ. Thus p11​=sin2(2θ). Averaging over the four equally likely inputs gives
pwin​(θ)=41+2cos2θ+sin2(2θ)​=43+cos2θ−cos2(2θ)​.​
(8)
The maximum winning probability for noncommunicating classical players is 3/4, the game form of the CHSH inequality. Setting t=cos2θ, strict improvement requires t−t2>0, or 0<t<1. On the stipulated interval this gives
0<θ<4π​.​
(9)
The endpoints give equality with the classical bound. Within this reflected real-basis CHSH strategy, the maximum is 13/16 at t=1/2, namely θ=π/6; this follows by completing the square in t.

Use entanglement-assisted discrimination of a Pauli basis. Define the known single-qubit unitary operator V by V∣0⟩=∣ψ⟩ and V∣1⟩=∣ψ⊥⟩. Prepare the device input A and a reference ancilla qubit R in
∣Ω⟩AR​=(VA​⊗IR​)∣ϕ+​⟩=2​∣ψ⟩A​∣0⟩R​+∣ψ⊥⟩A​∣1⟩R​​.
(10)
Apply the unknown device once to A, then apply the known inverse V† to A. Since
V†U0​V=I,V†U1​V=Z,V†U2​V=X,V†U3​V=iY,
(11)
the possible two-qubit outputs are
U0​:U1​:U2​:U3​:​(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​,(∣00⟩−∣11⟩)/2​,(∣01⟩+∣10⟩)/2​,(∣01⟩−∣10⟩)/2​.​
(12)
These are the four mutually orthogonal Bell states. A Bell-basis measurement therefore identifies the device with certainty. Explicitly, apply a controlled-NOT gate A→R, then a Hadamard gate on A, followed by computational-basis measurements. The readouts 00,10,01,11 identify U0​,U1​,U2​,U3​, respectively. Thus one use of the device and one reference qubit suffice.

For normalized vector representatives in the same Hilbert space, two pure states are ε-close in pure-state norm distance when
​∣ψ⟩−∣ϕ⟩​2​≤ε.​
(13)
Here ∥⋅∥2​ is the Euclidean norm of the coefficient vector in any orthonormal basis. Equivalently, 2−2Re⟨ψ∣ϕ⟩≤ε2. Two unitary transformations U,V on the same Hilbert space are ε-close in operator-norm distance between unitary gates when
∥U−V∥op​=∥ψ∥2​=1sup​​U∣ψ⟩−V∣ψ⟩​2​≤ε.​

An entangling gate is a unitary operator on two quantum systems for which some pure product input is mapped to an entangled state:
∃∣u⟩A​,∣v⟩B​:U(∣u⟩A​⊗∣v⟩B​) is entangled.​
(15)
It need not create quantum entanglement from every product state. For example, a controlled-NOT gate entangles ∣+⟩∣0⟩ but leaves ∣00⟩ a product state.

The gate set is universal. Since the Hadamard gate satisfies H†=H and H2=I, the supplied U is a unitary operator, and
U2=2(I−iH)2​=−iH.
(16)
Two applications of U therefore realize a Hadamard gate up to a global phase. Together with the supplied controlled-NOT gate and T gate, this simulates the given universal quantum gate set.
An exact matrix representative of H can also be synthesized if desired. Put A=U2=−iH and S=T2. Direct multiplication gives (AS)6=−iI, hence
(AS)18A=H.​
(17)
This finite product uses only U and T, so the conclusion does not rely on discarding a global phase.

b OurBigBook.com
Solution
Words: 105
ii OurBigBook.com
The gate set is not universal. The displayed two-qubit matrix is the SWAP gate:
W(∣u⟩⊗∣v⟩)=∣v⟩⊗∣u⟩.
(18)
Both V and the Hadamard gate act on one qubit. Thus every gate preserves a fully factored product state, and induction gives the same property for every quantum circuit built from them. This is the product-state obstruction to SWAP universality.
For a quantitative obstruction to even approximate universality, every two-qubit product state has squared overlap at most 1/2 with a Bell state. Indeed, for normalized coefficient vectors u,v,
∣⟨ϕ+​∣u⊗v⟩∣2=2∣u0​v0​+u1​v1​∣2​≤21​
(19)
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. A controlled-NOT gate acting on ∣+⟩∣0⟩ would instead produce that Bell state, so these circuits cannot approximate all unitary transformations. The fact that V2=e−iπ/8T supplies a T gate up to global phase does not supply an entangling gate.

Use the entanglement-assisted promised equality protocol. Alice applies the phase oracle Ox​∣i⟩=(−1)xi​∣i⟩ on A, and Bob applies Oy​∣i⟩=(−1)yi​∣i⟩ on B. These are unitary operators because their diagonal entries have modulus one. After Alice sends A to Bob, the joint pure state is
∣Ωxy​⟩=n​1​∑i=0n−1​(−1)xi​+yi​∣ii⟩.
(20)
Let ∣Ω⟩=n−1/2∑i​∣ii⟩ be the original maximally entangled state. If d=dH​(x,y) is the Hamming distance, then
⟨Ω∣Ωxy​⟩=n1​∑i​(−1)xi​+yi​=nn−d−d​=1−n2d​.
(21)
Bob performs the projective measurement with projectors P=∣Ω⟩⟨Ω∣ and I−P. Under the promise,
d=0⟹Pr(P)=1,d=n/2⟹Pr(P)=0.​
(22)
He declares equal strings for the first outcome and distance n/2 for the other. This is exact because the promised outputs occupy orthogonal subspaces. A basic measurement also suffices: extend ∣Ω⟩ to an orthonormal basis and group all its other outcomes together. Alice transmits one n-dimensional system, realizable using ⌈log2​n⌉ qubits; n need not be a power of two.

Use Hadamard conjugation of a controlled gate to obtain the Controlled-Z gate:
CZ=(I⊗H)CNOT1→2​(I⊗H)=diag(1,1,1,−1).
(23)
Conjugating by Pauli X gates on both qubits moves its negative entry from ∣11⟩ to ∣00⟩:
(X⊗X)CZ(X⊗X)=I−2∣00⟩⟨00∣.
(24)
Finally (H⊗H)∣00⟩=∣+⟩∣+⟩=∣e0​⟩, so a two-qubit diffusion circuit is
C=(H⊗H)(X⊗X)(I⊗H)CNOT1→2​(I⊗H)(X⊗X)(H⊗H)=−W.​
(25)
In temporal order, apply Hadamard gates on both qubits, Pauli X gates on both, a Hadamard gate on the second, a controlled-NOT gate 1→2, a Hadamard gate on the second, Pauli X gates on both, then Hadamard gates on both. Every gate is permitted. The overall minus sign is the allowed global phase, so this implements the required inversion about the mean for Grover search algorithm.

The two conditions on a density matrix are positive semidefiniteness and unit trace:
ρ≥0,Trρ=1.​
(26)
Here positive semidefinite includes being a Hermitian matrix, with v†ρv≥0 for every vector v. Equivalently, a density matrix is Hermitian with nonnegative eigenvalues summing to one.

The Pauli Z gate gives a rotation through π about the z axis of the Bloch sphere. In Bloch vector coordinates,
(rx​,ry​,rz​)⟼(−rx​,−ry​,rz​).​
(27)
For example, this follows from ZXZ=−X, ZYZ=−Y and ZZZ=Z in the Pauli matrix expansion ρ=(I+rx​X+ry​Y+rz​Z)/2.

For input ∣0⟩S​, the controlled-NOT gate fixes ∣00⟩SC​, which is the ideal two-copy pure state. Thus its global cloning fidelity is F0​=1. For input ∣+⟩S​, the actual output is the Bell state ∣ϕ+​⟩, whereas the desired output is ∣++⟩. Their overlap is
⟨++∣ϕ+​⟩=2​1​,F+​=21​.
(32)
With the given equal input probabilities,
F0​=1,F+​=21​,F=21​(1+21​)=43​.​
(33)
These values use the specified squared quantum fidelity for the full output, rather than a one-copy marginal fidelity.

Assume perfect cloning of both specified pure states. Allowing irrelevant output global phases, this would require
U∣00⟩=eiα∣00⟩,U∣+0⟩=eiβ∣++⟩.
(34)
A unitary operator preserves inner products, and therefore their magnitudes. But the input overlap and required output overlap have different magnitudes:
∣⟨00∣+0⟩∣=2​1​=21​=∣⟨00∣++⟩∣.​
(35)
This contradiction proves that no such two-qubit unitary exists, without assuming the no-cloning theorem.

Construct an economical two-state quantum cloner by moving both actual outputs slightly toward one another relative to the two desired copies. Set ∣a⟩=∣00⟩ and ∣b⟩=∣++⟩. Their overlap is 1/2, so their sum and difference yield the orthonormal vectors
∣u⟩=3​∣a⟩+∣b⟩​,∣v⟩=∣a⟩−∣b⟩.
(36)
The desired outputs have the symmetric expressions
∣a⟩=cos6π​∣u⟩+sin6π​∣v⟩,∣b⟩=cos6π​∣u⟩−sin6π​∣v⟩.
(37)
Choose the actual outputs to be
∣c0​⟩=cos8π​∣u⟩+sin8π​∣v⟩,∣c+​⟩=cos8π​∣u⟩−sin8π​∣v⟩.
(38)
They are normalized and satisfy
⟨c0​∣c+​⟩=cos4π​=2​1​=⟨00∣+0⟩.
(39)
Consequently the prescription ∣00⟩↦∣c0​⟩, ∣+0⟩↦∣c+​⟩ preserves the Gram matrix of the two inputs and defines a linear isometry on their span. The unitary extension of a finite-dimensional isometry gives a unitary operator on the same four-dimensional, two-qubit Hilbert space; no extra workspace is required.
For an explicit full-space construction, define c=cos(π/8), s=sin(π/8) and the orthonormal basis
∣p⟩=c∣00⟩+s∣10⟩,∣m⟩=s∣00⟩−c∣10⟩,∣01⟩, ∣11⟩.
(40)
Choose two further orthonormal vectors perpendicular to u,v,
∣w⟩=2​∣01⟩−∣10⟩​,∣t⟩=6​∣01⟩+∣10⟩−2∣11⟩​.
(41)
Then
U=∣u⟩⟨p∣+∣v⟩⟨m∣+∣w⟩⟨01∣+∣t⟩⟨11∣
(42)
maps one orthonormal basis to another and is therefore unitary. Since ∣00⟩=c∣p⟩+s∣m⟩ and ∣+0⟩=c∣p⟩−s∣m⟩, it has exactly the required two outputs.
Finally,
⟨a∣c0​⟩=⟨b∣c+​⟩=cos6π​cos8π​+sin6π​sin8π​=cos24π​.
(43)
Both inputs therefore have the same global cloning fidelity, and so does their average:
F0​=F+​=F=cos224π​≈0.9829629131.​
(44)
This proves the requested existence with a two-qubit unitary gate.
