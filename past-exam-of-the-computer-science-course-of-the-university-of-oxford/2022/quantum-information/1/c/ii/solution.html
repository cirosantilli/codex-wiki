Let pab​ denote the conditional winning probability in the CHSH game. With both settings zero, computational-basis measurements of the Bell state always agree, so p00​=1. When just one setting is one, Bob's conditional pure state from part (i), or the corresponding calculation with Alice and Bob exchanged, gives agreement probability cos2θ. Hence p01​=p10​=cos2θ.
For a=b=1, winning requires different output bits. Alice obtains each of her two outcomes with probability 1/2. Given her first outcome, Bob's desired overlap is
⟨f1​(θ)∣e0​(θ)⟩=2sinθcosθ=sin2θ;
(7)
given her second outcome, the other desired overlap is ⟨f0​(θ)∣e1​(θ)⟩=−sin2θ. Thus p11​=sin2(2θ). Averaging over the four equally likely inputs gives
pwin​(θ)=41+2cos2θ+sin2(2θ)​=43+cos2θ−cos2(2θ)​.​
(8)
The maximum winning probability for noncommunicating classical players is 3/4, the game form of the CHSH inequality. Setting t=cos2θ, strict improvement requires t−t2>0, or 0<t<1. On the stipulated interval this gives
0<θ<4π​.​
(9)
The endpoints give equality with the classical bound. Within this reflected real-basis CHSH strategy, the maximum is 13/16 at t=1/2, namely θ=π/6; this follows by completing the square in t.
