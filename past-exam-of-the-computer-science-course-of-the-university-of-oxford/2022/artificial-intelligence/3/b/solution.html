Interpret MyAC as doing at least one initial sweep, then repeating while the preceding sweep deleted a value. Its loop guard otherwise has no initialized truth condition. Both procedures apply the same support-based deletion rule, so both preserve every solution and terminate because finite constraint domains only shrink. Both return false on a detected empty constraint domain; neither successful return establishes satisfiability.
If a sweep changes no constraint domain, every directed arc has been revised against unchanged constraint domains, so all arcs satisfy arc consistency. Conversely, AC-3 places every potentially affected incoming arc back on its work list after a deletion. Its empty work list therefore also establishes arc consistency. When both run to successful completion, they compute the same arc-consistency closure, independently of revision order. To see maximality, any subdomains of the initial constraint domains that satisfy arc consistency survive every revision: each of their values retains a support inside those subdomains. Thus neither procedure can delete a value belonging to any such fixed point. Early failure may leave different intermediate constraint domains, but both failures correctly prove impossibility.
Their scheduling costs differ. AC-3 directs work toward arcs that may have lost supports, with standard time complexity O(v+ed3). The full-sweep arc consistency method revisits all 2e arcs after even one remote change. There are at most vd value deletions, so at most vd+1 sweeps; a direct implementation including a scan for empty constraint domains costs
O((vd+1)(ed2+v)).​
(16)
If empty-domain detection is maintained during revisions rather than rescanned, the familiar comparison is O(ved3) versus O(ed3), apart from initialization. These are upper bounds, not exact costs on every input.
In practice MyAC is simple and may have low overhead or benefit from a fortunate sweep order. It can also waste many scans after changes propagate slowly opposite to that order. AC-3 incurs work-list bookkeeping but normally avoids those unrelated revisions. Both inherit the inability of arc consistency to decide arbitrary constraint satisfaction problems.
