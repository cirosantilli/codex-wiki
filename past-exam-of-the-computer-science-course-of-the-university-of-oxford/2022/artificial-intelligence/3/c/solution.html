For a finite constraint satisfaction problem with v variables, all complete assignments have depth v when each step assigns one new variable. Thus the shallowest-solution property of breadth-first search offers no advantage: every complete solution is already at that depth.
Depth-first search with backtracking stores a path and pending choices, using O(vd) auxiliary space with ordinary explicit choice bookkeeping, or O(v) when each level stores only a current value index. Breadth-first search may store an exponentially large frontier, up to O(dv) partial assignments. Depth-first search can also combine naturally with immediate constraint propagation and undoing changes. The finite depth avoids the infinite-branch obstruction of unrestricted depth-first tree search. Neither traversal removes the possible exponential time complexity.
