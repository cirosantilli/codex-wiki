Use AND-OR search for a strong plan. At an OR node choose a joint action; at its AND node require successful continuations for every possible outcome. A repeated state on the current branch cannot by itself establish progress. On a finite state space, the equivalent strong planning winning-set iteration starts with W0​={(p,p):p∈C} and repeatedly adds states with an action whose nonempty outcome set is contained in the current winning set:
Wt+1​=Wt​∪{s:∃a, ∅=Results(s,a)⊆Wt​}.
(13)
A newly admitted state has a plan with at most t+1 remaining steps in the worst case. This algorithm terminates on the finite state space and is complete for finite guaranteed-success plans. In a general nondeterministic planning problem it cannot guarantee that a strong plan exists: an uncontrollable self-loop can prevent inevitable progress.
For this particular unobstructed grid, a strong plan does exist. Command Mr Robot to wait throughout and move Mrs Robot along a shortest route to his initial room. Her movement is deterministic and his waiting is unaffected by the horizontal uncertainty. This meets in D(p0​,q0​) steps, regardless of every uncertain outcome, because no uncertain command is used. Thus a complete finite AND-OR search will find a solution here. With an unspecified disconnected maze, existence would instead require both robots to lie in the same reachable graph component.
