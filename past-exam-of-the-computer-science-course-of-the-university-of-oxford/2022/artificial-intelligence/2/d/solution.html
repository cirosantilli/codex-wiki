Write h∗(s) for the optimal remaining cost and suppose hi​(s)≤h∗(s) for i=1,2. The conclusions hold pointwise:
  • max(h1​,h2​) is an admissible heuristic, because both arguments are at most h∗.
  • min(h1​,h2​) is an admissible heuristic, since it is no larger than either argument.
  • h1​+h2​ is not necessarily an admissible heuristic. A state with a single cost-one move to a goal can have h1​=h2​=1 at that state and both zero at the goal. Both original heuristic functions are exact, but their sum is 2>1.
  • αh1​+(1−α)h2​ is an admissible heuristic for 0≤α≤1, since nonnegative weighting gives αh1​+(1−α)h2​≤αh∗+(1−α)h∗=h∗.
These are admissible heuristic combinations. An unweighted sum needs an extra condition, such as distributing action costs between the component estimates, to prevent counting the same required work twice.
