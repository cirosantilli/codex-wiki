Choose a quadratic uniform B-spline segment on a unit spline knot interval. This choice is useful because adjacent pieces can later share de Boor points at simple, equally spaced spline knots. Its three active basis pieces on local t∈[0,1] are
b0​(t)=2(1−t)2​,b1​(t)=21+2t−2t2​,b2​(t)=2t2​.
(23)
They follow from the Cox-de Boor recurrence for degree two and consecutive integer spline knots. If the three de Boor points are D0​,D1​,D2​, then the equivalent Bézier curve handles are
A=2D0​+D1​​,B=D1​,C=2D1​+D2​​.
(24)
One can verify this conversion simply by substituting these expressions into the Bernstein basis formula and collecting each Di​. Solving gives
D0​=2A−B=(−4,−1),D1​=B=(0,1),D2​=2C−B=(0,−1).​
(25)
The desired matrix representation is therefore
F(t)=21​[t2 t 1]​1−21​−221​100​​​−400​−11−1​​.​
(26)
The left row is again the monomial row, the middle matrix contains the monomial coefficients of b0​,b1​,b2​, and the right matrix lists the de Boor points. Unlike the Bézier curve basis matrix, the constant row averages two handles. A clamped B-spline with repeated end spline knots could instead reuse A,B,C directly; the simple uniform convention above specifies which representation is being used and supplies the shared handles required for the extension.
