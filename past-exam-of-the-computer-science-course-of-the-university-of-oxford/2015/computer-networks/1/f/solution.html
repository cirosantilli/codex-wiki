Represent the data bits, most significant first, by a polynomial M(x) whose polynomial coefficients lie in the field with two elements F2​. Here addition and subtraction are both exclusive or, with no carries. Choose a fixed CRC generator polynomial G(x) of polynomial degree r, with leading and constant polynomial coefficients one. Both endpoints know G and the bit ordering.
Multiply M by xr, which appends r zero bits. Use polynomial division to obtain
xrM(x)=Q(x)G(x)+R(x),R=0 or degR<r.
(6)
Replace the appended zeros by the r bits of the polynomial remainder R, retaining leading zeros when necessary. The transmitted polynomial is
T(x)=xrM(x)+R(x),T(x)modG(x)=0.​
(7)
The last equality holds because adding R cancels the existing polynomial remainder in the field with two elements. It is the simple systematic cyclic redundancy check convention used here; practical variants can add agreed initialization and final transformations.
The receiver divides the entire received polynomial by G. A nonzero polynomial remainder detects corruption. If the received word is T+E, where the ones in the error polynomial E mark flipped bits, its polynomial remainder is EmodG. Thus the undetected nonzero errors are exactly the multiples of G; a zero result does not prove that transmission was error-free. Operationally, polynomial long division repeatedly cancels a leading one using a shifted copy of G, implemented with exclusive or operations.
