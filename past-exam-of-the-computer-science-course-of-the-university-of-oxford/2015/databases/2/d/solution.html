Past exam of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2015 / Databases / 2 / d / Solution

 ... Computer science course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the computer science course of the University of Oxford 2015 Databases 2 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
A serial database schedule is obtained by running the three database transactions in the order T1​,T2​,T3​:
W1​(A),R1​(B),W1​(C), W2​(A),W2​(C),R2​(B), R3​(B),R3​(A),R3​(D).​
(16)
A genuinely interleaved conflict-serializable database schedule with the same serial order is
W1​(A),R3​(B),R1​(B),W1​(C),W2​(A),W2​(C),R2​(B),R3​(A),R3​(D).​
(17)
The early R3​(B) conflicts with nothing: all accesses to B are reads. The conflicts on A give edges T1​→T2​, T1​→T3​ and T2​→T3​ in the database precedence graph, while the writes to C give the already present edge T1​→T2​. This database precedence graph has no directed cycle and admits the order T1​,T2​,T3​. Moving the early R3​(B) after R2​(B) swaps it only past non-conflicting operations, recovering the displayed serial database schedule.
For a database schedule that is not a conflict-serializable database schedule, take
W1​(A),W2​(A),W2​(C),R1​(B),W1​(C),R2​(B),R3​(B),R3​(A),R3​(D).​
(18)
On A, W1​(A) before W2​(A) forces T1​→T2​. On C, W2​(C) before W1​(C) forces T2​→T1​. The resulting directed cycle prevents any serial ordering from preserving all conflicts. Each of the three displayed database schedules preserves the operation order inside every database transaction.
