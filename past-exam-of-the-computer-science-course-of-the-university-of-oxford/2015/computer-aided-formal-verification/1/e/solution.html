Choose the variable ordering of a binary decision diagram y<x<z. The Boolean cofactor with y=1 is the constant one, while the Boolean cofactor with y=0 is x∧z. Consequently the right panel in part (d) has the following arrows:
y: (0→x, 1→1),x: (0→0, 1→z),z: (0→0, 1→1).​
(3)
Here an entry such as 0→x denotes the zero arrow to the x decision node. There are 3+2=5​ vertices: three decision nodes and two terminal nodes, saving one decision node relative to either of the previous orderings. Testing y first is effective because its one branch settles the entire Boolean function immediately. The ordering y<z<x works equally well.
This is also minimal among ordinary binary decision diagrams for this Boolean function. Each Boolean variable is essential: setting y=0,z=1 leaves f=x; setting y=0,x=1 leaves f=z; and setting x=z=0 leaves f=y. If a Boolean variable were never tested, the represented Boolean function could not depend on it. Thus at least three decision nodes are necessary, and the construction attains that bound.
