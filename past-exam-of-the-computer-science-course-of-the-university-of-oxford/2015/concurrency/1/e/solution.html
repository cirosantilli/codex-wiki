The obstruction is that the property is about where a transition originates, whereas the three standard CSP semantic models identify processes that can disagree about this fact. Take fresh h=a and compare
G=a→STOP,B=(a→STOP□h→a→STOP)∖{h}.
(12)
In G, the only a transition leaves a CSP stable state, so the property holds. Initially B has both an a transition to STOP and a CSP internal action to a→STOP. Its initial state is therefore not a CSP stable state, and its direct a transition violates the property.
Nevertheless both have exactly the CSP traces ⟨⟩ and ⟨a⟩. Initially their possible stable observations offer exactly a, so their initial CSP stable failures are precisely (⟨⟩,X) with a∈/X. After a, both can refuse every CSP event. Neither has a CSP divergence: the hidden h can occur at most once. Therefore
G=T​B,G=F​B,G=FD​B,but the property separates G and B.​
(13)
No CSP refinement test in one of these models can separate semantically equal operands. This also rules out placing the operand in a fixed context of ordinary CSP operators before the test, since those operators respect the model's equality. A model recording more about the interleaving of stability and acceptance, or an additional operator that explicitly observes stability, would be needed.
