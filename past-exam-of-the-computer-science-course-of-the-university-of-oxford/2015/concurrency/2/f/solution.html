Past exam of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2015 / Concurrency / 2 / f / Solution

Use fresh event tags to tell apart an action by P and an interrupting action by Q. Put B=Σ∖A and introduce a disjoint copy B′={a′:a∈B}. Let f fix A and map each a∈B to a′, and put S=A∪B′. Define a process that always accepts an event from S by
RUN(S)=□x∈S​x→RUN(S),
(28)
and an ordinary CSP interrupt handler by
I=□a∈B​a′→RUN(S).
(29)
Finally let g fix every original event and send a′ back to a. A simulation containing no occurrence of the new operator is
R=g((P△I)S∥​f(Q)).​
(30)
Here △ is the ordinary CSP interrupt, and the CSP event renaming g is applied after the CSP parallel composition. It is non-injective, so part (c) explains why moving it inside would be unsafe.
Before the interrupt, P's events in A synchronize with f(Q) on the same events. Its events outside A are unprimed and do not synchronize with f(Q). Meanwhile Q's events outside A are primed; they synchronize with I and thereby interrupt and discard P. After the first such event, the left operand is RUN(S), so it imposes no further restriction on f(Q); final CSP event renaming then yields exactly the remaining behaviour of Q.
For a formal strong bisimulation, relate a pre-interrupt state JA​(P0​,Q0​) to
g((P0​△I)S∥​f(Q0​)),
(31)
and an already-interrupted state Q0​ to
g(RUN(S)S∥​f(Q0​)).
(32)
The three visible cases match the rules in part (d), including both transitions when both operands offer the same event outside A. A CSP internal action of Q0​ never activates I, while a CSP internal action of P0​ remains possible exactly until the interrupt. The controller adds no CSP internal actions. Thus transitions match in both directions and related derivatives remain related.
Consequently this simulation is stronger than merely CSP trace equivalent:
R=T​JA​(P,Q),R=F​JA​(P,Q),R=FD​JA​(P,Q).​
(33)
It preserves CSP stable states, their enabled-event sets and their CSP stable failures, and matches every possible infinite run of CSP internal actions. In particular, it introduces neither a premature commitment nor a new CSP divergence. A different simulation that guesses an interrupt with a hidden handshake would need a separate refusal analysis; the visible tagging construction above needs no hidden handshake.
