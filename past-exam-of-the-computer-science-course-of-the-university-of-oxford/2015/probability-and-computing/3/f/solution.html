Order the edges arbitrarily and fix their colours one at a time. After a partial assignment P, regard all unassigned edges as independent fair red/blue choices, and compute
Q(P)=E[Z∣P].
(46)
The conditional expectation of a monochromatic clique count is easy to compute. For each four-vertex subset A, let r be the number of its six edges still unassigned. Its contribution qA​(P) to Q(P) is
qA​(P)=⎩⎨⎧​0,2−r,21−r=1/32,​both colours already occur among its fixed edges,at least one edge is fixed, all in the same colour,none of its six edges is fixed.​
(47)
In the second case, every remaining edge must match the already fixed colour; this includes a completely fixed monochromatic copy, for which r=0 and the contribution is one. In the last case, the six unassigned edges may become either all red or all blue. By linearity of expectation,
Q(P)=∑∣A∣=4​qA​(P).
(48)
For the next unassigned edge e, compute Q(P,e=red) and Q(P,e=blue). The law of total expectation gives
Q(P)=21​Q(P,e=red)+21​Q(P,e=blue).
(49)
Choose the colour with the smaller value, resolving a tie by a fixed rule. At least one is at most their average, so the loop invariant is that Q never increases. Initially Q=(4n​)/32; once every edge is fixed, there is no randomness left and Q=Z. Therefore the method of conditional expectations guarantees
Z≤321​(4n​).​
(50)
A direct implementation recomputes both sums over all O(n4) four-vertex subsets for each of the O(n2) edges, requiring O(n6) arithmetic operations. The comparisons can be exact: every contribution is a multiple of 1/32, so store 32Q as an integer with O(logn) bits. This gives O(n6logn) bit complexity with elementary arithmetic, and hence a deterministic polynomial time construction. No random sampling is performed by this derandomization.
