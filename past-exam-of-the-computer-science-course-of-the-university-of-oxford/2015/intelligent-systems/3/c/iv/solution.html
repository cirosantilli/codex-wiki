Always true. Replace every all-different constraint by the pairwise inequalities on its scope. Then solutions are precisely the proper graph colourings of the primal graph using values from D as colours. Put t=tw(P).
A graph of treewidth t has a proper graph colouring with at most t+1 colours. Here is a constructive proof. In a width-t tree decomposition, remove any leaf bag contained in its neighbor. If more than one bag remains, a leaf bag has a vertex absent from its neighbor. The running-intersection property means that vertex occurs only in the leaf bag, so every neighbor of the vertex belongs to that bag. It has at most t neighbors. If only one bag remains, it has at most t+1 vertices and the same degree bound holds.
Remove the chosen vertex and repeat; deleting vertices cannot increase the width of the restricted tree decomposition. Now colour in reverse removal order. At most t already coloured neighbors forbid colours, so one of t+1 colours is always available. Since the integer inequality ∣D∣>t gives ∣D∣≥t+1, this produces a satisfying assignment:
∣D∣>tw(P) ⟹ Sol(P)=∅.​
(35)
The common unrestricted constraint domain and the exclusively all-different constraints are essential: arbitrary constraint relations do not reduce to graph colouring in this way.
