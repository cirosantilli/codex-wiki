The formal language is not a regular language. Let K denote it and consider the infinitely many prefixes
ui​=(ab)i,i≥0.
(39)
For any i=j, append the word zi​=(bc)i. Then ui​zi​∈K, whereas uj​zi​∈/K: the latter word has j occurrences of a and i occurrences of c, and every word of K has equal numbers of those two symbols.
Thus ui​ and uj​ are not Myhill-Nerode equivalent. There are infinitely many Myhill-Nerode equivalence classes, so the Myhill-Nerode theorem rules out a finite-state automaton recognizing K. Equivalently, if two such prefixes left a deterministic finite automaton in the same automaton state, appending zi​ could not distinguish their acceptance, giving the same contradiction.
K is not regular.​
