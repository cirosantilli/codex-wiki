The standard semantics of ALC and EL do not impose the unique name assumption. Therefore the two individual names may denote the same element. A minimum-size model of either normalized TBox from part (d), together with the ABox, is
ΔI={d},aI=bI=d,AI=CI=XI={d},BI=∅,RI=PI={(d,d)}.​​
(28)
The R self-loop supplies the ABox fact and makes both ∃R.A and ∃R.X true at d. The P self-loop supplies exactly the existential restriction defining C. Disjointness holds because B is empty. Hence every normalized general concept inclusion is satisfied. A domain must be nonempty, so ∣ΔI∣=1​ is minimal.
If a separate unique name assumption were imposed, the minimum would instead be two. One can take a=b, A={a}, C={b}, R={(a,b)}, P={(b,b)} and B=X=∅. There is then no R-edge ending at an A element, so the normalized requirements involving X hold vacuously. That additional convention is not part of standard description logic semantics.
