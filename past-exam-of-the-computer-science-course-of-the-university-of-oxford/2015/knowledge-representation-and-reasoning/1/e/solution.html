The algorithm is incorrect: it can return true for a non-entailed ground atom​. Its recursive calls return only Booleans. They do not return and propagate the first-order substitutions obtained while satisfying earlier rule premises.
Take distinct constants a,b,c, facts
F={P(a),Q(b)},
(14)
and the single Datalog rule
P(x)∧Q(x)→H(c).
(15)
Ask for H(c). The head matches the goal without binding x. In line 15, the recursive call for P(x) succeeds by first-order unification with P(a), but returns only true. The next recursive call still receives Q(x) with x unbound, and succeeds by first-order unification with Q(b). Neither subgoal is the visited head H(c), so the visited test does not stop these calls. Both recursive results are true, and the algorithm returns true for H(c).
However, the least Herbrand model is just {P(a),Q(b)}. No single constant makes both P(x) and Q(x) true, so the rule cannot derive H(c). The two individually successful premises need one consistent first-order substitution. A correct backward chaining procedure must thread answer first-order substitutions through the remaining premises and backtrack when they cannot be extended; forward chaining on ground Datalog instances is another way to avoid this error.
