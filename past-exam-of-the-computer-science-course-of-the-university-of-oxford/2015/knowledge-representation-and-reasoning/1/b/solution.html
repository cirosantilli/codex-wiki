Write S,T,U,M,F,Q,J for the atomic formulas Student(x), Takes(x,KRR,2015), Undergrad(x), Masters(x), FacultyMember(x), GreaterOrEqual(CountSupervise(x),2), and Teach(x,y), respectively. These are abbreviations for formulas, not new symbols in the vocabulary. In clausal normal form, with all remaining variables implicitly universally quantified, the three translations become:
(i)(ii)(iii)​¬S∨¬T∨U∨M,¬S∨¬T∨¬U∨¬M;¬U∨SupervisedBy(x,f(x)),¬M∨SupervisedBy(x,f(x));¬F∨¬Q∨¬J∨Busy(x).​
(4)
For (ii), Skolemization introduces the fresh Skolem function f. One supervisor suffices even when both categories hold, so the same f can be used in both first-order clauses. For (iii), moving the existentially quantified course out of an antecedent produces a universally quantified variable in the resulting first-order clause:
((F(x)∧Q(x)∧∃yTeach(x,y))→Busy(x))≡∀y((F(x)∧Q(x)∧Teach(x,y))→Busy(x)).
(5)
The resolution with free selection calculus for first-order Horn clauses is sound and refutationally complete for the clause sets from (ii) and (iii)​. For example, select the unique positive first-order literal in every definite first-order Horn clause, and select one negative first-order literal in every nonempty clause without a positive one. On the displayed definite first-order Horn clauses, this selects SupervisedBy(x,f(x)) or Busy(x). A fair saturation must still consider all permitted inferences. Functions do not invalidate this completeness theorem, although they can prevent termination. [ref]
The first first-order clause of (i) has two positive first-order literals, so the Horn resolution with free selection theorem does not apply to that sentence. General first-order resolution, with its usual completeness requirements, can handle it. Soundness of an individual resolution inference is not the issue; the issue is applicability and completeness of this particular restricted calculus.
