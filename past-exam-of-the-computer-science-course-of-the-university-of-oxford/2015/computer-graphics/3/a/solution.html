At a surface point p, let N^ be the outward unit normal vector, V^ the unit vector from p toward the viewer, and L^ the unit vector from p toward the light. The incoming propagation direction is therefore −L^. The specular reflection direction R^ points away from the surface along the ideal reflected ray.
Resolve L^ into its normal and tangential components:
L^=(N^⋅L^)N^+(L^−(N^⋅L^)N^).
(25)
Reflection of the incoming direction preserves its tangential component and reverses its normal component. Equivalently, the outgoing direction has the same normal component as L^ and the opposite tangential component, giving
R^=2(N^⋅L^)N^−L^.​
(26)
The dot product identity shows ∥R^∥=1 when ∥L^∥=∥N^∥=1.
In the Phong reflection model, ambient lighting approximates unmodelled indirect illumination and is independent of these directions. The diffuse term models Lambertian reflectance and varies as max(0,N^⋅L^). The specular term models a highlight around R^ and varies as max(0,R^⋅V^)n, where n>0 controls its sharpness. For a visible front surface, a convenient per-colour-channel equation is
I=ka​Ia​+IL​[kd​max(0,N^⋅L^)+ks​χmax(0,R^⋅V^)n],​
(27)
where Ia​ is ambient illumination, IL​ is the source strength at the surface including any chosen attenuation, ka​,kd​,ks​≥0 are material weights, and χ is one when N^⋅L^>0 and zero otherwise. The weights can be colour-dependent. An idealized point source's incident strength decreases as inverse distance squared; the empirical equation can also use a prescribed attenuation law.
In degenerate geometric cases the effective weights switch off. A light behind or tangent to the surface contributes neither direct diffuse nor specular illumination. If R^⋅V^≤0, the specular term is zero. A viewpoint on the back side is rejected by back-face culling for a one-sided surface. A material with no highlight has ks​=0; with no diffuse response, kd​=0; without ambient illumination the ambient term vanishes. These zeros do not require renormalizing the other weights, and the empirical Phong reflection model does not impose a universal condition ka​+kd​+ks​=1. At coincident light/surface or eye/surface positions a direction cannot be normalized; such singular configurations require a separate convention, rather than substituting an undefined unit vector into the formula.
