Three advantages of integer arithmetic for raster graphics are:
  • Cheap, fast operations. Historically, integer addition, subtraction and shifts needed much less hardware and execution time than floating-point arithmetic, especially on machines without a floating-point unit. An incremental Bresenham's line algorithm needs only a few additions and a decision per pixel.
  • Exact decisions. Provided integer overflow is excluded, the scaled midpoint line decision is exact. There is no accumulated rounding error that can move a decision across a half-pixel boundary; equality has a reproducible tie rule.
  • Simple storage and hardware. Integer coordinates already identify pixels. An implementation can keep coordinates and decision variables in ordinary registers and use a small add/compare datapath, avoiding conversion between floating-point numbers and integer screen addresses.
These are historical and structural advantages, not a claim that every modern processor executes every integer operation faster. An integer implementation still needs sufficient width for its intermediate results. The compact answer is
fast elementary operations; exact decisions; simple hardware and storage.​
