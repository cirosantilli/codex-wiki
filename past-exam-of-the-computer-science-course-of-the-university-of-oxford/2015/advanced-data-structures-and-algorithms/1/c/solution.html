For an initially zero binary counter, bit i changes precisely on increments whose indices are divisible by 2i. Weighting each such change by 2i gives the exact total
C(n,k)=i=0∑k−1​2i⌊2in​⌋.​
(3)
For n≥1 and k≥1, put r=min{k,1+⌊log2​n⌋}. All summands with i≥r vanish; each of the other summands is at most n. Therefore
C(n,k)≤nr=O(nmin{k,1+log2​n}).​
(4)
This is sharper than simply charging n for each of all k positions when n is small. To show that the order is best possible, use ⌊x⌋≥x/2 for x≥1. For every 0≤i<r,
2i⌊2in​⌋≥2n​,2nr​≤C(n,k)≤nr.
(5)
Thus the asymptotic analysis gives a tight bound. If wraparound is excluded, n<2k, and this becomes Θ(n(1+log2​n)), often written Θ(nlogn) for n≥2. If wraparound is allowed and n≥2k, it becomes Θ(nk). For n=0, the cost is zero.
For completeness, the same conclusion cannot be asserted uniformly over arbitrary initial binary counter states. An initial value 2k−1 makes the first increment change every bit, costing 2k−1. A general upper bound follows by replacing each floor function in the count by a ceiling function:
Carbitrary​(n,k)≤∑i=0k−1​2i⌈2in​⌉≤nr+2k−1.
(6)
To see the second inequality, split at r: for i<r, use ⌈n/2i⌉≤n/2i+1, and for i≥r, use ⌈n/2i⌉=1. For n≥1 the worst initial-state cost is consequently Θ(nr+2k). This qualification separates initialization from the usual amortized analysis starting at zero.
