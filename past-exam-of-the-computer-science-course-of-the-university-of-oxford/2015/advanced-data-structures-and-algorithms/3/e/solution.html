Let G=(L∪R,E) be the given bipartite graph. A matching in a graph selects edges with no shared endpoint. For each edge e, introduce an indicator xe​ for selection. The integer program for a maximum cardinality matching is
maxsubject to​e∈E∑​xe​e∋v∑​xe​≤1xe​∈{0,1}​​(v∈L∪R),(e∈E).​​
(34)
Its linear programming relaxation is
P={x∈RE:xe​≥0 (e∈E),∑e∋v​xe​≤1 (v∈L∪R)},
(35)
with the same objective function. Explicit upper bounds xe​≤1 may be included, but they already follow from a constraint at either endpoint.
We prove that every extreme point of P is integral by showing that any feasible point with a fractional coordinate admits a two-sided perturbation. Given such an x, form the fractional support graph H with exactly those edges for which 0<xe​<1. This is a nonempty bipartite graph. A vertex incident to one of these edges cannot also meet an edge with value 1, because its incident sum would exceed 1.
First suppose H contains a graph cycle. Choose a simple one, e1​,…,eq​. Its length q is even since H is a bipartite graph. Define a nonzero vector h by alternating +1,−1 along the graph cycle and putting 0 on other edges. At each vertex on the graph cycle, the two changed coordinates have opposite signs, so its incident sum is unchanged. Choose
0<ε<mine on the cycle​{xe​,1−xe​}.
(36)
Then x+=x+εh and x−=x−εh are distinct feasible points: no coordinate leaves [0,1] and all incident sums are unchanged. Since x=(x++x−)/2, it is not an extreme point.
Otherwise H is a forest. Choose a connected component of a graph containing an edge, and a simple graph path in it between two tree leaves. Alternate +1,−1 along the graph path to define h. Every internal vertex of the graph path still has unchanged incident sum, including any additional edges branching from it. At either endpoint, exactly one incident edge has fractional value; all other incident coordinates are 0, since a coordinate 1 is impossible there. Thus each endpoint has strictly positive slack 1−∑e∋v​xe​ in its degree constraint. Choose ε>0 smaller than these two slacks and smaller than every xe​ and 1−xe​ along the graph path. Both x+εh and x−εh remain feasible: the endpoint sums can increase or decrease by ε, and the internal sums do not change. Again x is the midpoint of distinct feasible points, contradicting extremality.
Both cases exclude fractional coordinates at an extreme point. Therefore
every extreme point of P has xe​∈{0,1} and represents a matching.​
(37)
The nonempty bounded linear polyhedron P has an optimal extreme point for the stated linear function, so the relaxation yields a maximum cardinality matching. This is stronger than the existence of one integral optimum: it proves integrality of every extreme point. It does not claim that every optimal feasible point is integral; convex mixtures of distinct optimal matchings in a graph can be fractional.
