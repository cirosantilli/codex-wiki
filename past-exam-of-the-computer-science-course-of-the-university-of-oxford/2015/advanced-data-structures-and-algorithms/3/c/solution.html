Assume job sizes ti​≥0 and machine speeds sj​>0, with at least one machine. Let yij​≥0 be the amount of job Ti​ assigned to machine Mj​, and let T≥0 be the proposed makespan. Use the linear program
minsubject to​Tj=1∑m​yij​=ti​i=1∑n​yij​≤sj​Tyij​≥0T≥0.​​(1≤i≤n),(1≤j≤m),(1≤i≤n, 1≤j≤m),​​
(30)
The first family of constraints assigns every job completely. The second bounds each machine's completion time by T, since Lj​/sj​≤T is equivalent to Lj​≤sj​T. It is a linear inequality because sj​ is an input constant. There is no integrality restriction: jobs are divisible as specified in this scheduling model.
To verify the formulation, any actual allocation defines the variables above, with T equal to its largest completion time, and satisfies every constraint. Conversely, from a feasible solution, assign amount yij​ to machine Mj​ and process its assigned pieces consecutively. Its total time is at most T, so the linear program realizes the claimed makespan.
There is also a useful exact check. Put W=∑i​ti​ and S=∑j​sj​. Summing machine constraints gives W≤ST, so T≥W/S. Assigning
yij​=ti​Ssj​​

makes every machine's completion time W/S, proving
T∗=∑j​sj​∑i​ti​​.​

This relies on the paper's divisible-work model, in which machine completion times depend only on total assigned size. If W=0, the optimum is 0.
