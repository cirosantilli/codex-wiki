Choose one global direction and impose it on every adjacent pair. Keep Xi​∈{1,…,m}, introduce D∈{+,−}, and impose for each i<n the relation
R(D,Xi​,Xi+1​)⟺(D=+∧Xi​<Xi+1​)∨(D=−∧Xi​>Xi+1​).
(10)
Because the same D occurs everywhere, transitivity gives either a strictly increasing whole sequence or a strictly decreasing one. Choosing independently between < and > at each edge would merely require adjacent values to differ and would incorrectly allow zigzags.
If a binary formulation is required, replace each ternary relation by a hidden-variable encoding. Add a variable Yi​ with domain
{(+,a,b):1≤a<b≤m}∪{(−,a,b):1≤b<a≤m}.
(11)
Use three binary constraints: the sign component of Yi​ equals D, its first numeric component equals Xi​, and its second equals Xi+1​. These enforce exactly the relation above while leaving the original variable domains unchanged. Each auxiliary domain has m(m−1) values.
For n≥2, the projected solutions in the original Xi​ variables number 2(nm​) when m≥n, and zero otherwise. In particular m=n=3 gives exactly (1,2,3) and (3,2,1). For n=1, direction is irrelevant and the projected solutions number m, not 2m.
