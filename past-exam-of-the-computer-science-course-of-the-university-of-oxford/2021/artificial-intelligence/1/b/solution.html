None of the three variants is an optimal search algorithm in general. The following single search tree supplies counterexamples for all three. Its heuristic function is nonnegative, admissible and even a consistent heuristic.
The least-cost goal is G4​, reached at cost 1+3=4; the other goal G5​ costs 5. Assign h(S)=0, h(P)=3, and zero to both goals. For the first variant, expanding S generates G5​ and immediately returns it, before P can be expanded. A goal test at generation therefore returns cost 5.
For priority g(n)+h(n′), the root's children have priorities 1 for P and 5 for G5​. Expanding P generates G4​ with priority 4+3=7. The algorithm next selects G5​, again returning cost 5.
For priority g(n′)+h(n), the root's children instead have priorities 3 for P and 0 for G5​. The suboptimal goal is selected immediately. The example uses no tie-breaking assumption. In contrast, ordinary A-star search with its goal test on selection compares the genuine estimated total costs and returns G4​.
