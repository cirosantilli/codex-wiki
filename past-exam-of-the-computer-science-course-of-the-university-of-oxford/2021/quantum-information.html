⟨ϕ0​∣ϕ1​⟩=41​−41​+21​=21​=0.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
Identification is stronger than quantum state exclusion. Here an outcome only needs to rule out its associated preparation. Use the following orthonormal basis for a basic measurement
∣b0​⟩=2​∣0⟩−∣1⟩​,∣b1​⟩=2​∣0⟩+∣1⟩​,∣b2​⟩=∣2⟩.
(6)
Its defining overlaps satisfy ⟨bm​∣ϕm​⟩=0 for m=0,1,2. By the Born rule, observing bm​ makes preparation ϕm​ impossible. Measure in this basis and send its outcome label m; the prize is guaranteed.
For completeness, the conditional probability matrix, with rows m and columns the prepared-state label j, is
[Pr(m∣ϕj​)]m,j​=​01/21/2​1/201/2​1/21/20​​.
(7)
Every column sums to one and the diagonal vanishes. Each result leaves two possible preparations, so this projective state exclusion does not contradict the impossibility of perfect identification. The family consists of antidistinguishable quantum states.

Prepare Bob in ∣0⟩B​ and encode the bit using Alice's preparation ∣m⟩A​. The controlled-NOT gate gives
CNOTA→B​∣m⟩A​∣0⟩B​=∣m⟩A​∣m⟩B​.

ii OurBigBook.com
Reverse communication is also possible. Prepare Alice in ∣+⟩A​. Bob encodes m=0 as ∣+⟩B​ and m=1 as ∣−⟩B​. Since X∣±⟩=±∣±⟩, phase kickback gives
CNOTA→B​(∣+⟩A​∣(−)m⟩B​)=2​∣0⟩A​+(−1)m∣1⟩A​​⊗∣(−)m⟩B​,
(9)
where ∣(−)0⟩=∣+⟩ and ∣(−)1⟩=∣−⟩. Alice measures in the Hadamard basis: + means zero and − means one, deterministically.
The target's Pauli X gate eigenvalue becomes a relative phase on the control. This is the operational content of CNOT control reversal in the Hadamard basis; the computational basis control arrow alone does not determine every possible direction of communication.

Solution
Words: 111
iii OurBigBook.com
Charlie cannot send a bit to Alice with the stated preparation, fixed interaction and final local measurements. Write V=CNOTB→A​ and W=CNOTB→C​, so the complete gate is U=WV with the rightmost gate acting first. Alice has finished interacting before the gate involving Charlie acts.
For arbitrary product input density matrices, put τAB​=V(ρA​⊗ρB​)V†. The final reduced density matrix of Alice is
ρA′​=TrBC​[W(τAB​⊗ρC​)W†]=TrB​τAB​.
(10)
The equality follows because a unitary operator on the traced-out systems leaves the partial trace unchanged. The right-hand side is independent of Charlie's preparation. Therefore every outcome probability of every local measurement by Alice is independent of Charlie's message. Quantum no-signalling applies after Alice's last interaction, even though phase kickback can allow communication between the two qubits of an individual CNOT gate.

Solution

iv OurBigBook.com
Alice cannot send a bit to Charlie either. An argument based only on the apparent chronological chain would be insufficient: Alice interacts with Bob before Bob interacts with Charlie. The extra fact is that these particular gates commute.
In Bob's computational basis the complete gate is
U=IA​⊗∣0⟩⟨0∣B​⊗IC​+XA​⊗∣1⟩⟨1∣B​⊗XC​.
(11)
Both controlled-NOT gates preserve Bob's control label, and their target operations act on different systems. Thus WV=VW. We may consequently regard Bob–Charlie as the first interaction and Bob–Alice as the last one. The final operation on AB cannot change Charlie's reduced density matrix, and Charlie's earlier state cannot depend on Alice's initially separate preparation.
The target no-signalling in a common-control unitary formula makes the independence explicit:
ρC′​=⟨0∣ρB​∣0⟩ρC​+⟨1∣ρB​∣1⟩XρC​X.​
(12)
Alice's density matrix does not appear. The formula includes arbitrary complex initial amplitudes and every final measurement on Charlie. This conclusion uses the specified pair of gates; a general intervening operation on Bob could change the communication possibilities.

The displayed computational basis expansion is already a Schmidt decomposition, with coefficients 1−p​ and p​. For 0<p<1 both are nonzero, so the Schmidt rank is two. A pure product state has Schmidt rank one. The state is therefore entangled throughout the specified open interval.
Equivalently, the reduced density matrix is
ρA​=(1−p0​0p​),Tr(ρA2​)=1−2p(1−p)<1.
(14)
The reduced state is a mixed state, which is impossible for one factor of a pure product state. At the excluded endpoints the Schmidt rank drops to one, giving ∣00⟩ or ∣11⟩.

Put Dθ​=diag(1,eiθ). Factoring the target operator gives
U=Dθ​⊗V,
(15)
whereas U′=I⊗V. Their difference is a relative phase on the first qubit, not one overall global phase.
For an explicit witness, choose the product input ∣+⟩⊗∣χ⟩, with any normalized ∣χ⟩. The first-qubit outputs are respectively (∣0⟩+eiθ∣1⟩)/2​ and ∣+⟩. A Hadamard basis measurement returns + with probabilities
cos2(θ/2)and1.
(16)
These differ because θ is not a multiple of 2π. The two unitary operators implement different quantum channels. The global phase of the conditional target operation has become a physically observable phase on its control.

No: U is not an entangling gate. Its factorization gives, for every product input,
U(∣a⟩⊗∣b⟩)=(Dθ​∣a⟩)⊗(V∣b⟩),

Either experiment is possible. In the sequential experiment choose U=X. The first computational basis measurement on I/2 returns a uniform bit j and prepares ∣j⟩. The Pauli X gate changes it to ∣1−j⟩, so the second result is always opposite. The two possible records have probabilities Pr(0,1)=Pr(1,0)=1/2.
In the two-qubit experiment choose the fixed pure state
∣ψ⟩=∣Ψ+⟩=2​∣01⟩+∣10⟩​.
(18)
The Born rule gives the same two possible records, again with probability 1/2 each. The observed computational basis anticorrelation alone does not identify whether the correlation was generated sequentially or shared between two qubits.

Either experiment is again possible. The sequential experiment with U=I repeats its first measurement outcome in every basis: the first projective measurement has prepared one of that basis's eigenstates, and the identity leaves it unchanged.
For the two-qubit experiment choose
∣ψ⟩=∣Φ+⟩=2​∣00⟩+∣11⟩​=2​∣++⟩+∣−−⟩​.
(19)
The two expansions exhibit identical outcomes in both specified bases. Each allowed equal-label record has probability 1/2. These are fixed choices of U and ∣ψ⟩, respectively; neither depends on which of the two bases Alice selects.

Solution
Words: 227
iii OurBigBook.com
Only the two-qubit experiment is possible. Choose the spin singlet state
∣ψ⟩=∣Ψ−⟩=2​∣01⟩−∣10⟩​.
(20)
It has eigenvalue −1 under each of X⊗X, Y⊗Y and Z⊗Z. Thus measuring either qubit in the same Pauli basis as the other always gives opposite eigenvalue labels. Each opposite-label pair occurs with probability 1/2. More generally, collective-unitary covariance of the two-qubit singlet ensures opposite outcomes in any common orthonormal qubit basis.
For the sequential experiment, the first outcome is uniformly distributed and the Lüders rule prepares the corresponding eigenstate. Certain opposite outcomes in the computational basis force the fixed unitary matrix to have the form
U=(0b​a0​),∣a∣=∣b∣=1.
(21)
Certain opposite Hadamard basis outcomes require ⟨+∣U∣+⟩=(a+b)/2=0, hence b=−a. Therefore U=iaY. But this operator preserves the two Pauli Y eigenstate rays: it gives the same Y-basis label on the second measurement, rather than the opposite one. This contradicts the third set of observations.
Equivalently, flipping all three bases would require U†XU=−X, U†YU=−Y, and U†ZU=−Z. Conjugating XY=iZ in these two ways gives both iZ and −iZ, an impossibility. This three-axis obstruction to unitary qubit orthogonalization is a finite test of the obstruction behind the universal NOT gate.

Write ρ0​=∣ϕ⟩⟨ϕ∣=(I+rx​X+ry​Y+rz​Z)/2. Conjugating by a Pauli matrix keeps its own Bloch component and reverses the other two; for example,
Xρ0​X=21​(I+rx​X−ry​Y−rz​Z).
(24)
In the average of the four conjugations, the coefficient of every nonidentity Pauli matrix is zero. Consequently
4ρ0​+Xρ0​X+Yρ0​Y+Zρ0​Z​=2I​.​
(25)
The Pauli operator basis gives the Bloch representation of a qubit density operator.[ref]
This is the qubit Pauli twirl, the dimension-two case of the Heisenberg-Weyl twirling channel. The argument uses only trace one, so it also works for mixed qubit inputs. The preparation label must be forgotten: conditioning on the label would retain a known pure branch rather than the maximally mixed state obtained by the ensemble average.

Solution
Words: 151
i OurBigBook.com
No test on Alice and Bob's systems gives any information about which global state was prepared, even with arbitrarily many independent copies. In the pure GHZ density matrix, the off-diagonal terms contain ∣0⟩⟨1∣C​ or its adjoint. Their partial traces vanish. Hence both preparations have the same reduced density matrix
σAB​=TrC​(∣GHZ⟩⟨GHZ∣)=TrC​ρ=21​(∣00⟩⟨00∣+∣11⟩⟨11∣).
(28)
For any POVM effect E acting jointly on AB, the outcome probability is Tr(EσAB​) in either case. Equal reduced states give identical local statistics, where local here means local to the entire accessible laboratory AB, not separate operations on A and B.[ref]
For n independent copies, taking the partial trace of each inaccessible C system yields σAB⊗n​ under either hypothesis. Even an arbitrary collective quantum measurement on all 2n accessible qubits therefore has identical outcome probabilities. Adaptive local processing cannot change equality of the accessible density matrices. With equal prior probabilities the optimal guessing probability remains 1/2; with other priors, no observation updates their ratio.

Solution
Words: 353
ii OurBigBook.com
Charlie's Hadamard basis measurement, Alice's Bell-basis measurement and communicated correction bits give deterministic GHZ-assisted quantum teleportation. Let the unknown input be ∣χ⟩Q​=α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩. Charlie measures C in the Hadamard basis and labels + by c=0, − by c=1. The identity
∣GHZ⟩ABC​=2​1​(∣Φ+⟩AB​∣+⟩C​+∣Φ−⟩AB​∣−⟩C​)
(29)
shows that AB is conditionally a Bell pair with phase label c. Charlie sends that one classical bit to Bob. The procedure for localizing a Bell pair from a GHZ state supplies precisely the missing conditional information; forgetting c leaves the mixed reduced state of the preceding part.
Alice performs the usual Bell-basis measurement on QA. Operationally, she applies CNOTQ→A​, applies HQ​, and measures Q and A in the computational basis, obtaining bits s,t. She sends both bits to Bob. To track the correction without a convention ambiguity, conditional on s,t the normalized state on BC is
α∣t⟩B​∣t⟩C​+(−1)sβ∣1⊕t⟩B​∣1⊕t⟩C​.
(30)
Projecting Charlie's factor onto his recorded Hadamard basis state gives Bob, up to the global phase (−1)ct,
XtZs⊕c∣χ⟩B​.
(31)
Consequently the inverse Pauli correction is
Zs⊕cXt:XtZs⊕c∣χ⟩⟼∣χ⟩.​
(32)
Bob applies Xt first and Zs⊕c second. The measurements on Charlie and on Alice commute because they act on different systems, so deriving the conditional state with Alice's measurement first gives the same protocol as Charlie measuring first. Each triple (s,t,c) has probability 1/8, independent of the unknown input, and every triple succeeds after correction.
This uses one previously shared GHZ state, two classical bits from Alice and one from Charlie, with no quantum transmission during the transfer. The standard Bell-measurement/Pauli-correction mechanism was established in the original quantum teleportation proposal.[ref] Here Charlie's record supplies the Bell-pair phase, giving a controlled quantum teleportation protocol. Alice's input is consumed by her measurement; Bob reconstructs its state without learning α,β and without making a second surviving copy.
