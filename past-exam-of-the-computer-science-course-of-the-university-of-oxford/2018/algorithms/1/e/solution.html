Past exam of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2018 / Algorithms / 1 / e / Solution

 ... Computer science course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the computer science course of the University of Oxford 2018 Algorithms 1 e
OurBigBook.com Words: 191
Write y=x+1. After splaying on x, that key is at the root, and y is the smallest key in its right subtree. This uses the integer assumption: no stored key lies strictly between x and y. Thus y has an empty left subtree while it remains within the right subtree of x.
Now perform ordinary bottom-up splaying on y. Until the final step involving x, all tree rotations occur below x and leave it at the root. There are only two possible final cases.
  • If y is the right child of x, a single zig step rotates y above x, placing x immediately to its left.
  • Otherwise y is a grandchild of x. Its parent p lies in the right subtree of x and must satisfy p>y. Hence the last two directions are right then left. A zig-zag step rotates y above p and then above x, again placing x immediately to its left. A right-right final configuration is impossible because y is the minimum of this subtree.
Therefore x becomes the left child of x+1. The argument applies more generally to consecutive keys in a binary search tree, even when their numerical difference is not one.
