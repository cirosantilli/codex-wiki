OurBigBook.com Words: 204
With rows and columns ordered Head, Tail, the payoff matrix of ordered pairs is
HT​H(−2,2)(Y,−Y)​T(X,−X)(−2,2)​​.
(2)
This is a zero-sum game. There is no pure Nash equilibrium: when the coins match, player 1 gains by switching to a mismatch; when they do not match, player 2 gains by making them match. Since X,Y>0, these improvements are strict.
Let p and q be the respective probabilities that players 1 and 2 choose Head. Player 1's payoffs from Head and Tail are X−(X+2)q and (Y+2)q−2. Player 2 is indifferent when the corresponding player-1 payoffs against its two pure actions, Y−(Y+2)p and (X+2)p−2, agree. The resulting unique fully mixed Nash equilibrium is
p=X+Y+4Y+2​,q=X+Y+4X+2​.​
(3)
Both numbers lie strictly between zero and one. If either player instead used a pure action, the other's unique best response would force the pure-action cycle above, so there are no additional partly mixed equilibria. Substitution gives player 1's expected payoff (XY−4)/(X+Y+4) and its negative for player 2.
For X=1,Y=3, the best response correspondences are
BR1​(q)=⎩⎨⎧​{1},[0,1],{0},​q<3/8,q=3/8,q>3/8,​BR2​(p)=⎩⎨⎧​{0},[0,1],{1},​p<5/8,p=5/8,p>5/8.​
(4)
Their intersection is (p,q)=(5/8,3/8), with expected payoffs (−1/8,1/8).
