In the final round, player 1 strictly accepts every q2​>0. In any subgame perfect equilibrium it must also accept q2​=0 with probability one. Otherwise, if that acceptance probability were a<1, offering zero would give player 2 only aα. A sufficiently small positive offer would be accepted and yield more. No positive offer maximizes player 2's payoff either, because a smaller positive offer improves it. The same supremum argument rules out a mixed offer distribution attaining the missing maximum. Thus the final-round equilibrium uniquely has q2​=0, accepted, with payoffs (0,α).
In round one, player 2 therefore rejects q1​<α and accepts q1​>α. At q1​=α it is indifferent, but equilibrium again forces acceptance with probability one: if acceptance were less likely, player 1 could improve on any proposed optimum with an offer just above α. Since α<1, player 1 strictly prefers the resulting positive payoff to rejection.
Consequently the unique equilibrium outcome is immediate agreement at q1​=α, giving payoffs (1−α,α). The full strategies are: player 1 offers α initially and accepts every nonnegative final offer; player 2 accepts initial offers at least α, rejects smaller ones, and offers zero in every reached final-round subgame. These prescriptions cover off-path histories too. Acceptance at indifference follows from the continuous-offer bargaining acceptance lemma, rather than an arbitrary tie-breaking convention.
