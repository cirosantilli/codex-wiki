Rows now record voter 2's initial choice and columns voter 3's choice. In a three-way split, the specified tie-break produces A, which explains, for example, the off-diagonal B,C entry.
Voter 3 optimizes its ranking B≻C≻A in each row, giving the continuation winners A,B,C. Voter 2 then chooses C, its favourite of the three. The winner is therefore C in every subgame perfect equilibrium, again by unique outcome under strict outcome preferences. In particular, if voter 2 starts with C, voter 3 must also vote C: either other vote lets the last voter force A.
