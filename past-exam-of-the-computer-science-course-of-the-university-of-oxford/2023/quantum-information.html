A density matrix is a complex d×d Hermitian matrix that is positive semidefinite and has unit trace:
ρ=ρ†,v†ρv≥0 for every v∈Cd,Trρ=1.​
(1)
Equivalently, its eigenvalues are nonnegative and sum to one, with an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.[ref]

p(j)=⟨bj​∣ρ∣bj​⟩=Tr(ρ∣bj​⟩⟨bj​∣).​

∣ϕ+​⟩⟨ϕ+​∣=21​∑j,k=01​∣j⟩⟨k∣A​⊗∣j⟩⟨k∣B​,ρB​=21​∑j,k=01​⟨k∣j⟩∣j⟩⟨k∣.

ρB​=21​(∣0⟩⟨0∣+∣1⟩⟨1∣)=2I​.​
(4)

Write the proposed quantum state ensemble as {(∣u⟩,p),(∣v⟩,1−p)} with normalized pure states, so that
2I​=p∣u⟩⟨u∣+(1−p)∣v⟩⟨v∣.
(5)
Taking the expectation values in ∣u⟩ and ∣v⟩, and setting c=∣⟨u∣v⟩∣2, gives
21​=p+(1−p)c≥p,21​=pc+1−p≥1−p.
(6)
Consequently p≤1/2 and p≥1/2. Substitution then gives c=0. The two pure states are therefore orthogonal, with equal probabilities:
p=21​,∣v⟩=∣u⊥⟩ up to a global phase.​
(7)
Conversely, any orthonormal basis of a qubit has projectors summing to I, so such a quantum state ensemble does give I/2. This proves the characterization of two-state ensembles of a maximally mixed qubit.

Alice uses conjugate-basis steering of a Bell pair. Write the desired orthonormal basis for Bob as ∣ψ⟩=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩ and ∣ψ⊥⟩=c∣0⟩+d∣1⟩. Entrywise complex conjugation in the computational basis gives Alice's orthonormal basis
∣ψ​⟩=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩,∣ψ⊥​⟩=c∣0⟩+d∣1⟩.
(8)
For either ∣u⟩ in Bob's chosen orthonormal basis, the corresponding unnormalized conditional quantum state is
(⟨u∣A​⊗IB​)∣ϕ+​⟩AB​=2​1​∑j=01​⟨u∣j⟩∣j⟩B​=2​∣u⟩B​​.
(9)
Its squared norm is 1/2. Thus Alice measures in the complex-conjugate basis, obtaining the required Bob pure states with probability 1/2 each. Averaging over Alice's undisclosed outcome leaves Bob's density matrix equal to I/2, consistent with the no-communication theorem.

Use deterministic two-qubit entanglement dilution, retaining the specified complex probability amplitudes. Alice implements a quantum instrument on A with Kraus operators
M0​=(α0​0β​),M1​=(β0​0α​).
(10)
The Kraus representation completeness condition holds even for complex α,β:
M0†​M0​+M1†​M1​=(∣α∣2+∣β∣2)I=I.
(11)
To realize this using the permitted quantum gates and a basic measurement, Alice initializes an ancilla qubit C to ∣0⟩. Conditional on her computational-basis state being j, she applies to C the unitary matrix
V0​=(αβ​−β​α​),V1​=(βα​−αβ​​).
(12)
Each has orthonormal columns. Therefore W=∣0⟩⟨0∣A​⊗V0​+∣1⟩⟨1∣A​⊗V1​ is a unitary gate, with
W(∣u⟩A​∣0⟩C​)=M0​∣u⟩A​∣0⟩C​+M1​∣u⟩A​∣1⟩C​.
(13)
A computational-basis measurement of C implements the desired quantum instrument. Its two unnormalized branches on AB are
(M0​⊗I)∣ϕ+​⟩=2​α∣00⟩+β∣11⟩​,(M1​⊗I)∣ϕ+​⟩=2​β∣00⟩+α∣11⟩​.
(14)
Each has probability 1/2. Alice sends her outcome m as one classical bit. For m=0, both parties keep their qubits unchanged. For m=1, both apply a Pauli X gate, giving
(X⊗X)(β∣00⟩+α∣11⟩)=α∣00⟩+β∣11⟩.
(15)
Thus both outcomes produce the exact target using one classical bit and local operations. The construction includes α=0 or β=0 and arbitrary relative quantum phases; it requires no quantum channel between the parties.

Use three-way qutrit sign-flip discrimination. Prepare the qutrit in the uniform superposition state
∣s⟩=3​∣0⟩+∣1⟩+∣2⟩​.
(23)
For an unknown label j∈{0,1,2}, the supplied unitary operator is Uj​=I−2∣j⟩⟨j∣. Apply it once and then apply the known inversion about the mean
D=2∣s⟩⟨s∣−I=31​​−122​2−12​22−1​​.
(24)
Since D†=D and D2=I, this is an allowed unitary transformation. Using ⟨s∣j⟩=1/3​ gives
DUj​∣s⟩=D(∣s⟩−3​2​∣j⟩)=−31​∣s⟩+3​2​∣j⟩=33​5∣j⟩−∑k=j​∣k⟩​.
(25)
Perform a computational-basis measurement and guess that its outcome is the unknown label. The Born rule gives
Pr(correct∣Uj​)=​33​5​​2=2725​.​
(26)
Each incorrect outcome has probability 1/27, verifying normalization. The success probability is 25/27 separately for every label, so no assumption about their prior probabilities is needed.

d OurBigBook.com
We prove the one-query constancy obstruction for phase oracles while allowing arbitrary quantum ancillas. By purification of a density operator, the quantum state immediately before the sole query can be taken as
∣Ψ⟩=∑x=0M−1​∣x⟩∣ax​⟩,px​=⟨ax​∣ax​⟩,∑x​px​=1.
(27)
The ancilla vectors need not be normalized or orthogonal. Classical randomness and earlier quantum measurements can be included in additional ancillas; allowing access to these systems only gives the proposed algorithm more power.
For the zero constant function, the phase oracle is I, leaving ∣Ψ⟩ unchanged. For each j, consider the Boolean function that is one at j and zero elsewhere. Since M>2, it is nonconstant, and its phase oracle produces
∣Ψj​⟩=∣Ψ⟩−2∣j⟩∣aj​⟩,⟨Ψ∣Ψj​⟩=1−2pj​.
(28)
Any later fixed unitary transformation preserves these inner products. An arbitrary final quantum measurement cannot distinguish the constant output from each nonconstant output with certainty unless they are orthogonal, by perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality. Explicitly, an accepting POVM effect E would satisfy E∣Ψ⟩=∣Ψ⟩ and E∣Ψj​⟩=0, forcing ⟨Ψ∣Ψj​⟩=0.
Therefore exact classification requires pj​=1/2 for every j. Summing gives
1=j=0∑M−1​pj​=2M​,​
(29)
which contradicts M>2. Thus one query cannot decide constancy with certainty. The other constant function gives −I, differing only by a global phase, so it does not change the argument. For comparison, M=2 permits p0​=p1​=1/2; the impossibility arises from demanding the test work for every nonconstant Boolean function.

A bipartite pure state is entangled exactly when it is not a product state: there are no normalized quantum states ∣u⟩A​,∣v⟩B​ such that
∣ψ⟩AB​=∣u⟩A​⊗∣v⟩B​.
(30)
Equivalently, its Schmidt rank exceeds one, or either reduced density matrix is a mixed state. This definition concerns quantum entanglement across the specified division into systems A and B.

An entangling gate is a unitary transformation U on two quantum systems for which at least one pure product input has an entangled output:
∃∣u⟩A​,∣v⟩B​:U(∣u⟩A​⊗∣v⟩B​) is entangled.​
(31)
It need not create quantum entanglement on every product state. For example, the controlled-NOT gate leaves ∣00⟩ a product state but maps ∣+⟩∣0⟩ to a Bell state, so it is an entangling gate.

i OurBigBook.com
Alice holds the input qubit Q in ∣ψ⟩Q​=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩ and her half A of a shared Bell pair ∣ϕ+​⟩AB​; Bob holds B. Alice applies a controlled-NOT gate Q→A, then a Hadamard gate on Q. In the system order Q,A,B, the initial quantum state is
2​1​(a∣000⟩+a∣011⟩+b∣100⟩+b∣111⟩).
(35)
After these two quantum gates, direct expansion yields the quantum teleportation identity
21​[∣00⟩QA​(a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩)B​+∣01⟩QA​(b∣0⟩+a∣1⟩)B​+∣10⟩QA​(a∣0⟩−b∣1⟩)B​+∣11⟩QA​(−b∣0⟩+a∣1⟩)B​]=21​∑m,n=01​∣mn⟩QA​XBn​ZBm​∣ψ⟩B​.
(36)
Alice performs computational-basis measurements on Q,A, and sends the two classical bits m,n to Bob. Each outcome has probability 1/4, independently of the input pure state. Bob applies the indicated Pauli gates: first Xn, then Zm, so his total correction is ZmXn. In every branch,
ZmXnXnZm∣ψ⟩=∣ψ⟩.​
(37)
This proves exact quantum teleportation using one Bell pair and two classical bits. Alice's original qubit has been measured; the protocol transfers the quantum state without leaving her another copy.

Write the arbitrary initial pure state as
∣χ⟩QR​=∣0⟩Q​∣r0​⟩R​+∣1⟩Q​∣r1​⟩R​,⟨r0​∣r0​⟩+⟨r1​∣r1​⟩=1.
(38)
The reference vectors need not be normalized or orthogonal. By linearity, the same quantum teleportation calculation, with the identity operator on R, gives the post-gate quantum state
21​∑m,n=01​∣mn⟩QA​[(XBn​ZBm​)⊗IR​](∣0⟩B​∣r0​⟩R​+∣1⟩B​∣r1​⟩R​).
(39)
Thus each outcome still has probability 1/4. Bob's Pauli gate correction leaves the normalized joint quantum state
∣χ⟩BR​=∣0⟩B​∣r0​⟩R​+∣1⟩B​∣r1​⟩R​.​
(40)
All operations act trivially on R, so the result is independent of its holder or location. This establishes teleportation as an identity channel on a reference, preserving quantum entanglement with the untouched reference. Since every corrected branch is the same, discarding the classical outcomes also leaves the required joint density matrix.

Combine the hypothetical protocol with superdense coding. Before a message is chosen, Alice and Bob share a further Bell pair ∣ϕ+​⟩QR​, with Q at Alice and R at Bob, in addition to any resources needed for the hypothetical quantum teleportation protocol. To encode two classical bits u,v, Alice applies the Pauli gates ZQu​XQv​, preparing
∣βuv​⟩QR​=(ZQu​XQv​⊗IR​)∣ϕ+​⟩QR​.
(41)
The four choices are the four mutually orthogonal Bell states. Alice now uses the hypothetical protocol to teleport Q to Bob using only one classical bit. Its assumed preservation of the joint quantum state gives Bob both B and R in
∣βuv​⟩BR​=(ZBu​XBv​⊗IR​)∣ϕ+​⟩BR​.
(42)
Bob performs a Bell-basis measurement. Explicitly, a controlled-NOT gate B→R, followed by a Hadamard gate on B, gives
(HB​⊗IR​)CNOTB→R​∣βuv​⟩BR​=∣u⟩B​∣v⟩R​.​
(43)
A computational-basis measurement therefore recovers both message bits with certainty after only one transmitted classical bit. The initial Bell pairs are shared before the message is chosen and carry no message themselves. This proves the classical communication lower bound for exact qubit teleportation: a perfect protocol preserving an external reference needs at least two transmitted classical bits.
