A basic measurement is specified by an orthonormal basis {∣bj​⟩}j=0d−1​, with outcome j associated with the rank-one orthogonal projection Pj​=∣bj​⟩⟨bj​∣. For a normalized pure state ∣ψ⟩, the Born rule gives
p(j)=⟨ψ∣Pj​∣ψ⟩=∣⟨bj​∣ψ⟩∣2.​
(1)
The probabilities sum to one because ∑j​Pj​=I. Conditional on an outcome with p(j)>0, the post-measurement state is
p(j)​Pj​∣ψ⟩​=∣⟨bj​∣ψ⟩∣⟨bj​∣ψ⟩​∣bj​⟩,​
(2)
which represents ∣bj​⟩ up to a global phase. An outcome of zero probability has no conditional state to normalize. For a density matrix ρ, the corresponding expressions are p(j)=Tr(Pj​ρ) and Pj​ρPj​/p(j). If the outcome is ignored, the nonselective projective measurement instead produces ∑j​Pj​ρPj​, generally a mixed state.[ref]

A bipartite unitary operator is an entangling gate if it maps at least one product state to an entangled state. It need not entangle every product input. For deterministic classical responses, denote the two possible local answers by x0​,x1​, y0​,y1​ and z0​,z1​. Certainty in the GHZ game would require
x0​⊕y0​⊕z0​x0​⊕y1​⊕z1​x1​⊕y0​⊕z1​x1​⊕y1​⊕z0​​=0,=1,=1,=1.​
(18)
Taking the exclusive or of all four equations gives zero on the left, since every answer occurs twice, but one on the right. This contradiction rules out deterministic certainty. Pre-shared randomness cannot cure it: conditioning on the random seed fixes a deterministic response table, and none succeeds on every allowed input. Thus noncommunicating classical players cannot win with certainty. No uniform distribution on the referee's questions is needed for this worst-case claim.
For the quantum strategy, share the GHZ state and measure the Pauli X gate observable when the local question is 0, or the Pauli Y gate observable when it is 1. Return bit 0 for measurement eigenvalue +1 and bit 1 for eigenvalue −1. The four relevant operator identities are
(X⊗X⊗X)∣GHZ⟩(X⊗Y⊗Y)∣GHZ⟩(Y⊗X⊗Y)∣GHZ⟩(Y⊗Y⊗X)∣GHZ⟩​=∣GHZ⟩,=−∣GHZ⟩,=−∣GHZ⟩,=−∣GHZ⟩.​
(19)
For the first identity, the three bit flips exchange ∣000⟩ and ∣111⟩. For each remaining identity, the two Y actions contribute i2=−1 on ∣000⟩ and (−i)2=−1 on ∣111⟩, so they exchange the terms with a minus sign. Local measurements on separate systems commute, and the product of their observed eigenvalues is therefore fixed by the displayed joint eigenvalue. That product equals (−1)x+y+z, giving exactly the required output parity for each promised input. Hence the quantum strategy wins with certainty without communication. This is the GHZ theorem in nonlocal-game form; it does not let a player control another player's individual outcome.

For every nontrivial dimension d≥2, no such pure state exists. The literal claim needs this qualification: for d=1, the only measurement outcome is necessarily the same for both parties. The contradiction below requires allowing all complex orthonormal bases, as quantum theory does.
If equal outcomes were certain in the computational basis, all amplitudes with different labels would vanish, so the normalized joint state would have the form
∣Ψ⟩=∑j=0d−1​cj​∣j⟩∣j⟩.
(23)
Choose distinct labels j,k and replace their computational vectors in both local bases by ∣+⟩jk​=(∣j⟩+∣k⟩)/2​ and ∣−⟩jk​=(∣j⟩−∣k⟩)/2​, leaving all other vectors fixed. The joint amplitude for the different outcomes +,− is (cj​−ck​)/2. Certainty of equal outcomes would force cj​=ck​.
Now use the complex pair ∣+i⟩jk​=(∣j⟩+i∣k⟩)/2​ and ∣−i⟩jk​=(∣j⟩−i∣k⟩)/2​ in the same way on both systems. The amplitude for different outcomes +i,−i is (cj​+ck​)/2, because the conjugated bra factors on the ∣kk⟩ term multiply to (−i)(i)=1. Thus equal outcomes would also force cj​=−ck​. Combining the two requirements gives cj​=ck​=0. Every label belongs to such a pair when d≥2, forcing the zero vector and contradicting normalization. This proves impossibility of universal identical-basis perfect correlations.
The maximally entangled state d−1/2∑j​∣jj⟩ is not a counterexample: it correlates matching labels when one party uses a basis and the other its coordinatewise complex conjugate basis. Requiring the two complex bases to be identical is different. This distinction also follows from conjugate-state steering from a maximally entangled pair; restricting attention to real bases would miss the contradiction.

Both the controlled-NOT gate and Pauli X gate permute computational basis vectors. Any finite composition remains a permutation matrix, and so sends ∣0⋯0⟩ to another computational basis vector. It cannot prepare a superposition such as ∣+⟩⊗∣0⋯0⟩.

Hence {CNOT,X} is not universal. Basis permutations can create entanglement on some superposed inputs, but this gate set cannot create the required superposition from the specified basis input.

Prepare ∣s⟩=N−1/2∑x​∣x⟩; apply the supplied phase oracle to negate amplitudes on the N/4 marked inputs; apply the Grover diffusion operator D=2∣s⟩⟨s∣−I; and measure in the computational basis. If F prepares ∣s⟩ from ∣0⟩, the reflection is D=F(2∣0⟩⟨0∣−I)F†, requiring no further oracle query.
After the oracle the amplitudes are 1/N​ on the 3N/4 unmarked labels and −1/N​ on the marked labels. Their average is
μ=N1​(4N​3N​−4N​N​)=2N​1​.
(32)
The reflection performs inversion about the mean, ax​↦2μ−ax​. It therefore sends every unmarked amplitude to zero and every marked amplitude to 2/N​. The resulting normalized state is
N​2​f(x)=1∑​∣x⟩.​

Each marked label has Born rule probability 4/N, every unmarked label has probability zero, and the marked probabilities sum to (N/4)(4/N)=1. This is one-step exact Grover search with a quarter of the inputs marked, using one reduced-oracle query. The positive integer N must be divisible by four; a qubit implementation may take N=2m, but the stated reflection proof does not assume that register convention.

Use the Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm: initialize n qubits in ∣0n⟩, apply H⊗n, query the supplied phase oracle once, apply H⊗n again, and measure all n computational bits. Immediately after the query the state is
2n​1​∑x​(−1)a⋅x∣x⟩,
(34)
where the dot product in the exponent is taken modulo two. The Walsh-Hadamard transform gives amplitude
⟨y∣H⊗nOf​H⊗n∣0n⟩​=2n1​x∈{0,1}n∑​(−1)(a⊕y)⋅x=j=1∏n​21+(−1)aj​⊕yj​​.​
(35)
Every factor is one if yj​=aj​ and zero otherwise. Thus the final state is exactly ∣a⟩, and the measurement outputs a with certainty after one oracle query. This is the character cancellation behind Bernstein-Vazirani phase kickback; because access here is already to the reduced phase oracle, no extra target ancilla or conversion from a bit-flip oracle is required.
