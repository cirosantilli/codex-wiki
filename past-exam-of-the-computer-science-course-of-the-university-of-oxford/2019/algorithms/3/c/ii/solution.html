Write U′=U∖{y} and F′={B∖{y}:B∈F}. If A is a basis for the original instance, replace each member A by A∖{y}. Deletion commutes with unions, so the resulting family generates every member of F′ and has at most ∣A∣ members.
Conversely, take a basis A′ for the reduced instance. Lift each of its members by
L(A′)={A′∪{y},A′,​x∈A′,x∈/A′.​
(29)
If B∖{y} is represented as a union of reduced basis sets, the same union of their lifts agrees with B on U′. It contains y exactly when one of the selected reduced basis sets contains x, namely exactly when x∈B. By the assumed incidence equivalence in a set family, this occurs exactly when y∈B. Thus the lifted union is precisely B, and the lifted basis has no more members.
Therefore deleting y preserves the yes/no answer with the same parameter k. The reverse construction does not require a reduced basis set to be an input set.
