Let B=AT and choose any column probability vector q, for example the first coordinate unit vector. Because A has nonnegative entries and each row sums to one, B maps the probability simplex
Δ={v∈Rn:v≥0, 1Tv=1}
(24)
into itself: Bv≥0 and 1TBv=(A1)Tv=1Tv=1.
The iterates Bjq need not converge, so take their arithmetic averages,
xN​=N1​∑j=0N−1​Bjq∈Δ.
(25)
A telescoping sum gives
BxN​−xN​=NBNq−q​.
(26)
Both vectors on the right before subtraction have L1 norm one, so ∥BxN​−xN​∥1​≤2/N→0.
The probability simplex is closed and bounded in finite-dimensional Euclidean space, hence compact by the Heine-Borel theorem. It follows that a subsequence xNj​​ converges to some x∈Δ. Passing to the limit in the displayed identity, using continuity of the linear map B, gives ATx=Bx=x, with x≥0 and ∑i​xi​=1.
This proves stationary distribution existence for a finite Markov chain without any Markov chain irreducibility or aperiodicity assumption. Those stronger hypotheses concern uniqueness and convergence, not the existence required here.
