For an action sequence π, let Tπ​(B) be the set of all physical states obtainable by applying it to the initial belief state B. Under a deterministic model this is an image; for nondeterministic outcomes it is the union of all permitted images. For B′⊆B,
Tπ​(B′)⊆Tπ​(B).
(5)
If each action is applicable to every current possibility from B, it is also applicable to every possibility from B′. If the final belief state for B is contained in the physical goal set G, then Tπ​(B′)⊆G. Thus the same plan solves every subset of B.
Nothing comparable follows for an arbitrary superset. For example, let an action take s1​ to a goal and leave s2​ at a non-goal. It solves {s1​} but not {s1​,s2​}. A larger belief state is solved by this particular sequence exactly when all added states also permit it and end in G. It may succeed on some supersets and fail on others; it cannot be guaranteed merely from success on B.
