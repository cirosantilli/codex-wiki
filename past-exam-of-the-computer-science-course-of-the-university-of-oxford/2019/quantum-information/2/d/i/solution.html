The two-qubit product-state determinant criterion therefore says that this branch is entangled whenever a=0. Its probability is (∣b∣2+2∣a∣2)/3>0, so such a branch is physically possible and can be normalized without changing whether its determinant vanishes.
For an orthonormal basis {∣u0​⟩,∣u1​⟩}, completeness gives ∣⟨0∣u0​⟩∣2+∣⟨0∣u1​⟩∣2=1. At least one vector has a=0. Hence at least one nonzero-probability outcome leaves AB entangled, proving conditional entanglement of the three-qubit W state. Some bases give two entangled branches; the computational basis gives one entangled and one product branch. The claim is not that every outcome must entangle AB.
