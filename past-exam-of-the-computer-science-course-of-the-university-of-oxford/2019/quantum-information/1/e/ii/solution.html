At the last step, ∣ψ0,n​⟩=∣1⟩ and ∣ψ1,n​⟩=∣0⟩. Ignoring the recorded outcome, the final density matrix is
ρn​=qn​∣1⟩⟨1∣+(1−qn​)∣0⟩⟨0∣.
(13)
For large n, the Taylor expansion logcosx=−x2/2+O(x4) gives
nlogcos(π/n)=−2nπ2​+O(n−3)⟶0.
(14)
Thus qn​→1, and the final state tends to ∣1⟩⟨1∣, or ∣1⟩ as a pure-state representative. In fact 1−qn​∼π2/(4n), and its trace distance from ∣1⟩⟨1∣ is exactly 1−qn​. With records retained, the final conditional state is ∣1⟩ with probability tending to one. These are compatible descriptions, not a coherent sum of the two outcome branches. This quantum Zeno effect guides a known preparation through slowly rotating measurement bases; it is not a unitary Pauli X gate acting coherently on an arbitrary unknown input.
