Velocity (v=dr/dt)

OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
Velocity is the vector giving the rate of change of position with time, measured in a specified reference frame. For a differentiable path r(t), its instantaneous velocity and its average velocity between two times are
v(t)=dtdr​,vavg​=t2​−t1​r(t2​)−r(t1​)​,t2​>t1​.
(56)
The displacement in the numerator retains direction. In Cartesian coordinates, each vector component is a derivative, for example vx​=dx/dt; its sign tells whether the corresponding coordinate is increasing or decreasing. The SI unit of velocity is m/s. [ref]
For a concrete reversal, let x(t)=(1m)+(1m/s2)t2−(2m/s)t for 0≤t≤2s. Its signed velocity is vx​(t)=(2m/s2)t−2m/s: at t=0 it is −2m/s, at t=1s it is zero, and at t=2s it is +2m/s. The opposite endpoint velocities describe opposite directions, although the endpoint speeds agree. The position moves from 1m to 0 and back to 1m.
Recovering displacement from a measured velocity is an integration problem: r(t2​)−r(t1​)=∫t1​t2​​v(t)dt, with the integral taken component by component. In the reversal example, ∫02s​vx​(t)dt=0: the negative and positive contributions cancel. This distinction is useful in navigation, where the final position depends on direction as well as speed.
