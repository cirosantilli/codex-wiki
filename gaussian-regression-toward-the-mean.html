Gaussian regression toward the mean (Y∣X=x∼N(0.6x,0.64))

Take the original synthetic model T∼N(0,0.6) and ε1​,ε2​∼N(0,0.4), mutually independent random variables, with the second argument denoting variance. The pair (X,Y) has a multivariate normal distribution with mean zero, unit marginal variances, and Pearson correlation coefficient 0.6. Its conditional multivariate normal distribution is
Y∣X=x∼N(0.6x,1−0.62)=N(0.6x,0.64).
(20)
For a first reading x=2, the second reading has conditional expectation 1.2 and conditional variance 0.64, hence standard deviation 0.8. There is still conditional probability 1−Φ(1)≈0.1587 of Y>2, a movement farther from zero, where Φ is the standard normal distribution function. The line 0.6x describes a conditional mean, not an individual trajectory.
A short derivation isolates R=Y−0.6X. Its covariance with X is zero and its variance is 0.64. Because (X,R) has a multivariate normal distribution, zero covariance implies independence: R∼N(0,0.64) independently of X. This also explains why reversing the two measurements gives E[X∣Y=y]=0.6y. The statistical association does not identify a direction of causal effect.
