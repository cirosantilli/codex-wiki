Hydrostatic stress (σ=−pI)

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology
OurBigBook.com Words: 178
With tension taken positive, hydrostatic stress is the stress tensor σ=−pI, where I is the identity matrix. Every plane has the same compressive normal stress, and no tangential traction. More generally,
p=−31​trσ,σ=−pI+s,trs=0,
(32)
where s is deviatoric stress. Two samples can share the same mean pressure while experiencing different deviatoric stresses. A stationary liquid cannot sustain equilibrium shear stress; a solid transmitting medium can support it. Consequently, a load divided by surface area does not by itself establish hydrostatic sample conditions. A transmitting medium's state at the actual pressure and temperature, and contact between sample and walls, matter.
Bridgman's Figure 4 compares mild steel fractured in tension at atmospheric pressure with steel stretched further in a liquid at 25000kgfcm−2.[ref] The imposed tension remains a directional load: the experiment combines confinement with deformation, rather than measuring a hydrostatic stress alone. Its comparison motivates recording the loading path and the surrounding medium as well as pressure; it does not establish a universal fracture threshold for all steels.
