Continuous transmission need not preserve normal derivatives ([u]=0 ⟹ [∂ν​u]=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Mixed-type partial differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 127
A function continuous across an interface can have different one-sided normal derivatives. For instance, u(x,y)=y when y<0 and u(x,y)=0 when y≥0 is continuous, satisfies uxx​+sign(y)uyy​=0 on each open side, and has zero tangential second derivative on the interface. Its lower y-derivative is one and its upper y-derivative is zero. Thus the normal derivative does not exist there as a two-sided derivative.
More generally, for functions smooth on each side with one-sided first derivative traces, global C1 regularity would force both traces to equal the derivative on the interface. Unequal traces therefore preclude a globally classical C2 solution as well. A condition on the function value alone is not a condition on its normal derivative; a specified transmission law or an appropriate weak formulation is additional information.
