Topological quantum matter

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 81
Topological quantum matter has robust low-energy properties controlled by global topology rather than local order parameters.
The central question is which quantum states can be joined by a smooth change of a local Hamiltonian while keeping a nonzero bulk spectral gap. A Chern insulator makes this question concrete: two band insulators can have different integer Chern numbers, forcing a gap closure between them. The Haldane phase supplies a one-dimensional example in which a protecting symmetry matters. These are distinct from intrinsic topological order, with its anyons and topology-dependent ground-state degeneracy. The Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition concerns a separate mechanism involving vortices; topology organizes more than one kind of physical phenomenon.

Topological insulator

Words: 65
Topological quantum matter OurBigBook.com
A topological insulator has an insulating bulk electronic spectrum and boundary electronic behavior constrained by a nontrivial bulk band topology and the relevant protecting symmetries. In common three-dimensional time-reversal-invariant examples, the conducting surface can be approximated at low energy by a Dirac cone. The number of surface cones and the range of the linear approximation depend on the material; boundary conduction and thermal bulk phonons therefore need separate state counts.

Topological quantum order

Words: 114 Articles: 1
Topological quantum matter OurBigBook.com
The local-pair condition for topological quantum order asks for an orthogonal partner state with local indistinguishability on supports of diameter at most a fixed fraction of the system diameter, with a fixed error smaller than one. This criterion captures a form of macroscopic information hidden from local observables; it is not a complete definition of every topological phase.

Backward preservation of local indistinguishability

Words: 61
Topological quantum order OurBigBook.com
Undo a short evolution of two orthogonal locally indistinguishable states. Orthogonality is preserved exactly. A Lieb-Robinson bound localizes every backward-evolved observable within an expanded region and bounds its norm error. After normalizing the approximate observable, the initial expectation difference is at most ε(1+δ)+2δ. Polynomial volume growth in the system diameter controls the localization prefactor and yields preservation for sufficiently small linear-time coefficient.

Local indistinguishability

Words: 47
Topological quantum matter OurBigBook.com
Two quantum states are locally indistinguishable at a prescribed scale if all operators of norm at most one supported below that scale have nearly equal expectation values. This is a statement about restricted observables, not equality of the global states. An orthogonal pair can be locally indistinguishable on macroscopically large regions.

Aharonov-Bohm effect (δ=qΦ/ℏ(mod2π))

Words: 1k Articles: 4
Charge-flux composite OurBigBook.com
The Aharonov-Bohm effect is the phase qΦ/ℏ acquired when a charge q encircles magnetic flux Φ, even when the magnetic field vanishes along the charge's path.
In the magnetic Aharonov-Bohm effect, a coherent electron wave takes two routes around inaccessible magnetic flux. Recombination converts their relative wave phase into displaced interference fringes. In 1959, Yakir Aharonov and David Bohm proposed a shielded solenoid geometry and identified the obstruction to removing the magnetic vector potential throughout the accessible region.[ref]
Use International System of Units, electric charge q, and magnetic minimal coupling H=(−iℏ∇−qA)2/(2m). Let γ1​,γ2​ run from the same source S to the same detector point D. The oriented closed curve C follows γ1​ and then γ2​ backwards. For a static magnetic vector potential, the magnetic contribution to the relative wave phase is
δ=ℏq​(∫γ1​​A⋅dl−∫γ2​​A⋅dl)=ℏq​∮C​A⋅dl=ℏqΦC​​(mod2π).
(2)
Here ΦC​=∫Σ​B⋅dS is the oriented magnetic flux through a spanning surface. Equal or calibrated nonmagnetic propagation phases are assumed. For an electron, q=−e, where e>0 is the elementary charge; reversing C reverses the sign. The observable is the gauge-invariant holonomy eiδ, rather than a unique local value of the magnetic vector potential.
The accessible region is a multiply connected region: C cannot contract to a point without entering the excluded flux region. Stokes theorem does not set the line integral to zero, because a spanning surface must cross that excluded region, where the magnetic field need not vanish. There is real magnetic flux in the apparatus; only the accessible paths are idealized as free of the magnetic field. The exterior of a finite straight solenoid also permits routes around its ends, so its exact topology and leakage differ from those of an infinite solenoid or an excluded solid torus.
An explicit model makes the obstruction visible. Outside an infinite flux tube of radius a, choose cylindrical coordinates (r,θ,z) and
A=2πrΦ​θ,r>a.
(3)
Its curl vanishes there, but a loop with winding number n has ∮C​A⋅dl=nΦ. Locally A=∇(Φθ/(2π)); the angular coordinate changes by 2π on a circuit. For generic qΦ/ℏ, that candidate gauge removal changes the wave function's single-valued boundary condition. On a simply connected region, a smooth static magnetic vector potential with zero curl instead has a globally defined scalar primitive and is a pure gauge potential. Thus local zero magnetic field and globally removable magnetic vector potential are different conditions.
For an original interference example, take mutually coherent scalar amplitudes with constant individual intensities I1​,I2​ and an imposed carrier phase ky, as in off-axis holography. Then
I(y)=I1​+I2​+2I1​I2​​cos(ky+δ),Δy=−kδ​(mod2π/k).
(4)
The interference fringe spacing is d=2π/k. An electron enclosing Φ=h/(4e) has δ=−π/2, shifting maxima by d/4 for this orientation. Increasing the magnetic flux by h/e changes δ by −2π and restores the same pattern: the electron Aharonov-Bohm effect has magnetic flux quantum h/e. This uses h=2πℏ, the Planck constant. It does not require the source's magnetic flux to vary continuously during a single exposure.
For I1​=1, I2​=1/4, the extrema are Imax​=9/4, Imin​=1/4, and the interferometric visibility is 4/5. A fixed Aharonov-Bohm phase translates these fringes without changing their contrast. Averaging unresolved fluctuating phases can reduce the contrast; a darkened pattern alone cannot identify enclosed magnetic flux. These formulas assume coherence, indistinguishable alternatives and stable beam envelopes.
The appeal of the Aharonov-Bohm effect is that a local Lorentz force calculation along either ideal path misses information carried by their global arrangement. It gives a physical example of holonomy and supplies the encircling phase used in Chern--Simons flux attachment. This alone does not establish intrinsic topological order. The static comparison also matters: changing magnetic flux can induce an electric field, so a time-dependent experiment requires the scalar-potential contribution as well as the displayed spatial line integral.

Cosine-potential gap in a quantum ring

(5)

Shielded toroidal-flux electron interference
Words: 253
Aharonov-Bohm effect OurBigBook.com
In 1986, Akira Tonomura and collaborators coated a ferromagnet shaped as a torus with superconducting niobium and copper. A cooled stage held it in a 150 kV field-emission electron microscope. An electron biprism overlapped object and reference waves; film holograms were reconstructed optically. Room-temperature leakage screening selected samples; varying copper thickness tested electron penetration.[ref]
Below the superconducting transition, through-hole and outside electron waves differed in wave phase by zero or π modulo 2π. Warming through it changed the interference reversibly. Leakage and coherent-penetration bounds do not prove zero magnetic field.[ref]
Why these two phases? The shield's superconducting flux quantum is h/(2e), whereas the probing electron has electric charge −e. Substituting Φ=Nh/(2e) gives
eiδ=e−iNπ=(−1)N.
(6)
An odd N therefore moves a carrier fringe by half its spacing; an even N leaves it unchanged modulo a full spacing. The phase quantization provides an internal check on the superconducting enclosure. It does not mean that zero wave phase proves zero trapped magnetic flux.[ref]
The readout is electron holography in the off-axis holography geometry: a known tilted reference converts an unknown relative wave phase into a spatial carrier displacement. Reconstructing a magnetic wave phase assumes stable reference phase, adequate coherence and a calibrated carrier; electrostatic charging and material traversal can also shift wave phase. Optical wave phase amplification rescales the displayed fringe displacement. The experiment's shielding and comparison tests support the magnetic inference; an interferogram by itself measures the total relative wave phase, rather than separately certifying every contribution. The ideal field-free model describes the limiting geometry, while finite leakage and penetration remain questions of experimental bounds.
Discrete gauge cancellation in two-path interference (WC​=∏(i,j)∈C​Uij​)
Words: 247
Aharonov-Bohm effect OurBigBook.com
A four-vertex model proves the essential gauge invariance without solving an electron scattering problem. Use two directed paths S→U→D and S→L→D. Assign an edge phase aij​ and a complex unit link Uij​=eiaij​ to each directed edge, with Uji​=Uij−1​. These links represent discretized magnetic line integrals multiplied by q/ℏ. Under a gauge transformation with arbitrary vertex phases χi​,
aij′​=aij​+χj​−χi​,P1​=USU​UUD​,P2​=USL​ULD​.
(7)
Both path products acquire the same factor ei(χD​−χS​). Their gauge-invariant ratio is the closed-loop holonomy
WC​=P1​P2∗​=exp(i[aSU​+aUD​−aSL​−aLD​])=eiδ.
(8)
Every intermediate vertex phase cancels. For any fixed nonnegative real amplitudes a,b, the interference quantity ∣aP1​+bP2​∣2 is unchanged too. A gauge-dependent open-path phase consequently cannot be interpreted as an observable by itself.
For an exact example, measure phases in units of π and order edges as (SU,UD,SL,LD). Choose edge phases (0,0,0,1/2) and vertex phases (χS​,χU​,χL​,χD​)=(1/3,−1/4,2/5,1/7)π. The transformed edge phases are
(aSU′​,aUD′​,aSL′​,aLD′​)=(−127​,2811​,151​,7017​)π.
(9)
The upper and lower sums become −4π/21 and 13π/42; their difference remains −π/2. With amplitudes a=1, b=1/2, the intensity remains 5/4. Sweeping one physical loop phase through 2π instead produces extrema 9/4 and 1/4, agreeing with the interferometric visibility example above.

Chern insulator (C=0)

Words: 1k Articles: 4
Topological quantum matter OurBigBook.com
A Chern insulator is a two-dimensional band insulator whose occupied energy bands have a nonzero total Chern number. It has a bulk band gap, yet an interface with a topologically trivial band insulator carries a net excess of quantum Hall edge states propagating in one direction. Time-reversal symmetry must be broken. The Haldane model shows that a nonzero average magnetic flux is unnecessary.
Why distinguish two solids that both have a band gap? The energy eigenvalues tell us whether an excitation costs energy; the energy eigenstates also describe how the state changes around the Brillouin zone. Their Berry curvature can integrate to a nonzero integer. A small deformation changes the Berry curvature continuously, but cannot continuously change an integer. A Chern insulator and a trivial band insulator therefore cannot be connected through a path of isolated occupied energy bands with a common bulk band gap. The distinction survives sufficiently weak local perturbations. It does not make every microscopic quantity or every boundary state immune to every disturbance.
For a single filled energy band, set the Berry connection to Aj​=i⟨u−​∣∂kj​​u−​⟩, and orient the momentum plane by (kx​,ky​). Then
Ω−​=∂kx​​Ay​−∂ky​​Ax​,C=2π1​∫BZ​Ω−​d2k.
(10)
This is the First Chern class evaluated on the Brillouin zone, a torus. Smooth eigenvectors may require multiple patches; their transition phases have integer winding numbers. For charge −e and conventional electric current ji​=σij​Ej​, these conventions give σyx​=Ce2/h and σxy​=−Ce2/h. The measurable magnitude agrees with Chern number and integer Hall conductivity. Reversing the momentum orientation or the sign of the Berry connection reverses the displayed C.

Cold-atom realization of the Haldane model

Haldane model OurBigBook.com
Gregor Jotzu and collaborators realized an artificial Haldane model in 2014. They loaded spin-polarized potassium-40 fermions into a laser-made honeycomb lattice. Piezoelectric mirrors shook the optical lattice along two axes; their relative phase controlled time-reversal symmetry breaking. Optical phase control adjusted the sublattice offset.[ref]
A magnetic field gradient drove Bloch oscillations. Band mapping measured interband transfer to locate valley-gap closures. Opposite-force drift measurements distinguished cancelling from reinforcing Berry curvature contributions; reversing shaking handedness reversed the drift. Interband transfer and harmonic confinement affected this readout, including drift beyond ideal transition lines. This neutral-atom Floquet Hamiltonian realization did not measure electrical sheet conductivity. Magnetic fields were used for preparation and forcing.[ref]
