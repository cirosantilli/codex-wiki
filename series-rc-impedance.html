Series RC impedance (Z=R−i/(ωC))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electrical impedance
OurBigBook.com Words: 386
Consider a stipulated series circuit containing an ideal resistor of electrical resistance R=100Ω and an ideal capacitor of capacitance C=1μF. The same electric current passes through both. Their terminal electric potential differences add by Kirchhoff's voltage law, so
V=VR​+VC​=RI+iωCI​,Z=R−ωCi​.
(35)
A quick derivation uses i=CdvC​/dt: taking the derivative of the complex exponential multiplies the capacitor voltage phasor by iω, hence VC​=I/(iωC). Dimensionally, 1/(ωC) has units s/F=V/A=Ω, matching R.
At frequency f=1000Hz, the angular frequency is ω=2πf. The signed capacitive electrical reactance, modulus and complex argument of the total electrical impedance are
XC​=−159.154943Ω,∣Z∣=187.963549Ω,ϕ=−57.858092∘.
(36)
Apply Vrms​=1.00V across the complete series circuit. Its root mean square electric current is
Irms​=187.963549Ω1.00V​=5.320180mA.
(37)
With voltage as the zero-phase reference, the current phasor has complex argument +57.858092∘: electric current leads. The phasor drops have moduli VR,rms​=0.532018V and VC,rms​=0.846733V; these add as complex numbers, not as positive magnitudes. Their squared magnitudes sum to (1.00V)2.
The mean absorbed power uses the root mean square values:
P=Vrms​Irms​cosϕ=RIrms2​=2.830432mW,cosϕ=∣Z∣R​=0.532018.
(38)
The ideal capacitor has zero cycle-averaged absorbed power; all the mean absorbed power becomes Joule heating in the resistor.[ref] To see why the phase factor appears, average p(t)=v(t)i(t) over a full cycle with v(t)=2​Vrms​cosωt and i(t)=2​Irms​cos(ωt−ϕ). The cosine addition formula reduces the average to Vrms​Irms​cosϕ; the term at 2ω averages to zero. Independently, pC​=d(CvC2​/2)/dt, and the capacitor energy returns to its starting value after each cycle.
At fixed applied Vrms​, the limit ω→0+ gives ∣Z∣→∞, ϕ→−π/2 and Irms​→0: the ideal capacitor blocks steady electric current. The limit ω→∞ gives Z→R, ϕ→0 and Irms​→Vrms​/R. These are limits of the ideal lumped electrical circuit; unwanted lead inductance and component losses can invalidate that model at high frequency. At ωRC=1, the equal legs of the impedance right triangle give ∣Z∣=2​R and ϕ=−π/4 without numerical evaluation. Measuring both the magnitude and the sign of the phase difference distinguishes this capacitor response from an equally large positive electrical reactance.
