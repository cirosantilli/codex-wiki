Injectivity of a module map is detected at maximal localizations (f injective⟺fm​ injective for every maximal m)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Localization of a ring Localization of a module Exactness of localization
OurBigBook.com Words: 100
For a module homomorphism f:M→N over a commutative ring A, f is injective if and only if every fm​ is injective, with m ranging over maximal ideals. In a localization of a module, x/s maps to zero exactly when some denominator t satisfies tf(x)=0. If f is injective this gives tx=0, so the original fraction is zero. In general it identifies ker(fm​) with (kerf)m​. A nonzero element of kerf has a proper annihilator, contained in some maximal ideal; it remains nonzero in that localization. Thus vanishing of all localized kernels forces kerf=0. No finiteness or Noetherian assumption is required.
