Graphic matroid

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Matroid
OurBigBook.com Words: 178
For a finite undirected graph, the graphic matroid has its edges as the ground set. A subset is a matroid independent set when its edges form a forest on the original vertices, including isolated vertices. Its matroid bases give a spanning tree in each connected component of a graph; for a connected graph, each matroid basis is a spanning tree.
For a graph triangle on vertices P,Q,R, label the edges a=PQ, b=QR, and c=PR, with weights 8,5,6. The empty set, all singletons, and all pairs are forests; the triple contains the graph cycle P,Q,R,P. Thus its eight subsets have exactly the independence pattern checked in the weighted three-vector matroid, and the same fifteen exchange comparisons pass. The matroid greedy algorithm chooses PQ, chooses PR, and rejects QR, producing the heaviest spanning tree, of weight 14.
Exchange outline. Deleting edges cannot create a graph cycle. If forests I,J have ∣I∣<∣J∣, some edge of J joins different connected components of a graph of I: otherwise, within each such component, J would have at most its number of vertices minus one edges, giving ∣J∣≤∣I∣. Adding that edge keeps a forest. This is the matroid independence axioms expressed through connectivity.
