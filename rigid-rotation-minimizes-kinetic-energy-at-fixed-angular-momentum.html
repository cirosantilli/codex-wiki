Rigid rotation minimizes kinetic energy at fixed angular momentum (K≥L2/(2I))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Kinetic energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
For rotational motion about a fixed axis, let r be cylindrical radius, v the signed tangential velocity, and dm the mass measure. Define the kinetic energy, axial angular momentum, and moment of inertia by
K=21​∫v2dm,L=∫rvdm,I=∫r2dm>0.
(12)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives L2≤(∫v2dm)(∫r2dm)=2KI. More precisely, direct expansion gives the nonnegative energy gap
K−2IL2​=21​∫(v−IL​r)2dm.
(13)
Thus the minimum at fixed L and fixed mass distribution occurs exactly when v=(L/I)r almost everywhere with respect to dm. This means constant angular velocity Ω=L/I, or solid-body rotation. If the profile is continuous, the equality holds at every point in the mass support.
For a uniform unit-density cylinder of radius a, height h, and volume V=πa2h, the moment of inertia is I=Va2/2. Therefore K≥L2/(Va2), with equality for ω(r)=2L/(Va2). Multiplying by a uniform nonunit density changes the mass measure and I consistently.
