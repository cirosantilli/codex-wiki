Solubility product and material balance

OurBigBook.com Words: 401
A solubility product constrains a product of thermodynamic activities; the available inventories determine which combinations of concentrations are possible. Their intersection determines the molar solubility. Consider an original, abstract AB solid, with the preceding one-to-one dissolution chemical equation. Stipulate c∘=1M, γA​=γB​=1 and Ksp,c​=10−4M2 at fixed temperature and pressure. Thus Ksp​=10−4 is dimensionless. These are chosen model parameters, not data for an identified material.
Initially, a completely dissociated soluble XB salt supplies B anions at molar concentration c0​≥0 and X cations at the same molar concentration; A is absent. All three ions have unit electric charge magnitude. X remains a spectator ion, and only AB can form a solid. Assume constant volume, no additional chemical reactions, and negligible contributions from self-ionization of water. Dissolving AB raises both reacting-ion concentrations by the same s:
cA​=s,cB​=c0​+s,cX​=c0​.
(7)
Charge neutrality is satisfied: cA​+cX​−cB​=s+c0​−(s+c0​)=0. Adding B alone without its accompanying cation would omit the initial electric charge balance.
In the plane of the two reacting-ion concentrations, this inventory constraint is the line cB​=cA​+c0​. Saturation supplies the hyperbola cB​=Ksp,c​/cA​ for cA​>0. Their positive intersection solves a quadratic equation:
s(s+c0​)=Ksp,c​,s∗​=2c02​+4Ksp,c​​−c0​​=c02​+4Ksp,c​​+c0​2Ksp,c​​.
(8)
The quadratic formula supplies two roots of a polynomial; only the positive one is a physical concentration. Uniqueness also follows because s(s+c0​) strictly increases for s≥0, starting at zero and growing without bound. The second expression, obtained by multiplying by the conjugate, avoids subtraction of nearly equal numbers at large c0​.
For pure water, c0​=0 gives s∗​=Ksp,c​​=0.010M. For c0​=0.030M,
s∗​≈0.003027756M,cB​≈0.033027756M.
(9)
Their product is 10−4M2, although neither concentration equals the pure-water value. This is the useful geometrical distinction: an inventory line and an equilibrium constant specify different constraints.
Check the solid inventory before imposing saturation. If the initially added AB has amount of substance n0​ in volume V, the saturated branch requires n0​>Vs∗​ for excess solid to remain. For example, choose n0​=0.0020mol and V=0.100L. In pure water, 0.0010mol dissolves and the same amount of substance remains as solid. At c0​=0.030M, approximately 0.0003027756mol dissolves, leaving 0.0016972244mol as solid. In this model, an insufficient supply instead gives
s=min{n0​/V,s∗​}.
(10)
If n0​/V<s∗​, all AB dissolves and the ion product is below the solubility product. At n0​/V=s∗​, the last solid disappears exactly at saturation.
