Take (xj​,yj​,zj​) as fractional coordinates in the chosen unit cell. In the kinematic approximation the crystal basis structure factor is
F(hkl)=j∑​fj​(Q)exp[2πi(hxj​+kyj​+lzj​)]​,Q=λ4πsinθ​.
(1)
Equivalently, in Cartesian coordinates the phase is Ghkl​⋅rj​. Reversing every phase sign complex-conjugates the nonabsorbing amplitude and leaves its intensity unchanged. The atomic scattering factor is the Fourier transform of the atom's electron density, in electron scattering units:
fj​(Q)=∫ρe,j​(r)eiQ⋅rd3r.
(2)
At zero scattering angle the normal form factor counts electrons: fj​(0)=Zj​ for a neutral atom, modified by the ionic charge for an ion. At increasing Q, different parts of the electron cloud scatter with different phases, reducing the coherent amplitude. Typical nonresonant atomic curves fall from their forward value toward small values at large angle. A localized cloud falls more slowly in reciprocal space. Anomalous scattering near an absorption edge adds energy-dependent complex terms and is excluded from this simple sketch.
For a fixed reflection and fixed instrumental and geometric corrections,
Ihkl​∝∣F(hkl)∣2.​
(3)
The full amplitude also contains the lattice sum. At its reciprocal-lattice peak, Nc​ coherently illuminated identical cells give a peak amplitude Nc​F; peak height and integrated peak intensity have different size dependences because the peak narrows with crystal size. Powder multiplicity, polarization, absorption, thermal motion and measurement geometry also affect observed intensities. The basis factor determines relative interference, not every experimental prefactor.

The two sites give phases one and exp[iπ(h+k+l)]=(−1)h+k+l. Thus
FCsCl​=fCs+​+(−1)h+k+lfCl−​,Ihkl​=Khkl​​fCs+​+(−1)h+k+lfCl−​​2.​
(4)
For real nonresonant form factors, even h+k+l gives the square of the sum and odd h+k+l the square of the difference. The factor Khkl​ represents the remaining experimental corrections, not another basis-interference term. Since the two ions differ, a half-body-diagonal translation does not preserve the decorated crystal. Its Bravais lattice is therefore simple cubic, with a two-ion basis; CsCl is not a monatomic body-centered cubic Bravais lattice.
Replacing both sites by identical Fe atoms changes the amplitude to
FFe​=fFe​[1+(−1)h+k+l],IhklFe​={4Khkl​∣fFe​∣2,0,​h+k+l even,h+k+l odd.​​
(5)
Now the body-centering translation does preserve the structure. It is a body-centered cubic lattice with one Fe atom per primitive cell, or two per conventional cubic cell.
For 111 the parity is odd: CsCl generally has a nonzero amplitude fCs+​−fCl−​, whereas the identical-Fe 111 reflection is systematically absent. A numerical absolute CsCl intensity requires the angle-dependent form factors and experimental normalization; no such values are supplied. Forward electron counts, 54 for Cs+ and 18 for Cl-, explain why their amplitudes are substantially unequal, but are not substituted for the actual finite-angle factors without an additional approximation.

a OurBigBook.com
In the free electron model, conduction electrons occupy independent plane-wave states with energy εk​=ℏ2k2/(2m) in a uniform mean background. Crystal-periodic band structure and explicit electron–electron correlations are omitted. Electrons still obey the Pauli exclusion principle and Fermi–Dirac statistics; free does not mean distinguishable classical particles. A scattering time can be added phenomenologically to describe transport.
The Fermi energy is the highest occupied single-particle energy at zero temperature, measured here from the bottom of the parabolic band. The Fermi temperature is the corresponding scale TF​=EF​/kB​, not the actual thermal temperature of the sample. Metallic electron densities are large, so filling all distinct states up to the required momentum gives Fermi energies of several electronvolts, equivalent to tens of thousands of kelvin or more. At ordinary temperatures, only a narrow region around the Fermi surface has appreciably changing occupations.
A room-temperature metal and its lattice are nevertheless at room temperature, not at TF​. High occupied kinetic energies are present even in the zero-temperature ground state and cannot all be liberated as heat without violating the allowed occupancy structure. A metal below skin temperature often feels cold because it transfers heat rapidly out of the skin; its thermal conductivity and thermal effusivity matter, not the value of its Fermi temperature. At the same actual temperature, a poorly conducting material can therefore feel warmer while neither material is thermodynamically hotter.

N=2(2πL​)dvd​kFd​,kFd​=2vd​(2π)d​n.
(13)
Since EF​=ℏ2kF2​/(2m),
ad​=2vd​(2π)d​,a1​=2π​,a2​=2π,a3​=3π2.​
(14)
The density n means number per length, area or volume as appropriate; it is not assigned three-dimensional units in every case.
For the same spin-summed states, the cumulative number per d-dimensional volume below energy E is n(E)=2vd​(2mE/ℏ2)d/2/(2π)d. Differentiating yields g(E)=(d/2)n(E)/E. Evaluating at the filling energy,
g(EF​)=bd​EF​n​,b1​=21​,b2​=1,b3​=23​.​
(15)
In one, two and three dimensions the density of states varies respectively as E−1/2, a constant and E1/2. The normalization counts both signs of momentum and both spin states exactly once. A single-spin density of states or extra valley degeneracy would require a different counting convention, not an extra unexplained factor in these constants.

Write the two allowed moment projections as ±μ, where μ=gμB​/2 for spin one-half. Their Zeeman energies in the prescribed flux density are ∓μB. The single-particle partition function and the mean moment along the field are
z=eβμB+e−βμB=2coshx,⟨μz​⟩=μex+e−xex−e−x​=μtanhx,x=kB​TμB​.
(23)
For noninteracting moments, magnetization is moment per volume:
M=Ms​tanh(ηB/T),Ms​=VNμ​,η=kB​μ​.​
(24)
For electron-like spin-only moments g≃2, these become Ms​=NμB​/V and η=μB​/kB​.
Differentiate with respect to the specified B to obtain
χB​=(∂B∂M​)T​=VkB​TNμ2​sech2x.​
(25)
This B derivative is not a dimensionless SI volume susceptibility. In the weak-feedback approximation B≃μ0​H, the usual dimensionless differential susceptibility is χdiff​≃μ0​χB​. The small-field limit gives Curie's law χ0​=μ0​Nμ2/(VkB​T). At finite bias a secant response M/H≃μ0​M/B=χ0​tanh(x)/x differs from the differential slope χ0​sech2x. For an internal B=μ0​(H+M) retained self-consistently, the exact local conversion is χH​=μ0​χB​/(1−μ0​χB​); demagnetizing geometry can further affect an applied-field measurement. Stating the field convention avoids a hidden factor of μ0​ or confusion between a slope and a ratio.

A 3d9 shell has one hole in the otherwise filled d shell. Hund's rules give S=1/2, L=2, and, for a more-than-half-filled shell, J=L+S=5/2: the free-ion term is 2D5/2​. Its Landé factor is
gJ​=1+2J(J+1)J(J+1)+S(S+1)−L(L+1)​=56​.
(26)
Distinguish the Curie effective moment from a saturated projection:
μefffree​=gJ​μB​J(J+1)​=3.55μB​,μsatfree​=gJ​JμB​=3μB​.​
(27)
The question then explicitly asks us to use orbital quenching in the crystal. Ligand fields split and mix orbital states, suppressing their freely orientable angular momentum. The spin-only approximation has g≃2, S=1/2, so μeff​=3​μB​ but the two field-axis projections are ±μB​. The latter, not 3​μB​, belong in the tanh argument and saturation magnetization.
The sulfate and water environment supplies mainly closed-shell, weakly magnetic surroundings, stabilizes localized Cu2+ sites and separates their moments so that exchange can be weak on the temperature scale considered. Paramagnetism here is an approximation of weakly interacting local spins, not a claim that dilution guarantees no magnetic ordering at any temperature. Elemental copper has a filled 3d band and itinerant conduction electrons rather than one localized Cu2+ spin per atom. Pauli spin response and orbital/core contributions then replace a local-spin Curie law; ordinary pure copper has a weak net diamagnetic response.[ref]
There is one Cu2+ per formula unit, hence ns​=NA​/Vm​=5.5148×1027 m−3. The spin-only weak-field SI volume susceptibility is
χ0​(T)=kB​Tμ0​ns​μB2​​=T0.0431708 K​,χ0​(300 K)=1.4390×10−4,χ0​(2 K)=2.1585×10−2.​
(28)
These neglect exchange, anisotropic g factors and background diamagnetism. At high field and low temperature, saturation requires the nonlinear response in a; a field-independent Curie value then describes only the zero-field slope.

a OurBigBook.com
Inelastic neutron scattering measures both momentum and energy exchanged with the crystal. Comparing incident and outgoing neutron wavevectors gives Q=ki​−kf​, while their energy difference gives a created or annihilated phonon energy ℏω. Crystal momentum conservation identifies the reduced phonon wavevector modulo a reciprocal-lattice vector. Varying scattering geometry maps dispersion curves and polarization-dependent intensities across a substantial part of the Brillouin zone.
Inelastic X-ray scattering provides a complementary energy- and momentum-resolved probe, particularly when small samples or high-pressure environments favor photon beams. Raman and infrared spectroscopy measure optical-frequency resonances subject to different symmetry selection rules. Photon momentum at these frequencies is usually small on the Brillouin-zone scale, so these methods mostly sample near the zone center rather than an arbitrary phonon wavevector. Brillouin scattering and ultrasonic measurements probe low-frequency acoustic motion and infer sound velocities or elastic constants.
Heat-capacity measurements constrain the thermally weighted vibrational density of states and its low-frequency behavior, but do not by themselves reconstruct a unique wavevector-resolved dispersion. Elastic diffraction and temperature-dependent displacement factors also constrain mean-square motion, while energy-resolved inelastic measurements distinguish individual excitations directly. A monatomic scalar chain has only the acoustic branch derived below; it should not be assigned an optical branch merely because Raman spectroscopy exists for more complicated crystals.

The right spring changes length by un+1​−un​ and pulls atom n to the right with force C(un+1​−un​). The left spring contributes −C(un​−un−1​). Adding these restoring forces and applying Newton's second law gives
mu¨n​=C(un+1​−2un​+un−1​).
(32)
Uniform displacement makes both spring extensions vanish, as translational invariance requires. Search for a normal mode un​=u0​ei(kna−ωt). Substitution yields
−mω2=C(eika+e−ika−2)=−4Csin2(ka/2),ω(k)=2C/m​∣sin(ka/2)∣.​
(33)
One representative first Brillouin zone is −π/a≤k≤π/a. This is one positive-frequency acoustic branch: near zero wavevector, ω≃aC/m​∣k∣, so the sound speed is vs​=aC/m​. Opposite signs of k label opposite propagation directions, not a second polarization branch.
The model assumes small displacements, harmonic nearest-neighbor forces, one scalar displacement per atom and uniform equilibrium spacing. It excludes damping, anharmonic expansion and transverse polarizations. In a three-dimensional monatomic crystal there would normally be three acoustic polarizations; that counting cannot be imported into this explicitly one-dimensional scalar model.

For a long periodic chain of length L=Na, neighboring allowed signed wavevectors differ by 2π/L. Counting both signs once gives a state density per length dk/π on the positive half-zone. Therefore the density per angular-frequency interval and per length is
g(ω)=π1​​dωdk​​=πvs​1−(ω/ωm​)2​1​​,0<ω<ωm​.
