The high-pressure atomic basis of a crystal has the caesium chloride structure. Its chemically labelled Bravais lattice is simple cubic lattice: exchanging Na and Cl is not a translation symmetry. Its structure factor is
Fhkl​=fNa​+(−1)h+k+lfCl​.
(7)
Because ionic bonding gives approximately Na+ and Cl−, their bound-electron counts are 10 and 18. In the stipulated electron-count approximation, odd-index-sum reflections have ∣F∣=8, while even-index-sum reflections have ∣F∣=28. In particular, the first ring {100} is weak but present; it must not be discarded using a body-centering extinction rule.
The next ring is {110}, and Bragg's law gives
sin2ψ110​​=2​sin229.7∘​,ψ110​=42.50∘.​
(8)
The powder diffraction multiplicities are six and twelve. Counting all symmetry-equivalent reflections, while neglecting other angular factors, therefore gives
I100​I110​​≃612​(828​)2=24.5.​
(9)
The corresponding single-directed-reflection ratio would be 12.25. Neutral-atom counts would give the wrong contrast because the material is ionic.
For measured integrated photon counts, the geometry must also be specified. More generally the ratio is 2∣F110​/F100​∣2 times the ratio of Lorentz-polarization factors, thermal-motion factors and detection efficiencies. For example, the common unpolarized symmetric-powder convention has
LP(θ)∝sin2θcosθ1+cos2(2θ)​,
(10)
and gives 24.5LP(21.2506∘)/LP(14.85∘)≃11.18 if all other corrections are suppressed. Thus 24.5 is the multiplicity-and-structure-factor estimate, rather than a geometry-independent precision count ratio; actual angle-dependent ionic form factors are also not supplied.
