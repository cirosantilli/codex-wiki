Robust extrinsic dominance requires ND​≫ni​; at the nominal equality, n=1.618ND​ and p=0.618ND​, so intrinsic contributions are already appreciable. For a quantitative tolerance p/n≤η, the minimum ionized dopant density is ni​(1−η)/η​. For example, η=0.1 would require ND​≥2.85ni​ at the upper temperature.
Using the nominal crossover estimate and the complete-ionization approximation at room temperature, n≃ND​ and
p(300K)≃ND​ni​(300K)2​=1.19×1015m−3≃1.19×109cm−3.​

For the twofold neutral-donor degeneracy, a consistency calculation gives about 91.3% ionization at 300K, raising this minority density to 1.30×1015m−3, about 9.6% above the complete-ionization estimate. Stronger extrinsic dominance uses more donors and correspondingly fewer electron holes. Actual germanium has temperature-dependent band parameters and multiple valleys; the numerical estimate consistently uses the fixed effective data supplied rather than adding an unsupplied multiplicity.
