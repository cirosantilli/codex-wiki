A Wigner-Seitz cell is the region of real space at least as close to a chosen Bravais lattice point as to any other. It is bounded by perpendicular bisectors of vectors to neighboring lattice points and is a primitive unit cell.
A reciprocal lattice vector G satisfies eiG⋅R=1 for every lattice translation R. With primitive vectors ai​, reciprocal primitive vectors satisfy ai​⋅bj​=2πδij​, and G=∑j​nj​bj​ for integers nj​.
The first Brillouin zone is the Wigner-Seitz cell of that reciprocal lattice, centered at k=0. Its boundaries are Bragg planes, k⋅G=G2/2. Higher Brillouin zones are the regions separated from the origin by successively more such planes: an interior point of zone j has crossed exactly j−1 planes. A second Brillouin zone is generally disconnected in this extended-zone representation; it is not just a translated first-zone rectangle.
