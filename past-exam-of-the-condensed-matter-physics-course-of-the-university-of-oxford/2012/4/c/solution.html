For the first two Brillouin zones of a rectangular lattice, write K=π/a and L=π/b, so 0<K<L and the reciprocal lattice vectors are (2nK,2mL). The perpendicular bisectors to the four nearest axial reciprocal points enclose
B1​={(kx​,ky​):∣kx​∣<K, ∣ky​∣<L}.​
(35)
The second Brillouin zone consists of points beyond exactly one Bragg plane. In the first quadrant, it is the union of the following two regions:
K<kx​<2K,0<kx​<K,​0<ky​<LK2+L2−Kkx​​,L<ky​<LK2+L2−Kkx​​.​
(36)
Reflect these regions in both axes. The first is a trapezoidal side lobe; the second is half of a triangular top lobe. The sloping boundary is the bisector for G=(2K,2L). On the side lobe, kx​<2K is the bisector condition for G=(4K,0): crossing that plane too would enter the third or a higher zone. These additional bisectors are why four rectangles pasted onto the first zone would be incorrect.
Equivalently, the full right lobe has vertices
(K,−L),(2K,−L+K2/L),(2K,L−K2/L),(K,L),
(37)
and the top lobe has vertices (−K,L),(K,L),(0,L+K2/L). Add their left and bottom reflections. Their total area is
2(2KL−LK3​)+2LK3​=4KL,
(38)
equal to the first-zone area, as required by reciprocal-space state counting. These formulas remain valid for every a>b, including very elongated rectangles; boundaries themselves can be shared by convention.
