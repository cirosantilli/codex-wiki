For one Cartesian displacement, the harmonic potential is mAr​ωE2​x2/2. At T≫TE​, the equipartition theorem assigns kB​T/2 to this quadratic potential term. Hence
⟨x2⟩=mAr​ωE2​kB​T​=mAr​kB​TE2​ℏ2T​.​
(27)
This is a one-coordinate mean squared displacement, not the total three-dimensional displacement variance, which is three times larger in the isotropic model.
For comparison, the exact Einstein mean-square displacement is
⟨x2⟩=2mAr​ωE​ℏ​coth(2TTE​​).
(28)
Using cothy≃1/y recovers the requested high-temperature result. Its nonzero zero-temperature limit is the displacement contribution of the zero-point energy.
