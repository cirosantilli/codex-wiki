With equal constant electron mobility and hole mobility M, the sheet electrical conductivity is σ=eM(n+p). In the intrinsic regime, n+p≃2ni​∝Te−Eg​/(2kB​T), so
logσ=constant+logT−2kB​TEg​​.
(43)
It decreases steeply with increasing 1/T on the high-temperature side. There is a small logarithmic curvature rather than a perfectly straight activation line.
In the fully ionized extrinsic regime, charge neutrality and the semiconductor mass-action law give n−p=nd​, np=ni2​, hence
n+p=nd2​+4ni2​​,σ≃eMnd​(ni​≪nd​).
(44)
This gives a nearly horizontal region passing through σ(200K)=0.01Ω−1. At lower temperatures semiconductor carrier freeze-out decreases the ionized donor density and bends the curve downward again.
To locate that bend realistically, include donor charge-state degeneracy. For a neutral donor with two spin states and an empty donor with one state, the ionized density is
nd+​=1+2(n/Nc​)eEB​/(kB​T)nd​​.
(45)
Solve n−ni2​/n=nd+​ for the mobile density. In the asymptotic freeze-out regime, where p is negligible and n≪nd​,
n≃2nd​Nc​​​e−EB​/(2kB​T),logσ≃constant+21​logT−2kB​TEB​​.
(46)
Its activation scale EB​/2≃3.14meV is much smaller than the intrinsic scale Eg​/2=180meV.
Within the specified interval the low-temperature downturn is modest: Nc​(30K)≃2.81×1010cm−2, and the partial-ionization equation gives n(30K)≃0.65nd​. The sample is not yet deep in the asymptotic freeze-out limit at 30 K. Calibrating the mobility to the stated 200 K value gives representative values
σ(30K)≃0.0066,σ(200K)=0.010,σ(300K)≃0.024,σ(600K)≃1.39in Ω−1.​
(47)
The donor statistical weights were not explicitly supplied, so they are a stated assumption for these illustrative curve details; a one-state donor approximation changes the low-temperature rounding but not the three physical regimes or their activation scales.
