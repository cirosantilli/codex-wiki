Add the electronic diamagnetism of each helium atom to the nuclear magnetic susceptibility, using the same n3​ for both contributions in the helium-3 sample:
χ3,tot​≃μ0​n3​[kB​T(2.13μN​)2​−3me​e2⟨r2⟩​].
(53)
The density cancels when the two terms balance. The sign-change temperature in the independent-atom approximation is therefore
T0​=e2kB​⟨r2⟩3me​(2.13μN​)2​.​
(54)
No numerical atomic radius was supplied, so the exact numerical value remains conditional on that electronic length scale. Taking ⟨r2⟩∼a02​, where a0​ is the Bohr radius, gives T0​≃0.32K. A slightly more explicit estimate uses the helium variational effective charge ζ=27/16, for which a hydrogenic 1s orbital has ⟨r2⟩=3a02​/ζ2≃1.05a02​, giving
T0​≈0.3Kas an atomic-model estimate.​
(55)
Above this scale the electronic contribution is negative and dominates; below it the positive nuclear contribution dominates within the independent-moment approximation. The estimate is not a precision prediction of a real interacting helium phase.
