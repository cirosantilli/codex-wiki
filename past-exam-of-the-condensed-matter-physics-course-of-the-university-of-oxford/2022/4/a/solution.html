For a fixed open-shell electron configuration in the LS coupling regime, Hund's rules select the free-ion ground term in the following order:
  • Maximize the total spin S, subject to the Pauli exclusion principle.
  • Among terms with that S, maximize the total orbital angular momentum L.
  • For a less-than-half-filled subshell choose the smallest total angular momentum, J=∣L−S∣; for a more-than-half-filled subshell choose the largest, J=L+S. A half-filled subshell has L=0 and J=S.
The first two rules come from the Coulomb interaction between electrons within the partially filled subshell, including its exchange interaction. Parallel spins require an antisymmetric spatial wavefunction, reducing close approach and often lowering the repulsive Coulomb interaction energy. Among the allowed high-spin terms, the angular dependence of the Coulomb interaction generally favours the largest L; a simple magnetic alignment argument alone does not derive this second ordering rule.
The weaker spin-orbit coupling then splits a selected L,S term. In LS coupling, its term-level energy is proportional to
⟨L⋅S⟩=2ℏ2​[J(J+1)−L(L+1)−S(S+1)].
(60)
The effective sign appropriate to less-than-half versus more-than-half filling gives the third rule. These rules assume the electrostatic term separation dominates spin-orbit coupling; strong crystal field splitting or other coupling regimes require further analysis.
