Regard V(x) as an electron potential energy, and take G=2π/a. Each cosine supplies half its coefficient to each of its two Fourier coefficients. The nonzero coefficients are

The Fourier coefficient connecting the two plane waves at k=±π/a is VG​, so the two-wave band gap is
Δ=2π∣V0​∣∣δ∣,ϵ=EZB​π∣V0​∣​∣δ∣.​

At δ=0 the actual periodic potential has period a/2 and no Fourier harmonic at G. The crossing at the artificially halved Brillouin zone boundary then has zero band gap. Higher-order weak-potential corrections can change the proportionality coefficient at nonzero δ; the displayed coefficient belongs to the given two-wave approximation.
There is also a small-displacement qualification in the source: the claimed positions of the potential energy minima are correct to first order in δ, not exactly. Put t=2πx/a. Then
V0​1​dtdV​=−2sin2t+2πδ(sint−3sin3t).
(38)
At δ=0.01 and x/a=1/4−δ=0.24, this equals approximately −2.802×10−3, rather than zero. For V0​>0, expanding about x/a=1/4 and 3/4 gives minima at 1/4−δ+O(δ3) and 3/4+δ+O(δ3). This does not alter the leading Fourier coefficient or the linear opening of the band gap.
