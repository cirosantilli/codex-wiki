Use the same normalization for the elastic energy and electronic energy density: if κδ2 is an energy per unit cell, divide it by a first; if it is already per length, use it directly. Write its coefficient per length as K>0 and put A=π2V02​/(4aEZB​)>0. The small-distortion change in total energy density is
Utot​(δ)−Utot​(0)=δ2[K−A+2Aln4C∣δ∣​]+O(δ4).
(41)
No minimization calculation is needed. For arbitrarily small nonzero ∣δ∣, the logarithm is negative with arbitrarily large magnitude. Hence the negative electronic Peierls energy gain, proportional to δ2ln(1/∣δ∣), eventually exceeds any fixed positive quadratic elastic energy. There are distorted configurations with lower energy than the undistorted configuration for every finite K and nonzero coupling. The ideal zero-temperature infinite-chain model therefore has
∣δeq​∣>0.​
(42)
The source's δeq​>0 is correct when δ denotes a nonnegative distortion amplitude. If δ is signed, the energy density is even and the two signs describe equally stable patterns related by a lattice translation. For example, V(x+a/2;δ)=V(x;−δ), so a negative equilibrium distortion has the same energy as the positive one. The zero-coupling case V0​=0 instead has its minimum at δ=0. The argument assumes a coupled chain, complete lower-energy band filling and zero temperature; it does not by itself establish finite-temperature long-range order in an isolated one-dimensional system.
