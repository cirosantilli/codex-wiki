ii OurBigBook.com
Without the Peierls distortion, adjacent chemical atoms are separated by a/2. The primitive unit cell contains one chemical atom and contributes one electron. A energy band with two spin states accommodates two electrons per primitive unit cell, so the first energy band is half filled. Its true first Brillouin zone extends to ±2π/a, while the Fermi points are at ±π/a.
Describing the same chain with a doubled unit cell of length a merely folds the half-filled energy band into two branches that touch at those Fermi points. The unbroken lattice translation by a/2 prevents an artificial band gap there. A Peierls distortion breaks that smaller lattice translation and supplies the missing Fourier coefficient at 2kF​=G; the lower folded energy band can then be completely filled and separated from the upper energy band. Thus the zero-band gap result at δ=0 agrees with the original one-electron band filling.

Write its coefficient per length as K>0 and put A=π2V02​/(4aEZB​)>0. The small-distortion change in total energy density is

There are three regimes. At the lowest temperatures, most donor impurities retain their electrons; thermal ionization gives semiconductor carrier freeze-out. The result from part (b) is

For a fixed open-shell electron configuration in the LS coupling regime, Hund's rules select the free-ion ground term in the following order:
  • Maximize the total spin S, subject to the Pauli exclusion principle.
  • Among terms with that S, maximize the total orbital angular momentum L.
  • For a less-than-half-filled subshell choose the smallest total angular momentum, J=∣L−S∣; for a more-than-half-filled subshell choose the largest, J=L+S. A half-filled subshell has L=0 and J=S.
The first two rules come from the Coulomb interaction between electrons within the partially filled subshell, including its exchange interaction. Parallel spins require an antisymmetric spatial wavefunction, reducing close approach and often lowering the repulsive Coulomb interaction energy. Among the allowed high-spin terms, the angular dependence of the Coulomb interaction generally favours the largest L; a simple magnetic alignment argument alone does not derive this second ordering rule.
The weaker spin-orbit coupling then splits a selected L,S term. In LS coupling, its term-level energy is proportional to
⟨L⋅S⟩=2ℏ2​[J(J+1)−L(L+1)−S(S+1)].
(60)
The effective sign appropriate to less-than-half versus more-than-half filling gives the third rule. These rules assume the electrostatic term separation dominates spin-orbit coupling; strong crystal field splitting or other coupling regimes require further analysis.

The Weiss temperature Θ is the extrapolated zero of 1/χ in a Curie–Weiss law fit. It summarizes the leading high-temperature effect of interactions between magnetic moments. For the sign convention
Hex​=−∑i<j​Jij​Ji​⋅Jj​,
(65)
where Ji​ are dimensionless angular momentum operators, mean-field theory gives
Θ=3kB​J(J+1)​∑j​Jij​.
(66)
Thus positive net exchange interaction in this convention favours parallel magnetic moments, giving Θ>0 and tendencies towards ferromagnetism; negative net exchange interaction favours antiparallel magnetic moments, giving Θ<0 and antiferromagnetic tendencies. Competing interactions can partially cancel in this sum. The Weiss temperature need not equal the actual ordering temperature, and its sign alone is not a complete determination of the low-temperature ordered structure.
