At low temperature the electronic and lattice heat-capacity crossover in a monovalent metal is found from
Cph​Ce​​=24π2TF​T25θD3​​=(TT∗​​)2,T∗​=(24π2TF​5θD3​​)1/2=3.38 K​.

Thus the electronic term dominates for 0<T≲3.4 K within the normal-metal model. Both low-temperature approximations are well controlled at that crossover. At exactly zero temperature both heat capacities vanish; below sufficiently small finite-size energy scales a continuum model also needs revision.
Room temperature is not T≪θD​: using the cubic formula at 300 K would incorrectly predict Cph​≃142nkB​, far above the harmonic high-temperature limit 3nkB​. Instead use the Dulong-Petit law as an estimate:
Cph​Ce​​≃3nkB​(π2/2)nkB​(300/TF​)​=6.0×10−3​.
(13)
Keeping the finite cutoff in the Debye heat capacity gives
Cph​=9nkB​(θD​T​)3∫0θD​/T​(ex−1)2x4ex​dx.
(14)
At 300 K this is 2.8006nkB​, whereas Ce​=0.017994nkB​, giving Ce​/Cph​=6.42×10−3​. Either appropriate room-temperature estimate shows that the lattice contribution dominates. The electronic approximation remains excellent because 300≪TF​.
