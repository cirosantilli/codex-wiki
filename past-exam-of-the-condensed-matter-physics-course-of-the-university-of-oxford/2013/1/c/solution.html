At fixed radiation wavelength, differentiating Bragg's law gives cotθdθ=−da/a. With constant linear thermal expansion coefficient αL​,
aΔa​≃αL​ΔT=0.0018,Δψ=2Δθ≃−2αL​ΔTtanθ.​
(9)
Both scattering angles decrease as the lattice expands. Substitution gives
Δψ111​≃−0.0416∘,Δψ220​≃−0.0702∘.​
(10)
These are shifts of 2θ, not of the Bragg angle alone. As a finite-step check, using a′=a(1+0.0018) gives ψ′=2arcsin[sin(ψ/2)/(1.0018)]. It produces shifts −0.04151∘ and −0.07008∘, consistent with the first-order estimate. A strictly constant differential coefficient would instead give a′/a=eαL​ΔT; its difference from the linear length approximation is second order in this small expansion.
