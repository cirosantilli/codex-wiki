a OurBigBook.com
Determining antiferromagnetic magnetic order is a good reason to use neutron scattering. The neutron has a magnetic dipole moment, so its scattering amplitude includes the spatial arrangement of electronic magnetic moments. Alternating magnetic moments can produce magnetic neutron diffraction peaks even when there is no change of the nuclear crystal structure. Ordinary nonresonant X-ray diffraction primarily measures the electron density, and is much less sensitive to this magnetic order.

dhkl2​1​=a2h2+k2+l2​.

d OurBigBook.com
An applied magnetic field changes the alignment of the manganese magnetic moments. The component of their magnetic structure factor perpendicular to the scattering vector contributes to magnetic neutron diffraction. A field-induced uniform magnetization has the same translational periodicity as the nuclear crystal structure, so its scattering amplitude can occur at the existing reciprocal lattice vectors. The diffraction wave intensities can therefore change without moving the peaks. Their angular positions still measure the same interplanar spacings. This mechanism does not require a measurable change of the lattice constant.

A consistent mechanism is a transition to G-type antiferromagnetism at the Néel temperature. On the manganese primitive cubic lattice, take the magnetic moments to be
mjx​jy​jz​​=m0​(−1)jx​+jy​+jz​.
(7)
Every nearest neighbour has the opposite magnetic moment. The nuclear crystal structure can retain its original lattice constant, while the magnetic order introduces the propagation wavevector
Q=a2π​(21​,21​,21​).
(8)
Summing the scattering amplitude from the magnetic moments over sites gives peaks at q=G+Q, where G is a nuclear reciprocal lattice vector. Thus all three components of the new Miller indices, expressed relative to the original unit cell, are half-integers. The two smallest squared magnitudes in units of (2π/a)2 are
43​for {21​,21​,21​},411​for {23​,21​,21​}.
(9)
The first predicts 2θ≃21.29∘, agreeing with the new ring. The next squared magnitude is 19/4, outside the measured angular range. Taking the observed first magnetic ring as the calibration, Bragg's law predicts
sinθ2​=311​​sin(10.65∘),2θ2​≃41.45∘≃41.4∘.​
(10)
Using the independently fitted lattice constant gives 41.42∘, within the input rounding. One ring alone does not uniquely determine a magnetic order; this proposed nearest-neighbour antiferromagnetic pattern explains both the observed ring and the count of two new rings in the specified interval.

The Bravais lattice is a face-centered cubic lattice. Four inequivalent representatives of its lattice points in the conventional cubic unit cell, in fractional crystal coordinates, are
(0,0,0),(0,21​,21​),(21​,0,21​),(21​,21​,0).
(11)
Boundary copies in the drawing do not constitute additional independent lattice points. Attach the two-atom atomic basis
Al:(0,0,0),P:(41​,41​,41​)​
(12)
to each lattice point. The resulting aluminium phosphide crystal structure is the zinc blende structure. For example, the four phosphorus positions reduced into the conventional unit cell are (1/4,1/4,1/4), (1/4,3/4,3/4), (3/4,1/4,3/4), and (3/4,3/4,1/4), reproducing the heights in the source drawing. There are four aluminium and four phosphorus chemical atoms per conventional unit cell, but only two chemical atoms per primitive unit cell.

There are two chemical atoms in the primitive unit cell, hence six displacement coordinates and six phonon branches at each wavevector. Three rigid translations cost no restoring potential energy in the long-wavelength limit. Consequently
3 acoustic phonon branches.​
(18)
For propagation along [100], one acoustic phonon has longitudinal polarization along x and two have transverse polarization along y,z. The cubic crystal symmetry makes those two transverse directions equivalent. In terms of the elastic stiffness tensor and the mass density,
vL​=ρm​C11​​​,vT,y​=vT,z​=ρm​C44​​​.
(19)
Thus there are two distinct acoustic velocities in general, one longitudinal and a doubly degenerate transverse value. This degeneracy of normal modes concerns the two transverse polarizations; it does not imply equality with the longitudinal speed of sound.

Subtracting the three acoustic phonon branches from the six displacement normal modes leaves
3 optical phonon branches.​
(20)
At small wavevector, these involve relative motion of the two atomic basis sites rather than a rigid translation of the primitive unit cell. Use the long-wavelength linear dispersion relation ω≃vL​∣k∣ as an estimate. The phonon energy is then
E=ℏω≃ℏvL​a2π​=0.5431×10−9mh(8433ms−1)​,E≃0.0642eV.​

In the diamond cubic structure of silicon, the longitudinal acoustic phonon branch and longitudinal optical phonon branch meet at X=(2π/a,0,0). The two atomic basis sites are identical, and the associated crystal symmetry protects their boundary degeneracy of normal modes. In aluminium phosphide, the two sites contain different chemical atoms. The corresponding zinc blende structure lacks the site-exchange symmetry, and these two longitudinal branches generally split at X.
A simple way to see the splitting is a diatomic-chain dispersion relation with equal springs, representing successive [100] atomic planes by alternating masses M1​,M2​ at spacing d=a/4. With effective spring constant K, its angular frequencies obey
ω±2​=K(M1​1​+M2​1​)±K(M1​1​+M2​1​)2−M1​M2​4​sin2(kd)​.
(22)
At X, kd=π/2, so the inter-sublattice coupling cancels and the two squared angular frequencies are 2K/M1​ and 2K/M2​. Equal masses give a contact; unequal masses give
ωacoustic​(X)≃Mheavy​2K​​,ωoptical​(X)≃Mlight​2K​​.​
(23)
In aluminium phosphide, the heavier phosphorus plane moves in the lower-frequency edge mode, and the lighter aluminium plane in the higher-frequency mode in this model. Their similar atomic masses explain why the splitting is relatively small. Different electronic environments can also change the force constant matrix. The chain calculation illustrates the mechanism; a quantitative three-dimensional phonon spectrum requires its full dynamical matrix. It does not assert that all transverse acoustic and optical branches meet in silicon.

In the harmonic approximation, each in-plane displacement coordinate contributes one quadratic kinetic energy term and one quadratic restoring potential energy term. The equipartition theorem assigns kB​T/2 to each. There are two such coordinates per chemical atom, hence
U≃2Na​kB​T,Na​Cv​​⟶2kB​.​
(24)
Here the fixed-size heat capacity is at constant area in this two-dimensional model. On cooling, phonon excitation freezes out and the heat capacity decreases. For each nonzero angular frequency, the quantum harmonic oscillator contributes
cω​=kB​(ex−1)2x2ex​,x=kB​Tℏω​,
(25)
which is below kB​ at finite x and approaches zero as T→0. Two linearly dispersing in-plane acoustic phonon branches give the familiar two-dimensional Debye model scaling Cv​∝T2 at sufficiently low temperature. Out-of-plane modes are excluded by the specified model.

b OurBigBook.com
For a nondegenerate two-dimensional electron gas, the two quadratic momentum terms supply kB​T/2 each by the equipartition theorem. There is no lattice restoring potential energy for a free electron. Thus
U=Ne​kB​T,Ne​Cv​​⟶kB​.​
(26)
On cooling below the Fermi temperature, the Pauli exclusion principle restricts the thermally active electrons to within approximately kB​T of the Fermi energy. The linear low-temperature heat capacity of a two-dimensional Fermi gas is
Ne​Cv​​≃3π2​kB​EF​kB​T​⟶0.​
(27)
The heat capacity therefore decreases from its classical value as quantum degeneracy becomes important. The particle number of electrons and sample area are held fixed when differentiating the internal energy.

Let the sample area be A, set the band touching energy to zero, and write m∗=me∗​=mh∗​. Assume one electronic valley in each energy band with two spin states. The near-edge parabolic dispersions are
Ec​(k)=2m∗ℏ2k2​,Ev​(k)=−2m∗ℏ2k2​.
(28)
Under periodic boundary conditions, each spin occupies reciprocal-space area (2π)2/A. Counting the quantum states inside a circle gives
Nc​(E)=2(2π)2A​πk2=πℏ2Am∗​E(E>0).
(29)
Its derivative is the constant two-dimensional free-electron density of states. Defining D=Am∗/(πℏ2), the signed-energy density of states is
gc​(E)=DΘ(E),gv​(E)=DΘ(−E).​
(30)
The total electronic density of states is therefore D on either side of zero, not 2D at every signed energy. If instead one counts positive-energy excitations, the electron and hole species each have density of states D, giving 2D when summed. A common additional valley degeneracy multiplies all these quantities by that factor. The effective mass approximation assumes thermally sampled energies remain within the near-edge regions of both energy bands.

Equal effective masses and equal spin and valley degeneracies give electron-hole symmetry. In the intrinsic semiconductor, charge neutrality requires equal numbers of conduction band electrons and valence band holes. It therefore pins the chemical potential to the band touching point, μ=0. The Fermi-Dirac distribution gives
Nc​=D∫0∞​eE/(kB​T)+1dE​=DkB​Tln2,Nc​(T)=πℏ2Am∗kB​T​ln2.​
(31)
Measured relative to the filled valence band at zero temperature, the thermal internal energy includes both species. Removing a valence band electron at −E costs the positive hole energy E, just as adding a conduction band electron at E does. Hence
ΔU=2D∫0∞​eE/(kB​T)+1EdE​=6π2​D(kB​T)2.
(32)
Differentiate at fixed area and fixed total electronic particle number, allowing creation of electron-hole pairs:
Cvtot​=3π2​DkB2​T,Nc​Cvtot​​=3ln2π2​kB​≃4.746kB​.​
(33)
There is no conflict with the equipartition theorem. The normalization Nc​ counts only the excited conduction band electrons, while the heat capacity includes both electrons and holes; moreover, Nc​∝T is not fixed. In fact ΔU/Nc​=π2kB​T/(6ln2), and differentiating Nc​(T) supplies another equal contribution to the heat capacity. Finally, μ=0 gives occupation 1/2 at the touching point, so the excitation gas is not in the classical dilute limit even though kB​T exceeds the vanishing band gap.

Ug​(T)−Ug​(0)=6π2​Dg​(kB​T)2,Cv​=3π2​Dg​kB2​T.

a OurBigBook.com
Undoped gallium arsenide is expected to be diamagnetic at zero temperature. Its valence band is filled and its conduction band empty, so there are no mobile conduction band electrons to give Pauli paramagnetism. The specified absence of localized magnetic moments removes a Curie law response and the usual mechanism for ferromagnetic ordering. The remaining induced orbital response is diamagnetism. This classification uses the ordinary weak-field band insulator picture; the band gap suppresses thermally generated carriers as T→0.

The number of formula units per volume follows from the mass density:
nf​=(69.7+74.9)(1.66053906892×10−27kg)5.9×103kgm−3​=2.4572×1028m−3.
(36)
Each neutral formula unit contains 31+33=64 electrons, so ρe​=64nf​=1.5726×1030m−3. Let R2 be the electron-weighted mean-square orbital radius, averaged over all these electrons. The supplied Larmor-Langevin susceptibility then gives the dimensionless SI volume magnetic susceptibility
χ0​=−6me​μ0​ρe​e2​R2,χ0​=−2.5990×10−5(a0​R​)2,​
(37)
where a0​=0.0529177nm is the Bohr radius. An explicit rough atomic-scale assumption R≃a0​ gives χ0​≃−2.6×10−5.
The quoted bulk data do not determine R: a nuclear charge does not specify an orbital radius in a screened many-electron chemical atom. Consequently the numerical estimate just made has atomic-model uncertainty; it is not a deduction with guaranteed 30 percent accuracy. For example, the alternative assumed R=2a0​ gives four times the magnitude while leaving every supplied bulk number unchanged. Atomic wavefunctions, an explicit screened-orbital model, or measured atomic magnetic susceptibilities of materials exhibiting diamagnetism would be needed to support that accuracy. Keeping R explicit makes the later comparison independent of an unadvertised radius assumption. The bare electron mass belongs in this atomic formula; the conduction band effective mass is relevant instead to the large donor envelope in part (c).

A hydrogenic donor state has an exponentially decaying envelope ψ(r)∝e−r/ab​. Neighbouring donors at typical separation rd​∼n−1/3 therefore have exponentially small orbital overlap. The corresponding hopping integral is of order Eb​e−rd​/ab​, apart from algebraic factors and factors from the number of neighbours. Hybridizing many such localized states produces an impurity band with a bandwidth of the same scale:
W∼Eb​exp(−αab​n−1/3​).​
(44)
The order-one constant α absorbs details of the donor spacing and envelope. The exponential dependence expresses quantum tunnelling between weakly overlapping donor orbitals, rather than thermal excitation over the donor binding energy.

χimp​≃Px3ex​,P=2Eb​ab3​μ0​g2μB2​​=1.179×10−8.

W=Eb​e−16.02≃6.04×10−10eV,T≪W/kB​≃7.0×10−6K.​
