Take uncompensated donor impurities and initially approximate them as ionized. Charge neutrality then gives n−p=Nd​, while the semiconductor mass-action law gives np=ni2​. Solving the quadratic without prematurely discarding minority carriers,
n=2Nd​+Nd2​+4ni2​​​,p=Nd​+Nd2​+4ni2​​2ni2​​.
(44)
Since Nd​≫ni​, the hole density is
p≃Nd​ni2​​=4.5816×1010m−3,n≃1.0000×1020m−3.​
(45)
Use the conduction population to locate the chemical potential:
Ec​−μ=kB​TlnnNc​​=0.269180eV,μ−Ev​=0.840820eV.​
(46)
It moves toward the conduction band but remains about 10.4kB​T below it, so the mobile electrons are nondegenerate. Despite the donor binding energy being comparable with kB​T, the large number of available conduction states makes ionization almost complete at this dilute doping. For a singly occupied two-spin donor, Nd+​/Nd​=[1+2e(μ−ED​)/(kB​T)]−1, with ED​=Ec​−0.02584eV. The inferred value is 0.99984, confirming the approximation to much better than one percent. Including this small correction raises the hole density only to about 4.5824×1010m−3.
