The density of states g(E) counts one-electron states per energy interval, including both electron spin orientations. It may be a total or a per-volume quantity; U must have the matching normalization. The Fermi-Dirac distribution
f(E,T)=e(E−μ(T))/(kB​T)+11​
(12)
is the occupation probability of such a state. In the fixed-volume free electron model, the integration variable E and the density of states do not depend on T. The occupation depends explicitly on T and implicitly through the chemical potential μ(T) needed to preserve electron number. Thermal expansion or a changing electronic band structure would require additional effects outside this model.
At T≪TF​, only states within order kB​T of the Fermi energy change their occupation. There are order g(EF​)kB​T such states, each carrying an excitation energy of order kB​T. Thus U(T)−U(0)∝g(EF​)(kB​T)2 and the electronic heat capacity is proportional to g(EF​)kB2​T. Using the supplied coefficient,
CV​=γT,γ=3π2​kB2​g(EF​),U(T)−U(0)=21​γT2.​

The shell argument fixes the scaling, not the numerical coefficient; the controlled Sommerfeld expansion fixes that coefficient at fixed electron number. Filled states far below EF​ cannot accept arbitrary thermal excitations because of the Pauli exclusion principle.
