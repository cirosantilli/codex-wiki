Use periodic boundaries in a box of side L and include two spin states for each wavevector. One orbital state occupies (2π/L)d in wavevector space. Let vd​ be the volume of the unit ball in dimension d, namely 2,π,4π/3. Filling the ball of radius kF​ gives
N=2(2πL​)dvd​kFd​,kFd​=2vd​(2π)d​n.
(13)
Since EF​=ℏ2kF2​/(2m),
ad​=2vd​(2π)d​,a1​=2π​,a2​=2π,a3​=3π2.​
(14)
The density n means number per length, area or volume as appropriate; it is not assigned three-dimensional units in every case.
For the same spin-summed states, the cumulative number per d-dimensional volume below energy E is n(E)=2vd​(2mE/ℏ2)d/2/(2π)d. Differentiating yields g(E)=(d/2)n(E)/E. Evaluating at the filling energy,
g(EF​)=bd​EF​n​,b1​=21​,b2​=1,b3​=23​.​
(15)
In one, two and three dimensions the density of states varies respectively as E−1/2, a constant and E1/2. The normalization counts both signs of momentum and both spin states exactly once. A single-spin density of states or extra valley degeneracy would require a different counting convention, not an extra unexplained factor in these constants.
