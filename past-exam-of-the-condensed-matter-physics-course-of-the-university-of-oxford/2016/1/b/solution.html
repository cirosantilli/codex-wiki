The two sites give phases one and exp[iπ(h+k+l)]=(−1)h+k+l. Thus
FCsCl​=fCs+​+(−1)h+k+lfCl−​,Ihkl​=Khkl​​fCs+​+(−1)h+k+lfCl−​​2.​

For real nonresonant form factors, even h+k+l gives the square of the sum and odd h+k+l the square of the difference. The factor Khkl​ represents the remaining experimental corrections, not another basis-interference term. Since the two ions differ, a half-body-diagonal translation does not preserve the decorated crystal. Its Bravais lattice is therefore simple cubic, with a two-ion basis; CsCl is not a monatomic body-centered cubic Bravais lattice.
Replacing both sites by identical Fe atoms changes the amplitude to
FFe​=fFe​[1+(−1)h+k+l],IhklFe​={4Khkl​∣fFe​∣2,0,​h+k+l even,h+k+l odd.​​
(5)
Now the body-centering translation does preserve the structure. It is a body-centered cubic lattice with one Fe atom per primitive cell, or two per conventional cubic cell.
For 111 the parity is odd: CsCl generally has a nonzero amplitude fCs+​−fCl−​, whereas the identical-Fe 111 reflection is systematically absent. A numerical absolute CsCl intensity requires the angle-dependent form factors and experimental normalization; no such values are supplied. Forward electron counts, 54 for Cs+ and 18 for Cl-, explain why their amplitudes are substantially unequal, but are not substituted for the actual finite-angle factors without an additional approximation.
