A 3d9 shell has one hole in the otherwise filled d shell. Hund's rules give S=1/2, L=2, and, for a more-than-half-filled shell, J=L+S=5/2: the free-ion term is 2D5/2​. Its Landé factor is
gJ​=1+2J(J+1)J(J+1)+S(S+1)−L(L+1)​=56​.
(26)
Distinguish the Curie effective moment from a saturated projection:
μefffree​=gJ​μB​J(J+1)​=3.55μB​,μsatfree​=gJ​JμB​=3μB​.​
(27)
The question then explicitly asks us to use orbital quenching in the crystal. Ligand fields split and mix orbital states, suppressing their freely orientable angular momentum. The spin-only approximation has g≃2, S=1/2, so μeff​=3​μB​ but the two field-axis projections are ±μB​. The latter, not 3​μB​, belong in the tanh argument and saturation magnetization.
The sulfate and water environment supplies mainly closed-shell, weakly magnetic surroundings, stabilizes localized Cu2+ sites and separates their moments so that exchange can be weak on the temperature scale considered. Paramagnetism here is an approximation of weakly interacting local spins, not a claim that dilution guarantees no magnetic ordering at any temperature. Elemental copper has a filled 3d band and itinerant conduction electrons rather than one localized Cu2+ spin per atom. Pauli spin response and orbital/core contributions then replace a local-spin Curie law; ordinary pure copper has a weak net diamagnetic response.[ref]
There is one Cu2+ per formula unit, hence ns​=NA​/Vm​=5.5148×1027 m−3. The spin-only weak-field SI volume susceptibility is
χ0​(T)=kB​Tμ0​ns​μB2​​=T0.0431708 K​,χ0​(300 K)=1.4390×10−4,χ0​(2 K)=2.1585×10−2.​
(28)
These neglect exchange, anisotropic g factors and background diamagnetism. At high field and low temperature, saturation requires the nonlinear response in a; a field-independent Curie value then describes only the zero-field slope.
