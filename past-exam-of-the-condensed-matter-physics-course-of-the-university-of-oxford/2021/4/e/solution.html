Use the stipulated noninteracting impurity band approximation, with one donated electron per donor and two available spin states per donor orbital. The energy band is half filled. Approximating its spin-summed density of states per volume near the Fermi energy by Dv​(EF​)≃2n/W gives the zero-temperature Pauli paramagnetism
χimp​=μ0​(2∣g∣μB​​)2Dv​(EF​)≃2Wμ0​g2μB2​n​.

(46)
A factor of order one depends on the density of states shape; using the conduction band g-factor rather than silently setting g=2 matters here.
To locate the comparison with the magnitude of the undoped Larmor-Langevin susceptibility, put x=n−1/3/ab​>1. Numerically,
χimp​≃Px3ex​,P=2Eb​ab3​μ0​g2μB2​​=1.179×10−8.

x−3lnx=lnP∣χ0​∣​,x≃16.02.

well below ab−3​=9.45×1023m−3. Within this model the positive spin response exceeds the negative background magnitude for lower dopant densities on this dilute branch: the exponential narrowing of W wins over the loss of donors. Indeed dln(ex/x3)/dx=1−3/x>0 for x>3. This is not a claim of ferromagnetism.
The degenerate Pauli paramagnetism calculation requires kB​T≪W, with an unimportant order-one filling factor. At the estimated crossing,
W=Eb​e−16.02≃6.04×10−10eV,T≪W/kB​≃7.0×10−6K.​

The weak-field condition also requires ∣g∣μB​B≪W. For a different assumed atomic R, use ∣χ0​∣=2.599×10−5(R/a0​)2 in the displayed equation for x. An order-of-magnitude background ∣χ0​∣∼10−5 gives the same density order and a crossover scale of order 10−5K. At higher temperature than this extremely narrow bandwidth permits, the n/W enhancement cannot be used; the independent-donor Curie law is the appropriate thermal scale if interactions remain negligible. At very small dopant density, disorder, localization and Coulomb interactions can also invalidate the assumed noninteracting impurity band. The formal divergence as n→0 is therefore a limit of the stated zero-temperature band model, not a prediction for isolated donors at a fixed nonzero temperature.
