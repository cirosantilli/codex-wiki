A Bravais lattice is the set of points
Λ={n1​a1​+n2​a2​+n3​a3​:ni​∈Z},
(1)
where the primitive lattice translation vectors ai​ are linearly independent. Every lattice point has the same translational environment.
An atomic basis is the motif of atoms attached to each point of the Bravais lattice, specified by positions rp​ and atomic species. The full crystal consists of atoms at R+rp​, with R∈Λ. The Bravais lattice describes translations; the atomic basis describes what is repeated. A primitive cell contains one lattice point but can contain several atoms.

In cubic coordinates write G=(2π/a)(h,k,l). The face-centred cubic selection rule allows integer triples only when h,k,l are all even or all odd. One way to derive it is to sum the four conventional-cell lattice positions:
Ffcc​=1+(−1)h+k+(−1)h+l+(−1)k+l.
(13)
This vanishes for mixed parity and equals four otherwise. The two-atom diamond atomic basis adds the factor
Fbasis​=b(G)[1+eiπ(h+k+l)/2].
(14)
For all-odd indices this is nonzero. For all-even indices it vanishes when h+k+l=2(mod4) and survives when the sum is divisible by four: this is diamond-basis diffraction extinction.
Exclude the forward beam 000 and order the remaining families by h2+k2+l2. The first is 111, with squared length three. The candidate 200, with squared length four, is extinguished by the diamond atomic basis. The next allowed family is 220, with squared length eight. Thus

(15)
For fixed incident wavelength, ∣G∣=2ksinθ increases with scattering angle, so these are the two lowest-angle powder rings. The two atoms are counted per primitive cell; a conventional silicon cube contains four lattice points and eight atoms.

The Einstein model of a solid replaces the vibrations of N identical atoms by 3N independent quantum harmonic oscillators with a common frequency ωE​. Each atom has three displacement directions; the restoring force is harmonic, coupling between different atoms is replaced by a fixed local restoring potential, and anharmonicity and frequency dispersion are neglected. The crystal volume and ωE​ are held fixed when calculating the vibrational heat capacity at constant volume.
The one-oscillator partition function is
z=∑n=0∞​e−βℏωE​(n+1/2)=1−e−βℏωE​e−βℏωE​/2​,Z=z3N.
(16)
Consequently, with TE​=ℏωE​/kB​,
U=−∂β∂logZ​=3NℏωE​[21​+eTE​/T−11​].
(17)
Differentiating at fixed volume gives
CV​=3NkB​(TTE​​)2(eTE​/T−1)2eTE​/T​.​
(18)
The zero-point energy does not contribute to this derivative. The result tends to the Dulong-Petit law 3NkB​ at high temperature and vanishes exponentially at low temperature; the latter is a limitation of the single-frequency model for a real acoustic spectrum.

The Boltzmann carrier density of a two-dimensional parabolic band can be derived directly. Use one isotropic parabolic conduction band, E(k)=Ec​+ℏ2k2/(2me∗​), with two spin states per wave vector. In the nondegenerate limit the Fermi-Dirac distribution reduces to its Boltzmann factor. The number per area is therefore
n​=2∫(2π)2d2k​exp[−β(Ec​−μ+2me∗​ℏ2k2​)]=πe−β(Ec​−μ)​∫0∞​ke−βℏ2k2/(2me∗​)dk=πℏ2me∗​kB​T​e−β(Ec​−μ).​
(31)
The integral uses ∫0∞​ke−ak2dk=1/(2a). Thus
C=πℏ2kB​​,a=1,b=1.​
(32)
Equivalently, the two-dimensional density of states is constant, Dc​=me∗​/(πℏ2), and n=Nc​e−β(Ec​−μ) with Nc​=Dc​kB​T. These expressions include twofold spin degeneracy and one valley. With a general total band degeneracy gc​, replace the factor 1/π by gc​/(2π); extra valleys or occupied confinement subbands would change the coefficient.

A material showing paramagnetism has an induced magnetization in the direction of a weak applied field, giving positive magnetic susceptibility, and no spontaneous magnetization once the field is removed in the paramagnetic phase. Alignment of permanent atomic or nuclear moments is one mechanism.
A material showing diamagnetism develops an induced moment opposing the applied field, giving negative magnetic susceptibility. Closed electronic shells supply a familiar orbital contribution; the response need not require permanent moments.
A ferromagnet has a phase with a spontaneous ordered magnetization below its Curie temperature, produced by cooperative alignment of moments. Its magnetic domains can cancel in a macroscopic zero-field sample, so a measured zero net moment alone does not show that the material lacks ferromagnetism.

The helium atom has a closed 1s2 electronic shell with total electronic spin and orbital angular momentum zero. The nucleus of helium-4 also has spin zero. At low temperature there is no Curie response from permanent atomic or nuclear moments; the leading atomic response is diamagnetism.
Let n4​ denote the number of atoms per volume and let ⟨r2⟩ be the mean squared radius of one electron. Use the symmetric magnetic vector potential A=B×r/2. Expanding the electron kinetic Hamiltonian (p+eA)2/(2me​) gives the quadratic field term
ΔEatom​=∑a=12​8me​e2B2​⟨xa2​+ya2​⟩=12me​2e2B2​⟨r2⟩,
(48)
where isotropy gives ⟨x2+y2⟩=2⟨r2⟩/3. The term linear in orbital angular momentum vanishes in the spherical electronic ground state. Thus
M=−n4​∂B∂ΔEatom​​=−3me​n4​e2⟨r2⟩​B.
(49)
For weak susceptibility, B≃μ0​H, so the Langevin diamagnetic susceptibility is
χ4​=−3me​μ0​n4​e2⟨r2⟩​.​
(50)
If the supplied density is a mass density ρ4​, use n4​=ρ4​/(4u), with u the atomic mass unit. The result is approximately temperature independent at fixed density. The factor two for the two electrons is already included; ⟨r2⟩ has not been defined as their summed radius.

Let s=⟨Siz​⟩ be uniform, with dimensionless spin operators S=1/2. The factor 1/2 in the source Hamiltonian removes double counting of pairs. Decoupling Si​⋅Sj​ about a uniform polarization gives the one-site mean-field theory Hamiltonian
HiMF​=−sJ0​Siz​+constant,J0​=∑j​Jij​.
(56)
A body-centred-cubic site has eight nearest neighbors and six next-nearest neighbors, so
kB​J0​​=8(1mK)+6(−0.5mK)=5mK.
(57)
For a spin one half in this effective field, the mean-field self-consistency equation is
s=21​tanh(2kB​TJ0​s​).
(58)
Linearizing at the continuous onset gives s=J0​s/(4kB​T)+O(s3), hence
TCMF​=4kB​J0​​=1.25mK.​
(59)
Equivalently, the general spin-S result is kB​TCMF​=S(S+1)J0​/3. The antiferromagnetic next-nearest-neighbor exchange reduces the uniform ordering scale rather than contributing its absolute magnitude. The negative nuclear g-factor does not change this exchange instability; aligning the spins also aligns their moments with one another.
For these particular competing couplings, the uniform mode is indeed the largest exchange eigenvalue. Writing x=cos(qx​a/2), y=cos(qy​a/2) and z=cos(qz​a/2) gives
kB​mKJ(q)​=8xyz−2(x2+y2+z2)+3≤5,

The expression is at most 8t3−6t2+3, whose maximum on 0≤t≤1 is five. Thus another modulation does not preempt the stated ferromagnetic onset at this level of mean-field theory. Fluctuations can change the actual transition temperature.
