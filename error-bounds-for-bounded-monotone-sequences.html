Error bounds for bounded monotone sequences (∣an​−L∣→0)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence Monotone sequence Bounded monotone sequence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 429 Articles: 1
For an increasing sequence converging to L, its absolute error is en​=L−an​≥0 and is nonincreasing. For a decreasing sequence, the absolute error is en​=an​−L≥0 and is also nonincreasing. This tells us that later terms cannot be worse, but does not supply a computable stopping index for a chosen tolerance.
Suppose an increasing lower approximation an​ and a decreasing upper approximation bn​ enclose the same limit of a sequence L. Their observable gap wn​=bn​−an​ gives
an​≤L≤bn​⟹∣an​−L∣≤wn​,∣bn​−L∣≤wn​,​2an​+bn​​−L​≤2wn​​.
(14)
If wn​→0, the squeeze theorem certifies a common limit of a sequence; the midpoint has a certified absolute error at most half the gap. This is the principle behind the bisection method. In the symmetric example an​=1−1/n, bn​=1+1/n, the gap is 2/n, each endpoint's exact absolute error is 1/n, and the midpoint happens to equal one exactly. The bound is valid even when such symmetry is absent.
A fixed upper bound by itself supplies less. If an​↑L and an​≤M for all n, then L≤M and 0≤L−an​≤M−an​, but the last gap need not vanish. Taking an​=1−1/n and M=2 makes M−an​→1. A shrinking bracket or an explicit remainder formula adds information that qualitative convergence does not provide.
