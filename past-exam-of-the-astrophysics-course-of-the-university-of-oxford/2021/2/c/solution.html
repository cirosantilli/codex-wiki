First retain the exact initial normalization. The scale factor obeys a/ai​=(t/ti​)2/3, so the cosmological redshift is
1+z=(1+zi​)[B(θ−sinθ)−Tti​​]2/3.
(24)
Also ρ=3M/(4πr3) and ρm​=1/(6πGt2). Using A3=GMB2 gives
1+δ=29​(1−cosθ)3[θ−sinθ−T/B]2​.
(25)
These equations exactly accommodate the finite-time initial conditions in b.
Now neglect T and retain leading order in δi​≪1. Since ti​/B=4δi3/2​/[3(1+δi​)], the cosmological redshift relation reduces to
1+z≃(34​)2/3(θ−sinθ)2/3δi​(1+zi​)​.
(26)
Define δ0​=53​δi​(1+zi​). Then
1+z≃35​(34​)2/3(θ−sinθ)2/3δ0​​,δ≃29​(1−cosθ)3(θ−sinθ)2​−1.​
(27)
The second equality is exact for the ideal common-bang growing-mode spherical-collapse model; it is approximate for the paper's finite-time initial Hubble-velocity data.
The factor 3/5 has a physical origin. Linear mass conservation gives δ˙i​=0 when the initial local expansion equals the background Hubble flow. The general linear dust solution is δL​=Cg​(t/ti​)2/3+Cd​(t/ti​)−1. Imposing δL​(ti​)=δi​ and δ˙L​(ti​)=0 yields
δL​(t)=53​δi​(ti​t​)2/3+52​δi​(ti​t​)−1.
(28)
Thus δ0​ is the present extrapolation of the growing component, after neglecting the decaying transient. It is not the exact present value of the full linear solution for finite ti​.
