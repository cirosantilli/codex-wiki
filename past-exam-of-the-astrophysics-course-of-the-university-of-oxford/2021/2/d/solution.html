In the growing-mode spherical-collapse model, use θ−sinθ=θ3/6−θ5/120+⋯ and 1−cosθ=θ2/2−θ4/24+⋯. Their ratio in c gives δ=3θ2/20+O(θ4). Meanwhile t∝θ−sinθ, and the linear growing density contrast scales as t2/3. Matching at small θ therefore fixes
δL​=53​[43​(θ−sinθ)]2/3.​
(29)
This is a linearly extrapolated growing-mode amplitude at any epoch, including epochs where the actual density contrast is nonlinear. It carries the initial-condition qualification in c.
Numerical comparison is

The last column measures the fractional underestimate of the actual density contrast. Linear theory is accurate at δ≪1, but substantially underpredicts it by the time δ approaches unity. At θ=π, the shell reaches spherical-collapse turnaround; 1+δ=9π2/16≃5.55.
At θ=2π, r=0 and the ideal pressureless density diverges at finite time tc​=2πB. Yet the extrapolated linear value remains finite:
δc​=53​(23π​)2/3=1.68647.​
(31)
This is the linear spherical-collapse threshold, not the true density contrast at collapse. Actual nonspherical collisionless systems undergo shell crossing and virialization rather than becoming uniform point singularities. The ideal virial theorem and conservation of energy estimate a final radius rm​/2 if the structural form is preserved.
