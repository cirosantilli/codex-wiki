The stated identical intrinsic specific intensities must refer to comparable intrinsic states. If the blobs fade with their proper ages, equal observer times sample different ages and need that correction. A steady continuous jet has a different boosting exponent, 2+α; the discrete ejecta and the specified specific intensity transformation justify 3+α here.
For optically thin synchrotron radiation from an isotropic electron distribution dN/dE∝E−p, the characteristic emitted frequency scales as BE2 and the total single-particle power as B2E2. Thus jν​∝E−pE2/(dν/dE)∝ν−(p−1)/2; integration over the full single-particle spectrum preserves this exponent. Hence
p=2α+1≃2.50.​
(43)
The test-particle strong nonrelativistic diffusive shock acceleration prediction is p=(r+2)/(r−1)=2 for compression ratio r=4, giving α=0.5. The inferred spectrum is steeper. If interpreted literally within that uncooled formula, p=2.50 would correspond to r≃3, but cooling, escape, and other acceleration conditions can also steepen it.
The observed Galactic cosmic ray differential energy spectrum below the knee is approximately E−2.7, steeper still; its formal synchrotron radiation equivalent would be α≃0.85. Thus p≃2.50 lies between the simplest source prediction and the propagated cosmic ray slope. The comparison is qualitative: most measured cosmic rays are nuclei, whereas the radio slope here probes electrons, and propagation and radiative losses alter the spectra differently.
