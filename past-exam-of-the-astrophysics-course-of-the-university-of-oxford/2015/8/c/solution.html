The projected quasar-to-cloud separation is
R⊥​=10(180×3600π​)DA​=87.6 kpc.
(56)
For the usual transverse-separation estimate, take the cloud to lie at the same line-of-sight proper distance as the quasar. Since the cloud and galaxy are gravitationally bound, the local source-to-cloud path does not acquire the redshift factor 1+z=3 that separates that system from us. Neglect internal relative Doppler shifts and attenuation. The observer in the cloud sees
λcloud​≃200 nm,Fν,cloud​≃4πR⊥2​Lν,em​​=1.15×10−20 Wm−2Hz−1​.
(57)
This uses the quasar's isotropic intrinsic emission at the same rest frequency; anisotropic radiation or obscuration would modify it.
An angular separation only fixes R⊥​. If s is a source-to-cloud displacement toward us, the true local distance is r=R⊥2​+s2​ and the general flux is Lν​/(4πr2). The quoted transverse value is therefore an upper estimate at fixed projected separation. Equality of cosmological redshift and gravitational binding do not by themselves measure the small internal line-of-sight displacement; a peculiar velocity can also mask it.
