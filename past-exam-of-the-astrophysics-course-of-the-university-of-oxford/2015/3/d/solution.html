A power spectrum describes the variance of Fourier amplitudes as a function of wavenumber. In a statistically homogeneous continuum convention,
⟨ΔM​(k)ΔM∗​(k′)⟩=(2π)3δD​(k−k′)PM​(k).
(27)
The paper's ⟨∣ΔM​(k)∣2⟩ is the corresponding finite-volume or normalization-suppressed statement. For statistical isotropy only k=∣k∣ remains. The dimensionless variance per logarithmic interval is k3PM​(k)/(2π2), a different quantity.
The response changes around 2k2∼9H2, or k∼2.1H: the velocity contribution to the relativistic constraint and the effective growth equation change relative importance across the horizon-scale transition. Large-scale ΔM​ is approximately constant for its regular mode, while short-scale density perturbations grow gravitationally. A smooth initial potential spectrum can therefore acquire a different k dependence in the density spectrum. For illustration, a constant matter-era potential gives ΔM​=−(5+k2η2/6)Ψ, so sufficiently small-scale PM​ has four additional powers of k relative to PΨ​, while the very large-scale limits have the same power dependence.
This identifies a dynamical transition, not a universal prediction of the primordial spectrum or an exact spectral break at 2H for arbitrary initial data. The familiar late-time matter-spectrum turnover also retains the history of horizon entry around radiation–matter equality; it is not generically tied to the current Hubble scale. Initial conditions and the intervening expansion history are needed for an actual spectrum.
