Let the mean particle mass be mˉ=μmH​, with nu​ counting total particles so that ρu​=nu​mˉ. The ideal-gas law and the strong-shock pressure give the immediate post-shock temperature
Td​=ρd​kB​pd​mˉ​=16kB​3mˉVs2​​=100kB​3mˉξ2​(ρu​E​)2/5t−6/5​.
(32)
The robust prediction is Td​∝t−6/5. For a transparent numerical convention take μ=1, nu​=106 m−3 and ξ=1. Then
R≃4.63(103 yrt​)2/5 pc,Td​≃7.45×107(103 yrt​)−6/5 K​.
(33)
For an ionized mixture with μ=0.6 under the same total-particle-density convention, these normalizations become 5.13 pc and 5.49×107 K. The paper does not specify composition or whether its quoted density instead counts hydrogen nuclei; that convention must be stated, not hidden in a precise age.
The ambient-temperature limit of an adiabatic blast wave gives a formal matching estimate, Td​∼104 K, gives
tT​∼1.7×106 yr,RT​∼90 pc(μ=1, ξ=1)​.
(34)
With μ=0.6 the age is about 1.3×106 yr and the radius stays about 90 pc. This matching criterion already lies outside a very strong shock: Td​=Tu​ in the strong-shock formula corresponds to Ms​=16/(3γ)​≃1.79. A separate sonic estimate Vs​∼cs,u​ gives about 4.4×106 yr and 133 pc for μ=1; neither extrapolation is a precise transition solution. Using ξ=1.15 increases these inferred matching ages and radii by factors ξ5/3.
In a real supernova remnant, substantial radiation losses commonly end the energy-conserving phase before these formal pressure-merging limits. The relevant comparison is tcool​∼(3/2)nkB​T/(ne​ni​Λ(T)) against the expansion time. A cooling function, composition and ionization state are needed to calculate that earlier age and radius; they are not supplied. The numerical values above answer the ambient-temperature breakdown estimate under explicit assumptions and must not be presented as a uniquely determined radiative shell-formation age.
After cooling, an interior-pressure-driven shell can approximately follow R∝t2/7 while pressure remains important. In the limiting momentum-conserving stage, Ms​R˙≃const and Ms​∝ρu​R3 give R∝t1/4​. Ultimately ambient pressure and turbulent motions make the disturbance merge with the ISM. These slower stages respect the reduced energy budget rather than continuing an adiabatic strong-shock law indefinitely.
