Let s=0 be closest approach on a line of sight at projected radius R. Euclidean spherical geometry gives r2=R2+s2. On either half-line, ∣ds/dr∣=r/r2−R2​, and the two halves contribute equally. With identical stellar luminosity L, L=Lν, so
I(R)=2L∫R∞​r2−R2​ν(r)rdr​​.
(49)
For an isotropic velocity distribution, the variance of any unit-direction projection equals σr2​(r). Explicitly, the line-of-sight contributions have weights s2/r2 from the radial component and R2/r2 from a tangential component; equality of their variances makes the sum σr2​. Consequently
I(R)σlos2​(R)=2L∫R∞​r2−R2​ν(r)σr2​(r)rdr​​.
(50)
Without isotropy the integrand instead has the factor 1−β(r)R2/r2. Thus the projection assumption is substantive, rather than something that can be inferred from spherical surface brightness alone. The missing illustration does not prevent recovering these algebraic expressions from the prose.
