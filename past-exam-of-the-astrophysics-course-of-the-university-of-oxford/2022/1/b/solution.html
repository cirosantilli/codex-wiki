Use the Einstein coefficients convention in which Bij​ multiplies spectral radiation energy density uν​, with uν​=c−1∫Iν​dΩ. A normalized spectral line profile obeys ∫φ(ν)dν=1. The spontaneous emission probability per upper-state atom per unit time is A21​; the stimulated emission and upward absorption rates per atom are respectively B21​∫uν​φ(ν)dν and B12​∫uν​φ(ν)dν.
For isotropic spontaneous emission, n2​A21​ transitions occur per unit volume and time, each supplying photon energy hν12​. Dividing among 4π steradians gives
jν​=4πhν12​​n2​A21​φ(ν).​
(2)
For a small incident beam, its contribution to uν​ is Iν​dΩ/c. Absorption removes and stimulated emission adds photons in the incident direction. Matching the net loss to αν​Iν​dΩ therefore gives
αν​=chν12​​(n1​B12​−n2​B21​)φ(ν).​
(3)
Replacing ν12​ by ν across the narrow spectral line profile gives the printed expressions. The negative sign is essential: an inverted population can amplify the incident specific intensity. Thermal equilibrium and the Planck function imply g1​B12​=g2​B21​ and A21​/B21​=8πhν123​/c3, where gi​ is the state degeneracy. A convention using mean intensity instead changes both B coefficients by 4π/c.
