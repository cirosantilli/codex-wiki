Assume an isolated, bound, nonrotating Newtonian gas cloud, negligible surface pressure, negligible magnetic and radiation stresses, no persistent bulk flow, and a stationary or time-averaged virialized state. The stellar virial theorem then reduces to
3∫P(x)dV+Ω=0,
(17)
where Ω<0 is the total gravitational potential energy. Define the volume-averaged pressure P=V−1∫PdV and average gravitational energy density ug​=Ω/V. Hence
P=−ug​/3.​
(18)
Equivalently, for a nonrelativistic monatomic gas, the thermal internal energy U=(3/2)∫PdV obeys 2U+Ω=0. This second derivation must not count the thermal motions again as a separate bulk kinetic energy. If the external pressure is Ps​, the correct result is P−Ps​=−ug​/3; non-negligible bulk motion adds 2Tbulk​ to the virial theorem. The initial sharp-edged r−2 profile need not survive virialization; maintaining that profile hydrostatically to its edge would generally require a surface term.
