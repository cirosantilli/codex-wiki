Diffusive shock acceleration is viable in principle at 511 GeV: repeated scattering off magnetic irregularities on opposite sides of a strong nonrelativistic shock wave can reach such energies. Actual viability requires a sufficiently large and long-lived accelerator with an acceleration time shorter than its escape and loss times; γ alone does not establish those conditions.

For a strong adiabatic shock wave in a gas with adiabatic index 5/3, the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions give ρd​/ρu​=4 and the shock-frame inflow speeds satisfy u1​=vs​, u2​=vs​/4. Scattering randomizes directions without changing energy in that plasma frame, and the reverse crossing has the same mean gain. Therefore

These are first-order mean gains. They are not an increase of energy in one fixed frame caused by an instantaneous boundary crossing. Literally choosing the electron's instantaneous rest frame gives incident E=me​c2, and the other gas approaches at nearly c, so the expansion in small V/c is invalid. This concrete frame check explains why the printed description of V must instead refer to the upstream/downstream scattering frames.
