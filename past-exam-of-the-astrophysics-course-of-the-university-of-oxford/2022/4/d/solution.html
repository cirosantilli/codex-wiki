Relativistic beaming concentrates the emission into a forward cone about the instantaneous velocity. The aberration of light maps a rest-frame ray at θ′=π/2 to cosθ=β, so the characteristic cone half-angle is θ≃γ−1. Thus its conventional full width is 2/γ=2×10−6 rad.
The isotropic-emission assumption has no beam nulls. For example, its received bolometric angular power is proportional to D4, where the Doppler factor is D=[γ(1−βcosθ)]−1>0. In particular it is nonzero at θ=1/γ, disproving a literal null there. A true dipole pattern for an accelerated electron is not isotropic in its rest frame and needs a specified polarization and viewing plane. The width just quoted is a characteristic beaming angle, not a null-to-null width under the stated premise.
