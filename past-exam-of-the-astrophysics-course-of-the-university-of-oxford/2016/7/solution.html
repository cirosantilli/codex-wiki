The main evidence combines a large luminosity with a compact source and a massive central gravitating object. Rapid continuum variability bounds the emitting size by a light-crossing scale, while broad emission lines and reverberation delays constrain the gas orbital speeds and characteristic radii. Their virial masses, together with stellar and gas dynamics in galactic nuclei, imply compact central masses far larger than ordinary stars. Thermal optical/ultraviolet disk emission, a high-energy coronal component, jets and relativistically broadened inner-disk features are naturally associated with an accretion flow. No one signature alone is a unique proof, but the combined compactness, mass and energy requirements strongly favor accretion onto supermassive black holes. Gravitational accretion releases a much larger fraction of rest energy than ordinary stellar burning, without requiring the luminosity to come from the black-hole interior.
For a fully ionized pure-hydrogen plasma, each electron receiving Thomson radiation force is coupled electrostatically to a proton carrying almost all the mass. With spherical isotropic luminosity L, the outward force per electron is σT​L/(4πr2c), while gravity per electron–proton pair is approximately GMmp​/r2. Equality defines the Eddington luminosity:
LEdd​=σT​4πGmp​cM​.​
(34)
Here G is the gravitational constant, mp​ the proton mass, c the speed of light, M the central mass and σT​ the Thomson cross-section. The estimate assumes electron-scattering opacity, tight electron–ion coupling and no other dominant force. Geometry, beaming, other opacities and photon trapping can change the relevant luminosity limit.
If M˙0​ is the supplied rest-mass rate and a fraction ϵ of its rest energy is radiated, L=ϵM˙0​c2. The associated Eddington accretion rate is consequently
M˙0,Edd​=ϵcσT​4πGmp​M​.​
(35)
This is a supply rate. The black-hole growth rate is M˙BH​=(1−ϵ)M˙0​ if the remaining supplied energy increases the hole's mass. Confusing these rates loses a factor relevant to the growth-time calculation.
The order-of-magnitude efficiency ϵ∼0.1 is motivated by orbital binding energy released near the innermost stable orbit. A Newtonian orbit at 6GM/c2 has binding energy per unit mass c2/12≃0.083c2; the exact nonspinning thin-disk value is 1−8/9​≃0.057. Black-hole spin changes the innermost stable orbit and can raise or lower the efficiency. Thus 0.1 is a useful radiatively efficient accretion estimate, not a universal constant; an advective or jet-dominated flow can have a different radiative efficiency.
Using the stipulated luminosity and σT​=6.65×10−29m2,
M/M⊙​LEdd​​≃1.26×1031W≃3.29×104L⊙​,M≃3.29×1046.3×1013​M⊙​≃1.92×109M⊙​.​
(36)
The source's redshift and age are treated as its supplied data; its record wording is historical, not a present-day claim about the most distant known quasar.
At continuous Eddington luminosity and fixed efficiency, growth is exponential:
M(t)=Mseed​eΔt/tS​,tS​=1−ϵϵ​4πGmp​σT​c​≃5.00×107yr(ϵ=0.1).​
(37)
This is the Salpeter time, including retained-mass efficiency. Even granting all 0.77Gyr since the big bang permits only about 15.4 e-folds, requiring a seed of at least roughly 400M⊙​. A 100M⊙​ seed would take about 0.84Gyr under these assumptions, before allowing any formation delay; a 105M⊙​ seed takes about 0.49Gyr. These numbers motivate early massive seeds, sustained high duty-cycle accretion, lower radiative efficiencies, super-Eddington phases or contributions from mergers. They do not prove a unique seed channel, since the luminosity-to-mass conversion and accretion history are model-dependent.
