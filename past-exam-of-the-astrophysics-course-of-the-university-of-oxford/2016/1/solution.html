Inverse Compton scattering transfers energy from fast electrons to lower-energy photons. In the Thomson scattering limit the scattering is approximately elastic in the electron's instantaneous rest frame, but the transformations into and out of that frame can greatly increase the photon energy in the original frame.
In a blazar, relativistic jet electrons can scatter their own synchrotron radiation, producing synchrotron self-Compton radiation, or scatter photons from an accretion disk and its surrounding reprocessing material. The two spectral humps constrain electron Lorentz factors, the electron distribution and, with assumptions about geometry and relativistic beaming, the magnetic field. For the same electron population in the Thomson regime, the ratio of inverse-Compton to synchrotron power scales as uph​/uB​ in the plasma rest frame. It does not determine the field without information about the photon energy density and beaming.
In a galaxy cluster, hot thermal electrons scatter cosmic microwave background photons and slightly raise their mean energy, producing the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect. The spectral distortion measures the line-of-sight integral of electron pressure, approximately y=σT​∫ne​kB​Te​dℓ/(me​c2) for nonrelativistic thermal electrons. Combined with X-ray emission, which weights density approximately quadratically, it constrains the gas density, temperature and mass distribution. These are distinct source populations and seed photon fields from the blazar example.
For the power derivation, use an isotropic incident radiation field of total photon energy density uph​ in the laboratory frame. Its stress-energy tensor has T00=uph​ and Tzz=uph​/3, with zero mean flux. Boosting to an electron moving at βc along z gives
T′0z=−γ2β(T00+Tzz)=−34​γ2βuph​.
(1)
The radiation energy flux in this rest frame is Sz′​=cT′0z. Elastic Thomson scattering exerts the drag Fz′​=σT​Sz′​/c. The parallel three-force has the same value in the laboratory frame, so the electron's positive energy loss rate is
PIC​=−Fz​βc=34​σT​cuph​β2γ2.​
(2)
This isotropic inverse-Compton drag assumes photon energies small compared with me​c2 in the electron rest frame, negligible recoil and an isotropic seed field in the specified frame. An anisotropic photon field requires its directional moments; the recoil-dependent differential scattering cross-section of Compton scattering becomes necessary at larger rest-frame energies.
There are two conventions to qualify in the printed expression. First, the density multiplying 4/3 must be uph​, not literally only the magnetic part of a propagating electromagnetic field. That field has equal electric and magnetic energy densities, so uph​=2umag​ and the corresponding formula is PIC​=(8/3)σT​cumag​β2γ2. Second, this is net electron energy loss, rather than all incident plus scattered photon energy. At β=0 an electron still scatters power σT​cuph​, but transfers no kinetic energy to the photons, so the inverse-Compton gain is zero.
For an individual incident photon of energy ε, the electron-frame energy is ε′=γε(1−βcosθ). For a head-on photon followed by forward emission in the laboratory,
εout​≃γ2(1+β)2ε≃4γ2ε.
(3)
Averaging an isotropic seed field gives ⟨εout​⟩≃(4/3)γ2ε for ultrarelativistic electrons. The original plot has an inverse-Compton maximum of order 108eV, a synchrotron maximum around 10−1eV and a disk component around 1eV. Thus
γ∼4εseed​3εout​​​∼9×103 to 3×104.​
(4)
The head-on maximum gives the same order of magnitude, about 5×103 to 1.6×104. These are approximate readings of a broad spectrum, not precise fitted parameters. Disk and synchrotron seed photons need not be scattered by the same part of the electron distribution, and bulk jet Doppler factors also enter the observed peak relation. The indicated rest-frame seed energies remain well below the electron rest energy in this estimate.
