Interpret the new force as attractive. With outward position vector r, this requires a minus sign in front of the source's printed expression; the literal positive sign and positive a0​ would repel a test particle. The radial force magnitude is GMd​(r)m/(a0​rα−1), so circular balance gives Vc2​=GMd​(r)r2−α/a0​. Constant exterior Md​ therefore requires
α=2,[a0​]=length,Vc2​(r)=a0​GMd​(<r)​.​

V∞​Vmod​(r)​={f(x)/ft​​,1,​0≤x≤5,x≥5.​​

Since f(x)=x2/2+O(x3), it rises linearly from zero at small radius and flattens outside. The enclosed-mass prescription defines this modified model; it is not a derivation of a general modified field equation for arbitrary flattened matter.
The central obstruction to an outer halo mass offset requires an explicit distinction. Extending the full distant-outer expression V∞2​r/G−M⋆​ to zero gives MDM​(0)=−M⋆​. It becomes negative for r<GM⋆​/V∞2​ and cannot be a physical halo. Its formal total squared speed, Vd2​+V∞2​−GM⋆​/r, even becomes negative near the centre. Thus there is no physical entire rotation curve under that literal extrapolation.
If instead one extends only the positive halo-dominated leading term, Mh​=V∞2​r/G, the entire Newtonian model is
VN2​(r)=V∞2​+Vd2​(r).​
(32)
For a full calculation of the disk contribution, the elliptic-integral circular-speed kernel of a thin disk gives
Vd2​(r)=2GPV∫0Rt​​sΣ(s)[r+sK(k)​+r−sE(k)​]ds,k=r+s2rs​​,
(33)
where K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind and E the complete elliptic integral of the second kind. The principal value is needed inside the disk; outside it the integral is ordinary. The disk term vanishes at the centre, so this halo extrapolation has VN​(0+)=V∞​, develops a stellar contribution at intermediate radii and approaches V∞​ from above. A discontinuous, exactly zero-thickness truncation has an artificial logarithmic force singularity right at the edge; a smooth edge or finite thickness removes it. The figure omits the single singular edge point.
Observed galaxy rotation curves generally rise from the central region rather than beginning with a nonzero constant. The modified toy curve therefore captures that qualitative behavior better than this particular singular isothermal halo extrapolation, without establishing that its force law is correct. If dark matter is the explanation, the inner halo cannot universally retain Mh​∝r, ρh​∝r−2 down to zero. For a spherical density ρh​∝r−γh​, Vh​∝r1−γh​/2 tends to zero when γh​<2; a constant-density core gives a linear rise. A shallower cusp can also tend to zero. Stellar mass-to-light ratio, baryonic structure and disk–halo degeneracy prevent this qualitative argument alone from proving that every halo has a constant-density core.
