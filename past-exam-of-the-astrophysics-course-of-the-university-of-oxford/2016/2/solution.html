For an optically thin collisionally excited line, the volume emissivity is approximately jul​=ne​n(Xi)qlu​(Te​)hνul​ below the transition's critical density. The excitation coefficient includes a factor Te−1/2​exp[−ΔE/(kB​Te​)], so temperature is needed to convert luminosity into an ionic abundance. Density is needed both to assess collisional quenching of a metastable line and to convert an emission measure into a gas mass. For a uniform recombining ion, for example, Mi​=mi​Lline​/[ne​αeff​hν]. Temperature structure, density clumping, extinction and unobserved ionization stages introduce further corrections.
A useful density doublet has closely spaced upper energies but different spontaneous decay rates and hence different critical densities. Nearly equal excitation energies reduce temperature dependence of the ratio; similar wavelengths reduce differential extinction and relative calibration errors. Metastable upper levels have sufficiently small Einstein coefficients for collisional de-excitation to compete at nebular densities.
Use statistical weights g2​=6, g3​=4. The source's O II ground-state term has a typographical error: it is 2s22p34S3/2o​, not 2S3/2​. A 2S term has J=1/2, so the printed term is internally inconsistent. The tabulated J values, wavelengths and rates can still be used.[ref]

The density sensitivity of a metastable doublet depends on the error measure. The fractional response ∣dlnR/dlnne​∣ is maximal at
ne​=nc,2​nc,3​​=6.87×109m−3=6.87×103cm−3.​
(10)
If absolute ratio errors are constant, maximizing ∣dR/dlnne​∣ instead gives ne​=nc,2​. An unspecified experimental noise model therefore does not define a unique sharper optimum than this transition-density range.
For Ne IV, the same calculation gives critical densities 4.23×1010 and 3.93×1011m−3, with fractional sensitivity peaking near 1.29×1011m−3. This is a useful higher-density diagnostic when O II is saturated. However, Ne3+ and O+ need not occupy the same ionization zone, so their inferred densities need not describe the same gas. The ultraviolet Ne IV pair also needs suitable observations and extinction correction. The proposal is useful with these qualifications, not a guaranteed replacement for the density of the O II emitting zone.
