A massive main-sequence donor loses material in a stellar wind. Gravitational capture by the neutron star can produce a nearly radial, supersonic infall when the captured material has little specific angular momentum. A magnetic field may channel the final infall toward polar accretion columns, where a standing shock decelerates the flow. Large angular momentum instead favours an accretion disk; strictly radial accretion is an idealization.

Here kB​T is the characteristic particle energy; the mean translational energy of a nonrelativistic thermal particle is 3kB​T/2. If one additionally chose R∗​=10km and μ=mp​, this ideal one-temperature estimate would correspond to gamma-ray-scale thermal photon energies. Those are conditional choices, not information recovered from the source. Real accreting neutron stars commonly emit X-rays: electron-ion disequilibrium, radiative cooling, radiation pressure, reprocessing and relativistic infall invalidate a direct identification of this ideal ion-heating estimate with the escaping photon spectrum.
