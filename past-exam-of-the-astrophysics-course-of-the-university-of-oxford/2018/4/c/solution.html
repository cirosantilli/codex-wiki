The infrared and optical transitions share the same upper level 3. Using the supplied infrared photon energy gives
n3​=(3.3×10−7)hc/(314×10−6)1.5×10−21​=7.185×106m−3.
(41)
With the same Te​,ne​ and three-level fraction f3​ used in the abundance calculation of b,
n(S+)=5.31×107m−3,ne​n(S+)​=5.31×10−5.​
(42)
This exceeds the optical abundance by a factor 5.53. Because the upper level is common, its population cancels in the intrinsic emissivity ratio:
j31​j32​​=A31​ν31​A32​ν32​​=2.715×10−6.
(43)
The observed ratio is 1.5×10−5. Thus changing only the electron temperature or a common level-population correction cannot reconcile these two lines from the same gas. Dust extinction suppressing the optical line is a natural explanation. If the far-infrared attenuation is negligible, the required optical attenuation is
A671.6​=2.5log10​(5.53)≃1.86 mag.​
(44)
Different apertures, filling factors, optical-depth assumptions or emissivity calibrations could also cause a discrepancy. A total sulfur abundance would still need an ionization correction beyond this ionic comparison.
