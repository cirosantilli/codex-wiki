Treat the stated 120Mpc as the radius of a complete, volume-limited search, with uniform galaxy density ng​. Its surveyed volume and number of galaxies are
V=34π​(120Mpc)3=7.24×106Mpc3,Ng​=ng​V=7.24×104.

The observing time is Δt=117/365.25=0.3203yr. One detection estimates
νBNS​≃Ng​Δt1​=4.31×10−5yr−1galaxy−1,​
(25)
or one merger per approximately 2.3×104 years per Milky-Way-type galaxy. The equivalent volume rate is about 431Gpc−3yr−1. The event's actual 40Mpc distance is not the survey radius and must not replace 120Mpc in the exposure.
A representative Galactic core-collapse supernova rate is about two per century, roughly 4.6×102 times larger than this merger-rate estimate. The historical 26Al estimate was 1.9±1.1 per century.[ref] The one-event Poisson uncertainty is large. Detector orientation, duty cycle, completeness and whether the quoted radius is an optimal horizon or an effective range also change the exposure; the boxed value is the specified simple-volume estimate.
