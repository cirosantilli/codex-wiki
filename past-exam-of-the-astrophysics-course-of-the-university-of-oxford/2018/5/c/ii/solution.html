The general thin gravitational lens equation is a two-component angular relation,
β=θ−α(θ),α=DS​DLS​​ξ.
(54)
For a spherical lens, image, source and lens centre lie in one plane containing the line of sight. Choose its sky direction as a signed axis. Then α,θ,β can be signed scalars and
β=θ−DS​DLS​​ξsgnθ.​
(55)
They are not fundamentally scalar quantities for a general lens; the reduction uses this symmetry and axis choice. An exactly aligned source requires the full ring geometry, not a unique preferred sky axis.
In general, DS​=DL​+DLS​. For example, in a spatially flat expanding cosmology, comoving radial distances χL​,χS​ give
DL​=1+zL​χL​​,DS​=1+zS​χS​​,DLS​=1+zS​χS​−χL​​.
(56)
Thus DS​−DLS​=χL​/(1+zS​) differs from DL​ when the redshifts differ. Ordinary Euclidean addition applies only when the same static physical-distance convention is appropriate.
