a OurBigBook.com
A shock wave is a thin, irreversible compression layer across which fluid density, pressure, temperature and velocity change rapidly, while conserved fluxes satisfy the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions. In the shock frame, a compressive hydrodynamic shock converts ordered upstream kinetic energy into downstream thermal energy and increases entropy. The upstream normal flow is supersonic; the downstream normal flow is subsonic for an ordinary gas-dynamic compression shock.
In an accreting binary star, a supersonic stellar wind can be captured by a compact companion and shocked. An accretion stream striking an accretion disk, an accretion-disk boundary layer, and a magnetically guided accretion column provide other sites where infall energy is dissipated and high-energy radiation is generated. Shocks heat and decelerate material; a disk also needs transport of angular momentum before its material can reach the compact star.
In diffusive shock acceleration, charged particles scatter from magnetic irregularities on both sides of a shock and repeatedly cross the converging flows. The average energy gain is first order in the velocity jump divided by the particle speed. Escape competes with acceleration, producing a nonthermal distribution rather than a single downstream temperature. Supernova remnants are important candidate sources of Galactic cosmic rays; shocks associated with active galactic nuclei, their jets and lobes, and gamma-ray bursts are candidate extragalactic accelerators. Ultrarelativistic energies require sufficient size, magnetic field and lifetime, and losses must not outpace acceleration. In particular a relativistic particle's Larmor radius must fit within the accelerator; the existence of a strong shock alone does not guarantee unlimited particle energy.

GW170817 supplied the first direct gravitational wave observation of a binary neutron star inspiral. The chirping waveform tests compact-binary general relativity, constrains masses and tidal effects, and links a class of known compact binaries to observable sources of gravitational waves. The electromagnetic counterpart identifies the host and distance, enabling complementary source and cosmological inference. This is distinct from the earlier binary-black-hole detections; it established a system involving matter that could also be observed electromagnetically.[ref]

The short gamma-ray burst GRB 170817A followed the merger signal by about 1.7s, providing direct evidence that at least some short gamma-ray bursts arise from neutron star mergers. The evolving optical and infrared kilonova was consistent with radioactive heating of neutron-rich merger ejecta. Rapid neutron capture, the r-process, can synthesize nuclei heavier than iron; decay of the newly formed unstable nuclei powers the transient. Its redward evolution and large inferred opacity supported production of heavy-element-rich ejecta. For the Double-degenerate Type Ia supernova scenario, measure the Type Ia supernova rate per formed stellar mass in old galaxies, grouped by the time since their main star-formation episode. Correct the event counts for observing time and detection efficiency. In an ideal burst population, the normalized rate measures the delay-time distribution directly. If gravitational waves inspiral dominates the delay and the initial white-dwarf separations are uniformly distributed in separation, c predicts a slope −3/4.
More generally, if dN/dA∝Aβ and tm​∝A4, the predicted rate scales as tm(β−3)/4​. A distribution uniform in logA has β=−1 and gives tm−1​. Thus a slope differing from −3/4 does not alone rule out mergers: the separation distribution must also be tested.
An extended star formation history gives a convolution, R(t)=∫dτSFR(t−τ)Ψ(τ), rather than the single-burst expression. The time since star formation stopped need not equal each progenitor's age. Stellar lifetimes before formation of the second white dwarf, common-envelope evolution, masses and mass ratios, metallicity, delay-dependent explosion efficiencies, galaxy-age estimates and survey selection all affect the comparison. Some CO-white-dwarf mergers need not make Type Ia supernovae, and other channels can contribute to the observed rate. Agreement in slope and normalization therefore supports the hypothesis under stated assumptions but is not a unique identification of the channel.

a OurBigBook.com
A spontaneous emission transition has probability per unit time Aji​ and emits a photon of energy hνji​. For an optically thin gas, its angle-integrated emissivity is jji​=nj​Aji​hνji​. Electron collisions populate excited levels at rates ne​ni​qij​; they also remove excitation collisionally at rates ne​nj​qji​, returning the energy to the gas rather than producing the line photon. A collisionally excited line is usually emitted by a subsequent spontaneous radiative decay; collisional de-excitation instead quenches it.
The critical density of an atomic transition compares radiative loss from an upper level with its collisional loss. For a two-level model, ncrit​=Aji​/qji​; for several lower or connected levels use the relevant sums. Below this density, excitation is commonly followed by a photon and the line cools the gas. Above it, collisions increasingly suppress radiative emission per excitation and drive the level populations toward Boltzmann ratios.
Optical forbidden emission lines have small A because an electric-dipole transition is forbidden by a selection rule. Metastable levels survive long enough to radiate in dilute ionized nebulae, although collisions quench them in dense gas. Ratios from closely spaced levels with different critical densities are electron-density diagnostics; ratios involving different excitation energies probe the electron temperature through Boltzmann factors. Combining line emissivities with electron density, temperature and an ionization model estimates ionic abundances.
Infrared fine-structure emission lines connect closely spaced sublevels. Their small excitation energies make them useful over temperature ranges where optical excitation is exponentially suppressed, and dust extinction is usually weaker in the far infrared. They are important coolants and density diagnostics in ionized gas, with the choice of ion specifying the ionization zone. Neither an optical nor an infrared ionic abundance automatically equals the total elemental abundance; unobserved ionization stages require an ionization correction.

The general thin gravitational lens equation is a two-component angular relation,
β=θ−α(θ),α=DS​DLS​​ξ.
(54)
For a spherical lens, image, source and lens centre lie in one plane containing the line of sight. Choose its sky direction as a signed axis. Then α,θ,β can be signed scalars and

a OurBigBook.com
In a massive star undergoing core-collapse supernova formation, an iron-rich core cannot gain further support from exothermic fusion. Electron captures and nuclear photodisintegration promote collapse. The inner core stiffens near nuclear density and bounces, launching a shock into the infalling outer core. Dissociation and neutrino losses drain its energy, so the prompt shock generally stalls while matter continues to accrete. Neutrino absorption behind the shock, aided by multidimensional flow, can revive it and eject the envelope; sufficiently rapid rotation and strong magnetic fields offer additional possibilities. The remnant may be a neutron star or black hole. Major uncertainties include progenitor structure, nuclear equation of state, neutrino transport and interactions, turbulent flow, rotation and magnetic fields.
The standing accretion shock instability is a nonradial instability of this stalled shock and accretion flow. Feedback between advected disturbances and acoustic response can amplify large-scale sloshing or spiral motions, producing an asymmetric shock even without an initially unstable convective gradient.[ref]
An asymmetric explosion can impart an opposite recoil to the remnant by momentum conservation. In neutrino-driven calculations, long-lasting gravitational attraction by asymmetric, slower inner ejecta can dominate the acceleration: the gravitational tug-boat mechanism. SASI and convection can seed the asymmetries, but the kick is not necessarily an instantaneous hydrodynamic push or uniquely attributable to SASI.[ref]

Let M0​=M1​+M2​, M′=MNS​+M2​=M0​−ΔM, and let the initial circular separation be A. The initial relative speed is v0​=v1​+v2​, with v02​=GM0​/A. In the new centre-of-mass frame, use the reduced mass μ′=MNS​M2​/M′; the orbital energy is
E′=μ′(21​v02​−AGM′​)=2AGμ′​(2ΔM−M0​).

Consequently
ΔM>2M0​​ ⟹ E′>0and disruption.​
(60)
Less than half the total initial binary mass lost gives a bound orbit, not necessarily a circular one. Exactly half gives zero orbital energy and the marginal parabolic escape case. The threshold concerns the total mass of both stars, not just the exploding star. Using the old centre-of-mass kinetic energy without subtracting the new systemic motion would give the wrong binding criterion; the relative-velocity form performs that subtraction automatically.

Write f=M′/M0​ for the retained fraction and q=v−/v0​ for an opposite kick. With more than half the mass lost, 0<f<1/2. A same-direction kick cannot bind the pair, because 1+v+/v0​≥1>2f​. An opposite kick can reduce the relative speed enough to leave negative energy:
1−2f​<q<1+2f​.​
(63)
Both endpoints are marginal zero-energy cases. Kicks somewhat larger than v0​ can still satisfy this interval, but then the direction of the relative orbital motion is reversed.
At v−=v0​=v1​+v2​, the two surviving stars have identical instantaneous velocities, so their relative velocity and orbital angular momentum vanish. Their energy is nevertheless negative,
E′=−AGMNS​M2​​.
(64)
They fall radially toward each other from rest in their new centre-of-mass frame. In the point-mass idealization the collision orbit has semimajor axis A/2 and zero pericentre; real finite-size stars collide or interact before reaching zero separation. It is not a stable circular binary. Zero relative angular momentum, rather than the negative energy alone, determines this plunge outcome.
