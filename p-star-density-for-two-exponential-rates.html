P-star density for two exponential rates (p∗(ψ​,λ))

For independent samples of size n from exponential distributions with rates λ and ψλ, the maximum-likelihood estimators are λ=n/S and ψ​=S/T, where S and T are the respective sample sums. Their normalized P-star approximation is exact:
p(ψ​,λ;ψ,λ)=Γ(n)2ψ​n+1λ2n+1n2nψnλ2n​exp{−λnλ​(1+ψ​ψ​)},ψ​,λ>0.
(60)
Indeed, S and T have independent gamma distributions with shapes n and respective rates λ,ψλ. The Jacobian determinant of their inverse transformation is n2/(ψ​2λ3), giving the displayed density. Alternatively the square root of the determinant of the fitted observed information is n/(ψ​λ); multiplying by the exponential of the fitted log-likelihood difference gives the same shape. Its normalizing constant in the P-star approximation is cn​=n2n−1e−2n/Γ(n)2. Integrating over λ gives
p(ψ​;ψ)=Γ(n)2ψΓ(2n)​(ψψ​​)n−1(1+ψψ​​)−2n,
(61)
so ψ​/ψ has the F-distribution with degrees of freedom (2n,2n).
