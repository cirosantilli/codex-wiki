Charge lattice inference from oil drops (qi​≃ni​e)

An integer pattern must survive the stated measurement uncertainty. For an original simulated example, choose a reference electric charge Q∗​>0, define yi​=∣qi​∣/Q∗​, and stipulate state means
y=(2.03, 2.96, 5.02, 3.99),σi​=0.08.
(7)
Here each σi​ is an independent standard uncertainty of a state mean, before shared calibration effects. The generator constructs repeated noisy observations with these means; the numbers are deliberately chosen, not historical data. There is no conversion of Q∗​ to coulombs and no measured value of the elementary charge in this example.
For a candidate spacing a>0 in units of Q∗​ and proposed integers ni​, use weighted least squares:
χ2(a,n)=∑i​σi2​(yi​−ni​a)2​,a=∑i​ni2​/σi2​∑i​ni​yi​/σi2​​,u(a)=(∑i​σi2​ni2​​)−1/2.
(8)
The last two expressions follow by differentiating the quadratic in a and propagating the independent errors. They are conditional on fixed, correct integer assignments and the stated error model. They do not include uncertainty from choosing those assignments. For n=(2,3,5,4), they give a=1, u(a)≃0.01089, and χ2=0.46875. Such a small residual is compatible with this chosen error scale; it is not evidence that the errors vanish. A chi-squared distribution interpretation additionally needs the appropriate normal distribution error model and allowance for the fitted parameter; searching many assignments changes that interpretation.
The obstruction is identifiability: replacing (a,ni​) by (a/m,mni​) for any positive integer m leaves every predicted reading unchanged. In particular, an e-spaced pattern also fits an e/2 lattice using even indices. Even the coarsest well-fitting spacing need not be the physical unit: a dataset containing only even charge states can have twice that unit. Worse, if arbitrarily fine spacings and unrestricted integers are allowed, rounding each yi​/a gives
minn​χ2(a,n)≤4a2​∑i​σi2​1​⟶0as a⟶0.
(9)
Consequently a finite fit cannot prove a minimal transferable electric charge. Reproducible smallest observed jumps supply additional evidence; interpreting them as single-carrier transfers requires independent physical support. One visibly abrupt transition could contain several unresolved transfers. In the simulation the successive differences are 0.93, 2.06, and −1.03, each with standard uncertainty 2​(0.08)≃0.1131. These support changes near a,2a,−a conditional on that unit; they equally support 2,4,−2 units of a/2. Neighboring differences share a state reading and are therefore correlated, even though the state means were stipulated independent.
A shared scale error can preserve every apparent integer relation. To first order, an independent fractional scale standard uncertainty s adds s2a2ni​nj​ to the covariance matrix of the yi​. For the simulation, stipulating s=0.02 changes the conditional uncertainty of a to approximately 0.010892+0.022​=0.02277, despite the same residual pattern. Repeating timings cannot average away this common scale contribution. In the uncorrected continuum model in elementary charge, holding the other measured inputs fixed gives ∣q∣∝η3/2, so a small fractional error in air dynamic viscosity causes 3/2 times that fractional error in inferred electric charge. The gas slip correction to Stokes drag requires its own propagation. Pattern agreement, drop-stability controls and absolute measurement calibration together make the inference persuasive within resolution.
