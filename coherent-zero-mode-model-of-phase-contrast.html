Coherent zero-mode model of phase contrast

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Optical microscope Phase-contrast microscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 360
The following original model isolates the conversion mechanism. Use a periodic image window, unit illumination, ideal unit magnification, and a phase plate that changes only the zero spatial frequency. Write c0​=⟨eiϕ⟩ for the spatial mean. Multiplying that single Fourier coefficient by aeiθ, with 0<a≤1, gives the exact image field in this model:
Eout​=eiϕ+(aeiθ−1)c0​,Iout​=∣Eout​∣2.
(25)
This formula follows by splitting eiϕ=c0​+(eiϕ−c0​): the second term has zero mean and is left unchanged. A real annular, partly coherent instrument alters a finite band of directions, so this calculation is a mechanism model rather than its full image-formation law.
For a weak phase object with ⟨ϕ⟩=0, one has c0​=1+O(ϕ2) and hence
Eout​=aeiθ+iϕ+O(ϕ2),Iout​=a2+2aϕsinθ+O(ϕ2).​
(26)
The cross term is 2Re(ae−iθiϕ)=2aϕsinθ. Thus θ=π/2 makes positive ϕ brighter and θ=−π/2 makes it darker under this stated phase convention for complex amplitudes. The sign comes from this convention and chosen plate; a manufacturer’s positive/negative contrast labels should not be inferred from the sign of θ alone.
For example, choose a=0.5 and a zero-mean periodic pattern whose peak phase is 0.084. The first-order peak intensities are 0.25±0.084, compared with the uniform ideal bright-field microscopy value 1. The relative change about the reduced background is approximately 2ϕ/a for a quarter-cycle shift. Attenuating the reference increases relative contrast while reducing the background photon count; it is not light amplification or a universal improvement of signal-to-noise ratio.
Two checks expose what the model can and cannot measure. A spatially constant phase gives Eout​=aeiθeiϕ and Iout​=a2, so no absolute phase is recovered. Orthogonality of the constant and zero-mean parts also gives
⟨Iout​⟩=1−(1−a2)∣c0​∣2.
(27)
For a≤1 this cannot exceed the incident mean light intensity. The image redistributes brightness and loses some reference light rather than creating energy.
