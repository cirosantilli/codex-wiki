Assume an initially unfractured homogeneous isotropic material described by a linear Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion, with the given coefficient of friction μf​=0.5 representing the relevant internal friction angle. Use spatially uniform pore fluid pressure, effective stresses σi′​=σi​−Pf​, and compression-positive signs. Then
∣τ∣=c+μf​σn′​,φ=arctanμf​=26.565∘.
(16)
The Mohr circle in the σ1′​,σ3′​ plane has centre C=(σ1′​+σ3′​)/2 and radius R=(σ1′​−σ3′​)/2. At first failure it is tangent to the failure envelope. The radius to the positive-shear tangent point is perpendicular to the line of slope tanφ, hence makes angle 90∘+φ with the positive normal-stress axis.
If θ is the angle between the fault normal and σ1​, the stress transformation is
σn′​=C+Rcos2θ,τ=Rsin2θ.
(17)
Thus 2θ=90∘+φ, θ=58.2825∘, and the angle of the fault itself to horizontal σ1​ is
δ=90∘−θ=45∘−2φ​=31.7175∘.​
(18)
The two conjugate thrust faults therefore strike approximately east–west and dip about 32∘ north and 32∘ south. This is the fault dip, not the 58.3∘ fault-normal angle. Both sediments and basement have the same optimum orientation under these assumptions because their stipulated friction slopes are equal; their cohesion and absolute failure stresses could still differ.
The first two panels in solution give the labeled construction. Constant cohesion shifts the straight failure envelope without changing its slope, and uniform pore fluid pressure translates the Mohr circle in normal-stress coordinates; neither changes this tangency angle. In the illustrative cohesionless case, R/C=sinφ, giving σ1′​/σ3′​=(1+sinφ)/(1−sinφ)≃2.618. Pre-existing weaknesses, a nonuniform stress field, anisotropic strength, or a nonlinear failure envelope invalidate the simple optimum-orientation prediction.[ref]
