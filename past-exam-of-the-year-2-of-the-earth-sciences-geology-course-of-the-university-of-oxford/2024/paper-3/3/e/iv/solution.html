For a unilateral finite-source rupture advancing at approximately constant rupture velocity Vr​, the along-fault propagation length is
L=Vr​T.​
(23)
There is no supplied Vr​ or calibrated seismic source time function, so a unique spatial extent cannot be read directly from Figure 4. For illustration, T∼4 s and an assumed Vr​∼2.5 kms−1 would give L∼10 km. Allowing T∼4–5 s and assumed Vr​∼2–3 kms−1 gives lengths of order 8–15 km. These are explicitly conditional scale estimates, not measured fault dimensions.
If equal-and-opposite ray geometry and phase pulse durations were known, the directivity model in solution would instead constrain a projected length,
L∥​=2c​(DS(phase)​−DN(phase)​),L∥​=Lcosψ,
(24)
where ψ is the angle between the rupture direction and the northern ray. Distinct P and S wave speeds, unknown takeoff angles, smooth pulse boundaries and the clipped southern S tail prevent a reliable independent numerical inversion here. This observation is consistent with a few-kilometre projected extent while the actual along-fault length can be larger. A rupture length is not the accumulated fault slip, the width of the whole fault plane, or the approximately 50 km station distance.
