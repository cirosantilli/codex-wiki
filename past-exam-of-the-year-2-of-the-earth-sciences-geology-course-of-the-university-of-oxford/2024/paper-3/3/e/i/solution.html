The northern station is closer, under comparable P wave and S wave paths through the same velocity model. In the actual Figure 4 the northern P and S pulses are separated by about 6 s, whereas the southern separation is roughly 8–8.5 s. The S–P time removes the unknown earthquake origin time:
Δt=tS​−tP​=D(Vs​1​−Vp​1​).
(19)
Both northern pulses also occur earlier on the shared absolute clock, consistent with the smaller distance. Earlier arrival alone would not be sufficient if clocks or velocity models differed.
For reproducible graph readings, the northern positive-to-negative zero crossings are approximately 13.3 s for P and 19.3 s for S; the southern crossings are approximately 16.7 s and 25.0 s. These are convenient corresponding pulse landmarks, not sharply defined first-break onsets. The first visible motion begins earlier: approximately 12 s and 18 s in the north, and 14–15 s and 22–23 s in the south, depending on the amplitude threshold. The horizontal axis is seconds since midnight on 1 January 2000, not elapsed time since rupture initiation.
