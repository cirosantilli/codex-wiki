Use the northern S–P time, approximately ΔtN​=6 s, rather than multiplying an absolute-clock arrival by a wave speed. For common approximately straight paths,
DN​=1/Vs​−1/Vp​ΔtN​​=Vp​−Vs​Vp​Vs​​ΔtN​.​
(20)
No numerical crustal velocities are specified in Q3. If the usual illustrative crustal values Vp​=6.0 kms−1 and Vs​=3.5 kms−1 are adopted, the distance is
DN​≃8.4×6≃50 km.​
(21)
These assumed crustal speeds are unrelated to Q1's offshore layer values. They are representative rather than measurements of the Swiss column; the British Geological Survey gives broad typical crustal ranges of 5–7 km/s for P waves and 3–4 km/s for S waves.[ref]
Using the corresponding northern central pulse times gives an effective source time near 13.3−50.4/6≃4.9 s after midnight, and the S estimate is the same to graph accuracy. The southern central landmarks similarly give a distance near 70 km and an effective source time near 5 s. That consistency supports the example, but pulse landmarks need not mark the rupture's true onset. DN​ is a source-to-station path or hypocentral distance in the uniform-speed approximation. An epicentral distance additionally needs the hypocenter depth; it cannot simply be equated to 50 km for an unknown depth.
