A reverse or thrust fault earthquake is the expected end member: a hanging wall moves upwards relative to its footwall, accommodating north–south tectonic shortening. A plausible double-couple seismic source focal mechanism has an approximately east–west fault strike, a moderately north- or south-dipping fault plane, and up-dip fault slip. For example, right-hand-rule strike 090∘, southward fault dip about 32∘, and fault rake +90∘ specify a pure thrust mechanism.
In a lower-hemisphere equal-area plot, with shaded regions denoting compressional P first motions, this focal mechanism has a shaded central region and two unshaded outer lobes. The P axis is approximately horizontal north–south and the T axis approximately vertical for a symmetric idealization; for a single 32∘ plane their axes are slightly tilted. The P and T axes of a double-couple seismic source need not exactly equal the regional principal stress axes. The nodal curves represent both the fault plane and its auxiliary plane; additional evidence is needed to choose between them.[ref]
