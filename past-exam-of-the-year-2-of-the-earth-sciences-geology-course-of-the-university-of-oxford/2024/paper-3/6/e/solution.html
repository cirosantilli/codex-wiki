A steady state c∗​ satisfies 0=Ac∗​−b, hence
Ac∗​=b.
(53)
The sign is positive on the right after rearranging the supplied equation. A solution exists exactly when b lies in the column space of A, which above was found to be the zero-sum vector subspace. Equivalently, multiply by the left null space vector 1T:
1Tb=b1​+b2​+b3​+b4​=0.​
(54)
This condition is necessary and sufficient for an algebraic steady state, since both the column space and that zero-sum vector subspace have dimension 3. It expresses global conservation of mass: in the closed box model, total tracer production must balance total tracer removal. With the paper's convention −b, a positive bi​ is a sink and a negative bi​ is a source. If the sum is nonzero, dM/dt=−∑i​bi​ and the inventory changes linearly in time, preventing a steady state.
When the condition holds, all algebraic steady states form the affine solution space of a linear equation cp​+α1, for any particular solution Acp​=b. The initial inventory selects one member through 1Tc∗​=1Tc(0). For a physically admissible solution, its four concentrations must additionally be nonnegative; the compatibility condition alone imposes no such inequalities.
