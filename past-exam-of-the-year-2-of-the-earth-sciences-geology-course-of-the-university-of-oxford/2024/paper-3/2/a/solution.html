A gravity anomaly is the difference between observed gravitational acceleration and a specified reference prediction after defined reductions. A free-air gravity anomaly, a Bouguer gravity anomaly, and a residual local gravity anomaly differ because they remove different reference contributions. State the reference ellipsoid, datum, reference density, and regional trend before interpreting an anomaly as a subsurface geophysical density contrast.
On land, tie gravimeter observations to a gravity base, calibrate them, correct instrumental drift and Earth tides, and determine station latitude and elevation accurately. Subtract latitude-dependent normal gravity γ(φ). Reduce to the reference elevation using a positive free-air correction for a station above that datum: about +0.3086h mGal for h in metres. A simple Bouguer gravity anomaly then subtracts attraction of the intervening rock slab and adds the conventional terrain correction:
ΔgB​≃gobs,corr​−γ(φ)+0.3086h−2πGρref​h+Cterrain​,
(8)
with all acceleration terms expressed consistently. In SI units 1 mGal=10−5 ms−2.
At sea, also correct vessel-motion effects, especially the Eötvös correction from east–west vessel motion and Earth rotation, and vertical ship acceleration; average or filter the moving-platform data appropriately. GPS navigation and tide information specify position and measurement height. Sea-surface measurements give a marine free-air gravity anomaly after motion, latitude, tide and height reductions. A marine Bouguer gravity anomaly additionally allows for measured bathymetry and the replacement of low-density water by reference rock; the simple water-column replacement correction is positive, 2πG(ρref​−ρw​)H, relative to the sea-surface free-air value. A consistent three-dimensional bathymetric correction is preferable near irregular coasts. Remove a justified broad regional field if the intended quantity is a residual anomaly of the local body.
