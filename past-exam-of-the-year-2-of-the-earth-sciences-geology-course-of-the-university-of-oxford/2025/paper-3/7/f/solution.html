Diffusion smooths and broadens the hot spot while conserving its integral. For an isolated two-dimensional initial profile u0​=e−r2/a, the Gaussian hot spot under diffusion is
u(r,t)=a+4αta​exp[−a+4αtr2​].
(35)
Here a=0.1cm2 and t=10s, giving a width parameter of 0.5cm2 and a peak of 0.20 in the initial temperature units. The periodic-grid calculation agrees to displayed precision because the periodic copies are far away. Running the endpoint-inclusive listing as written instead gives a sampled initial peak near 0.9695 and final peak near 0.1963; the difference is consistent with the coordinate-grid issue, rather than a different diffusion law.
Increasing positive thermal diffusivity at fixed time produces a wider, lower peak. Each nonconstant Fourier mode decays faster, while the spatial mean of a periodic heat equation remains fixed. The profiles approach a uniform mean at long times under these periodic boundary conditions.
