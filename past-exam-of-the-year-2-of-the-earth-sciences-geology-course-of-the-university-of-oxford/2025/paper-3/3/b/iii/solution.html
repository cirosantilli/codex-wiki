Pore fluid pressure P reduces the compressive load carried across grain contacts or an accessible fault. In the usual elementary approximation,
σ′=σc−PI,σi′​=σi​−P,σn′​=σn​−P,τ′=τ.
(21)
At fixed total stress, increasing P translates the entire Mohr circle left by P without changing its radius or the differential stress. The circle therefore approaches the frictional failure envelope even though the applied shear has not increased. Equivalently, in total-stress coordinates the strength on an existing plane is c+μf​(σn​−P), reduced by μf​P. For a known orientation and constant frictional parameters, the ideal critical pressure is
Pcrit​=σn​−μf​∣τ∣−c​,
(22)
provided the plane is in contact and sliding failure is reached before tensile opening.
For the diagram's illustrative σ1​=80, σ3​=20, c=10 MPa and μf​=0.6, the dry circle is below failure. The freely oriented tangency threshold is about P=8.4 MPa; the 15 MPa circle is already beyond that threshold and shows the loading tendency, not a stable post-failure state. If P grows enough that σ3​−P≤−T0​, fluid pressure can instead open a tensile fracture. A porous-continuum effective stress law may use σi′​=σi​−αP with a coefficient α different from one. Also, changing fluid pressure can change total stress through constrained deformation; pure horizontal translation assumes those total stresses remain fixed.[ref]
