Chlorophyll a occurs in nearly all oxygen-producing phytoplankton groups and is central to their photosynthesis. Its concentration can be measured reproducibly by pigment extraction or calibrated optical methods, and its absorption affects ocean colour, making it observable over large areas. It is therefore a practical common index of photosynthetic standing stock across diverse communities, unlike a pigment limited to one group.
It is a proxy, not a fixed conversion to phytoplankton biomass or primary production. The carbon-to-chlorophyll ratio varies with species, light acclimation, nutrient supply and physiological condition; low-light cells often contain more pigment per unit carbon. Dead pigment and methodological differences can also affect interpretation, and nonphotosynthetic plankton are not represented. A pigment increase need not mean the same proportional increase in living carbon biomass.[ref]
