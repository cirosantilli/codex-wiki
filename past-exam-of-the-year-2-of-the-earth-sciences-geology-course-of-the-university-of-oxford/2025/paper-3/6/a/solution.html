The principal non-water visible-light absorbers can be grouped into phytoplankton pigments, coloured dissolved organic matter (CDOM), and non-algal particles (NAP), including organic detritus and absorbing mineral material. A useful additive decomposition of the absorption coefficient is
a(λ)=aw​(λ)+aph​(λ)+aCDOM​(λ)+aNAP​(λ).
(28)
The supplied sketch separates the three non-water contributions; its amplitudes are arbitrary and are not concentrations or measured spectra.
Phytoplankton pigments give prominent absorption maxima around 440 and 675 nm, associated with chlorophyll a, and a relative minimum in the green. Accessory pigments, including carotenoids and phycobilins, broaden or add absorption bands, so an assemblage is not just a pure chlorophyll-a solution. Intracellular packaging and cell size also modify the shape and absorption per unit pigment.
Coloured dissolved organic matter consists of dissolved light-absorbing organic compounds, often including humic material from terrestrial runoff and marine degradation. Its absorption is strongest at the blue/ultraviolet end and declines approximately exponentially toward red, without the narrow chlorophyll red peak. A common local parameterization is ag​(λ)=ag​(λ0​)e−Sg​(λ−λ0​) with positive spectral slope Sg​; here Sg​ is an optical slope, not gas saturation.
Non-algal particles include nonliving organic detritus, pigment degradation products and absorbing suspended mineral particles. Their aggregate absorption often also decreases approximately exponentially from blue to red, with variable slope and mineral-dependent departures. Suspended sediments can strongly scatter as well as absorb; bright sediment-laden water is not evidence of a red absorption peak. Chlorophyll-containing living cells belong in aph​, so these categories should not be double counted.[ref]
