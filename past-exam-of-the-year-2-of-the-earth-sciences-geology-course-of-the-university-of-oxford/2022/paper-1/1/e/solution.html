The along-strike rupture time is L/vr​, where vr​ is rupture velocity. Taking a representative crustal vr​≃3kms−1 gives about 2 s for a 6 km unilateral finite-source rupture. A different assumed rupture velocity changes this estimate. The local slip rise time at one patch is not specified and need not equal the whole-fault rupture time.
For comparable distance and ground conditions, the eastern observer lies in the forward rupture directivity direction. Radiation from successive patches arrives closer together, producing a shorter, more concentrated and generally larger-amplitude ground-velocity pulse. The western observer receives the contributions spread over a longer interval, generally with less concentrated shaking. This concerns pulse duration and amplitude; it does not imply an equally large permanent displacement at the eastern site.[ref]
For distant observers approximately along strike, an elementary seismic travel time construction gives
ΔtE​≃L(vr​1​−β1​),ΔtW​≃L(vr​1​+β1​),
(3)
where β is the shear-wave velocity. With vr​=3 and β=3.5kms−1, these propagation contributions are about 0.29 and 3.71 s, respectively; finite local rise time broadens them. Double-couple radiation pattern, receiver geometry and local site response can change the observed amplitudes, so location east or west alone is not an unconditional intensity prediction.
