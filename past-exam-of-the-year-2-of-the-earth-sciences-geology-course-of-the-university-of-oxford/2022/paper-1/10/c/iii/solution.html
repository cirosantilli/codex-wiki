Successive jumps occur at times separated by T=6 s. Between −3 and 3 s the plotted function is f(t)=2−t, periodically extended, with maximum 5 and minimum −1. Its mean is therefore 2 m, and f(t)−2=−t is an odd function over that centered period.
For the Fourier series convention
f(t)=2a0​​+∑r=1∞​[ar​cosT2πrt​+br​sinT2πrt​],
(54)
a0​=4 m and ar​=0 for every r≥1; the nonconstant content consists of sine terms. If a0​ denotes the mean directly in the alternative convention f=a0​+∑⋯, its value is 2 m. Stating the convention resolves the otherwise ambiguous notation.
For completeness, integrating −t against the sine basis gives br​=6(−1)r/(πr) m. At a jump the Fourier series converges to the midpoint 2 m. The nonzero mean does not destroy the oddness argument for the mean-subtracted function; declaring the original function itself odd would incorrectly force its mean to vanish.
