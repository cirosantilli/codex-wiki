For an approximately isotropic elastic solid,
VP​=ρK+4μ/3​​,VS​=ρμ​​,
(14)
where K is bulk modulus, μ is shear modulus and ρ is mass density. Five relevant controls are:
  • Mineral composition and texture: different elastic moduli and densities give different seismic velocities; compliant clay-rich material generally differs from crystalline igneous framework rock.
  • Porosity, cracks and fractures: compliant voids reduce the effective moduli and usually reduce both speeds; aligned fractures produce seismic anisotropy.
  • Effective confinement: increasing effective stress closes compliant cracks and tends to raise speeds. Higher pore fluid pressure at fixed total confinement can reopen or preserve cracks and lower the effective stiffness.
  • Fluid saturation and phase: replacing gas by liquid commonly increases the saturated bulk modulus and VP​; fluids have no static shear rigidity, so VS​ changes through the frame, density and frequency-dependent interactions rather than a direct fluid shear modulus.
  • Temperature and alteration: heating commonly reduces elastic moduli and may create thermal cracks; hydrothermal alteration can replace strong minerals by clay or other weaker phases. The sign depends on alteration products and crack sealing, so reservoir-specific measurements are needed.
These controls interact: a dense rock is not automatically faster if its moduli are also different.
