Let a positive incident stress wavelet be w(t) and measure return times relative to a receiver just above the reservoir. The top interface gives a negative P return of amplitude −3/19 and no basal P return. For a separately generated and measured S wave, the top return is also negative, −3/19, while the basal return is positive, 1/9 at the lower interface.
If h is reservoir thickness, the basal return arrives 2h/VP2​ or 2h/VS2​ after the top return; no numerical h is supplied. Including downward and upward transmission through the top boundary, but ignoring seismic attenuation, spreading and seismic multiples, its observed S-wave amplitude is

Thus the synthetic S trace has an earlier negative wavelet and a later, smaller positive wavelet; the synthetic P trace has only the earlier negative wavelet. The figure chooses h=250 m solely to separate the pulses visually. Receiver electronics or a displacement convention can reverse the displayed trace polarity, so the convention must accompany the sketch.
A marine hydrophone streamer with a purely normal-incidence P source does not directly record the hypothetical S trace. A seabed multicomponent receiver and suitable S excitation or oblique-incidence seismic mode conversion are required. This distinguishes the calculated solid-layer response from what a particular acquisition system can measure.
