Retain the same regional-background assumption as in part (b). The northern pluton is about 2.0 km thick beneath the station near the northern trough, while its gravity anomaly is about −114 mGal. Its anomaly relative to the greenstone maximum is approximately −14 mGal. Therefore the contrast equation is
−14×10−5=2πG{(ρgr​−2670)(2000)−(ρgs​−2670)(1500)}.
(11)
Using the result of part (b), the second term contributes 15 mGal, leaving a net pluton anomaly of about +1 mGal. Hence ρgr​≃2680 kgm−3, effectively close to the host-rock density at the precision of this diagram.
The apparent trough is strongly negative relative to the greenstone belt, but hardly negative relative to the southern host-rock background. If a northward regional trend is intended instead, it must be stated: in general,
ρgr​≃2670+2πG(2000)[−114−gbg,N​]10−5​,
(12)
with both gravity values in milligals. For example, a northern background of −109 mGal would give ρgr​≃2610 kgm−3, but the same reference would also change part (b). No regional curve is explicitly identified in the supplied figure, so the numerical result must retain its reference-field assumption. This is the geological nonuniqueness of geophysical inversion of potential-field interpretation, rather than a reason to force a presumed textbook granite density onto the plot.
