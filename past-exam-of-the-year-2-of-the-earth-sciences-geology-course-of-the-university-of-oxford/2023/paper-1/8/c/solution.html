Apply upper-envelope stability for competing mineral dissolution reactions with ℓG​=10.53−0.6x and ℓK​=12.228−0.2x. For each chlorite dissolution reaction,
RTln10Δr​GG​​=5(y−ℓG​),RTln10Δr​GK​​=5(y−ℓK​).
(39)
Therefore high y suppresses dissolution and stabilizes chlorite. Low y favors the alteration products. To compare those products, the gibbsite-to-kaolinite reaction has Δr​G/(RTln10)=−2x−8.49: kaolinite is favored at x>−4.245, and gibbsite at x<−4.245.
The stable fields are consequently chlorite above y=max(ℓG​,ℓK​); gibbsite below ℓG​ for x<−4.245; and kaolinite below ℓK​ for x>−4.245. The stable chlorite–gibbsite boundary is the left-hand part x≤−4.245, and the stable chlorite–kaolinite boundary is the right-hand part x≥−4.245. Their opposite extensions are metastable because the third mineral is more stable. The vertical gibbsite–kaolinite boundary is stable below y=13.077 and metastable above it.
The figure displays the stable upper envelope. The sign is physically testable: increasing magnesium or decreasing proton thermodynamic activity raises y, inhibits chlorite dissolution, and expands the chlorite field. This direct Gibbs energy comparison avoids inferring stability merely from where three algebraic lines meet.
