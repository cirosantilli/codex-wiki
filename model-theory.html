Finite model property

Words: 32
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A logic or fragment has the finite model property if every satisfiable sentence has a finite model. This does not require every model to be finite, or a uniform bound on all finite model sizes.

Finite model theory

Words: 298 Articles: 4
Model theory OurBigBook.com
Finite model theory studies logical definability and equivalence on finite first-order structures. Unlike unrestricted model theory, first-order compactness is unavailable when models are required to be finite. Locality, bounded-variable games and descriptive characterizations of complexity classes are central tools.

Asymptotic equivalence of formulas

Words: 63
Finite model theory OurBigBook.com
Two formulas with the same free variables are asymptotically equivalent when the probability of their universal equivalence tends to one over the chosen finite-structure distribution. If a quantifier-eliminating extension theory has all its axioms of asymptotic probability one, compactness makes every formula asymptotically equivalent to a quantifier-free one: each particular equivalence follows from finitely many axioms, whose conjunction still has limiting probability one.

Partial fixed-point logic (PFP)

Words: 67
Finite model theory OurBigBook.com
Extend first-order logic by iterating definable relation operators from the empty relation. If iteration reaches a fixed point, use that relation; if it enters a nontrivial cycle, the partial fixed point is the empty relation. For a fixed arity on a finite universe, a relation requires polynomially many bits and there are exponentially many states, so iteration is evaluable in PSPACE. On finite ordered structures this logic captures PSPACE.

Gaifman graph

Words: 132 Articles: 1
Finite model theory OurBigBook.com
For a relational structure, the Gaifman graph has the same universe. Two distinct elements are adjacent if they occur together in some tuple of some interpreted relation. A tuple's radius-r neighbourhood is the induced structure on the union of its radius-r graph balls, with the tuple marked by constants.

Hanf locality lemma

Words: 81
Gaifman graph OurBigBook.com
For a finite relational signature and quantifier rank q, matching the numbers of all marked radius-3q one-point neighbourhood types suffices for two finite structures to satisfy the same first-order sentences of rank at most q. Equivalently, a bijection can match the isomorphism types of these marked neighbourhoods point by point. On bounded-degree structures, a threshold version permits large neighbourhood-type counts to differ once both exceed a fixed bound. Long cycles of equal total size give useful exact-count applications.

Recursive presentation of a structure

Words: 95 Articles: 1
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A recursive presentation identifies the domain of a countable structure with a computable set, with its basic operations given by total computable functions and its basic relations decidable. In a fixed finite signature this makes evaluation of atomic formulas effective. A countable structure may have a recursive presentation of a structure only for some choices of presentation, or none at all.

Computable isomorphism

Words: 45
Recursive presentation of a structure OurBigBook.com
A computable isomorphism is an isomorphism between presented structures whose underlying map is a total computable function. For presentations on N, its inverse is computable too: enumerate inputs until the required output occurs. Two abstractly isomorphic structures with a recursive presentation of a structure need not admit a computable isomorphism between the chosen presentations.

Algebraic theory

Words: 80 Articles: 1
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A finitary algebraic theory specifies sorts, finite-arity operations and equations between terms. Its internal models can be interpreted in any category with finite products. Finitely presented set-based models generate arbitrary models under filtered colimits and determine its classifying topos.

Finitely presented models of an algebraic theory (Tfp​)

Words: 42
Algebraic theory OurBigBook.com
A finitely presented model is described by finitely many generators and finitely many equations. Model homomorphisms from it commute with filtered colimits. A small skeleton of these models is equivalent to the opposite of the finite-equation part of the algebraic syntactic category.

Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game (Gn​(M,N))

Words: 257 Articles: 3
Model theory OurBigBook.com
In each round the Spoiler selects an element from either structure and the Duplicator responds in the other. The paired selected elements must preserve all atomic information and equality, giving a partial isomorphism of structures. In the infinite game the Duplicator wins by avoiding failure at every finite stage. In a timed game the Spoiler first announces the number of rounds, allowing a different response strategy for each horizon.

Interval threshold strategy for discrete linear orders (hk​=2k−1)

Words: 63
Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game OurBigBook.com
With k moves remaining, preserve corresponding gap sizes exactly when smaller than 2k−1, and otherwise require both gaps to have at least that many points. One move splits a gap; 2k−1=2(2k−1−1)+1 permits matching both smaller gaps. This strategy proves that (N,<) and (N+Z,<) agree at every finite quantifier rank despite the second order containing points with infinitely many predecessors.

Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé theorem

Words: 128 Articles: 1
Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game OurBigBook.com
For a finite relational first-order language, with finitely many constants allowed, the Duplicator wins the n-round game exactly when the structures are rank-n equivalent. Game-winning implies rank equivalence in arbitrary languages; the converse needs a finiteness condition and can fail even for an infinite unary vocabulary. Over a finite relational vocabulary, winning every announced finite horizon is equivalent to elementary equivalence.

Failure of finite-round game equivalence in infinite languages

Words: 69
Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé theorem OurBigBook.com
For countably many unary predicates Pi​, let M contain infinitely many copies of each finite membership pattern and let N add an element satisfying all predicates. Every finite vocabulary reduct has infinitely many elements in every Boolean cell in both structures, so M≡N. The Spoiler nevertheless wins the one-round full-language game by selecting the new element, whose complete atomic pattern has no counterpart in M.

Theory of formally real fields (FRF)

Words: 38
Model theory OurBigBook.com
The theory of formally real fields consists of the field axioms and all sentences asserting that −1 is not a sum of m squares, one for each positive integer m. The real closure theorem and model completeness make the Theory of real closed fields its model companion.

Theory of nontrivial torsion-free divisible Abelian groups (DAG)

Words: 197 Articles: 2
Model theory OurBigBook.com
This first-order theory has as models the nonzero torsion-free divisible Abelian groups. Rational division equips them with the structure of vector spaces over the rational numbers. A rational divisible hull gives an algebraically prime extension of each nonzero torsion-free base, while the zero base uses a one-dimensional rational space.

Quantifier elimination for torsion-free divisible Abelian groups

Words: 113
Theory of nontrivial torsion-free divisible Abelian groups OurBigBook.com
In a torsion-free divisible Abelian group, division by a nonzero integer is unique. The rational scalar operations are therefore a definitional expansion. Formulas reduce to Boolean combinations of rational linear equations: eliminate an existential variable in a conjunction by solving any equation with nonzero coefficient; if none fixes the variable, the finitely many nontrivial disequalities exclude only finitely many choices in an infinite group. Equations with zero coefficient reduce to parameter conditions.
Thus over a finite parameter set a complete one-variable type either specifies an element of its linear span, or excludes every element of that span. An uncountable rational vector space realizes the latter type because a finite-parameter span is countable. It is consequently an omega-saturated model in the additive language.

Theory of torsion-free divisible Abelian groups allowing the trivial group (DAG′)

Words: 51
Theory of nontrivial torsion-free divisible Abelian groups OurBigBook.com
Remove the nonzero-model axiom from DAG. The inclusion {0}⊆Q is then an inclusion of models but is not a simple closure, because x=0 has a witness only in the larger model. The theory does not have quantifier elimination: all closed group terms are zero, so no quantifier-free sentence distinguishes the two models.

Algebraically prime extension (j=h∘i)

Words: 56
Model theory OurBigBook.com
For a first-order theory T and A⊨T∀​, an algebraically prime extension is a structure embedding i:A→P⊨T such that every embedding j:A→N⊨T factors as j=h∘i for some structure embedding h:P→N. The embeddings need not be elementary or unique. A theory has algebraically prime models when every such A has an extension of this kind.

Reduced product (∏i∈I​Mi​/F)

Words: 54
Model theory OurBigBook.com
Given nonempty first-order structures Mi​ and a proper filter on a set F, a reduced product identifies product functions f,g when {i:f(i)=g(i)}∈F. Operations are interpreted coordinatewise, and relations hold when their coordinate truth sets belong to F. For an ultrafilter this is an ultraproduct. Primitive positive formulas transfer through an arbitrary proper filter, whereas logical disjunction and logical negation can fail to transfer.

Elementary equivalence (M≡N)

Words: 27
Model theory OurBigBook.com
Two first-order structures are elementarily equivalent when they satisfy the same first-order sentences. They need not be isomorphic or embed into one another. Every elementary embedding implies elementary equivalence of its source and target.

Complete theory

Words: 847 Articles: 14
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A consistent first-order theory is complete when it decides every sentence in its language.

Theory of a free bijection (TZ​)

Words: 69
Complete theory OurBigBook.com
The first-order theory of a bijection f with named inverse g and no finite cycles has first-order models consisting of disjoint integer orbits. Atomic logical formulas reduce to xj​=fk(xi​) for integer k. An existential logical variable is either fixed by such a logical equality or must avoid finitely many points. This proves quantifier elimination and completeness. There is one complete one-type; the complete two-types are the integer offsets and the nonprincipal different-orbit partial type. There are countably many finite-arity partial types but countable first-order models with different numbers of orbits are not isomorphic.

Theory of an infinite pure set (T∞​)

Words: 57
Complete theory OurBigBook.com
In the first-order language of logical equality, assert that there are at least n elements for every positive integer n. Eliminating one existential logical variable either substitutes a logical equality or avoids finitely many points, so this first-order theory has quantifier elimination and is complete. It is categorical in every infinite cardinal. Its complete n-types are the logical equality partitions of n coordinates and are counted by the Bell numbers.

Theory of successor (TS​=Th(N,s,0))

Words: 316 Articles: 1
Complete theory OurBigBook.com
The complete theory of the standard successor structure is axiomatized by injectivity of s, 0 outside its range, a predecessor for every nonzero element, and the sentences sn(x)=x for each positive integer n. Its models have a single distinguished one-sided chain generated by zero and any collection of two-sided integer chains. The classification of models of the successor theory proves completeness and explains why the theory is categorical at every uncountable cardinal but not at the countable cardinal.

Classification of models of the successor theory (N⊔⨆i∈I​Zi​)

Words: 250
Theory of successor OurBigBook.com
In a model of the axioms for the theory of successor, repeatedly taking successor and predecessor partitions the domain into components. Injectivity and the predecessor axiom leave only the zero-generated one-sided chain and two-sided chains; the no-cycle axioms exclude finite components. Choosing one origin in each remaining component gives the displayed structure.
These axioms have quantifier elimination. To eliminate one existential quantification, put its quantifier-free matrix in disjunctive normal form. In a conjunction, an equality between a successor iterate of the bound variable and a parameter term fixes at most one candidate, by injectivity. Existence of the finitely many necessary predecessors excludes only finitely many initial zero iterates; the other equalities and inequalities then translate to conditions on the parameters by cancellation of successor iterates. If there is no such fixing equality, only finitely many candidates are excluded, so some point on the infinite zero chain works. Equalities between two different iterates of the same variable are false by absence of cycles. Thus the existential formula reduces to a quantifier-free formula.
Every closed quantifier-free sentence is decided by the distinct zero iterates, proving completeness. A one-variable formula over the empty set is a Boolean combination of finitely many equalities x=sn(0), modulo this theory. All points in added integer chains therefore realize the same nonisolated complete type over the empty set. At an uncountable cardinal κ a model has exactly κ integer components, proving categoricity there. A countable model with infinitely many integer components is an omega-saturated model because finite parameters involve only finitely many components, leaving a fresh one for every new-component type.

Categorical theory (κ-categorical)

Words: 391 Articles: 9
Complete theory OurBigBook.com
A theory is κ-categorical when all of its models of cardinality κ are isomorphic.

No categorical cardinal for the complete named rational diagram

Words: 95
Categorical theory OurBigBook.com
For any infinite cardinal κ, insert a dense order without endpoints of size κ into an irrational cut of the named rationals. Compare it with an order of the same size having a dense tail above every named rational. Both models satisfy the complete named rational diagram by quantifier elimination, but only the second realizes the type of being above every named rational. This is an isomorphism invariant over the constants, even though it is an infinite type rather than one defining sentence. Thus the diagram is categorical in no cardinal with a model.

Infinite-cardinal categoricity forces completeness

Words: 58
Categorical theory OurBigBook.com
If a theory without finite models is categorical in an infinite cardinal at least the language size, it is complete. Otherwise its two consistent extensions deciding a sentence oppositely have infinite models. The Löwenheim-Skolem theorems produce models of both of the specified cardinality, contradicting their forced isomorphism. Categoricity includes existence at that cardinality, so it is not vacuous.

Morley categoricity theorem

Words: 29
Categorical theory OurBigBook.com
This theorem is the uncountable categoricity theorem for a categorical theory. If a countable first-order theory is categorical in one uncountable cardinal, then it is categorical in every uncountable cardinal.

Reduct need not preserve uncountable categoricity

Let a language partition the universe into two infinite unary predicates and include a bijection between them. Its complete theory is categorical in every infinite cardinal. The reduct forgetting the bijection is not categorical in any uncountable cardinal, because the two infinite parts can have different cardinalities.

Compactness gives a countable model elementarily equivalent to (Z,+,0) that contains a nonzero element divisible by every positive standard integer. It is not isomorphic to Z, so Th(Z,+,0) is not aleph-zero-categorical.

The complete theory of infinite-dimensional vector spaces over a fixed finite field is aleph-zero-categorical: every countably infinite model has countably infinite dimension and is therefore isomorphic to every other such model.

The method of diagrams adds the diagram or elementary diagram of a structure to a theory and applies compactness. A resulting model supplies an embedding or elementary embedding of the named structure.

Existential-theory embedding criterion (T∪Th∃​(A) satisfiable⟺A↪B⊨T)

Words: 120 Articles: 1
Method of diagrams OurBigBook.com
A finite conjunction from the signed atomic diagram of A becomes, after existentially closing its new names, a sentence in its existential theory. Joint satisfiability with T therefore makes the full diagram finitely satisfiable with T. Compactness gives the embedding, with inequalities ensuring injectivity and signed relation literals ensuring reflection. The reverse implication is preservation of existential truth under embeddings.
Universal theory and substructures of elementarily equivalent models (T∪Th∀​(A) satisfiable⟺B⊨T, B⊆A∗≡A)
Words: 56
Existential-theory embedding criterion OurBigBook.com
If B satisfies the universal theory of A, every existential sentence true in B is true in A, since its falsehood in A would supply a universal negation. Apply the existential-theory embedding criterion to B and the complete theory of A to obtain A∗. Conversely universal truth descends to substructures. The elementarily equivalent model need not be the original structure itself.

Diagram embedding criterion for universal theories (A⊨T∀​⟺A↪N⊨T)

Words: 53
Method of diagrams OurBigBook.com
A first-order structure A embeds into a model of T exactly when A⊨T∀​. For the nontrivial direction apply the compactness theorem to T∪Diag(A). If a finite diagram conjunction were inconsistent with T, its universal negation after replacing the new constants by variables would belong to T∀​ and contradict its truth in A.

If a type p(x) over a structure M is finitely satisfiable in M, then the elementary diagram of M together with p(c) is finitely satisfiable. Compactness gives an elementary extension of M realizing p.

Inductive first-order theory

Words: 40
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A first-order theory is inductive when unions of chains of its models under embeddings remain models. A theory is inductive exactly when it has an axiomatization by sentences of the form ∀xˉ∃yˉ​φ(xˉ,yˉ​) with quantifier-free φ.

Model-complete theory

Words: 236 Articles: 5
Model theory OurBigBook.com
A theory is model-complete when every embedding between its models is elementary. Every model-complete theory is inductive and hence admits a forall-exists axiomatization.

Model companion (T∗)

Words: 53
Model-complete theory OurBigBook.com
A model companion of T is a model-complete theory T∗ in the same language with T∀∗​=T∀​. Equivalently each model of either theory embeds into a model of the other. Alternating these embeddings and using the elementary chain theorem proves uniqueness up to logical equivalence. A model companion need not be a syntactic extension of its original theory.

The theory of real closed fields is complete and model-complete. In the language of ordered rings it eliminates quantifiers; in the pure language of rings its definable order prevents quantifier elimination.

Quantifier elimination for ordered real closed fields

Words: 42

In the language of ordered rings, the Theory of real closed fields has quantifier elimination. In its pure ring reduct the order is definable by nonzero squares, so model completeness survives, although quantifier elimination does not. Keeping these two language conventions separate is essential when constructing a model companion.

Non-Archimedean real closed field

Words: 32
Theory of real closed fields OurBigBook.com
A non-Archimedean real closed field contains a positive element larger than every standard integer. Compactness constructs one by adjoining a constant c and the formulas c>n for all natural numbers n.

Model-theoretic proof of Hilbert's seventeenth problem

Words: 49

Model completeness of real closed fields transfers positivity of a rational function from R to real closed extensions of R. If the function were not a sum of squares, the Artin-Schreier ordering criterion would produce a real closure in which it is negative, giving a contradiction.

A sequence (ai​)i∈I​ indexed by a total order is order-indiscernible when any two increasing finite subsequences of the same length satisfy the same first-order formulas.

Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski theorem

Words: 293 Articles: 2
Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski model OurBigBook.com
Let M be an infinite first-order structure and I a total order. After choosing a Skolem expansion of M, an elementary extension contains distinct elements (ai​)i∈I​ forming an order-indiscernible sequence in that expanded language. Their Skolem hull is an elementary substructure in the expanded language, and its reduct is a first-order model of Th(M). Every order automorphism of I extends uniquely to a structure automorphism of the hull preserving the chosen Skolem expansion, by transporting terms in the generators.
