KKT regularity at a local minimum

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
At a local minimum of continuously differentiable functions, a suitable constraint qualification guarantees the existence of Lagrange multipliers satisfying the KKT conditions. One sufficient local regularity assumption is that the gradients of the equality optimization constraints and the active constraints are jointly linearly independent. This assumption concerns the description of the feasible set, not just the smoothness of the objective function.[ref]
To see why a hypothesis is needed, minimize f(x)=x subject to g(x)=x2≤0. The feasible set is the singleton {0}, so x∗​=0 is a global minimum. Yet f′(0)+λg′(0)=1 for every finite Lagrange multiplier λ. Thus this convex optimization problem has an attained optimum without a KKT multiplier. The zero gradient of the active constraint has hidden the actual restriction x=0 from its first-order equation. Rewriting it as the equality h(x)=x=0 yields the unrestricted Lagrange multiplier μ=−1.
For convex optimization, Slater's condition requires strict inequalities and affine equalities at a point in the relative interior of the common domain of a function. A finite optimum value then gives strong duality and dual attainment. Primal attainment and differentiability yield KKT multipliers. Affine inequalities may be nonstrict in the refined version.[ref]
Slater's condition is sufficient, and need not hold for an optimum to exist. The singular example above also shows that mere primal attainment is not a substitute for a constraint qualification.
