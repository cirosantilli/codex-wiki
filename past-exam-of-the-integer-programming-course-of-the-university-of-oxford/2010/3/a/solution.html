The retained integer assignment constraints have only two feasible matrices,
D=(10​01​),O=(01​10​).
(7)
Their (a⋅x,b⋅x,c⋅x) totals are (4,5,10) and (3,6,8) respectively. For nonnegative Lagrange multipliers λ,μ, add λ(3.5−a⋅x)+μ(5.5−b⋅x) to the maximization objective. Each feasible point gains a nonnegative amount, so maximizing over the retained assignment set gives an upper bound:
θ(λ,μ)=max{10−2λ​+2μ​,8+2λ​−2μ​}.
(8)
The Lagrangian dual problem minimizes this convex piecewise-linear function over λ,μ≥0. Put d=λ−μ; every real d can occur. The two affine terms average to nine, so their maximum is at least nine, with equality exactly when d=2. Thus
λ,μ≥0min​θ=9,(λ,μ)=(2+t,t),t≥0.​
(9)
Neither integer assignment satisfies both side constraints: D violates the a bound and O the b bound. The finite dual bound is therefore not evidence that the integer primal is feasible.
