Use the minimization form in this part. Treat every diagonal arc as forbidden, not as a zero-cost loop. The assignment relaxation of the travelling salesman problem requires one incoming and one outgoing arc at each city but permits several disjoint directed cycles. Its optimum is a lower bound on the tour optimum.
At the root choose
u=v=(21​,21​,1,1,21​).
(19)
For every allowed arc, ui​+vj​≤cij​. Their total is seven. The assignment
(1→2→5→1)and(3→4→3)
(20)
uses only tight arcs and has cost 1+1+1+2+2=7. The minimization certificate from part (c) therefore proves
zAP,root​=7.​
(21)
It is not a tour, because it has a three-cycle and a two-cycle.
Branch on the two-cycle. Every five-city tour omits at least one of 3→4 and 4→3, so the children x34​=0 and x43​=0 cover every possible tour. They may overlap, which does not invalidate the bounds.
For the child x34​=0, use
u=(21​,21​,25​,1,21​),v=(21​,21​,1,25​,21​).
(22)
These satisfy ui​+vj​≤cij​ on every allowed arc of that child and sum to ten. The tour
1→2→5→4→3→1​
(23)
has cost 1+1+3+2+3=10 and uses tight arcs, certifying that the child assignment bound is exactly ten.
For the child x43​=0, interchange the roles of cities three and four in those potentials. Its lower bound is again ten, attained by
1→3→4→5→2→1​
(24)
with cost 3+2+3+1+1=10. Thus both first-level assignment optima are already tours.
There is a useful direct interpretation of the improved bound. Split the cities into A={1,2,5} and B={3,4}. A forbidden arc of the two-cycle forces an assignment arc from B to A, and balance forces at least one from A to B. Relative to the root potentials, each crossing arc has reduced cost 3−3/2=3/2, so any child assignment costs at least 7+2(3/2)=10.
The first branching therefore completes the search: each child's lower bound equals a feasible tour value. Hence
zTSP​=10.​
