Use the maximum-weight version of Kruskal's algorithm: sort the edges by decreasing weight, start with no edges, and add an edge exactly when it joins different current connected components of a graph. For the maximum over all forests, skip negative-weight edges; removing a negative edge always preserves a forest and increases its weight. Zero-weight edges are optional. The matroid greedy algorithm for the graphic matroid proves this procedure optimal; it maximizes over independent sets rather than requiring a full basis.
The positive edges are considered in the order
12:9,15:8,25:7,23:4,35:3.
(2)
Admit 12 and 15. Reject 25, since it closes the triangle on 1,2,5. Admit 23. Reject 35, since 3 and 5 are already joined by the path 3,2,1,5. Skip both negative edges incident with vertex four. Therefore
F∗={12,15,23},w(F∗)=9+8+4=21.​
(3)
Vertex four is isolated. Requiring a spanning tree would force a negative edge and solve a different optimization problem. With a disjoint-set data structure, sorting dominates the running time, giving O(∣E∣log∣E∣).
