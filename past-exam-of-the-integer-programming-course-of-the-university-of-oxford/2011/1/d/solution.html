The associated matroid is
M=(E,I),I={A⊆E:r(A)=∣A∣}.​
(5)
Thus the graph's edges are the ground set, and the members of I are its matroid independent sets.
For the specified rank, if (V,A) is a forest, A itself is an allowed choice in the rank maximum, so r(A)=∣A∣. Conversely, if r(A)=∣A∣, some forest F⊆A has ∣F∣=∣A∣. Finiteness forces F=A, so (V,A) is a forest. Hence the independent sets are exactly all forests in G, including the empty forest and forests with isolated vertices.
The matroid independence axioms can also be checked directly. The empty set is a forest, and deleting edges cannot create a cycle. For exchange, let forests I,J have ∣I∣<∣J∣. If every edge of J joined vertices within one component of (V,I), its restriction to each component S would have at most ∣S∣−1 edges. Summing would give ∣J∣≤∣I∣, a contradiction. Thus some edge of J∖I joins two components of I and can be added without creating a cycle. This proves augmentation and identifies M as the graphic matroid.
