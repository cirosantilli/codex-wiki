Cell wall

 Home Biology Biological cell
OurBigBook.com Words: 954 Articles: 9
A cell wall is a supporting structure outside a cell membrane. It helps preserve shape and bears the load when osmosis raises internal pressure. The cell membrane supplies the selective permeability described in its own article; the cell wall supplies mechanical reinforcement. These functions cooperate: a porous supporting layer can restrain expansion without being the boundary that retains dissolved solutes. Turgor pressure makes this cooperation visible in a firm leaf; plasmolysis and a lysozyme wall-removal experiment separate the two functions experimentally.[ref]
The material is not universal. Most bacteria use peptidoglycan, whose linked sugar chains and amino acid cross-links are explained below. A plant cell wall uses cellulose; a fungal cell wall contains chitin and glucans. Animals lack a cell wall, some bacteria are naturally wall-free, and archaea have different wall chemistry. Thus a cell wall is neither a universal cellular component nor one chemical recipe.[ref][ref][ref]
The bacterial outer membrane in typical Gram-negative bacteria is an additional membrane outside the peptidoglycan, not another name for that supporting mesh. The cell envelope includes the boundary layers together. An extra barrier can change access to the peptidoglycan, so response to lysozyme cannot be inferred from the mere presence of a cell wall.[ref]
