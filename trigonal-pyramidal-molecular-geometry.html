Trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 191
In trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry, three neighbouring chemical atoms form a triangular base and the central chemical atom lies outside its plane. The pyramid has four atomic nuclei; its central chemical atom is the apex, not the centre of the base. Ammonia, NH3​, has this arrangement. The [ref] lists an H–N–H bond angle of 106.67∘, commonly rounded to 106.7∘ or about 107∘.
The Lewis structure of Ammonia allocates eight valence electrons as three bond pairs and one lone pair. Four electron domains therefore suggest tetrahedral electron pair geometry; removing the lone pair marker leaves three hydrogen chemical atoms below nitrogen. The VSEPR model treats the lone pair as more space-demanding and predicts compression relative to 109.4712∘, but it does not calculate the precise 106.67∘ value. See Chemistry 2e, §7.6: lone-pair effects.
Three equal bond-dipole contributions in this pyramid cancel sideways, but retain a component along its axis. By contrast, three equal contributions lying in one plane at 120∘ would cancel completely. This shows how a small change in molecular geometry can permit a permanent electric dipole moment. Ammonia is a polar molecule; NIST lists a measured magnitude of about 1.476 debye in the same [ref]. The vector argument predicts allowed direction and cancellation, not the full measured magnitude.
