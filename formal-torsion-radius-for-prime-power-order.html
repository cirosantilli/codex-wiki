Formal torsion radius for prime power order (∣z∣≥∣p∣1/(pn−pn−1))

 ... Algebraic geometry Normalization of an algebraic curve Geometric genus Genus one curve Elliptic curve Formal group law
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
Let a one-dimensional formal group law be defined over the valuation ring of a complete discretely valued non-Archimedean valued field of characteristic zero and residue characteristic p. If z in the maximal ideal has exact formal order pn, its absolute value satisfies the displayed lower bound. Use the coefficient splitting [p](T)=pf(T)+g(Tp), where f(T)=T+O(T2) and g(0)=0. On the maximal ideal, ∣f(z)∣=∣z∣ and ∣g(zp)∣≤∣z∣p. If ∣z∣<∣p∣1/(p−1), the first term strictly dominates, so ∣[p]z∣=∣p∣∣z∣ and repeated multiplication never reaches zero. Every nonzero p-power torsion point is therefore at least that large.
On this remaining radius range, ∣[p]z∣≤∣z∣p. For n=1, cancellation in [p]z=0 gives ∣z∣p−1≥∣p∣. For larger n, apply induction to the point [p]z of order pn−1 and take a pth root. This yields the stated exponent. Over Qp​ for odd p, the radius already exceeds p−1, excluding torsion from the formal group on pZp​.
