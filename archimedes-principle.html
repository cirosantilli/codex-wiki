Archimedes' principle (Fb​=ρf​gVdisplaced​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Buoyancy
OurBigBook.com Words: 786 Articles: 3
Archimedes' principle states that buoyancy equals the downward gravitational weight, or weight, of the displaced fluid. In a resting fluid of constant mass density ρf​ under uniform gravitational acceleration g, its magnitude is
Fb​=ρf​gVdisplaced​.
(29)
Here Vdisplaced​ is the volume from which the body excludes the fluid: the whole external volume for a fully immersed solid body, and just the immersed volume for a partly immersed one. The body's own mass does not enter this buoyancy formula. [ref]
A short proof outline for a fully immersed body is to imagine replacing it by exactly the fluid it displaces. The surrounding hydrostatic pressure is unchanged. That replacement fluid is at rest, so its upward pressure force must balance its downward force from gravity. The original body receives the same inward pressure forces on the same closed surface, hence the same upward buoyancy.
Equivalently, let Ω be the displaced region, ∂Ω its closed surface and ez​ the upward unit direction. The divergence theorem, applied component by component to the surface integral, and hydrostatic pressure give
Fb​=−∫∂Ω​pndA=−∫Ω​∇pdV=ρf​gVdisplaced​ez​.
(30)
This also explains why horizontal forces cancel even for an irregular shape. A spatially constant surface pressure contributes zero to the closed-surface integral. If the mass density varies with depth, replace ρf​Vdisplaced​ by the mass ∫Ω​ρf​dV of the displaced fluid.
The closed-surface argument assumes that the fluid applies pressure over the entire boundary of a fully immersed body. A body sealing against a tank bottom may have no fluid beneath it; then the lower-face pressure force used in the proof is absent, and a supporting contact force must be treated separately. For partial immersion, the usual buoyancy formula assumes the exposed surface receives the common atmospheric pressure, neglects the much smaller buoyancy from the air, and neglects surface tension.
For the 0.10 m cube in the stipulated water, full immersion displaces 0.001 m3, so Fb​=1000×9.81×0.001=9.81 N. If its uniform mass density is 600 kgm−3, its mass is m=600×0.001=0.600 kg and its downward force from gravity is mg=5.886 N. To hold it fully immersed at rest requires an additional downward force
Fhold​=Fb​−mg=9.81−5.886=3.924 N.
(31)
On release from rest it initially moves upward; its eventual floating equilibrium has a smaller displaced volume. A same-size cube of uniform mass density 1200 kgm−3 has mass 1.200 kg and downward force from gravity 11.772 N. Full immersion still gives 9.81 N of buoyancy, leaving an initial net downward force of 11.772−9.81=1.962 N. Subsequent motion also involves drag.
