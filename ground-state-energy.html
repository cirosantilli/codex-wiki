Ground-state energy

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Ground state
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
The ground-state energy E0​ is the energy eigenvalue attained by a ground state. For a self-adjoint operator H bounded below, distinguish this attained value from the always-defined spectral bottom E∗​=infσ(H). When a ground state exists, E0​=E∗​. Some usage calls E∗​ the ground-state energy even when it is unattained; here an unattained bottom is explicitly identified as an infimum. The quantum variational principle states
E∗​=infψ∈D(H)∥ψ∥=1​​⟨ψ,Hψ⟩.
(55)
Approaching this infimum with trial wavefunctions does not ensure that a minimizing wavefunction exists. [ref].
For the whole-line free particle with mass m>0,
H=−2mℏ2​dx2d2​,σ(H)=[0,∞).
(56)
Its spectrum is continuous and contains no energy eigenvalues represented by normalizable wavefunctions. The Fourier transform makes H multiplication by ℏ2k2/(2m); a fixed energy restricts k to a set of zero length, on which a nonzero square-integrable function cannot be supported. [ref]. An explicit family of normalized Gaussian wave packets demonstrates how the bottom is approached:
ψs​(x)=(2πs2)−1/4e−x2/(4s2),⟨H⟩s​=8ms2ℏ2​⟶0(s⟶∞).
(57)
The formula follows from ψs′​=−xψs​/(2s2), integration by parts, and ⟨x2⟩s​=s2. Every finite width gives positive kinetic energy. At zero energy, Hψ=0 would require an affine function on the whole line, which is never a nonzero normalizable wavefunction. Spreading a Gaussian wave packet indefinitely produces an unattained infimum, rather than a zero-energy ground state.
An additive choice of energy origin changes the numerical ground-state energy without changing the ground-state subspace. For H′=H+CI, every energy eigenvalue becomes E′=E+C, while all differences Ej​−Ei​ stay fixed. Time evolution gains only the global phase e−iCt/ℏ. This makes excitation costs and spectroscopic differences more intrinsic than the isolated number E0​.
