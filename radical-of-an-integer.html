Radical of an integer (rad(n)=∏p∣n​p)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 418 Articles: 1
The radical of an integer n>0 is the product of its distinct prime factors, each included exactly once. If the prime factorization is n=p1a1​​⋯prar​​ with distinct prime numbers pi​ and positive integer exponents ai​, then
rad(n)=p1​⋯pr​=∏p∣n​p.
(1)
Thus the radical of an integer remembers which prime factors occur and forgets their prime valuations. At n=1, the empty product gives rad(1)=1. The domain of a function here is the positive integers; zero is excluded. [ref]
For example, 72=23⋅32 has distinct prime factors 2,3, so rad(72)=2⋅3=6. Similarly,
rad(30)=30,rad(32)=2,rad(36)=6,
(2)
using the prime factorizations 30=2⋅3⋅5, 32=25 and 36=22⋅32. The prime omega function counts the retained prime factors, while the total number of prime factors counts their occurrences before repetitions are removed.
The radical of an integer is its largest squarefree divisor. Indeed, a squarefree divisor chooses at most one copy of each prime factor, so it divides rad(n); the radical of an integer itself is a squarefree divisor. Consequently,
rad(n)∣n,rad(n)≤n,rad(n)=n ⟺ n is squarefree.
(3)
The last statement follows because every nonzero prime valuation is then one. It includes n=1, a squarefree integer. Applying the radical of an integer twice also changes nothing: rad(rad(n))=rad(n).
For any positive integers a,b, the exact product rule is
rad(ab)=rad(gcd(a,b))rad(a)rad(b)​.
(4)
To prove it, a prime factor dividing just one input occurs once in the numerator and not in the denominator. A prime factor dividing both occurs twice in the numerator and once in the denominator, since it divides their greatest common divisor. Each prime factor of ab therefore remains exactly once. For coprime integers, the denominator is rad(1)=1, proving the radical of an integer is a multiplicative function. It is not a completely multiplicative function: rad(4)=2=4=rad(2)2.
For a worked example with shared prime factors, take a=12 and b=18. Their greatest common divisor is 6, and ab=216=23⋅33, so
rad(216)=66⋅6​=6.
(5)
This explains why prime support of an integer is useful: questions about the presence of prime factors can discard their multiplicities. For example, the squarefree divisors of 72 are exactly the integer divisors of rad(72)=6, namely 1,2,3,6. Also gcd(m,n)=1 if and only if gcd(m,rad(n))=1, because coprimality depends only on shared prime factors.
A further characterization uses mutual divisibility of powers: for positive integers a,b,
rad(a)=rad(b)⟺there exist k,ℓ≥1 with a∣bk and b∣aℓ.
(6)
If their prime supports of an integer agree, sufficiently large k,ℓ make all the required prime valuations large enough. Conversely, these two divisibility relations force each prime factor of either input to divide the other. For 72 and 36, one can take k=2 and ℓ=1: 72∣362 and 36∣72.
