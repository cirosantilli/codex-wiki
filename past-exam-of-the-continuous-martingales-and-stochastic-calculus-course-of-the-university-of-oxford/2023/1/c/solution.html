For any real orthonormal basis (Gi​), the argument of (b)(ii) gives almost sure convergence and convergence in L2 of Wh. Finite linear combinations have normal distributions, and the Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis gives
E[(Wf)(Wg)]=∑i​⟨f,Gi​⟩⟨g,Gi​⟩=⟨f,g⟩.
(19)
Thus W and W are centered Gaussian processes with the same covariance function. More explicitly, their finite-dimensional characteristic functions are both
Eexp(ij=1∑m​uj​Whj​)=exp​−21​​j=1∑m​uj​hj​​H2​​.​
(20)
This proves the basis invariance of an isonormal Gaussian process: equality of all finite-dimensional distributions, and hence equality in law of the indexed collections.
For the specified cosine orthonormal basis, put λi​=π(i−21​). Its coefficient against an interval indicator is
⟨1[0,t]​,Gi​⟩=2​∫0t​cos(λi​s)ds=λi​2​sin(λi​t)​.
(21)
Consequently the Brownian half-integer sine expansion, a Karhunen–Loève expansion, is
Bt​=2​i=1∑∞​bi​π(i−21​)sin(π(i−21​)t)​.​
(22)
For every fixed t, the sum converges almost surely and in L2 space because the coefficient squares sum to ∥1[0,t]​∥2=t. Its continuous modification is a standard Brownian motion by (b)(v). The expansion also holds in L2(Ω×[0,1]): the expectation of the squared tail is the integral of the coefficient-square tail, which tends to zero by the dominated convergence theorem, with bound t.
To realize this expansion for the original B, choose bi​=WGi​. These are Jointly Gaussian with covariance matrix equal to the identity, hence are independent standard normal random variables. The finite orthogonal projections of 1[0,t]​ onto G1​,…,GN​ converge in H, and the identity E∣Wh∣2=∥h∥2 transfers that convergence to the displayed expansion for Bt​. With an independently chosen sequence (bi​) the resulting Brownian motion has the same law; a pathwise identification uses the coupling just given.
