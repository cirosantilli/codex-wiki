On a probability space (Ω,F,P), a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion is a real-valued stochastic process B=(Bt​)t≥0​ with B0​=0 almost surely, continuous sample paths almost surely, and independent increments, such that
Bt​−Bs​∼N(0,t−s)(0≤s<t).​
(1)
Here the second parameter is the variance. Thus its Brownian increments have the normal distribution with mean zero and variance equal to elapsed time. Equivalently, relative to a specified filtration (Ft​), require B to be an adapted process and Bt​−Bs​ to be independent of Fs​ with this normal distribution. Taking the natural Brownian filtration recovers the first definition.

In the Lévy construction of Brownian motion, start with B0​=0 and B1​=Z0​, where Z0​ has the standard normal distribution. Suppose values are defined at the endpoints u=k2−n and v=(k+1)2−n. Set
B(u+v)/2​=2Bu​+Bv​​+2v−u​​Zn,k​,
(2)
using fresh independent standard normal random variables Zn,k​, also independent of everything previously used. Join the values by straight segments to obtain a continuous piecewise linear process B(n) on the dyadic grid.
To verify the Brownian increments, write D=Bv​−Bu​ and ℓ=v−u. The two new increments are D/2+ℓ​Zn,k​/2 and D/2−ℓ​Zn,k​/2. If D∼N(0,ℓ), their variances are ℓ/2 and their covariance is zero. The optional stopping theorem for a right-continuous martingale M on a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions for a filtration states that, for bounded stopping times σ≤τ,
E[Mτ​∣Fσ​]=Mσ​,EMτ​=EMσ​.​
(55)
For a submartingale the conditional equality becomes E[Mτ​∣Fσ​]≥Mσ​, and for a supermartingale it becomes the reverse inequality.
For a uniformly integrable martingale, boundedness of the stopping times can be removed, with the terminal limit used if a stopping time is infinite. A useful application is to a bounded stopped martingale Mt∧τ​ with τ<∞ almost surely: apply the bounded-time theorem first and then the bounded convergence theorem to obtain EMτ​=EM0​. Finiteness of τ alone is not a sufficient hypothesis for an arbitrary martingale.
