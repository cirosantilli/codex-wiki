Seminorm (p)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Topological vector space Locally convex space
OurBigBook.com Words: 363 Articles: 3
A seminorm on a real or complex vector space E is a finite-valued function p:E→[0,∞) satisfying absolute homogeneity and the triangle inequality:
p(λx)=∣λ∣p(x),p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y)(x,y∈E, λ∈K).
(6)
In particular p(0)=0 and p(−x)=p(x). A seminorm is a norm exactly when p(x)=0 implies x=0: this is the extra definiteness of a norm axiom. The zero function is a valid seminorm. The missing definiteness is useful when a measurement deliberately ignores some directions, such as an individual coordinate or the constant term of a function whose derivative is measured.
For example, on C1([0,1]) the function p(f)=supt∈[0,1]​∣f′(t)∣ is a seminorm that vanishes on every constant function. Adding the value measurement gives the norm q(f)=∣f(0)∣+p(f): if q(f)=0, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives f(t)=f(0)+∫0t​f′(s)ds=0. A family of such partial measurements defines a seminorm topology.
