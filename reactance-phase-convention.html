Reactance phase convention (v(t)=Re(Veiωt))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electrical impedance Electrical reactance
OurBigBook.com Words: 238
Use complex amplitudes only for signals at one common positive angular frequency ω. Define the real terminal voltage and electric current by
v(t)=Re(Veiωt),i(t)=Re(Ieiωt),Z=IV​.
(43)
The complex amplitudes here use peak magnitudes; both can instead use root mean square magnitudes without changing their ratio. Choose positive electric current entering the terminal assigned positive voltage, so p=vi is absorbed power. For an ideal linear inductor and capacitor, their terminal laws vL​=Ldi/dt and iC​=CdvC​/dt give
VL​=iωLI,IC​=iωCVC​,ZL​=iωL,ZC​=iωC1​=−ωCi​.
(44)
The derivative multiplies a complex amplitude by iω: this single algebraic step explains both signs. Taking i(t)=I0​cosωt as a reference gives vL​(t)=−ωLI0​sinωt and vC​(t)=I0​sinωt/(ωC) for the sinusoidal response. Thus inductor voltage leads electric current by π/2, while capacitor voltage lags electric current by π/2. A constant offset or a free transient is outside this single-frequency representation. [ref].
With the alternative e−iωt convention, the same real signals use conjugated complex amplitudes and ZL​=−iωL, ZC​=+i/(ωC). Their physical leads and lags stay the same; the sign of ImZ changes. Also, some textbooks call the positive magnitude 1/(ωC) the capacitive reactance and write the series expression as XL​−∣XC​∣. This article consistently uses signed XC​=−1/(ωC) and hence X=XL​+XC​. [ref].
