Extinction bias in photometric distance (dinferred​/d=10A/5)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Astronomical magnitude Distance modulus
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
Let A≥0 be the interstellar extinction in magnitudes in the specified photometric passband. Compared with the same unobscured source at the same distance, its radiative flux is multiplied by 10−0.4A, so its apparent magnitude increases by A. Consequently,
m−M=5log10​(10pcd​)+A,μ0​=m−M−A.
(23)
Here μ0​ is the extinction-corrected distance modulus. The correction subtracts A before using the distance formula. Caltech's NED distance-method documentation explicitly distinguishes an apparent distance modulus from an extinction-corrected distance modulus. [ref].
Ignoring positive interstellar extinction attributes too much dimming to distance, producing an overestimate:
ddinferred​​=10A/5.
(24)
As an illustrative toy, put the source with M=0 at 100 parsecs behind A=1 magnitude of interstellar extinction. The measured apparent magnitude is m=6. Setting A=0 incorrectly gives dinferred​=10pc×106/5≃158.489pc, a factor 1.584893 too large. Subtracting the known A=1 recovers 100 parsecs. The diagram's fixed A=1 offset is a controlled illustration, not a model of how actual interstellar extinction grows with distance, and none of its values are star catalogue measurements.
