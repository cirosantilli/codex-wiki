Trapped surface

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Null hypersurface
OurBigBook.com Words: 246 Articles: 3
A trapped surface is a closed spacelike codimension-two surface whose two future-directed orthogonal null congruences both have negative expansion.
In four-dimensional spacetime, “closed” here means compact without boundary; “spacelike” means that the induced metric tensor is positive definite. Let k+​,k−​ be the two future null vector normals and qab​ the induced metric tensor. Their null expansions are
θ±​=qab∇a​(k±​)b​=dA1​dλ±​d(dA)​.
(33)
Here dA is an infinitesimal transverse area element and λ±​ an affine parameter along the respective normal null geodesics. A future trapped surface has θ+​<0 and θ−​<0 everywhere: even the outward-directed light front initially loses area. Multiplying either future normal by a positive function multiplies its initial null expansion by that function, preserving the sign. A small inward-converging patch is insufficient: the entire closed surface must satisfy both strict inequalities.
