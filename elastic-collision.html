Elastic collision

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Particle dynamics Collision mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 151
An elastic collision preserves total kinetic energy between the incoming and outgoing states. For isolated colliding bodies, momentum conservation also holds. The kinetic energy condition supplies information beyond momentum conservation alone. [ref]
Consider a stipulated one-dimensional example with rightward positive: m1​=2kg, m2​=1kg, incoming velocities u1​=3m/s and u2​=0. The total momentum is P=6kgm/s and total kinetic energy is K=9J. In SI numerical values, outgoing velocities v1​,v2​ satisfy
2v1​+v2​=6,v12​+21​v22​=9.
(164)
Substituting v2​=6−2v1​ gives 3(v1​−1)(v1​−3)=0. The root v1​=3, v2​=0 repeats the incoming state; the outgoing separating state is v1​=1m/s, v2​=4m/s. Its momenta add to 2+4=6kgm/s and its kinetic energies add to 1+8=9J. The incoming and outgoing relative velocities both have magnitude 3m/s, consistent with coefficient of restitution e=1.
