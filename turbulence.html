Reynolds averaging (f=f​+f′,f′​=0)

Words: 53
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
Reynolds averaging separates a resolved mean from zero-mean fluctuations. The idealized averaging operation is linear and idempotent, commutes with the derivatives under consideration and treats resolved mean factors as constant inside its averaging scale. Products then satisfy fg​=f​g​+f′g′​. Actual finite spatial filters need scale separation or corrections when these rules are only approximate.

External intermittency

Words: 29
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
A fixed observation point alternates between turbulent and non-turbulent fluid because a fluctuating interface passes it. This is distinct from internal intermittency, which concerns variations within fluid that remains turbulent.

Wave turbulence

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
Wave turbulence describes the statistical transfer of energy between interacting waves. The wave period, nonlinear interaction time, phase correlations and resonances determine the transfer rate. A weak wave cascade treats the change in one interaction as small; critical balance instead makes the nonlinear interaction rate comparable to the linear wave frequency.

Weak wave cascade (τcas​∼τnl2​/τw​)

Words: 54
Wave turbulence OurBigBook.com
If a wave packet experiences a fractional nonlinear change χ=τw​/τnl​≪1 per interaction, and successive interaction phases are decorrelated, N changes add as a random walk of size N​χ. Order-one transfer needs N∼χ−2 interactions, giving the displayed cascade time. Phase coherence or resonant constraints can alter this estimate; weakness alone does not justify random addition.

Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence

Words: 553 Articles: 10
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence is irregular nonlinear motion of an electrically conducting fluid coupled to a magnetic field. Alfvén waves and magnetosonic waves give propagation times in addition to the nonlinear mixing time. A strong guide field distinguishes directions parallel and perpendicular to it, so the resulting cascades need not be isotropic.

Dimensional freedom of an MHD spectrum (E(k)=ϵ2/3k−5/3Φ(vA​ϵ−1/3k1/3))

Words: 55
Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence OurBigBook.com
In a local isotropic hydrodynamic inertial-range model with only ϵ and k, dimensional analysis fixes the Kolmogorov 1941 theory spectrum. A mean magnetic field supplies the independent Alfvén speed, permitting the arbitrary dimensionless function in the displayed formula even before anisotropy adds parallel scales. Cascade-time assumptions such as weak decorrelated interactions or critical balance are therefore necessary to select an MHD spectrum.

Electron-magnetohydrodynamic cascade

Words: 124 Articles: 2
Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence OurBigBook.com
An electron-magnetohydrodynamic cascade transfers magnetic fluctuations through the nonlinear electron magnetohydrodynamics induction equation. Writing bl​=δBl​/B0​, its perpendicular nonlinear interaction time is τnl​∼l⊥2​/(vA​di​bl​). The anisotropic whistler wave time is τw​∼l∥​l⊥​/(vA​di​). Scaling predictions depend on locality, phase decorrelation and the assumed balance of these times.

Critical balance (τnl​∼τwave​)

Words: 63
Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence OurBigBook.com
Critical balance is a scale-dependent ordering in which the wave-propagation time and nonlinear interaction time are comparable. For strong Alfvénic turbulence, k∥​vA​∼k⊥​δu⊥​. For electron magnetohydrodynamics, the linear rate is vA​di​k∥​k⊥​ and the nonlinear rate is vA​di​k⊥2​δB/B0​, so critical balance instead gives δB/B0​∼k∥​/k⊥​. This assumption is an estimate of decorrelation dynamics, not an exact equality at every point.

Alfvénic turbulence (z⊥±​)

Words: 268 Articles: 4
Magnetohydrodynamic turbulence OurBigBook.com
Alfvénic turbulence is the turbulent interaction of fluctuations polarized primarily as Alfvén waves. In Elsässer variables z±=u⊥​±δB⊥​/4πρ0​​, each wave population is nonlinearly distorted by the oppositely propagating population. Equal populations give a balanced cascade; unequal populations give an imbalanced cascade. The absence of one population removes the leading nonlinear interaction in ideal reduced magnetohydrodynamics.

Weak Alfvénic cascade (E⊥​(k⊥​)∼(ϵvA​/l∥​)1/2k⊥−2​)

Words: 96 Articles: 1
Alfvénic turbulence OurBigBook.com
For balanced anisotropic Alfvénic turbulence, suppose τA​=l∥​/vA​≪τnl​=l⊥​/δz and resonant interactions preserve the parallel scale during the perpendicular cascade. Random encounter accumulation gives τcas​=vA​l⊥2​/(δz2l∥​). Constant energy flux then gives δz∝l⊥1/2​ and the displayed perpendicular turbulent energy spectrum. Unlike isotropic phenomenology, the fixed parallel scale is independent of the decreasing perpendicular scale.
Weak-to-strong transition of an Alfvénic cascade (l⊥,∗​∼L⊥​χ02​)
Words: 44
Weak Alfvénic cascade OurBigBook.com
In a weak Alfvénic cascade at fixed l∥​, χ=τA​/τnl​∝l⊥−1/2​ grows as the perpendicular scale shrinks. If its outer value is χ0​≪1 at L⊥​, it reaches order one at the displayed scale. Beyond it the weak-interaction ordering fails and a critical balance model may become appropriate.

Iroshnikov-Kraichnan spectrum (E(k)∼(ϵvA​)1/2k−3/2)

The Iroshnikov-Kraichnan spectrum phenomenology assumes balanced, isotropic local Alfvénic turbulence with δzl​≪vA​, decorrelated counterpropagating encounters, τA​=l/vA​ and τnl​=l/δzl​. A weak wave cascade takes τcas​∼τnl2​/τA​=vA​l/δzl2​. Constant energy flux gives δzl4​∼ϵvA​l and the displayed shell-integrated turbulent energy spectrum. Isotropy is an additional assumption, not a consequence of a strong guide field.

Goldreich–Sridhar turbulence (E(k⊥​)∝k⊥−5/3​)

Words: 71
Alfvénic turbulence OurBigBook.com
Goldreich–Sridhar turbulence combines critical balance, balanced counterpropagating Alfvén waves, a local perpendicular cascade, and constant energy flux per unit mass E. With τnl​∼ℓ⊥​/δuℓ​ and E∼δuℓ2​/τnl​, one obtains δuℓ​∼(Eℓ⊥​)1/3. Defining the one-dimensional perpendicular turbulent energy spectrum by δuℓ2​∼k⊥​E(k⊥​) gives E(k⊥​)∼E2/3k⊥−5/3​. Critical balance also gives k∥​∼E1/3k⊥2/3​/vA​. This scaling argument neglects scale-dependent alignment and intermittency corrections, which are additional physical effects rather than consequences of the stated assumptions.

Turbulence closure

Words: 206 Articles: 3
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
A turbulence closure approximates the unclosed higher-order moments in evolution equations for lower-order turbulent statistics. For example, an equation for the velocity correlation tensor involves triple correlations, whose evolution involves fourth-order correlations and pressure. A closure must be checked against conservation laws, realizability and boundary conditions; matching an energy spectrum alone does not establish all these properties.

Constant-skewness turbulence closure (S3​(r)=−4S2​(r)3/2/(5β3/2))

Words: 81 Articles: 1
Turbulence closure OurBigBook.com
This turbulence closure assumes the velocity increment skewness is the same throughout the equilibrium range, with its value fixed by the Kolmogorov two-thirds law and Kolmogorov four-fifths law. It yields a simple crossover between differentiable and inertial-scale increments, but suppresses the scale dependence of skewness and has no independent spectral-transfer dynamics.

Normalized constant-skewness structure-function equation (h′+2h3/2=2x)

Words: 42
Constant-skewness turbulence closure OurBigBook.com
Set S2​=15​β3/2vη2​h and r=(15β)3/4ηx. The Kolmogorov equation for structure functions and the constant-skewness turbulence closure give the displayed ordinary differential equation, with h(0)=0. Its small-separation balance is h∼x2; its large-separation balance is h∼x2/3. The first matches differentiable velocity increments, and the second recovers the assumed Kolmogorov two-thirds law.

Eddy-damped quasi-normal Markovian closure

Words: 73
Turbulence closure OurBigBook.com
EDQNM closure models the evolution of the turbulent energy spectrum through spectral triad interactions. Fourth-order correlations are approximated using products of second-order correlations, an eddy damping limits the resulting triple correlations, and their memory integrals are replaced by expressions using current spectra. The modeled damping times are additional physical input. This allows evolving spectra and scale-dependent transfer, but does not automatically recover coherent structures or internal intermittency. Bertoglio, Squires and Ferziger's simulation comparison explains the damping assumptions and tests their limitations.

K-epsilon turbulence model (νT​=Cμ​k2/ϵ)

Words: 207 Articles: 2
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
A two-equation closure transporting turbulent kinetic energy k and its turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate ϵ. Its eddy viscosity is Cμ​k2/ϵ. In a high-Reynolds-number wall-normal reduction, turbulent diffusion has coefficients νT​/σk​ and νT​/σϵ​; the energy equation has shear production P and destruction ϵ, while the dissipation equation has modeled production Cϵ1​Pϵ/k and destruction Cϵ2​ϵ2/k. The dissipation production/destruction terms are closure terms, not a second conservation of energy.

K-epsilon turbulence front (p=1/(2−σ),q=σ/(2−σ))

Words: 69
K-epsilon turbulence model OurBigBook.com
A stationary turbulence edge with no production has an advection–turbulent-diffusion leading balance. For k∝xp, ϵ∝xq and νT​=Cμ​k2/ϵ, this balance gives 2p−q=1 and q/p=σϵ​/σk​=σ. The integral length k3/2/ϵ and turnover frequency ϵ/k both tend to zero when 1<σ<3/2. The front viscosity is linear, with νT′​(0+)=(2σk​−σϵ​)U0​. This is formal high-Reynolds-number consistency, not a uniform justification for neglecting molecular viscosity at the exact edge.

Log-layer solution of the k-epsilon model (k=u∗2​/Cμ​​,κ2=σϵ​Cμ​​(Cϵ2​−Cϵ1​))

Words: 75
K-epsilon turbulence model OurBigBook.com
In the constant-stress overlap layer, the law of the wall gives Uy​=u∗​/(κy) and the Reynolds stress gives −u′v′≃u∗2​. Thus νT​=u∗​κy and local balance gives P≃ϵ=u∗3​/(κy). Combining with the K-epsilon model viscosity formula gives the displayed constant k. The energy diffusion term then vanishes. The dissipation diffusion term does not: it is u∗4​/(σϵ​y2). Balancing it against the dissipation-equation source terms yields the displayed Von Kármán constant relation. Neglecting dissipation diffusion would incorrectly require equal production and destruction constants.

Turbulent mixing

Words: 685 Articles: 12
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
Turbulent mixing redistributes a transported scalar, such as temperature or particle concentration, through irregular fluid motions and eventual molecular diffusion. A top-hat plume model assumes this mixing keeps the scalar approximately uniform within a selected cross section; that uniformity is an additional closure, not a consequence of thin geometry alone.

Richardson pair dispersion (⟨r2(t)⟩∼gϵt3)

Words: 213 Articles: 3
Turbulent mixing OurBigBook.com
Richardson pair dispersion concerns relative separation of two marked fluid elements, rather than their centre-of-mass motion. In an inertial range, scale-local relative diffusivity is of order ϵ1/3r4/3. A self-similar closure gives d⟨r2⟩/dt=Aϵ1/3⟨r2⟩2/3 and hence a t3 regime after loss of memory of initial separation. Stationarity and inertial-range separation alone do not imply this law at arbitrarily short times.

Three-regime turbulent pair-separation model

Words: 55
Richardson pair dispersion OurBigBook.com
For positive initial separation below the Kolmogorov length scale, a phenomenological pair-dispersion model has smooth-flow exponential stretching, inertial-range Richardson pair dispersion, and diffusive large-scale pair dispersion beyond the integral scale of turbulence. A positive Lyapunov exponent is needed for the first regime, scale locality and initial-memory loss for the second, and integrable relative-velocity autocorrelation for the third. The inertial-range crossing time is of order L2/3ϵ−1/3, one outer eddy turnover time.

Diffusive large-scale pair dispersion (E∣l(t)∣2∼2t∫0∞​Rrel​(s)ds)

Words: 57
Richardson pair dispersion OurBigBook.com
At separations well above the integral scale of turbulence, two fluid velocities can decorrelate spatially. If the relative-velocity autocorrelation Rrel​ is stationary and integrable, the displacement identity used in Taylor turbulent dispersion gives the displayed long-time linear mean-square growth. With speed U and Lagrangian integral time of order L/U, its relative diffusivity is of order UL. The characteristic separation grows as t1/2, unlike inertial-range Richardson pair dispersion.

Richardson constant (g)

Words: 42
Richardson pair dispersion OurBigBook.com
The dimensionless Richardson constant is the coefficient in the mean-square pair-separation law. Under the simple self-similar diffusivity closure, g=(A/3)3. Its universality requires scale locality, adequate scale separation and loss of initial-condition memory; a finite-time fit need not isolate this asymptotic coefficient.

Taylor turbulent dispersion (⟨∣X(t)∣2⟩=2∫0t​(t−τ)RL​(τ)dτ)

Words: 197 Articles: 3
Turbulent mixing OurBigBook.com
Taylor turbulent dispersion describes the absolute displacement of a marked fluid element in stationary homogeneous turbulence. Here RL​ is its Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation. Continuous correlation at zero gives ballistic spreading at short times, and an integrable correlation gives diffusive spreading at long times. This is distinct from shear-induced Taylor dispersion and from Richardson pair dispersion.

Power-law velocity-correlation dispersion (⟨ΔX2⟩=2σv2​∫0t​(t−s)(1+s)−αds)

Words: 65
Taylor turbulent dispersion OurBigBook.com
For a zero-mean stationary velocity process of variance σv2​ and normalized autocorrelation (1+∣s∣)−α, integrating velocity twice gives the displayed expression, exactly rather than just asymptotically. For α>1 the long-time diffusion coefficient is σv2​/(α−1). At α=1, the displacement variance is asymptotic to 2σv2​tlogt. For 0<α<1, it is asymptotic to 2σv2​t2−α/[(1−α)(2−α)]. Linear variance growth does not by itself imply a central limit theorem or a normal distribution for displacement.

Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation (RL​(s)=E[u(X(t+s),t+s)⋅u(X(t),t)])

Taylor turbulent dispersion OurBigBook.com
For stationary statistics along a Lagrangian trajectory X, the velocity autocorrelation depends on the lag s. Integrating velocity twice gives the mean-square displacement 2∫0t​(t−s)RL​(s)ds. If RL​ is integrable, the long-time displacement is diffusive. Its normalized one-sided area is the Lagrangian integral time.

Lagrangian integral time (tL​=∫0∞​RL​(τ)dτ/RL​(0))

Words: 43

The Lagrangian integral time is the area under a normalized velocity autocorrelation along a Lagrangian trajectory. If this correlation is integrable, Taylor turbulent dispersion has asymptotic mean-square displacement 2RL​(0)tL​t. The integral time need not equal every short-time decorrelation scale, particularly when the autocorrelation oscillates.

Forced stratified mixing with square-root power input (K˙=K​(A−K/Lv​))

Words: 72
Turbulent mixing OurBigBook.com
For A=U02​(1/Lf​−1/Lρ​)>0, define K∞​=ALv​ and y=K/K∞​​. The positive-energy solution is y(t)=[y0​+tanh(K∞​​t/(2Lv​))]/[1+y0​tanh(K∞​​t/(2Lv​))]. Energies above and below K∞​ approach it from their respective sides. The zero-energy equation is not Lipschitz and admits both permanent rest and delayed startup, so a unique positive steady limit needs a positive seed or an additional startup prescription. With negligible available and internal energy exchange, the long-time instantaneous mixing efficiency fraction is Lf​/Lρ​, independent of Lv​.

Stratified mixing energy budget (K˙=F−B−ϵ,A˙=B−M)

Words: 156 Articles: 2
Turbulent mixing OurBigBook.com
With boundary fluxes suppressed, let B transfer kinetic energy to potential energy, M transfer available potential energy irreversibly to background potential energy, and W transfer internal energy to potential energy. Then P˙=B+W, P˙b​=M+W, A˙=B−M, and K˙=F−B−ϵ. The internal reservoir has I˙=ϵ−W, conserving total energy apart from forcing F. The distinction between reversible buoyancy exchange and irreversible mixing is essential outside a quasi-steady negligible-A regime.

Instantaneous mixing efficiency (η(t)=M/(M+ϵ))

Instantaneous mixing efficiency is the fraction of instantaneous irreversible mechanical-energy loss that raises background potential energy, after subtracting internal-to-potential exchange. The ratio Γ=M/ϵ is a different quantity, related by η=Γ/(1+Γ). Replacing M by buoyancy exchange B additionally assumes negligible change of available potential energy.
Cumulative mixing efficiency (ηc​=∫Mdt/∫(M+ϵ)dt)
Words: 49
Instantaneous mixing efficiency OurBigBook.com
Cumulative mixing efficiency is the ratio of total irreversible density-mixing conversion to total irreversible mechanical-energy loss over an interval. It is a loss-rate-weighted average of instantaneous mixing efficiency, not an unweighted time average. Stored kinetic energy, changes in available potential energy and boundary work must be accounted for before replacing its denominator by integrated forcing.

Turbulent plane jet similarity (ψ=M0​x​f(y/x))

Words: 168 Articles: 1
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
For a slender plane jet with conserved specific momentum flux M0​=∫U2dy and a mixing length proportional to transverse position, dimensional balance gives width proportional to x and centreline speed proportional to M0​/x​. The streamfunction ψ=M0​x​f(η), η=y/x, yields U=M0​/x​f′ and V=M0​/x​(ηf′−f/2). With l=C1​∣y∣, the upper-half Reynolds-averaged momentum equation integrates to ff′=2C12​η2(f′′)2 for a finite centreline velocity and zero centreline stress. The momentum normalization is 2∫0ηw​​(f′)2dη=1 for a compact jet, or the same integral to infinity for a decaying jet. These formal equations do not justify an independently imposed edge condition.

Finite-edge stress condition for a mixing-length jet

Words: 78
Turbulent plane jet similarity OurBigBook.com
For a symmetric localized turbulent plane jet similarity profile, integrated momentum balance gives ff′=2C12​η2(f′′)2 on η>0. At a finite free edge ηw​>0, f′(ηw​)=0 and finite f(ηw​) force f′′(ηw​)=0. Thus the mixing length model l=C1​∣y∣ cannot simultaneously impose zero velocity and a nonzero maximum velocity gradient at the edge. Its eddy viscosity vanishes there. Moreover, a nonzero finite centreline speed f′(0)=a forces ∣f′′∣∼a/(2​C1​η​) near the centre, displaying a singular-shear defect of that local closure. The molecular-viscosity neglect cannot be uniform in this core.

Mixing length (l)

Words: 130 Articles: 1
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
A mixing length is the characteristic transverse displacement over which a turbulent parcel retains its streamwise momentum before mixing with its surroundings. Taylor expansion of a mean velocity profile gives a fluctuation of magnitude l∣Uy​∣. Combining comparable transverse fluctuations with down-gradient Reynolds stress transport gives the closure u′v′=−l2∣Uy​∣Uy​. This is a phenomenological approximation, not a consequence of the Navier-Stokes equation. In a free jet, a prescribed local mixing length must also be compatible with centreline regularity and vanishing outer momentum flux.

Mixing-length closure (νt​∼ℓ2∣∂z​U∣)

Words: 52
Mixing length OurBigBook.com
A mixing-length closure uses a prescribed mixing length ℓ to approximate turbulent transport. For mean shear ∂z​U, a standard model gives eddy viscosity νt​=ℓ2∣∂z​U∣ and down-gradient Reynolds stress. Scalar-diffusion versions replace momentum transport by an appropriate scalar flux. The choice of length and correlations is a modelling assumption rather than an exact closure of the Navier-Stokes equation.

Turbulent kinetic energy (et​=21​ui′​ui′​​)

Words: 236 Articles: 3
Turbulence OurBigBook.com
Kinetic energy per unit mass in velocity fluctuations. Mean shear, buoyancy and transport supply or redistribute it, while molecular viscosity dissipates it. Its local budget motivates the local turbulent kinetic energy balance when storage and transport are negligible.

Mean-shear production of turbulent kinetic energy (P=−ui′​uj′​​∂j​Ui​)

Words: 44

The work of the Reynolds stress against the mean velocity gradient transfers kinetic energy from the mean flow into fluctuations. In a plane shear flow U(y) this is P=−u′v′Uy​. The eddy viscosity closure gives P=νT​Uy2​≥0. Buoyancy production and transport are separate terms in the turbulent kinetic energy budget.

Local turbulent kinetic energy balance (ϵ=P+B0​,P=−u′w′Uz​)

Words: 159 Articles: 1
Turbulent kinetic energy OurBigBook.com
In stationary locally equilibrated turbulence, mean-shear production and buoyancy production balance viscous dissipation after transport terms are neglected. Stable buoyancy flux is negative and consumes part of the shear input. This approximation gives ϵ=u∗3​/(κz) in a neutral logarithmic layer and ϵ∼B0​ in buoyancy-dominated free convection.
