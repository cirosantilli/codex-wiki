Chiral projection by squared supercovariant derivatives (Dˉα˙​Dˉ2X=0)

 ... Supersymmetry Four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry Superspace Superfield Chiral superfield Supersymmetric covariant derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
In flat four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry, let Dˉ2=Dˉα˙​Dˉα˙, contracting the two spinor indices with the antisymmetric epsilon invariant tensor. The supercovariant derivative algebra with left derivatives has {Dˉα˙​,Dˉβ˙​​}=0. There are only two dotted components, so every product of three barred derivatives repeats one component and vanishes. Therefore Dˉα˙​Dˉ2X=0 for any superfield X: Dˉ2X is a chiral superfield. This map is called chiral projection, although Dˉ2 is not an idempotent projector.
If Φ is a chiral superfield, holomorphic closure of chiral superfields also makes W′(Φ) chiral. Thus an equation Dˉ2Φ†=4W′(Φ) is consistent with the chiral constraint: applying any Dˉα˙​ gives zero on both sides. Both sides are scalar superfields, and the squared supersymmetric covariant derivative commutes with the supercharges, so the equation is Lorentz invariant and supersymmetry covariant.
