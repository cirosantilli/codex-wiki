Frequency and wavelength spectral radiance conversion (Lν​=Lλ​(c/ν)c/ν2)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance
OurBigBook.com Words: 332
In vacuum, ν=c/λ, where c is the speed of light. Increasing wavelength decreases frequency, so the ordinary signed differentials satisfy dν=−cλ−2dλ. A spectral slice's positive contribution to radiance must be unchanged. The change of variables formula therefore gives the absolute Jacobian determinant
Lν​(ν)=Lλ​(c/ν)​dνdλ​​=Lλ​(c/ν)ν2c​,Lλ​(λ)=Lν​(c/λ)λ2c​.
(13)
These formulas use Lλ​ per metre and Lν​ per hertz. The factor c/ν2 has units ms=mHz−1, which converts the former density into the latter. If ℓnm​ denotes the numerical density per nanometre in Wm−2sr−1nm−1, its per-metre value is 109ℓnm​. Conversely, the per-nanometre value is 10−9 times the per-metre value. Likewise, a density per terahertz is 1012 times its per-hertz value because 1THz=1012Hz.
For a concrete stipulated spectrum, let Lλ​=C=109Wm−2sr−1m−1 between λ1​=500nm and λ2​=600nm, and zero outside. This is exactly 1Wm−2sr−1nm−1 throughout the band. Using the exact International System of Units value c=299792458ms−1, the corresponding frequency bounds are
ν1​=λ2​c​=499.654096666…THz,ν2​=λ1​c​=599.584916THz.
(14)
The transformed spectral radiance is Lν​=Cc/ν2 on this interval. At ν1​ it is approximately 1.200830743×10−12Wm−2sr−1Hz−1, and at ν2​ it is 8.339102380×10−13 in the same units. These are respectively 1.200830743 and 0.833910238 per terahertz. Thus a flat wavelength density becomes a decreasing frequency density; relabelling the horizontal axis alone would miss both the shape and the units. [ref].
The equality of band radiances has a short exact proof:
∫λ1​λ2​​Cdλ=C(λ2​−λ1​)=100Wm−2sr−1,
(15)
∫ν1​ν2​​ν2Cc​dν=Cc(ν1​1​−ν2​1​)=C(λ2​−λ1​).
(16)
The reversed bounds and the Jacobian determinant preserve the same spectral measure. Each half-band, 500–550nm and 550–600nm, carries 50Wm−2sr−1, although the corresponding frequency widths are about 54.508 and 45.423THz.
A spectrum's peak is consequently a statement about a particular density and coordinate. The frequency and wavelength peaks of Planck's law differ for precisely this reason. Integrated radiance over a specified physical band supplies a comparison that survives the change of variables.
