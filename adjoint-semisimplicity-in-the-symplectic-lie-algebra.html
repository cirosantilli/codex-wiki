Adjoint semisimplicity in the symplectic Lie algebra (ada diagonalizable ⟺ a diagonalizable on V)

OurBigBook.com Words: 183
Let V be a finite-dimensional symplectic vector space over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For a∈sp(V), write its Jordan–Chevalley decomposition as a=S+N. Applying the symplectic adjoint to this decomposition and using a∗=−a, uniqueness gives S∗=−S and N∗=−N. Thus both parts belong to the symplectic Lie algebra. The operators adS and adN commute. The former is diagonalizable on End(V) in a matrix unit basis for an eigenbasis of S, and the latter is a nilpotent linear map, as follows from
(adN)m(T)=∑j=0m​(−1)j(jm​)Nm−jTNj.

The restrictions to the invariant symplectic Lie algebra retain these properties. Hence these restrictions form the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition of ada.
If ada is diagonalizable, adN=0. For every v∈V, the rank-one map Tv​(w)=ω(v,w)v lies in sp(V), since the two terms in ω(Tv​w,u)+ω(w,Tv​u) cancel by alternation. For v=0 its image is kv, so commuting with Tv​ forces N to preserve kv. A linear map preserving every line is scalar: compare its action on two linearly independent vectors and on their sum. Since N is a nilpotent linear map, that scalar is zero. Thus a=S is diagonalizable. Conversely, if a is diagonalizable on V, its operator commutator action is diagonalizable on End(V) and therefore on its invariant symplectic Lie algebra. This proves both directions, including V=0.
