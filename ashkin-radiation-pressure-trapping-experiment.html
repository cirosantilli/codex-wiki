Ashkin radiation-pressure trapping experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical tweezers
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
Arthur Ashkin's 1970 first experiment suspended transparent latex spheres in water inside a 120μm glass cell. A horizontal 514.5nm argon laser beam, focused to a 6.2μm radius, illuminated individual beads viewed through a microscope. An off-centre 2.68μm bead moved toward the beam axis and downstream until reaching the cell wall; blocking the beam released it into Brownian motion. Transparent particles and liquid reduced competing thermal effects. This observed sequence separates transverse attraction from downstream radiation pressure: wall-assisted confinement is not free three-dimensional confinement by one beam.[ref]
The beam-blocking comparison is particularly informative: persistence against a wall while illuminated and release when illumination stops test an optical cause. To infer a force from the downstream velocity, one additionally needs a linear drag model and the bead's distance from the wall; the observed motion alone does not calibrate an unrestricted harmonic optical trap.
