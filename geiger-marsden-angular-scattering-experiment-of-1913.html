Geiger-Marsden angular-scattering experiment of 1913

 Home Physics Atomic physics Geiger-Marsden experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
The later angular test used an evacuated chamber with a radon source and scattering foil fixed in place. A diaphragm served as a collimator; a zinc sulfide screen and attached optical microscope rotated about the foil on a graduated platform. Particle scintillation counts sampled successive angles. Foil-out counts supplied background subtraction, with corrections for source radioactive decay and finite beam width. Shielding reduced interfering radiation, but screen glow limited large angles. This tested the Rutherford differential cross-section.[ref]
