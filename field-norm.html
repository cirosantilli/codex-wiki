Field norm (NL/K​(α))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Algebraic number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 626 Articles: 6
For a finite field extension L/K, multiplication by α∈L is a K-linear map mα​:L→L. Its determinant is the field norm:
NL/K​(α)=det(mα​)∈K.
(28)
This does not depend on the chosen basis. If L/K is a separable field extension, then
NL/K​(α)=∏σ:L↪K, σ∣K​=id​σ(α),
(29)
where the product runs over all [L:K] field embeddings into an algebraic closure of K. Embeddings are counted even when several give the same value on α: for r∈K, the result is r[L:K]. This avoids ambiguity in the phrase “product of algebraic conjugates”.[ref]
For L=Q(2​) over K=Q, write α=a+b2​. In the basis 1,2​, multiplication by α has columns (a,b) and (2b,a), hence
Mα​=(ab​2ba​),NL/Q​(α)=a2−2b2=(a+b2​)(a−b2​).
(30)
The determinant calculation and the product of the two field embeddings give the same answer. In particular,
N(2​)=−2,N(1+2​)=−1,N(3+22​)=1,N(2)=4.
(31)
A field norm is an arithmetic invariant; it is not a nonnegative norm measuring length, and need not be positive on nonzero elements.
The field norm is multiplicative, N(αβ)=N(α)N(β), because mαβ​=mα​mβ​ and determinants are multiplicative. It vanishes exactly at zero. For a number field over Q, it takes algebraic integers to integers, and an algebraic integer is a unit exactly when its field norm is 1 or −1.[ref][ref]
This makes the field norm a useful test for divisibility. If nonzero α,β∈OL​ and α divides β, then the integer N(α) divides N(β). For example, 3+2​ has field norm 7, so it cannot divide 2, whose field norm is 4. It does divide 7, with quotient 3−2​. This necessary condition can reject a proposed factor without solving for its quotient.
