The metric tensor in a basis ea​ is gab​=ea​⋅eb​. Differentiating this inner product with the product rule and using the assumed basis-derivative relation yields
∂c​gab​​=(∂c​ea​)⋅eb​+ea​⋅(∂c​eb​)=Γacd​ed​⋅eb​+Γbcd​ea​⋅ed​=Γacd​gdb​+Γbcd​gad​.​​
(25)
Thus the coefficients satisfy metric compatibility. For a coordinate basis with zero torsion tensor, symmetrizing this relation in a,c, adding the corresponding two equations and subtracting the third gives
Γacb​=21​gbd(∂a​gdc​+∂c​gda​−∂d​gac​).
(26)
They are therefore the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection. Metric compatibility by itself does not establish zero torsion tensor; that also uses the coordinate basis and its commuting coordinate derivatives.
For an embedded curved surface, the stated ambient basis-derivative relation needs a qualification: the Gauss formula is ∂c​ea​=Γacb​eb​+hac​n, where n is a unit normal and hac​ is the second fundamental form. The normal term has zero inner product with each tangent basis vector, so the displayed identity for ∂c​gab​ still holds. For a concrete counterexample to dropping that term, on a two-sphere one has ∂θ​eθ​=−Rn, whereas every Γθθb​ is zero. Thus the intrinsic connection describes the tangential projection of the ambient derivative.
