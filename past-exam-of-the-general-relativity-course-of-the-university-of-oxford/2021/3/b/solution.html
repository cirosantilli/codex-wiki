Let μ=GM/c2, f=1−2μ/r, q=1−3μ/r​=1/u0, and choose Ω>0. A torque-free test gyroscope moving on a geodesic has a spin four-vector obeying parallel transport,
dtdsμ​+Γαβμ​dtdxα​sβ=0,gμν​uμsν=0.
(24)
The orthogonality condition says the temporal spin component vanishes in the body's rest frame, not necessarily in Schwarzschild coordinates. Here it gives
st=fc2r2Ω​sϕ.
(25)
In addition to the radial Christoffel symbols in part (a), use Γtrt​=μ/(r2f) and Γrϕϕ​=1/r. On the equator the parallel transport equations become
dtdst​=−r2fμ​sr,dtdsr​=rΩq2sϕ,dtdsϕ​=−rΩ​sr,dtdsθ​=0.

The radial equation follows by substituting st into dsr/dt+Γttr​st+ΩΓϕϕr​sϕ=0. Eliminating sϕ gives d2sr/dt2+Ω2q2sr=0.
Let s0​ be the invariant spin magnitude. Initially radial spin means sr(0)=s0​f​ and st(0)=sϕ(0)=sθ(0)=0. The complete solution is
sr(t)sϕ(t)sθ(t)st(t)​=s0​f​cos(Ωqt),=−rqs0​f​​sin(Ωqt),=0,=−c2f​qs0​rΩ​sin(Ωqt).​​
(27)
To interpret the orientation, convert coordinate components into the orbiting orthonormal frame. The Lorentz factor relative to a Schwarzschild static observer is γ=f​/q, so the body's measured radial and tangential spin components are
Sr^=f​sr​=s0​cos(Ωqt),Sϕ^​=γrsϕ​=−s0​sin(Ωqt).
(28)
Their squared sum is s02​, and their angle relative to the outward radial direction decreases uniformly at rate
Ωr​=Ωq=u0Ω​.​
(29)
The differing coordinate amplitudes in sr,sϕ must not be interpreted as a nonuniform physical spin rotation. After one orbital period the radial basis has turned by 2π while the spin has turned by −2πq relative to it. The residual geodetic precession is Δψ=2π(1−q) in the orbital direction.
