For monotonically expanding scale factor R, dt=dR/(RH), so the elapsed cosmic time is
t0​−ti​=∫Ri​R0​​RH(R)dR​=∫0zi​​(1+z)H(z)dz​.​

The second expression uses cosmological redshift 1+z=R0​/R; the age of the universe is the limit to its earliest specified state, if that limit is finite.
An age comparison for an initial vacuum-dominated phase needs a common normalization. Match the two histories at the end of S1, with R=R1​ and H=H1​>0, and give them identical subsequent S2 and S3 evolution, including transition radii and today's state. Below R1​, the history with S1 has constant H=H1​. The comparison history extends S2 backwards, so ρ∝R−4 and H=H1​(R1​/R)2. Starting both at Ri​=ϵR1​, where 0<ϵ<1, their early durations are
ΔtS1​=H1​1​lnϵ1​,ΔtS2​=∫ϵR1​R1​​H1​R12​RdR​=2H1​1−ϵ2​.
(40)
The difference is positive because
ΔtS1​−ΔtS2​=H1​1​∫ϵ1​(x1​−x)dx>0.​
(41)
The common later evolution adds the same time to both histories. Thus S1 makes this matched universe older. More strongly, as Ri​→0, the exact S1 duration diverges logarithmically, whereas a radiation beginning has finite duration 1/(2H1​) to the match. The exact de Sitter spacetime expanding patch reaches R=0 only as t→−∞ for its comoving observers; a finite “age since R=0” does not exist in this ideal model. This does not establish past geodesic completeness of the patch.
The matching assumption is essential for a finite cutoff or realistic finite period of cosmic inflation: arbitrary changes of the present Hubble parameter or transition densities prevent a universal age comparison. The literal zero-size limit in this question is instead the infinite-duration idealization just derived.
