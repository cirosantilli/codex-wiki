Use metric signature −+++ and transverse-traceless gauge, gij​=δij​+hijTT​. To resolve the source's origin ambiguity, put the detector centre at z=0 and the source centre at z=−r0​, so the gravitational wave travels in the positive z direction. Reversing that direction replaces z by −z. Work in the weak-field approximation, with ωl≪c, r0​≫l,c/ω, and detector size a≪c/ω,r0​. The radiation reaction is neglected, so the rod maintains the prescribed steady motion.
At the retarded source time u=t−(r0​+z)/c, take the source positions as x±​=±l(cos(ωu+β),sin(ωu+β),0). The arbitrary constant β specifies the initial source phase. The second mass moment tensor Iij​=∑A​MxAi​xAj​ obeys
Ixx​=Ml2[1+cosΦ],Iyy​=Ml2[1−cosΦ],Ixy​=Iyx​=Ml2sinΦ,Φ=2ω(t−cr0​+z​)+2β.
(16)
The quadrupole formula hijTT​=2GI¨ijTT​/(c4r0​) projects away the transverse trace, which is already constant here. Differentiating twice therefore gives
hxx​=−HcosΦ,hyy​=HcosΦ,hxy​=hyx​=−HsinΦ,H=c4r0​8GMl2ω2​.​
(17)
All time and longitudinal metric perturbation components vanish in transverse-traceless gauge. The gravitational-wave frequency is 2ω in angular units; M is each source mass, not their sum, and the detector mass m does not enter the strain. The plane wave approximation holds the amplitude fixed at r0​; retaining spherical spreading replaces its denominator by r0​+z near the axis.
