Rλμνκ​=−Rμλνκ​=−Rλμκν​,Rλμνκ​=Rνκλμ​.​

A semicolon denotes the covariant derivative for the Levi-Civita connection. For example, the four lower indices of the Riemann curvature tensor contribute four connection terms:
Rλμνκ;η​=​∂η​Rλμνκ​−Γληa​Raμνκ​−Γμηa​Rλaνκ​−Γνηa​Rλμaκ​−Γκηa​Rλμνa​.​
(3)
Here ∂η​=∂/∂xη is a partial derivative, Γbηa​ are Christoffel symbols, and repeated indices are summed from 0 to 3. Write gμν​ for the metric tensor, gμν for its inverse metric, Rμν​=gλν′Rλμν′ν​ for the Ricci tensor, and R=gμνRμν​ for the Ricci scalar. This tensor contraction agrees with the paper's Rμν​=−Raμνa​.
By metric compatibility, the inverse metric may pass through each covariant derivative. Contracting the second Bianchi identity with gλν gives
∇η​Rμκ​−∇κ​Rμη​+∇λRλμκη​=0,
(4)
where ∇η​ is the same covariant derivative as the semicolon. Next contract with gμκ. Antisymmetry of the first pair gives gμκRλμκη​=−Rλη​, so
∂η​R−∇μRμη​−∇λRλη​=0.
(5)
Thus the contracted Bianchi identity is ∇μRμη​=21​∂η​R. Raising the remaining index and using metric compatibility proves
∇μ​Gμν=0,Gμν=Rμν−21​gμνR.​
(6)
The Einstein tensor Gμν therefore has identically zero covariant divergence, without imposing any field equation. This makes the Einstein field equations compatible with stress-energy conservation, ∇μ​Tμν=0, where Tμν is the stress-energy tensor. The connection terms describe how energy and momentum are compared at neighboring points; ordinary componentwise partial derivatives would not give a coordinate-independent conservation law in curved spacetime.

For a contravariant vector field Aλ, the covariant derivative is
∇ν​Aλ=Aλ;ν​=∂ν​Aλ+Γμνλ​Aμ.​
(20)
The Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection satisfying metric compatibility. Its Christoffel symbols in a coordinate basis are
Γμνλ​=21​gλσ(∂μ​gσν​+∂ν​gσμ​−∂σ​gμν​),Γμνλ​=Γνμλ​.​
(21)
There is generally no symmetry interchanging the upper index with a lower one. The Christoffel symbols themselves are not the components of a tensor.
An ordinary partial derivative of the components of a vector field includes the apparent change caused by the varying coordinate basis. The connection term corrects for that change, so ∇ν​Aλ obeys the tensor component transformation law. It gives coordinate-independent ways to express parallel transport, geodesics and stress-energy conservation in curved spacetime.

A photon follows a null geodesic, whose spatial path approaches straight lines far from the deflecting mass. Extend the incoming asymptote forward: this is the reference path the photon would follow if the mass were removed while its incoming direction and transverse offset were held fixed. The impact parameter b is the perpendicular distance from the mass to that incoming asymptote. It is not, in general, the distance of closest approach.
The positive deflection angle δ is the angle between the outgoing propagation direction and the continued incoming direction. The photon bends toward the mass, so a ray passing above it has a downward outgoing slope in the figure. In the weak-field approximation for an isolated spherical mass, Schwarzschild light deflection gives δ≃4GM/(bc2) for distant endpoints. This angle differs from the apparent image angle measured from the observer's line of sight to the mass.

In the thin-lens approximation for gravitational lensing, consider a ray crossing the lens plane at transverse distance b above the aligned source-observer axis. With the observer at axial position 0, the black hole at L and the source at D, the segment joining the lens plane to the source has slope −b/(D−L), while the segment joining the observer to the lens plane has slope b/L. The observer sees the ray at apparent angle θ≃b/L to the axis. When following the photon toward the observer, the two small angles to the axis have magnitudes b/(D−L) and b/L. The physical bend is their sum:
δ≃Lb​+D−Lb​=L(D−L)bD​.
(47)
Substitute the Schwarzschild light deflection, δ≃4GM/(bc2), and use b≃Lθ:
b2≃c24GM​DL(D−L)​,2θ≃2c24GM​LDD−L​​.​
(48)

Isotropic emission therefore supplies rays at all these azimuths, and the ideal image is an Einstein ring centered on the black hole, with Einstein angular radius θ and angular diameter 2θ. The Earth-Moon pair is a gravitationally bound system, so its separation does not obey r˙=H0​r. The Hubble flow describes the motion of the approximately homogeneous cosmological matter distribution; it is not a compulsory extra velocity assigned to the members of every local orbit. Thus the numerical proximity of the counterfactual rate to 3.8cmyr−1 is not evidence for that explanation.
The observed recession arises principally from tidal dissipation. The Earth rotates faster than the Moon orbits, so the displaced tidal response transfers angular momentum from the Earth's spin into the lunar orbit. For a nearly circular orbit, increasing orbital angular momentum increases its size. A model of this effect must include the appropriate tidal response, rather than add a universal Hubble flow recession.
A residual local cosmological tidal acceleration can exist, but its background scale is ∣(a¨/a)r∣∼H02​r, not an imposed first-order secular drift H0​r. At r=4×108m,
H02​r∼2×10−27ms−2,r2G(ME​+MM​)​∼2.5×10−3ms−2,
(51)
using G(ME​+MM​)≃4×1014m3s−2. The cosmological scale is about 10−24 of the mutual gravitational acceleration. Its detailed coefficient depends on how the local system is embedded in the cosmological background. Cosmological expansion can be neglected for the lunar recession calculation at the stated precision; this conclusion follows from binding and the tiny residual local cosmological tidal acceleration, rather than from comparing two recession speeds.

d(uV)=−pdV⟹4TdT​=−34​VdV​⟹TdT​=−RdR​.

Thus TR is constant along this adiabatic process, and the initial intrinsic contribution is ΔT/T=−ΔR/R. The smaller local expansion associated with the fluctuation leaves its radiation hotter; this is the compression or local expansion-history contribution, distinct from the clock-rate effect on the escaping radiation.
Multiplying the two first-order factors, and cancelling the common subsequent background redshift, gives
Tˉobs​Tobs​​≃(1−RΔR​)(1+c2δΦ​),TΔT​=−r0​c2GδM​−RΔR​.​
(62)
For the overdense region produced by adiabatic compression, ΔR<0, so the final term is positive. It competes with the negative gravitational redshift term. Their sum is the intrinsic-plus-endpoint part of the Sachs-Wolfe combination; computing a full observed Cosmic microwave background anisotropy can also require Doppler CMB anisotropy and the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect.[ref]
