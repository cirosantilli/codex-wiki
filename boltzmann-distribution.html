Boltzmann distribution (Pi​=Z−1e−βEi​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 821 Articles: 5
The Boltzmann distribution assigns probabilities to individual microstates in a canonical ensemble, a statistical ensemble for thermal contact. The system exchanges energy with a thermal reservoir at fixed positive absolute temperature T, while its particle number N and external parameters, such as volume, stay fixed. The thermal reservoir is large enough that these exchanges barely change its temperature; the coupling permits thermal equilibrium but contributes negligible interaction energy. All the microstates considered have the same N. No chemical potential term is needed in their weights; fixed N does not imply that the chemical potential is zero.
For a finite discrete set of microstates with energies Ei​, write
pi​=Ze−βEi​​,β=kB​T1​,Z=∑i​e−βEi​,∑i​pi​=1.
(64)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, β is the inverse temperature, e−βEi​ is a Boltzmann factor, and Z is the partition function. The Boltzmann factor is a weight; dividing by the partition function makes it a probability. [ref]
The useful surprise is that a fixed temperature still permits thermal fluctuations in the system's energy. The Boltzmann distribution predicts their probabilities and expected values without tracking every microscopic exchange. Its exponential function also makes rare excited microstates sensitive to temperature: an energy gap much larger than kB​T gives strong Boltzmann suppression, while warming increases their relative populations. A population ratio can therefore reveal a gap or serve as a thermometer, provided thermal equilibrium and the relevant state degeneracies are known.
Historically, Josiah Willard Gibbs's Elementary Principles in Statistical Mechanics (1902) presents the canonical ensemble in Chapter IV. His equations 90–92 give an exponential function of energy and its normalization, in classical phase space. This classical formulation exhibits the same weighting principle. [ref] [ref]
