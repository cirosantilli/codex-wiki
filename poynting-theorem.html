Poynting theorem (∂t​u+∇⋅S=−J⋅E)

The Poynting theorem is the local energy balance of classical electromagnetism. For vacuum field laws, with current density J, the electromagnetic energy per unit volume is
u=2ϵ0​∣E∣2​+2μ0​∣B∣2​,
(43)
and
∂t​u+∇⋅S=−J⋅E.
(44)
Thus a decrease in local electromagnetic energy can supply outward energy flux or work on electric charge. The term J⋅E is power transferred to matter per unit volume. Its sign matters: positive values remove field energy, while negative values describe matter supplying the field. The magnetic part of the Lorentz force supplies no work term.
For a fixed volume V, the divergence theorem gives the directly measurable balance
dtd​∫V​udV=−∫∂V​S⋅ndA−∫V​J⋅EdV.
(45)
For example, 2.0W entering a region with 0.50W leaving and 1.20W transferred to matter means stored electromagnetic energy increases at 0.30W. In source-free vacuum only the boundary flux changes the stored energy; a nonzero local Poynting vector can still have zero net closed-surface flux.
A brief proof takes the dot product of the Ampère-Maxwell equation with E/μ0​ and uses Faraday's law with B/μ0​. The identity ∇⋅(E×B)=B⋅(∇×E)−E⋅(∇×B) turns the remaining terms into the divergence of the Poynting vector. [ref].
