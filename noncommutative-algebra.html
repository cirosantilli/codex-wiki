Semiprime ring (I2=0⟹I=0)

Words: 39
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A ring is semiprime when it has no nonzero nilpotent ideals. Equivalently, a two-sided ideal with square zero must be zero. This is weaker than having no nonzero nilpotent elements: a matrix ring over a field is semiprime and can have nilpotent matrices.

Goldie ring

Words: 269 Articles: 4
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A ring is right Goldie if it has finite right uniform dimension and satisfies the ascending chain condition on right annihilators. The left-sided definition reverses the multiplication. These two definitions need not be equivalent.

Right Goldie ring

Words: 242 Articles: 3
Goldie ring OurBigBook.com
A right Goldie ring satisfies the right-sided conditions in the definition of a Goldie ring. Every right Noetherian ring does: its right annihilators are right ideals, and its right regular module has finite uniform dimension.

Goldie's theorem (R semiprime right Goldie⟺Qclr​(R) semisimple Artinian)

Words: 216 Articles: 2
Right Goldie ring OurBigBook.com
A semiprime ring has a classical right ring of quotients that is a semisimple ring and Artinian exactly when it is a right Goldie ring. In particular, the theorem applies to a semiprime ring that is a right Noetherian ring. It extends the fraction-field construction from commutative domains to rings whose quotient can be a finite product of matrix rings over division rings.
Prime classical quotient of a prime right Goldie ring (Qclr​(R)≅Mn​(D))
Words: 71
Goldie's theorem OurBigBook.com
A nonzero two-sided ideal of the classical right ring of quotients contains a nonzero element of the original ring: multiply a nonzero fraction by its regular right denominator. If the original ring is prime, the contractions of any two such ideals have nonzero product, so the quotient ring is prime too. The Goldie theorem makes it semisimple Artinian, and the Artin–Wedderburn theorem expresses it as a finite product of matrix rings over division rings. Primeness excludes more than one factor.
Semisimplicity of a classical quotient from regular elements in essential ideals
Words: 98
Goldie's theorem OurBigBook.com
Suppose R is a right Noetherian ring and every essential right ideal contains a regular element of a ring. Preimages of essential regular principal right ideals under left multiplication are essential, so they contain regular elements and prove the right Ore condition. In the classical right ring of quotients Q, every right ideal is generated by its contraction to R, making Q right Noetherian. An essential right ideal of Q contracts to an essential right ideal of R, hence contains a regular element and is all of Q. A maximal disjoint complement to any right ideal has essential sum with it, so every right ideal is complemented. The right regular module is therefore a finite direct sum of simple modules, making Q semisimple Artinian.

Regular element of a ring (cr=0 or rc=0⟹r=0)

Words: 25
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A regular element of a ring is a two-sided non-zero-divisor. The two cancellation conditions must both hold in a noncommutative ring. Products of regular elements remain regular.

Semi-local ring (R/J(R) is semisimple Artinian)

Words: 83 Articles: 1
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A ring is semilocal when its quotient by the Jacobson radical is a semisimple ring that is Artinian. Equivalently, that quotient is a right Artinian ring: it has zero Jacobson radical, and the Artinian structure theorem makes it semisimple. A commutative ring is semilocal exactly when it has finitely many maximal ideals.

Surjectivity from injectivity modulo the Jacobson radical (α−1(VJ)⊆VJ⟹α(V)=V)

Words: 41
Semi-local ring OurBigBook.com
For a finitely generated module V on the right over a semilocal ring, the displayed condition makes the induced module endomorphism on V/VJ injective. This quotient is an Artinian module, so Artinian modules are co-Hopfian makes that map surjective. The remaining quotient module V/α(V) equals its product with J, and Nakayama lemma makes it zero.

Generalized Weyl algebra (D(σ,t))

Words: 81 Articles: 1
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
For a ring D, an automorphism σ and central t, adjoin X,Y with Xd=σ(d)X, Yd=σ−1(d)Y, YX=t and XY=σ(t). This construction includes Weyl algebras and rank-one universal enveloping algebras.

Krull dimension criterion for rank-one generalized Weyl algebras

Words: 52
Generalized Weyl algebra OurBigBook.com
For a commutative Noetherian base D of finite dimension d, the noncommutative Krull dimension of D(σ,t) equals d unless a height-d maximal ideal is periodic under σ or contains infinitely many translates σn(t). This is the commutative-base specialization of Theorem 5.3 in Krull dimension of Generalized Weyl Algebras with non-commutative coefficients.

Noncommutative Krull dimension (KdimR​M)

Words: 91
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
Set the dimension of the zero module to −1. A nonzero module has dimension zero exactly when it is an Artinian module. Inductively, dimension at most d means that in every descending chain the successive quotients eventually have dimension less than d. The least such ordinal is the Krull dimension; the ordinal definition also covers dimensions beyond finite integers. The left dimension of a ring is that of its left regular module. In noncommutative rings this is not defined simply as the length of a chain of two-sided prime ideals.

Noncommutative domain

Words: 36
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A noncommutative domain is a nonzero unital ring without zero divisors; commutativity is not required, but commutative integral domains are included in this usage. Left and right cancellation hold for nonzero elements. A right Noetherian domain embeds in a division ring by the right Ore condition.

Quantum plane (kq​[X,Y])

Words: 256 Articles: 4
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
For a field k and q∈k×, the quantum plane is kq​[X,Y]=k⟨X,Y⟩/(YX−qXY). Its ordered monomials XiYj, i,j≥0, are a basis, and their multiplication is XiYjXuYv=qjuXi+uYj+v. Leading monomials show it is a noncommutative domain. Some sources interchange q with q−1 in the defining relation.

Quantum enveloping algebra action on the quantum plane (E(xrys)=[s]q​xr+1ys−1)

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Quantum plane OurBigBook.com
For yx=qxy, the quantum enveloping algebra of sl2 acts by K(xrys)=qr−sxrys, E(xrys)=[s]q​xr+1ys−1 and F(xrys)=[r]q​xr−1ys+1. Negative exponents here have zero coefficients and are omitted. The quantum integer identity [r][s+1]−[s][r+1]=[r−s] verifies the commutator. These operations preserve total degree and make the quantum plane a module algebra.

Cubic-root quantum-plane socle (soc(kq3​[x,y])=⟨x3,y3⟩)

Words: 64
Quantum enveloping algebra action on the quantum plane OurBigBook.com
At a primitive cube root of unity, the cubic homogeneous module has basis x3,x2y,xy2,y3. Its only simple modules occurring as submodules are the one-dimensional subspaces of ⟨x3,y3⟩, on which K=1 and E=F=0. Its quotient by this two-dimensional submodule is simple, but it has no simple complement: any lift of the highest K weight space contains x2y, whose E image is x3=0. Thus the cubic module is not a semisimple module.

Quantum Laurent plane (uv=qvu,u invertible)

Words: 57
Quantum plane OurBigBook.com
For q=0, define a vector space with basis uivj, i∈Z, j≥0, and product (uivj)(ukvℓ)=q−jkui+kvj+ℓ. Bilinearity of the exponent verifies associativity. This constructs the algebra and proves the basis independently of its presentation. It localizes the quantum plane at powers of u and gives a quotient witness for noncommutativity and noncocommutativity of the coordinate Hopf algebra of quantum SL2.

Quantum torus (kq​[X±1,Y±1])

Words: 53
Quantum plane OurBigBook.com
The two-dimensional quantum torus is the algebra with invertible generators X,Y and relation YX=qXY, for q∈k×. Its basis consists of XiYj with i,j∈Z, with the same multiplication rule as the quantum plane. It is a right Noetherian ring: successive adjunction of X−1, Y, and Y−1 satisfies the hypotheses of the noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem.

Right Artinian ring

Words: 294 Articles: 4
Noncommutative algebra OurBigBook.com
A ring is right Artinian if its right ideals satisfy the descending chain condition, equivalently its right regular module is an Artinian module. Left and right chain conditions should be distinguished for a general ring.

Right Artinian triangular ring that is not left Noetherian (T=(k0​KK​))

Words: 80
Right Artinian ring OurBigBook.com
Let K/k be an infinite-dimensional field extension. For the triangular ring in the display, the right regular module has length three: e1​T has its off-diagonal simple submodule and simple quotient, and e2​T is simple. Thus T is right Artinian. Its off-diagonal ideal is, on the left, the infinite-dimensional k-vector space K. Every k-subspace determines a left ideal, so an ascending sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces violates left Noetherianity. For example take K=k(z) and the spans of 1,z,…,zm.

Semisimplicity of a right Artinian ring with zero radical

Words: 77
Right Artinian ring OurBigBook.com
The finite intersections of maximal right ideals of a right Artinian ring have a minimal member by the descending chain condition. Intersecting it with any other maximal right ideal changes nothing, so it equals the Jacobson radical. If that radical is zero, the diagonal map from the regular module into a finite direct sum of its simple quotients is injective. A submodule of a finite direct sum of simple modules is semisimple and has finite length. This proves semisimplicity of the regular module without assuming Noetherianity first.

Hopkins-Levitzki theorem

Words: 109 Articles: 1
Right Artinian ring OurBigBook.com
A right Artinian ring is a right Noetherian ring. Its right regular module therefore has finite composition length. Its Jacobson radical is a nilpotent ideal, and its quotient by that Jacobson radical is a semisimple ring. In particular every finitely generated right module over such a ring has finite composition length.

Minimal-right-ideal proof of radical nilpotence (J(R)m=0 for some m)

Words: 74
Hopkins-Levitzki theorem OurBigBook.com
In a right Artinian ring, the powers of its Jacobson radical stabilize at an ideal I satisfying I2=I. If I=0, choose a minimal right ideal K with KI=0. Since (KI)I=KI, minimality gives KI=K. Choosing k∈K with kI=0 gives K=kR by the same minimality. Consequently k=kj for some j∈J(R). The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical makes 1−j invertible, forcing k=0, a contradiction. This proof avoids incorrectly applying the finitely generated form of Nakayama lemma to an arbitrary Artinian module.

Finite-module extension preserves Noetherianity

Words: 77
Right Noetherian ring OurBigBook.com
Suppose A⊆B are unital rings and B is a finitely generated module over A on the right. If A is a right Noetherian ring, then BA​ is a Noetherian module. Every right ideal of B is an A-submodule, hence has finitely many generators over A; these also generate it over B. Thus B is a right Noetherian ring. The same argument works on the left under the corresponding hypotheses. Finite-index group ring extensions give an important example, using coset representatives as a finite module basis.

Right Noetherian domain

Words: 34
Right Noetherian ring OurBigBook.com
A noncommutative domain whose right ideals satisfy the ascending chain condition is a right Noetherian domain. Its right regular module is a uniform module, so any two nonzero principal right ideals intersect. Thus its nonzero elements satisfy the right Ore condition and it embeds in a division ring by Ore localization.

Noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem

Words: 252 Articles: 2
Right Noetherian ring OurBigBook.com
If an algebra B is generated by a subalgebra A that is a right Noetherian ring and one element x, and A+xA=A+Ax, then B is a right Noetherian ring. The proof uses Fn​=∑i≤n​Axi=∑i≤n​xiA and, for each right ideal I, the ascending right ideals Ln​={a:axn∈I+Fn−1​}. After these stabilize, finitely many lifts of generators for L0​,…,LN​ generate I by induction on degree. Neither unique normal forms nor an automorphism moving coefficients is required.

Leading-coefficient proof for a central polynomial extension (R right Noetherian⇒R[t] right Noetherian)

Words: 99
Noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem OurBigBook.com
For a right ideal I⊆R[t], let Ld​ be the set of degree-d coefficients of its elements of degree at most d. These are right ideals of R, and multiplication by the central variable makes Ld​⊆Ld+1​. They stabilize, and every Ld​ before stabilization is finitely generated. Choose polynomial lifts of those generators. Subtracting a suitable right linear combination of the lifts, shifted by powers of t, cancels the leading coefficient of any element of I. Induction on degree proves that the finitely many chosen lifts generate I.

Skew Hilbert basis theorem (A left Noetherian⟹A[t;σ] left Noetherian)

Words: 86
Noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem OurBigBook.com
For an automorphism σ of a left Noetherian ring A, the skew polynomial ring of an automorphism A[t;σ] is left Noetherian. For a left ideal L, the normalized leading-coefficient ideals In​={σ−n(an​):∑j=0n​aj​tj∈L} form an ascending chain of left ideals of A. Choose finite generating lifts through the stabilization degree. Every element of L has its leading coefficient cancelled by a suitable left multiple of one of these lifts, and induction on degree proves finite generation. Passing to the skew Laurent polynomial ring preserves the property by clearing negative powers. The right-sided statement follows by applying the argument to the opposite ring.

Order filtration of a Weyl algebra (Dm=∑∣β∣≤m​C[x]∂β)

Weyl algebra OurBigBook.com
For the relative differential operators over C on a polynomial ring, coordinates have order zero and partial derivatives have order one. The associated graded ring is C[x,ξ] with degrees zero for xi​ and one for ξi​. This differs from the Bernstein filtration, in which both sets of generators have degree one and every filtration piece is finite-dimensional.

Simplicity of a Weyl algebra in characteristic zero

Words: 58
Weyl algebra OurBigBook.com
In a nonzero two-sided ideal choose an element of least Bernstein filtration degree. Commuting it with any position or differentiation generator lowers degree, so all such commutators vanish. The ordered monomial basis of a Weyl algebra and characteristic zero then force it to be a nonzero scalar. The ideal is the whole ring. The argument fails in positive characteristic, when powers of the generators can be central.

Ordered monomial basis of a Weyl algebra ({xα∂β})

Words: 51
Weyl algebra OurBigBook.com
The defining commutators [∂i​,xj​]=δij​ reorder every word into the displayed monomials. Over a characteristic-zero field, the polynomial representation proves independence: iterated commutation with the coordinate multiplications extracts a highest derivative coefficient, multiplied by the nonzero factorial β!. Hence the representation is faithful and every polynomial-coefficient operator has a unique ordered expression.

Weyl algebra in positive characteristic (k[x1​,…,xn​]/(x1p​,…,xnp​))

Words: 69
Weyl algebra OurBigBook.com
In characteristic p>0, multiplication by xi​ and formal differentiation by xi​ act on the displayed vector space, because differentiation preserves the ideal generated by the pth powers. They satisfy the Weyl algebra relations and yield a nonzero module of dimension pn. The central elements xip​ and ∂ip​ also show why the characteristic-zero simplicity argument cannot apply. In particular the characteristic-zero Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules fails for this finite-dimensional module.

Jacobson radical as greatest quasinilpotent ideal (J(A)=max{I◃A:I⊆{x:r(x)=0}})

Words: 64
Jacobson radical OurBigBook.com
The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical implies that every radical element is a quasinilpotent element, because 1−x/λ is invertible for every nonzero λ. Conversely, if a two-sided ideal consists of quasinilpotent elements, then yx is quasinilpotent for every member x and every y∈A. Hence 1+yx is invertible and x lies in the Jacobson radical. The maximality statement concerns ideals contained in the quasinilpotent set, not the whole set itself.

Jacobson radical is independent of handedness (⋂Lmax​=⋂Imax​)

Words: 89
Jacobson radical OurBigBook.com
The intersection of maximal left ideals is the intersection of annihilators of simple modules on the left, hence is a two-sided ideal. If j belongs to it, 1−rj has a left inverse for every r. Its left inverse also differs from 1 by a radical element and has a left inverse; this makes the original inverse two-sided. The identity (1−ba)−1=1+b(1−ab)−1a then shows that 1−jr is invertible too. If j were outside a maximal right ideal I, one would have 1−jr∈I for some r, a contradiction. Applying the same argument to the opposite ring proves equality.

Unit criterion for the Jacobson radical (x∈J(R)⟺1−axb∈R× for all a,b∈R)

Words: 116
Jacobson radical OurBigBook.com
The Jacobson radical is the intersection of the ideals AnnR​(S) over all simple modules, where Ann is the annihilator of a module, so it is a two-sided ideal. If y∈J(R), the left ideal R(1−y) cannot be proper, since a containing maximal left ideal would also contain y and hence 1. Thus c(1−y)=1 for some c. Now c=1+cy also differs from 1 by a radical element and has a left inverse d. Multiplying c(1−y)=1 by d gives d=1−y, and hence (1−y)c=1. This proves the unit assertion. Conversely, if x is outside a maximal left ideal L, then ax+l=1 for some a∈R, l∈L. Consequently 1−ax∈L cannot be a unit, proving the reverse direction.

Nilpotent ideal with semisimple quotient radical criterion (IN=0, J(A/I)=0⇒I=J(A))

If I is a nilpotent ideal and J(A/I)=0, then I=J(A). Every 1−ax, x∈I, has a finite geometric-series inverse, giving I⊆J(A). The radical's image lies in J(A/I)=0, giving the reverse inclusion. A semisimple algebra as quotient supplies the required zero radical.

Radical equality for finitely generated integer algebras (Jac(R)=(0)​)

Words: 78
Jacobson ring OurBigBook.com
For a finite-type integer algebra, a nonnilpotent element r gives a nonzero localization of a ring R[1/r]. A maximal ideal there has finite residue field by the finite-field theorem for finitely generated integer algebras. The image of R in that field is a finite integral domain, hence a field. Its kernel is therefore maximal in R and avoids r. Nilpotents lie in every maximal ideal, proving the radical equality. Applying the same argument to every prime quotient shows that these algebras are Jacobson rings. Merely contracting a localized maximal ideal without the finite-image argument would not prove maximality.

Injective cogenerator of abelian groups (Q/Z)

Words: 98
Injective module OurBigBook.com
The divisible group D=Q/Z is injective: every homomorphism nZ→D extends to Z by choosing an nth division of its value at n, and the Baer criterion applies. It is a cogenerator because any nonzero element generates a cyclic subgroup admitting a character nonzero on that element, and injectivity extends that character to the whole group. Consequently a map u:T→U of abelian groups is injective exactly when restriction U∗→T∗ is surjective: injectivity gives extension of characters, while a nonzero kernel element is detected by a character that cannot extend along u.

Local criterion for injectivity over a Noetherian ring (M injective⟺MP​ injective for every prime P)

Words: 58
Injective module OurBigBook.com
For a Noetherian ring, injectivity of a module can be tested at all prime localizations. By the Baer criterion, test ExtR1​(R/J,M) for every ideal J. Localization of Ext over a Noetherian ring identifies its localization with the corresponding ideal test over RP​. Every ideal of RP​ is extended from its contraction, and localization detects zero elements applies to the resulting Ext module even when it is not finitely generated. These observations prove both directions.

Injective hull of an Ore domain (E(R​R)=Q)

Words: 81
Injective hull OurBigBook.com
For a domain satisfying the left Ore condition, its division ring of fractions Q is an injective essential extension of the left regular module. For a homomorphism f:I→Q on a nonzero left ideal, choose a∈I∖{0}. A common left multiple sx=ta gives sf(x)=tf(a), hence f(x)=xa−1f(a); right multiplication by a−1f(a) extends it. The Baer criterion proves injectivity. Clearing a left denominator proves essentiality. The same calculation gives EndR​(R​Q)≅Qop, with the opposite ring required because right multiplications compose in reversed order.

Local endomorphism ring of a uniform injective module

Words: 83
Injective hull OurBigBook.com
The injective hull of a uniform module is uniform. An injective endomorphism of a uniform injective module splits its image, so its complementary summand must vanish and it is an automorphism. Consequently all nonunits are precisely the noninjective endomorphisms. Their nonzero kernels are essential; intersection of two such kernels is nonzero, making their sum noninjective. Composition on either side preserves noninjectivity, unless the other map is a unit, which merely transports the kernel. Thus the nonunits form the unique maximal ideal, and the quotient is a division ring.

A prime ring is a nonzero ring whose zero ideal is a prime ideal of a noncommutative ring. Equivalently, the product of any two nonzero two-sided ideals is nonzero. Prime radical of a noncommutative ring (N(A))

Words: 156 Articles: 1
Prime ideal of a noncommutative ring OurBigBook.com
The prime radical is the intersection of all prime ideals of a noncommutative ring. For a nonzero ring satisfying the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals, there exist prime ideals of a noncommutative ring P1​,…,Pr​ with P1​⋯Pr​=0. Indeed, a maximal counterexample among two-sided ideals could not itself be a prime ideal of a noncommutative ring, and two larger witness ideals would contradict its maximality. Every prime ideal of a noncommutative ring contains one Pi​, so the intersection equals ⋂i​Pi​, and its rth power is zero. This nilpotence assertion fails without a chain condition.

Finite minimal primes and nilpotent prime radical

Words: 86
Prime radical of a noncommutative ring OurBigBook.com
In a left Noetherian ring, Noetherian induction shows that every proper two-sided ideal contains a finite product of prime ideals of a noncommutative ring that contain it. A maximal counterexample would be nonprime and contain a product of two strictly larger ideals; their prime products give the contradiction. Every prime over the original ideal contains a factor, so the inclusion-minimal factors give all its minimal primes over an ideal. Replacing each factor by a minimal prime beneath it gives a product of minimal primes inside the original ideal, with repetitions allowed. Applying this to zero makes a power of the prime radical zero.
