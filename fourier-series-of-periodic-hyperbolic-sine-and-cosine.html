Fourier series of periodic hyperbolic sine and cosine

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier series
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
For β>0, periodically extend cosh(βx) and sinh(βx) from −1<x≤1 with period 2. Their Fourier series are
cosh(βx)=βsinhβ​+2βsinhβ∑n=1∞​β2+n2π2(−1)ncos(nπx)​,
(11)
and, away from odd integers,
sinh(βx)=2πsinhβ∑n=1∞​β2+n2π2(−1)n+1nsin(nπx)​.
(12)
The first identity follows by integration of cosh(βx)cos(nπx) on [−1,1], using the antiderivative
β2+n2π2βsinh(βx)cos(nπx)+nπcosh(βx)sin(nπx)​.
(13)
For the second identity, integration by parts and dcosh(βx)/dx=βsinh(βx) show that its sine coefficient is −nπ/β times the first series' cosine coefficient. This avoids assuming that a Fourier series can always be differentiated term by term. The periodic hyperbolic cosine is continuous, so its series converges to it everywhere. The periodic hyperbolic sine has a jump discontinuity at each odd integer: the Dirichlet-Jordan convergence theorem gives the average of its two limiting values, namely zero, there. Both identities hold in the sense of mean-square convergence, which suffices for Parseval's identity.
