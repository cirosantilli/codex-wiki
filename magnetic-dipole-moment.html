Magnetic dipole moment (m)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic vector potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 11
A magnetic dipole moment is a vector that summarizes the strength and orientation of a localized magnetic source. For a planar current loop, it is the electric current times the vector area: m=IAn. The right-hand rule fixes the unit normal vector n: curl the fingers of the right hand along the conventional current, and the thumb points along m. This single vector predicts both the distant magnetic dipole field and the turning effect of an imposed magnetic field.[ref]
The SI unit is ampere times square metre, Am2, equivalently joule per tesla. A magnetic dipole moment is neither a magnetic field nor a magnetic monopole: a closed current loop can have a nonzero magnetic dipole moment while its net outward magnetic flux through any closed surface remains zero, as required by Gauss's law for magnetism.
For a localized, steady electric current density J(r), the more general definition is
m=21​∫R3​r×J(r)d3r.

Here the cross product is integrated over the complete source, including return paths. Steadiness requires ∇⋅J=0, and localization requires sufficiently rapid decay for the boundary terms to vanish. Under these assumptions the localized steady-current moment identities give ∫Jd3r=0: shifting the origin by a changes the proposed magnetic dipole moment by −21​a×∫Jd3r=0. An isolated open wire segment does not satisfy these assumptions. [ref].
