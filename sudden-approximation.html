Sudden approximation

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
The sudden approximation treats a rapid, finite change of a Hamiltonian operator as leaving the quantum state approximately unchanged during the switch. The energy measurement can nevertheless change immediately: the same quantum state has different probability amplitudes in the new eigenbasis. Compute their inner products with the new energy eigenstates, then apply the Born rule. The two-level sudden quench below makes this distinction quantitative without assuming the change is small.
An ideal finite step has exact quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step; a finite-duration implementation has a small evolution error under the bounded-model conditions below. An impulsive quantum kick is different: its Hamiltonian operator grows as its duration shrinks, retaining a finite integrated action and a finite change of quantum state. A sudden approximation therefore concerns short evolution during switching, not conservation of energy during externally driven change. The subsequent unitary time evolution can be substantial.[ref]
