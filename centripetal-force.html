Centripetal force (Fr​=−mv2/r)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Circular motion Centripetal acceleration
OurBigBook.com Words: 68
A centripetal force is the inward resultant force needed for circular motion. Newton's second law and centripetal acceleration give magnitude mv2/r for a particle of mass m, speed v, and path radius r. It is a role played by an actual interaction, such as tension or an electric force, rather than an additional interaction to add to a free-body diagram. A purely radial centripetal force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and leaves the speed unchanged.
