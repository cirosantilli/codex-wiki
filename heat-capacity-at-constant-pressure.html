Heat capacity at constant pressure (Cp​=(∂H/∂T)p​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Enthalpy
OurBigBook.com Words: 228 Articles: 3
For a fixed-composition closed system, the heat capacity at fixed pressure is Cp​=(∂H/∂T)p​, with enthalpy H. Under the quasistatic process assumptions in heat capacity, only pressure-volume work is permitted, so dp=0 gives δQ=dH=Cp​dT. Thus supplied heat is Q=ΔH, or Q=Cp​ΔT within a single thermodynamic phase when Cp​ is constant. For an ideal gas, the expansion work explains why this exceeds the required heat at fixed volume for the same temperature increase. Its per-unit-mass counterpart is specific heat capacity at constant pressure.
