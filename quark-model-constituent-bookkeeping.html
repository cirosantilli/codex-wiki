Here Q denotes electric charge in units of the positive elementary charge e, B is baryon number, S is strangeness, I3​ is the third component of isospin, and Y=B+S is flavor hypercharge. An antiquark reverses all five displayed values. These are flavor coordinates, not spatial coordinates or electroweak hypercharge.

Adding these columns immediately explains familiar hadrons. A proton with valence quarks uud has Q=2/3+2/3−1/3=1 and B=1; a neutron with udd has Q=0 and B=1. A positive kaon with usˉ has Q=2/3+1/3=1, B=0 and S=+1. Thus the same constituent rules describe both baryons and mesons.
Two hadrons can have identical additive labels while differing in their internal state. The Lambda baryon and neutral Sigma baryon both have valence content uds, yet their total isospin differs. Likewise, the neutral pion flavor direction (uuˉ−ddˉ)/2​ and the Eta octet state (uuˉ+ddˉ−2ssˉ)/6​ both have Q=Y=0, but their inner product vanishes. Expanding that inner product gives (1−1+0)/12​=0. Constituent counting must therefore be supplemented by quantum superposition and the symmetry of the state.
