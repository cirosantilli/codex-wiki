Hölder's inequality (∥fg∥1​≤∥f∥p​∥g∥q​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lp norm
OurBigBook.com Words: 666 Articles: 5
Hölder's inequality controls a product using separate information about its two factors. If one factor is large on part of a domain, the other factor's complementary Lp norm limits how much their product can accumulate there. The same statement bounds finite sums, integrals and expected values; no independence is required in the probabilistic version.
Let p,q∈[1,∞] be conjugate exponents, meaning
p1​+q1​=1,∞1​=0.
(70)
On a measure space, if f∈Lp and g∈Lq, then fg∈L1 and
∫∣fg∣dμ≤∥f∥p​∥g∥q​.​
(71)
Here the Lp norms are finite; at exponent ∞ the norm uses the essential supremum. For a finite vector, integration with counting measure gives
∣∑i​xi​yi​∣≤∑i​∣xi​yi​∣≤(∑i​∣xi​∣p)1/p(∑i​∣yi​∣q)1/q,1<p,q<∞.
(72)
The first step is the triangle inequality; the second is Hölder's inequality.[ref]
For finite vectors, the endpoint case p=1, q=∞ is ∣xTy∣≤∥x∥1​∥y∥∞​: bound every ∣yi​∣ by its maximum, then sum the ∣xi​∣. Swapping the factors gives the other endpoint. The choice p=q=2 gives the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
