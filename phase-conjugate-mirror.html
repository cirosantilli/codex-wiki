Phase-conjugate mirror (Ureturn​(x,y)=γUincident​(x,y)∗)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Phase conjugation
OurBigBook.com Words: 411 Articles: 1
A phase-conjugate mirror is an optical device that implements phase conjugation at its return surface. It may generate the return using an independently supplied beam, as in digital optical phase conjugation; its name specifies the field transformation, rather than a particular reflecting material. Conjugating the transverse complex amplitude and launching the returning longitudinal carrier produces the complete reversed wavevector. A spatially constant γ leaves the shape of the wavefront unchanged; a spatially varying γ would add its own wavefront error.
An ordinary flat mirror obeys the law of reflection. At a surface z=L, its reflected plane wave has wavevector (kx​,ky​,−kz​), whereas a phase-conjugate mirror returns (−kx​,−ky​,−kz​). Ordinary specular reflection preserves the tangential wavevector. At normal incidence a single plane wave can therefore look identical in the two cases, apart from an overall wave phase and strength; oblique incidence or a curved wavefront reveals the distinction. In boundary-envelope notation an ideal ordinary mirror multiplies the incident complex amplitude by a constant reflection factor r, while an ideal phase-conjugate mirror returns its complex conjugate times γ.
