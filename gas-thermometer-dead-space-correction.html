Gas thermometer dead-space correction

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature Thermometer Gas thermometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 170
Gas outside the uniform-temperature bulb of a gas thermometer contributes to its conserved particle inventory. An idealized calculation divides the connecting tube into small regions with volumes Vj​ and local temperatures Tj​. Neglect hydrostatic pressure offsets and thermal transpiration, assume a common pressure P and a local ideal gas law in each region, and let the bulb have volume Vb​ and temperature Tb​. Adding the particle inventories gives
N=kB​P​(Tb​Vb​​+∑j​Tj​Vj​​).
(84)
Thus even an ideal gas in a perfectly rigid sealed apparatus need not obey P∝Tb​: some of its particles remain in regions with other temperatures. For a stipulated bulb of 100cm3, a connecting volume of 1cm3 maintained at 300K, and bulb reference and test temperatures of 300 and 400K, conservation of N gives
P0​P​=100/400+1/300100/300+1/300​=76101​≈1.328947,Tapp​=300KP0​P​≈398.684K.
(85)
These are toy dimensions and a calculated bias, not measured data. Reducing N reduces both pressures proportionally and leaves this ratio unchanged: extrapolating away gas nonideality does not eliminate the connecting-volume error. Actual apparatus also requires the tube's thermal expansion, a measured temperature profile, and the pressure corrections omitted in this model.
