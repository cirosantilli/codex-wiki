Static-one logic hazard

 ... Theoretical computer science Computational complexity theory Circuit complexity Boolean circuit Combinational logic Hazard (logic)
OurBigBook.com Words: 256
A logic hazard in which an output intended to remain one briefly falls to zero. In a sum of products, cover every adjacent pair of one-cells in a Karnaugh map with a common product implicant. That term omits the changing input and remains one throughout the transition, holding the OR output high regardless of the other path delays. This argument assumes one input changes at a time and the circuit starts settled.
For a concrete circuit, let x=aˉcˉ, y=bc, z=cˉd, w=y+z and q=x+w. Give every gate, including each inverter, propagation delay 10 ns. Starting from a=b=c=d=1, lower c at time zero. Signal y falls at 10 ns, while z rises only at 20 ns. Their gap passes through the two OR gates, making q fall during 30–40 ns even though its steady-state value remains one.
Adding the two consensus theorem products aˉb and bd preserves the Boolean function and holds the output high across every potentially hazardous single-input transition. The resulting five-term expression factors as q=bc+(aˉ+d)(b+cˉ). When a change of c could leave the output steadily one, b=1 and aˉ+d=1 keep the second product steadily one. In the zero-to-zero case with changing c, b=0 and aˉ+d=0 keep both output products zero. Changes of a,b,d use only one polarity each and therefore have no opposite-polarity race. This factored implementation uses three OR gates, two AND gates and two NOT gates, with a 40 ns maximum input-to-output path at the stated delays.
