Simple bivector (B=u∧v)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Exterior algebra Bivector
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
A simple bivector can be expressed as one exterior product of two vectors. Changing the pair need not change its area element: u∧(v+cu)=u∧v for every scalar c, by bilinearity and u∧u=0. This operation shears the represented parallelogram while preserving its oriented area.
Every bivector in a real vector space of dimension at most three is simple, but this fails in dimension four. For an ordered basis e1​,…,e4​, consider
B=e1​∧e2​+e3​∧e4​.
(18)
The two components describe independent coordinate planes. Their square is
B∧B=2e1​∧e2​∧e3​∧e4​=0,
(19)
whereas (u∧v)∧(u∧v)=0 because the factors repeat. Thus this B cannot be one simple bivector. Over the real numbers in dimension four, the converse also holds: a nonzero bivector with B∧B=0 is simple. The nonzero factor two is important; that criterion cannot be applied unchanged in characteristic two.
