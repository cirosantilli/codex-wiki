Chemical bond

 Home Chemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 14k Articles: 87
A chemical bond associates chemical atoms or groups of chemical atoms into a sufficiently stable structure to treat it as a distinct entity. The concept covers connections within molecules and in extended solids; see the IUPAC Gold Book, chemical bond. It is useful because composition alone does not specify how the chemical atoms are connected, and those connections help determine a chemical substance's properties and possible chemical reactions.
In a covalent bond, Electrons are shared between chemical atoms. Two hydrogen chemical atoms can form a molecular hydrogen molecule, with its Electrons interacting with both atomic nuclei. In the usual description of this diatomic molecule, the potential energy as a function of nuclear separation has a minimum: moving the nuclei too close or too far raises the energy. See 7.2 Covalent Bonding.
Separating bonded chemical atoms requires energy; forming the corresponding chemical bonds releases energy. Whether an overall chemical reaction releases energy depends on both the chemical bonds broken and those formed. The lines in the stoichiometry diagram indicate connections schematically, rather than literal rods or a sequence of microscopic events.
