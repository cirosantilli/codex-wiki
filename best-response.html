Best response (BRi​(σ−i​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Game theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 150 Articles: 2
A best response maximizes a player's expected payoff while the other players' strategies are held fixed. In a finite game, write the full set of mixed strategy best responses as
BRi​(σ−i​)={τi​∈Δ(Si​):Ui​(τi​,σ−i​)=maxρi​∈Δ(Si​)​Ui​(ρi​,σ−i​)}.
(28)
This is generally a set-valued mapping, since several choices may tie. Every mixture supported on maximizing pure strategies is a best response, and mixing in a strictly worse choice reduces expected payoff.
For example, against q=3/4 in matching pennies, the row player's heads and tails expected payoffs are 1/2 and −1/2, so heads is the unique best response. Against q=1/2, all mixtures are best responses. In the asymmetric coordination example, against q=3/4 the row player gets 9/4 from A and 1/2 from B, so A is optimal. A Nash equilibrium requires everyone to be a best response simultaneously; one optimal reply alone does not establish it.
