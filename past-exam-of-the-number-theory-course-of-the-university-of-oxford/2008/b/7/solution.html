Choose a nonzero integral ideal B whose class is [A]−1; one can multiply the fractional inverse A−1 by a suitable nonzero integer to obtain such a B. By the given bound choose 0=β∈B with ∣N(β)∣≤cK​N(B). Put IA​=(β)B−1. Since (β)⊆B, multiplication by B−1 makes IA​ an integral ideal. Its class is [B]−1=[A], and multiplicativity of ideal norms gives
N(IA​)=N(B)∣N(β)∣​≤cK​.​

This proves the class representative bound without accidentally constructing a representative of the inverse class instead.
To determine the Ideal class group of Q of square root minus thirty-four, write η=−34​. The given integral basis has discriminant −136, so the imaginary-quadratic bound is 434​/π<8. Every class therefore has a representative of ideal norm at most seven, and factoring such representatives shows that the ideal class group is generated by prime ideals of ideal norm at most seven.
Two ramifies: let P2​=(2,η), with P22​=(2). Three is inert because X2+34≡X2+1 is irreducible modulo three, so its prime ideal ideal norm is nine and supplies no small generator. At five and seven the roots are ±1, so the primes of ideal norm five and seven are conjugate pairs. Set P5​=(5,η−1) and P7​=(7,η+1). Their conjugates have inverse ideal classes. No new prime ideal has ideal norm four, since the only prime above two has residue degree one; six is not a prime power. Thus [P2​],[P5​],[P7​] generate the ideal class group.
The element field norms N(4+η)=50 and N(1+η)=35, together with reduction at the split primes, give
(4+η)=P2​P52​,(1+η)=Pˉ5​P7​.
(23)
The ideal norm products verify both exponents. Consequently [P5​]2=[P2​] and [P7​]=[P5​], so the whole group is generated by [P5​] and its order divides four. The class [P2​] is not trivial: a generator would be an element of the ring of integers a+bη of field norm two, but a2+34b2=2 has no integer solution. Since [P2​]2=1, its order is exactly two. Hence [P5​] has order four and
Cl(Q(−34​))≅C4​.​
