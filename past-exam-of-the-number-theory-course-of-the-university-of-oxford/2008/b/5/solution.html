For the basis w1​,…,wd​, its discriminant is
disc(B)=det(TrK/Q​(wi​wj​))i,j​.
(11)
Equivalently it is the square of the determinant of the matrix of the d embeddings applied to the basis vectors. It is nonzero because number fields are separable. An integral basis is a basis of the ring of integers as a free abelian group; in particular it is a rational basis of the field and expresses every algebraic integer uniquely with integer coefficients.
To prove existence, start with any rational basis and multiply each vector by a suitable nonzero integer to make it integral. This is possible by clearing coefficients in its monic rational minimal polynomial of an algebraic element and rescaling the variable. Let M be the lattice spanned over Z by these integral vectors, and let D=0 be its discriminant. For any algebraic integer α=∑j​cj​wj​, each trace Tr(αwi​) is an integer. The resulting linear system has integral coefficient matrix with determinant D. Its adjugate matrix shows cj​∈D−1Z. Thus
M⊆OK​⊆D−1M.
(12)
A subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian group is free and finitely generated. Here its rank is d, since it contains M and is contained in a rank-d lattice. Its free generators are the required integral basis.
For squarefree d=1, the quadratic integral basis is
{1,(1+d​)/2}(d≡1(mod4)),{1,d​}(d≡2,3(mod4)).​
(13)
The source allows squarefree nonzero integers, so the degenerate value d=1 gives K=Q and basis {1} instead of a two-element basis.
In the biquadratic field define the three automorphisms by changing respectively the sign of r​, the sign of 2​, and both signs. Their actions are
σ1​(μ)σ2​(μ)σ3​(μ)​=a+b2​−cr​−d2r​,=a−b2​+cr​−d2r​,=a−b2​−cr​+d2r​.​
(14)
Hence the three sums are 2a+2b2​, 2a+2cr​ and 2a+2d2r​. If μ is integral, each sum is integral and lies in its displayed quadratic subfield. Their radicands are squarefree and respectively congruent to two, three and two modulo four. The quadratic basis formula therefore forces all their two coefficients to be integers, proving
2a,2b,2c,2d∈Z.​
