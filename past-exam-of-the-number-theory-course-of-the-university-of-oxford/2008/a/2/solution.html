Every automorphism fixes zero and one, and preserves sums, products, negatives and multiplicative inverses of nonzero elements. Consequently elements fixed by every member of G are closed under these operations and form a field. Artin fixed-field theorem gives [L:LG]=∣G∣​ for a finite automorphism group.
Here Q(t) is the fraction field of Q[t], with t transcendental; its elements are fractions of rational-coefficient polynomials, with nonzero denominator and the usual equality by cross-multiplication. Substitution of 1−t or 1/t defines an automorphism, each its own inverse. With στ meaning first apply τ to a field element and then σ, its image on t is 1/(1−t). Iterating gives (t−1)/t and then t, so its order is three. The relations reduce every word to at most six possibilities, and the six resulting images are
t,1−t,1/t,1/(1−t),(t−1)/t,t/(t−1).
(3)
They are distinct rational functions, so the group has six elements. Its faithful permutation action on {0,1,∞} identifies it with S3​​.
For the first fixed field put u=t(1−t). It is fixed by σ, and t satisfies X2−X+u=0 over Q(u). The degree is exactly two because the nontrivial automorphism σ fixes that field. The fixed-field degree is also two, so
Q(t)⟨σ⟩=Q(t(1−t)).​
(4)
For the inversion put v=t+1/t. It is fixed and t satisfies X2−vX+1=0. The same degree argument gives
Q(t)⟨τ⟩=Q(t+1/t).​
(5)
The source requests polynomial generators in both cases. The second requirement is impossible. If a nonconstant polynomial p(t)=ad​td+⋯+a0​ were fixed by inversion, p(t)=p(1/t) would equate a Laurent polynomial with highest exponent d>0 to one with no positive exponent, a contradiction. Thus the only polynomial invariants of inversion are constants, which cannot generate its nonconstant fixed field. The fixed field of inversion in a rational function field therefore has the second displayed rational-function generator.
