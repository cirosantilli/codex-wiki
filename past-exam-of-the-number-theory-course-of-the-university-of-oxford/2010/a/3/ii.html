An algebraically closed field is a field in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root, equivalently every such polynomial splits. An algebraic closure of K is an algebraic extension of K which is algebraically closed.
For the displayed subset K⊆C, if α,β are algebraic over K, then K(α,β)/K is finite. Sums, differences, products and nonzero inverses of its elements are algebraic over K, so K is a subfield. If a nonconstant polynomial has coefficients in K, those finitely many coefficients generate a finite extension F/K. It has a root z∈C by algebraic closure of C, and z is algebraic over F. Part (i) makes it algebraic over K, so z∈K. Hence K is algebraically closed and is an algebraic closure of K.
Every z∈K lies in the finite subextension K(z), and every element of a finite subextension is algebraic over K. Therefore K is exactly the union of all finite subextensions inside C. The two examples follow.
