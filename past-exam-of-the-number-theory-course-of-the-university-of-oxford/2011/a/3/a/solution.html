The Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials states that every symmetric polynomial in n variables over a field is a polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials, uniquely so. For the three roots, Vieta's formulas give e1​=0, e2​=p, e3​=−q.
The squared product of root differences is symmetric. Expansion in elementary symmetric polynomials gives
(α−β)2(β−γ)2(γ−α)2​=e12​e22​−4e23​−4e13​e3​−27e32​+18e1​e2​e3​=−4p3−27q2.​​
(15)
One can verify the displayed identity simply by substituting the definitions of e1​,e2​,e3​ and expanding; it is the cubic polynomial discriminant.
If the cubic is irreducible, its roots are distinct in characteristic zero, so δ=0. Its Galois group is a transitive subgroup of S3​, hence either A3​ or S3​. A permutation of the roots multiplies δ by its sign. The fixed-field assertion of the Fundamental theorem of Galois theory therefore gives δ∈Q exactly when all group elements are even. Thus
Gal(f)=A3​ if δ∈Q,Gal(f)=S3​ otherwise.​
(16)
For example, f=x3−3x−1 has no rational root: neither 1 nor −1 is a root, and these are the only possibilities by the rational root theorem. Its discriminant is 108−27=81, so δ=±9∈Q and its group is A3​.
