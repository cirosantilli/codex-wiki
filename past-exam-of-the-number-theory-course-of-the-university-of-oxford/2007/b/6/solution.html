Dedekind factorization theorem states that if K=Q(θ) with integral θ and p does not divide [OK​:Z[θ]], then a factorization f​=∏i​f​iei​​ of its minimal polynomial of an algebraic element into distinct monic irreducible polynomials over Fp​ gives
pOK​=∏i​Piei​​,Pi​=(p,fi​(θ)),N(Pi​)=pdegfi​.
(21)
The factors are distinct prime ideals; the residue degrees and ramification indices satisfy ∑i​ei​degfi​=[K:Q]. Unique prime ideal factorization holds in the ring of integers.
For a square-free integer d≡1(mod4), OK​=Z[−d​], so the index condition is automatic. If pi​∣d, the polynomial X2+d reduces to X2 modulo pi​. Hence
pi​OK​=Pi2​,Pi​=(pi​,−d​),N(Pi​)=pi​.
(22)
Therefore dOK​=(∏i​Pi​)2=(−d​)2. By unique prime ideal factorization, ∏i​Pi​=(−d​).
Let S be a nonempty proper subset and put m=∏i∈S​pi​. For d>1, 1<m<d and m is a square-free integer. If ∏i∈S​Pi​=(u+v−d​), its ideal norm gives
m=u2+dv2.
(23)
The inequality m<d forces v=0, leaving m=u2, impossible for a square-free integer greater than one. Thus every such subproduct is nonprincipal.
These are ramified prime classes for discriminant minus four d. The ideal classes [Pi​] have order dividing two. Their product is the identity, and the preceding proof rules out every other nonempty subset relation. The homomorphism F2k​→Cl(K) defined by these classes consequently has kernel exactly {0,(1,…,1)}. Its image is therefore
C2k−1​.​
(24)
The boundary case d=1 has k=0 and makes the printed exponent k−1 meaningless; its field is Q(i) with trivial ideal class group. The subgroup statement is understood for d>1, while the prime-factor and proper-subset statements are empty for d=1.
