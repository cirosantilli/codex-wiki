Comparison sorting lower bound (Cmax​(n)≥⌈log2​(n!)⌉)

 Home Computer science Algorithm Sorting algorithm Comparison sort
OurBigBook.com Words: 288
Any deterministic comparison sort correct for all orders of n distinct sort keys needs at least ⌈log2​(n!)⌉ comparisons on some input. Here n! is a factorial, log2​ a logarithm, and the brackets denote the ceiling function. This is a worst-case requirement, not a requirement on every input.[ref]
For three distinct sort keys, the diagram has one leaf for each possible ordering of the original entries a,b,c. Some paths need two comparisons, others three; two comparisons cannot distinguish all six orders.
For the proof outline, represent adaptive comparisons by a binary tree: distinct sort keys give only two outcomes per comparison. A leaf fixes an output permutation; one leaf cannot correctly handle two different strict orders. Hence there must be at least n! leaves. Maximum depth h permits at most 2h leaves, so 2h≥n! gives the bound. Even a three-way comparator has only two reachable outcomes on this distinct-key family.
The growth follows without a precise approximation to the factorial. At least ⌊n/2⌋ of its factors are at least n/2, so for n≥2,
log2​(n!)≥⌊n/2⌋log2​(n/2).
(62)
The brackets denote the floor function. This gives a constant times nlogn lower bound for large n, matching the worst-case comparison growth of merge sort.
Distinct sort keys are essential to the n! counting argument. Identical sort keys do not create n! different strict orders. If all sort keys coincide, stable sorting returns the arrival order. This does not remove the worst-case bound for a comparison sort required to handle arbitrary distinct-key inputs.
