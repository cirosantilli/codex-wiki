Fermat's theorem on stationary points (f differentiable at an interior local extremum c⟹f′(c)=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 78
If a function is differentiable at an interior local minimum or local maximum, its derivative there is zero. At a local minimum c, for small h>0 the difference quotient (f(c+h)−f(c))/h is nonnegative, while for small h<0 it is nonpositive. The one-sided limit of a function from each side equals f′(c), forcing f′(c)≥0 and f′(c)≤0. Apply the same argument to −f at a local maximum.
The converse fails: f(x)=x3 has f′(0)=0 but no local minimum or local maximum at zero. The interior hypothesis is essential; a minimum attained only at an endpoint need not have derivative zero.
