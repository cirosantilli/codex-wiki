Specific heat capacity (c=C/m)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Heat capacity
OurBigBook.com Words: 338 Articles: 4
Specific heat capacity is heat capacity per unit mass:
c=mC​,cV​=mCV​​,cp​=mCp​​.
(55)
Its units are Jkg−1K−1, and the constraint remains part of the definition. In particular, specific heat capacity at constant volume and specific heat capacity at constant pressure are different responses. For homogeneous material, doubling the mass doubles its heat capacity while leaving its specific heat capacity unchanged. [ref]
As an illustrative material model, give a fixed-volume body mass m=2kg and constant specific heat capacity at constant volume cV​=400Jkg−1K−1. With no phase transition and no other work, increasing its uniform temperature by 10K requires
CV​=mcV​=800JK−1,Q=mcV​ΔT=8000J=ΔU.
(56)
These stipulated values describe no measured substance. The right panel of the diagram in heat capacity shows the straight line Q=mcV​ΔT: a temperature increase of 5K takes 4000J of heat. If the specific heat capacity at constant volume depends on temperature, use Q=m∫T1​T2​​cV​(T)dT instead. The corresponding fixed-pressure calculation uses specific heat capacity at constant pressure and gives Q=ΔH under the assumptions in heat capacity.
Choosing between specific heat capacity, molar heat capacity and volumetric heat capacity is choosing whether to compare equal mass, equal amount of substance or equal volume. A small sample with large specific heat capacity can still have less total heat capacity than a much larger sample.
