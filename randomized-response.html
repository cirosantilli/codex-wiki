Randomized response (q=(1−x)+(2x−1)p)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
Randomized response masks which of two complementary binary questions a person answered. Suppose the sensitive property has probability p, and independently the direct question is selected with known probability x, while the complementary question is selected otherwise. Truthful answers then have yes probability q=xp+(1−x)(1−p). The investigator observes only the answer, preserving uncertainty about an individual's status while permitting population statistical estimation when x=1/2.
For n independent responses, Y∼Bin(n,q), and the affine estimator p​=(Y/n−1+x)/(2x−1) is an unbiased estimator. Its variance is q(1−q)/[n(2x−1)2], by the mean and variance of a binomial distribution. Equivalently the variance is p(1−p)/n+x(1−x)/[n(2x−1)2], exposing the extra sampling uncertainty caused by the masking. At x=1/2, all p produce q=1/2 and identifiability fails. At x=0 or 1, there is no masking and the extra variance vanishes.
Because 0≤p≤1, maximum likelihood estimation gives p​=min(1,max(0,p​)). This follows by maximizing the binomial likelihood over the interval q∈[min(x,1−x),max(x,1−x)] and then inverting its monotone affine parametrization. Clipping can introduce bias: for n=1, x=3/4 and p=0, the clipped estimate has mean 1/4.
