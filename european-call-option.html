European call option (CT​=(ST​−K)+)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Fundamental theorem of asset pricing
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 24
A European call option gives its holder the right, without an obligation, to buy one unit of an underlying financial asset at the strike price K on the expiry date T. European describes exercise timing, not geography: exercise is allowed only at T. Here the underlying is one stock share, ST​ is its price at expiry, and the financial payoff is expressed as a cash amount. Physical exercise delivers a share against payment of K; immediate resale gives the same net amount under the assumed frictionless market.
The holder exercises only when buying at the strike price is advantageous. Writing x+=max(x,0) for the positive part, the terminal financial payoff is
CT​=(ST​−K)+={0,ST​−K,​ST​≤K,ST​>K.​
(3)
The European call option is a reusable building block for claim replication: its financial payoff is zero below the strike price and rises by one unit for each unit of stock price above it. The corner at K lets combinations of European call options construct other payoff shapes, as in static replication on a finite terminal support.

A financial payoff excludes the initial contract price. If the illustrative European call option costs C0​=10, its financial payoff at ST​=120 is 20, and subtracting its initial cost gives 10. If that cost is instead borrowed for one year at the constant continuously compounded interest rate r=0.05, the terminal net amount is 20−10e0.05≈9.48728904. At ST​=80 or 100, the corresponding net amount is −10e0.05≈−10.51271096. Keeping all cash amounts at the same date avoids confusing a financial payoff with a financed gain.
Robert Shiller's Options Markets lecture, Yale ECON 252 (2011) introduces these exercise rights and payoff shapes.
