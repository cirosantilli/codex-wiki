Lossless two-channel thickness scattering model (p0​=cos2(κt),pg​=sin2(κt))

OurBigBook.com Words: 377
Consider an original illustrative model with exactly two outgoing scattering channels: a central beam 0 and one diffracted beam g. Let t≥0 be specimen thickness and κ>0 a constant coupling with units of inverse length. Normalize the probability amplitudes A0​,Ag​ to incident probability current, assume equal longitudinal speeds, and specify
dtdA0​​=iκAg​,dtdAg​​=iκA0​,A0​(0)=1,Ag​(0)=0.
(3)
These ordinary differential equations define a lossless toy model, not a fit to the silicon example or a complete theory of crystal scattering. Differentiating once more gives A0′′​=−κ2A0​, so
A0​=cos(κt),Ag​=isin(κt),p0​=∣A0​∣2=cos2(κt),pg​=∣Ag​∣2=sin2(κt).
(4)
The identity p0​+pg​=1 expresses conservation within these two scattering channels. Alternatively, substituting the ordinary differential equations into d(∣A0​∣2+∣Ag​∣2)/dt makes the two cross terms cancel. Here ideal bright-field transmission electron microscopy accepts fraction p0​ and rejects fraction pg​; ideal dark-field transmission electron microscopy accepts precisely the rejected channel. This complementarity requires the declared two-channel completeness and absence of loss.
Even with known κ, thickness is ambiguous: κt=π/4 and 3π/4 both give (p0​,pg​)=(1/2,1/2). Near t=0, pg​≃(κt)2; extending that approximation through a full oscillation would erase the ambiguity incorrectly. The model isolates reversible transfer between probability amplitudes; it does not say that every thicker specimen must look darker.
Real dark-field transmission electron microscopy usually collects only a subset of the directions rejected in bright-field transmission electron microscopy. Other elastic scattering directions, inelastic scattering and detector losses prevent a general two-image conservation rule. To keep loss and background separate, extend the counting model explicitly. Let ℓ be the fraction absent from both recorded beam channels, p0​+pg​=1 describe the remaining fraction, N be incident count per exposure, ηj​ the detector efficiency and bj​ its mean additive background. Then the expected value of recorded count nj​ is
E[nj​]=ηj​N(1−ℓ)pj​+bj​,j∈{0,g}.
(5)
For separately acquired equal-illumination exposures with known bj​,ηj​, the two corrected mean counts sum to N(1−ℓ), not necessarily N. Removal from these channels is not synonymous with physical absorption: an electron can leave the chosen acceptance or change kinetic energy without being stopped in the specimen. Background subtraction removes a mean offset, but its counting variance remains.
