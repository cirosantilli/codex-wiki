Configuration model

Words: 194 Articles: 2
Random graph OurBigBook.com
For a prescribed degree sequence with even sum M=∑i​di​, attach di​ labelled half-edges to vertex i and choose a uniform perfect matching of the M half-edges. Each pair becomes an edge, giving a multigraph that may have self-loops or parallel edges. The number of pairings is M!/[2M/2(M/2)!]. Every simple graph with that degree sequence has exactly ∏i​di​! pairing representations, so conditioning on simplicity produces the uniform simple-graph model.

Bounded-degree pairing avoidance estimate (Pr(simple and avoiding G0​)=exp(−λ/2−λ2/4−μ)+o(1))

Words: 97
Configuration model OurBigBook.com
For bounded vertex degrees, total degree M→∞, and a forbidden graph G0​ of bounded maximum degree, put λ=M−1∑i​di​(di​−1) and μ=M−1∑ij∈E(G0​)​di​dj​. Count prescribed loop pairs, double-edge pairs, and forbidden-edge pairs in the configuration model. Their means are respectively λ/2+o(1), λ2/4+o(1), and μ+o(1). A fixed collection of t disjoint prescribed pairs occurs with probability [(M−1)(M−3)⋯(M−2t+1)]−1. Bounded degrees make intersecting bad configurations contribute O(M−1) to each fixed factorial moment. Thus the total bad-pattern count has factorial moments (λ/2+λ2/4+μ)j+o(1). The Bonferroni inequalities and uniformly bounded means give the displayed zero-pattern probability, without requiring the parameters to converge.

A half-edge records an incidence of an edge at a vertex. Two half-edges form each edge, including two incidences at the same vertex for a self-loop. In the configuration model these incidences are individually labelled before random pairing.

In preferential attachment, new edges favour existing vertices in proportion to a prescribed attractiveness, often their current degree of a vertex. Linearized chord diagram model (Gn(1)​)

Words: 259 Articles: 2
Preferential attachment OurBigBook.com
Start with one self-loop, and at step t add one vertex and one edge. An old vertex i is chosen with probability di​(t−1)/(2t−1), and the new vertex is chosen with probability 1/(2t−1), producing a self-loop. Self-loops contribute two to the degree of a vertex. There are n edges and total vertex degree 2n at time n. This exact normalization is distinct from treating the total vertex degree as the number of edges.

First-vertex degree in the LCD model (Pr(d1​(n)≥yn​)→e−y2/4)

In a uniform linearized chord diagram, Pr(n​R1​≥x)=(1−x2/n)n→e−x2. Conditional on the right endpoints, d1​(n)=2+∑i=2n​1{Li​≤R1​}​, with independent success probabilities R1​/Ri​. A Chernoff bound gives Ri​=(1+o(1))i/n​ uniformly for i≥n1/10. The early indicators are o(n​), and the remaining conditional mean is (2+o(1))nR1​. Their conditional variance is OP​(n​). Uniform linearized chord diagram

Words: 129
Linearized chord diagram model OurBigBook.com
Pair 2n linearly ordered endpoints uniformly. Each pair is a chord; its larger endpoint ends a vertex block, with all endpoints after the previous right endpoint merged into that vertex. Orient the chord from its right-endpoint block to its left-endpoint block. Removing the last endpoint and its mate leaves a uniform smaller pairing. Reinserting the mate into one of 2n−1 equally likely gaps chooses an existing block proportionally to its endpoint count, or the final gap creates a self-loop. This proves equivalence with the linearized chord diagram model.
An equivalent continuous representation draws n independent pairs of independent uniform points on [0,1], orders the pairs by their maxima R1​<⋯<Rn​, and calls their minima Li​. The Ri2​ are uniform order statistics; conditional on all the Ri​, the Li​ are independent uniform points on [0,Ri​].

Random k-out graph (Gk-out​)

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Random graph OurBigBook.com
Each vertex independently chooses k distinct other vertices uniformly, and an undirected edge exists when either endpoint chooses the other. This has dependent undirected edges and is not a binomial random graph. Coupling the choices as the first k entries of independent random permutations makes the graphs increasing in k.

Rado graph (Guniv(2)​)

Words: 71
Random graph OurBigBook.com
The Rado graph is the unique countably infinite graph with the two-uniform hypergraph extension property. Equivalently, for any disjoint finite vertex sets U,W, some new vertex is adjacent to every member of U and no member of W. The back-and-forth method proves uniqueness. Independent edge sampling on the natural numbers with any fixed p∈(0,1) produces this graph almost surely. It contains both edges and nonedges and therefore is not a complete graph. Its first-order axioms constitute the theory of the random graph.

Achlioptas process (Gm+1​=Gm​∪{em,ωm​​})

Words: 755 Articles: 4
Random graph OurBigBook.com
At each step offer a fixed number of independent uniform candidate edges on a fixed vertex set, and let a rule select one. The standard two-choice model offers two candidates. The rule may depend on the entire previously exposed history. Repeated already present edges make no change in the independent-candidate version; another usual version offers only absent edges. The forced merging of large components and persistence of unsampled graph components apply to both over a linear number of steps. Fixed choice is essential in the continuity of fixed-choice percolation argument.

Continuity of fixed-choice percolation

Words: 109
Achlioptas process OurBigBook.com
A fixed-choice Achlioptas process cannot have its largest component of a graph jump from o(n) to a fixed positive fraction of n within o(n) steps, with high probability. The proof combines forced merging of large components and persistence of unsampled graph components. An assumed jump forces positive vertex mass in each band [k,Dk) slightly before the jump: insufficient smaller components can feed the final large component, while enough components above Dk would merge too early. Persistence carries a fixed positive amount of each band to a common time. Taking sufficiently many disjoint bands with k=1,D,D2,… then counts more than n vertices. The number of offered candidates is fixed throughout this argument. Riordan and Warnke establish this obstruction and broader continuity results in their original research paper.

Persistence of unsampled graph components (N[k,Dk)​≥αn⇒N[k,Dk)​≥βn later)

Words: 308
Achlioptas process OurBigBook.com
Fix positive α, D≥2 and B>0. Suppose a size band [k,Dk) of an Achlioptas process contains at least αn vertices at time m. For every fixed k, at least βn of those vertices remain in unchanged graph components throughout the next T=⌈Bn/k⌉ steps, with high probability, where one may take β=αe−16BD/4. This is a deliberately nonoptimal positive constant.
For independent uniform candidate edges, an initial graph component with w<Dk vertices is untouched if none of the 2T candidate edges meets it. A uniform edge hits it with probability at most 3w/n. For sufficiently large n, (1−3w/n)2T≥e−16BD, so the expected value of the untouched vertex mass Y is at least αe−16BDn. Replacing one row of two candidate edges can change Y by at most 8Dk, because only the initial graph components hit by an old or new endpoint can change status. The McDiarmid inequality therefore makes Y≥αe−16BDn/2 except on an event of probability e−cn/k, for a positive constant c depending only on α,B,D. An untouched graph component stays unchanged whichever offered edge is selected.
For candidates restricted to absent edges, generate each by an independent uniform proposal stream and reject already present edges. During any interval of O(n) steps with O(n) present edges, each proposal has rejection probability O(1/n) conditional on the past. The number of rejections is at most O(logn) except on an event of probability smaller than any inverse power of n: if there were r rejections among 2T+r proposals, a union bound over their positions bounds this by (r2T+r​)(C/n)r. The first 2T proposals are independent and give the previous untouched-mass estimate. Extra proposals can touch at most 2r additional initial graph components, losing only O(Dklogn)=o(n) vertices for fixed k. This leaves the stated βn bound. A union bound makes these estimates simultaneous over starting times m≤3n. They hold through the entire interval, so deterministic or random stopping times within it are allowed.

Forced merging of large components (N≥K​(Gm​)≥an⇒L1​(Gm+⌈A(a)n/K⌉​)≥an/3)

Words: 205
Achlioptas process OurBigBook.com
For the two-choice Achlioptas process, fix a>0 and K≥1. Conditional on any current graph with N≥K​≥an, put W equal to the vertices in its large graph components. There are at most n/K such initial graph components. If after s more steps no graph component contains an/3 vertices of W, greedily assign final graph components to two groups so that each meets at least ∣W∣/3≥an/3 vertices of W. This induces a set partition of the initial large graph components, of which there are at most 2n/K possibilities.
For any fixed such split W=A∪B, a uniform candidate edge joins A to B with probability at least 2a2/9. If both candidates do so, the selected edge cannot avoid the crossing. Thus the probability that the split survives all s steps is at most (1−a4/81)s. The deliberately weaker constant also covers distinct candidate sampling for sufficiently large n. In the absent-edge version, as long as the split has survived, every A--B edge is absent, so conditioning on absence can only increase this crossing probability. A union bound with s=⌈A(a)n/K⌉ and A(a)=81(log2+2)/a4 bounds failure by e−2n/K. For fixed K, a further union bound makes the conclusion simultaneous over all starting steps m≤3n. This proof is independent of how the rule chooses between the two candidates.

This is the total number of vertices in graph components with orders in the specified half-open interval. It differs from the number of those graph components. Simultaneous giant for fixed random-edge choice (P(∀ω∈[k]cn:L1​(Gω​)≥2n/3)→1)

Words: 96
Random graph OurBigBook.com
Offer k independent uniform edges of the complete graph per row and permit any one to be selected from each row. If a chosen graph has no graph component of order at least 2n/3, the balanced component cut provides an empty cut whose sides both have at least n/3 vertices. Each candidate edge crosses that cut with probability at least 4/9, so a row can avoid crossing with probability at most 1−(4/9)k. The union bound over at most 2n cuts gives failure probability at most 2n[1−(4/9)k]cn. Any fixed integer c with c[−log(1−(4/9)k)]>log2 suffices. The guarantee is simultaneous even for choices made after seeing every offered edge.

Giant component (∣C∣=Θ(n))

Words: 246 Articles: 2
Random graph OurBigBook.com
A giant component in a sequence of random graphs on n vertices has order proportional to n. Its existence can change at a threshold function for a monotone graph property. A giant component need not contain every vertex, and its uniqueness requires proof for the model in question.

Barely-supercritical largest-component expectation (EL1​(G(n,(1+ε)/n))∼2εn)

Words: 106
Giant component OurBigBook.com
Suppose ε=o(1) and ε≥n−1/6. The breadth-first exploration of a binomial random graph has deterministic drift F(t)=εt−t2/(2n)+O(ε3n+ε) up to 3εn steps. Its weighted martingale has variance O(εn). For h=ε​+(nε3)−1/8, the Doob L2 maximal inequality makes its maximum smaller than hε2n/6 with high probability. Then At​>0 throughout [hεn,(2−h)εn], giving a graph component of order (2−o(1))εn.
For the upper expectation bound, let K=⌈ε−3⌉. The dominating binomial branching process has branching survival probability (2+o(1))ε by the binomial branching survival correction. Its branching process conditioned on extinction has mean 1−ε+O(ε2+ε/n) and total-progeny expected value O(1/ε). Therefore EN≥K​≤nρ+O(n/(εK)). Since L1​≤K+N≥K​ and K=o(εn), this yields the matching upper bound. Controlling rare large components is necessary to conclude an expected value asymptotic from a typical-size statement.

Logarithmic-regime giant component (L1​=n−(1+o(1))n1−γ,p=γlogn/n)

Words: 102
Giant component OurBigBook.com
Fix k≥1, γ0​>1/(k+1), and γ0​≤γ≤1−ω(n)/logn with ω(n)→∞. A binomial random graph has one giant component of the displayed order and all other graph components are tree components of orders at most k, with high probability. The expected value and variance of the isolated vertex count concentrate it around n1−γ→∞. A Cayley formula upper bound for connected sets excludes component sizes k+1 through a small fixed fraction of n; the empty-graph cut bound excludes the remaining sizes up to n/2. An extra-edge count excludes cyclic small graph components. The expected value of the number of vertices in small nonisolated tree components is O(nde−2d)=o(ne−d), where d=np. The Markov inequality completes the count of vertices outside the unique large graph component.

Breadth-first exploration of a binomial random graph (Ut​=(1−p)(Ut−1​−bt​)−Dt​)

Words: 93
Erdős-Rényi model OurBigBook.com
During breadth-first search of G(n,p) let Ut​ count unseen vertices, At​ active vertices, and t explored vertices. When At−1​=0 start a new unseen root, recorded by bt​=1; otherwise bt​=0. The newly discovered count is conditionally Bin(Ut−1​−bt​,p). Subtract its conditional mean to obtain a martingale difference Dt​. With q=1−p, U0​=n and A0​=0, iteration gives
At​=n−t−nqt+∑j≤t​qt−j+1bj​+qt∑j≤t​q−jDj​.
(1)
The last sum is a martingale. Positive At​ over an interval means that the graph component under exploration does not finish there. Independently adding phantom children couples each component exploration below a binomial branching process with offspring law Bin(n,p).

Uniform random graph process (Gn,m​)

Words: 345 Articles: 3
Erdős-Rényi model OurBigBook.com
Start with the empty graph and reveal the edges of the complete graph in a uniformly random order. After m steps its edge set is a uniform m-element subset. Independent uniform edge labels couple this process with all binomial random graphs: G(n,p)=Gn,M(p)​, where M(p) has a binomial distribution with parameters (2n​),p. For a monotone graph property, concentration of M(p) transfers a threshold estimate between edge probability and edge count.

Isolated clique appearance in a uniform random graph process

Words: 104
Uniform random graph process OurBigBook.com
In a uniform random graph process, realize edge arrivals by independent continuous uniform times. For a fixed set of r vertices to form an isolated clique at some time, all e=(2r​) internal edges must arrive before the first of the r(n−r) outgoing edges. Their relative arrival ordering is uniform, so the exact probability is
(er(n−r)+e​)−1.
(2)
A union bound over the vertex sets therefore bounds the probability that such an isolated clique ever appears by (rn​)/(er(n−r)+e​). For fixed r≥4 this is O(nr−e)→0, since e>r. This argument controls all process times at once, including the short interval during which an isolated component could exist, without discretizing time.

Pendant extensions of sparse clique copies
Words: 85
Sparse clique-count concentration OurBigBook.com
If H is formed by attaching one pendant edge to a fixed clique, each copy has a unique clique core. Its number of extensions is the number of ambient edges from that core to outside vertices. At a scale where the core count is tight and its expected extension count diverges sufficiently rapidly, a Chernoff bound uniformly over all possible cores shows that the total count, divided by the extension mean, has the same limiting law as the core count. Non-induced copies are intended; extra ambient edges are not forbidden.
