X-ray fluorescence

OurBigBook.com Words: 151
X-ray fluorescence is Characteristic X-ray emission following excitation by incident photons. In a simple initially unexcited chemical atom, photoionization can eject an inner electron if the incident photon supplies its binding energy. An outer electron can then fill the vacancy, releasing an emitted photon with the difference of shell binding energies. Nonradiative relaxation competes with this route, so one absorbed incident photon need not yield one escaping characteristic photon.
Choose a separate stipulated electron shell model with values BK​=10.0, BL​=1.5 and BM​=0.3, all in keV. Ignore subshell splitting, recoil and changes of binding energy during relaxation. The possible shell differences are
EKα​=BK​−BL​=8.5keV,EKβ​=BK​−BM​=9.7keV,ELα​=BL​−BM​=1.2keV.
(21)
These labels denote simplified shell groups, not resolved subshell electronic transitions. With hc=1.23984keVnm, their calculated wavelengths are respectively 0.145864, 0.127819 and 1.03320nm. An incident 9.8keV photon exceeds both K-line energies but cannot directly create the K vacancy in this initially unexcited model. A 12.0keV photon can, leaving approximately 2.0keV of photoelectron kinetic energy. This comparison fixes neither the excitation probability nor the radiative branching probability.
