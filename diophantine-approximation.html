Transcendence theory

Words: 1k Articles: 13
Diophantine approximation OurBigBook.com
Transcendence theory proves that particular numbers are not algebraic numbers, or that several numbers satisfy no nonzero polynomial relation with algebraic coefficients. A recurring method constructs an auxiliary expression which is simultaneously too small analytically and too large arithmetically if the proposed values were algebraic.

Schneider-Lang theorem (∣S∣≤[K:Q](ρ1​+ρ2​))

Words: 87
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
Let f1​,…,fs​ be meromorphic functions with fj′​∈K[f1​,…,fs​] for a fixed number field K. Suppose f1​,f2​ are algebraically independent over C and have finite orders ρ1​,ρ2​. The set S of points where every fj​ is finite and belongs to K satisfies the displayed bound. The orders are growth orders for meromorphic functions. For f1​=z, f2​=ez, the orders are zero and one and the functions are algebraically independent; an algebraic value of eα at nonzero algebraic α would produce infinitely many such points nα.

Symmetric Hermite integral obstruction (k+∑j​bj​eβj​=0)

Words: 107
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
Suppose nonzero algebraic numbers βj​ form a Galois-stable set, with Galois-invariant integer weights bj​, and k=0 is an integer. Choose P∈Z[X] vanishing at all βj​ with P(0)=0, and a positive integer c making all cβj​ algebraic integers. For a large prime p, put D=(1+degP)p−1 and fp​(X)=cDXp−1P(X)p/(p−1)!, and let Fp​ be the sum of its derivatives. The weighted sum kFp​(0)+∑bj​Fp​(βj​) is an integer, nonzero modulo p when p∤kcP(0). If the displayed exponential expression vanished, integrating (e−zFp​(z))′=−e−zfp​(z) along the finitely many segments to βj​ would bound that nonzero integer by Cp/(p−1)!→0. This contradiction proves the obstruction and supplies direct proofs for e and π.

Gelfond–Schneider theorem

Words: 108 Articles: 1
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
For algebraic a=0,1 and algebraic irrational b, every value exp(bλ) with eλ=a is transcendental. Consequently any quotient of chosen nonzero logarithms of algebraic numbers is either rational or transcendental.

Schneider lattice proof of the Gelfond–Schneider theorem

Words: 78
Gelfond–Schneider theorem OurBigBook.com
Assume a and ab algebraic with algebraic irrational b. Apply Siegel lemma to construct a small-coefficient polynomial P vanishing at the algebraic pairs (s+tb,as(ab)t) on a large square grid. The function P(z,eλz) is a nonzero exponential polynomial. Its many initial zeros make later grid values exponentially tiny, whereas an algebraic norm estimate forbids such tiny nonzero values. Iterating extends the zero grid indefinitely. Its quadratic zero count in disks contradicts the linear zero-count growth of an exponential polynomial.

Mahler method

Words: 283 Articles: 3
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
Mahler's method uses functional equations relating a function at z and at zl to transport an auxiliary polynomial to points rapidly approaching zero. High vanishing order gives an upper bound which conflicts with an algebraic norm or height lower bound under assumed algebraicity of the values. Functional transcendence ensures that the auxiliary expression is not identically zero.

Fredholm lacunary series (fl​(z)=∑n≥0​zln,l≥2)

Words: 226 Articles: 2
Mahler method OurBigBook.com
This lacunary series is analytic for ∣z∣<1 and satisfies fl​(zl)=fl​(z)−z. At roots of unity of l-power order, its radial tail diverges, giving a dense set of singularities on the unit circle and excluding algebraicity as a function. The Mahler method proves transcendence of its values at every algebraic z with 0<∣z∣<1. At z=1/2, reduced truncations have denominators 2lm and error less than twice their −lth power; for l≥3, the Roth theorem gives a shorter proof.
Roth criterion for rational Fredholm values (l(1−logp/logq)>2)
Words: 77
Fredholm lacunary series OurBigBook.com
For 0<p<q, truncating the Fredholm series at n gives a rational with denominator at most Qn​=qln and positive error at most 2Qn−l(1−logp/logq)​ for large n. The displayed strict inequality first rules out a rational value by the rational separation bound, and then rules out an algebraic irrational value by Roth theorem. Reduced denominators are at most Qn​ and tend to infinity because the truncations are distinct. In particular l≥3 and p<qδ, δ<1/3, imply transcendence.
Auxiliary-value height argument for the Fredholm series (PN​(z,fl​(z))=O(zTN​),TN​≥(N+1)2−1)
Words: 72

A nonzero integer polynomial of bidegree at most N can cancel the first (N+1)2−1 Taylor coefficients of the Fredholm series. Functional transcendence makes the resulting analytic function nonzero. If α and fl​(α) were both algebraic, the relation fl​(αlr)=fl​(α)−∑j<r​αlj would keep the auxiliary values in one fixed number field. Their Absolute logarithmic Weil height is at most Nlrh(α)(1+1/(l−1))+ON​(r+1), whereas their chosen absolute value is ON​(∣α∣TN​lr). The Liouville height inequality forces TN​=O(N), contradicting its quadratic lower bound for sufficiently large N.

E-function (f(z)=∑n≥0​an​zn/n!)

Words: 216 Articles: 2
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
In the modern strict sense an E-function satisfies a nonzero linear differential equation over Q​(z), has algebraic coefficients an​ with all conjugates bounded by Cn+1, and has common positive denominators dn​≤Cn+1 making dn​a0​,…,dn​an​ algebraic integers. Siegel's original arithmetic growth condition permits, for each ϵ>0, bounds of size Oϵ​(nϵn) instead. The strict and original growth formulations should be distinguished rather than asserted equivalent. Exponentials with algebraic exponent satisfy the stronger conditions and a first-order differential equation.

Addition closure of Siegel E-functions (f,g∈E⟹f+g∈E)

Words: 65
E-function OurBigBook.com
Work in the compositum of the two coefficient fields. Conjugate coefficient bounds add, while multiplying the common denominators clears both coefficient lists. For Siegel's original bounds Oϵ​(nϵn), use ϵ/2 in each denominator estimate. A nonzero linear differential equation for the sum follows because its derivatives lie in the sum of the two finite-dimensional derivative spaces over the rational function field. The stricter exponential arithmetic bounds are preserved as well.

Siegel–Shidlovsky theorem

Words: 70
E-function OurBigBook.com
Let a vector of E-functions satisfy f′=A(z)f with A∈Mats​(Q​(z)). At a nonzero algebraic point ξ which is not a pole of A, the transcendence degree of the values over Q​ equals the transcendence degree of the functions over Q​(z). In particular algebraically independent functions give algebraically independent values. The exponential system fj​=eγj​z, for rationally independent algebraic γj​, recovers the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem by evaluating at one.

Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem

Words: 164 Articles: 1
Transcendence theory OurBigBook.com
For distinct algebraic numbers α1​,…,αs​, the numbers eα1​,…,eαs​ are linearly independent over Q​. Equivalently, exponentials of numbers linearly independent over Q are algebraically independent over Q​. The special case of a nonzero algebraic exponent gives a transcendental exponential. In particular eiπ=−1 forces π to be transcendental.

Hermite auxiliary-polynomial proof for pi

Words: 114
Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem OurBigBook.com
Under an assumed algebraicity of iπ, form the product ∏j​(1+eαj​) over its conjugates; its one known vanishing factor makes the whole product zero and regroup the subset sums as k+∑bj​eβj​=0, with nonzero exponents and Galois-invariant integral weights. After clearing their denominators, use fp​(z)=(cz)p−1∏j​(cz−cβj​)p/(p−1)!. The sum of its derivatives at each βj​ is divisible by p in the algebraic integers, whereas at zero its first nonzero derivative is a fixed nonzero residue for all but finitely many primes. Summing with the weights gives a nonzero rational integer. Integrating the derivative of e−z∑fp(j)​(z) expresses that integer as a quantity bounded by Cp/(p−1)!, which tends to zero. This is the contradiction.

Badly approximable number (infq≥1​q∥qα∥>0)

Words: 95 Articles: 1
Diophantine approximation OurBigBook.com
An irrational real number is badly approximable when ∥qα∥≥c/q for some c>0 and every positive integer q, where the double bars mean distance to the nearest integer. Equivalently every rational approximation obeys ∣α−a/q∣≥c/q2. The Diophantine bound for the square root of two gives a concrete example and supplies separated rotations for bilinear cancellation for badly approximable phases.

Diophantine bound for the square root of two (∥q2​∥≥1/(4q))

Words: 47
Badly approximable number OurBigBook.com
For the nearest integer a to q2​, the nonzero integer 2q2−a2 has absolute value at least one. Dividing by q2​+a≤4q proves the bound. Thus the points 0,2​,…,W2​ modulo one are separated by at least 1/(4W). Ordering their distances from zero gives ∑h≤W​∥h2​∥−1≪Wlog(2W).

Diophantine frequency vector (∣k⋅ω∣≥γ∣k∣1−τ​)

Words: 85 Articles: 1
Diophantine approximation OurBigBook.com
A vector ω∈Rn satisfying ∣k⋅ω∣≥γ∣k∣1−τ​ for all nonzero integer vectors k, with γ>0. This excludes resonances and controls the small divisors in analytic cohomological equations on a Diophantine torus. Different choices of norm change the constant but not the nature of the condition.

Small divisor (k⋅ω)

Words: 45
Diophantine frequency vector OurBigBook.com
A denominator such as k⋅ω which may become small for large integer vectors k. Inverting a directional derivative on a torus divides the nonzero Fourier coefficients by ik⋅ω. A Diophantine condition bounds this loss polynomially, so exponential Fourier decay still gives convergence on a smaller complex strip.

Quantitative quadratic recurrence (∥αn2∥<ε)

Words: 54
Diophantine approximation OurBigBook.com
For a universal constant C>0 and 0<ε≤1, every real α admits 1≤n≤ε−C with distance to the nearest integer of αn2 less than ε. A Fejér kernel detects failure of recurrence as a large quadratic exponential sum. The Van der Corput inequality for finite scalar sequences then produces a short linear near-return, whose suitable multiple gives the quadratic return. To include ε>1, use the bound max{1,ε−C}.

Distance to the nearest integer (∥x∥R/Z​)

For a real number x, define ∥x∥R/Z​=mink∈Z​∣x−k∣. It lies in [0,1/2] and obeys ∥ℓx∥≤∣ℓ∣∥x∥ for an integer ℓ. It is a distance on the circle, rather than a norm on the real vector space.

Separated reciprocal-distance sum (∑j​min(Q,(2∥θj​∥)−1)≪Q+δ−1log(2Q))

Words: 71
Distance to the nearest integer OurBigBook.com
If finitely many points on R/Z are pairwise separated by distance modulo one at least δ>0, then the displayed bound holds for Q≥2. An arc of length 2r contains at most 1+2r/δ points. First sum over ∥θj​∥≤Q−1, then over the dyadic annuli 2j/Q<∥θj​∥≤2j+1/Q. Their contributions are O(Q+δ−1) and O(Q2−j+δ−1), respectively. Summing proves the estimate. A point at zero contributes Q, using the convention min(Q,∞)=Q.

Exponent bound for a binomial power equation (axn−byn=c=0,max(x,y)>1)

Words: 87

For fixed positive a,b and fixed nonzero c, with x=y, put Y=max(x,y) and take Λ=log(a/b)+nlog(x/y), swapping the variables and coefficients if necessary. The equation gives log∣Λ∣≤−nlogY+O(1) when the larger-variable power is sufficiently large. A two-logarithm lower bound gives log∣Λ∣≥−C(a,b)logYlog(2n). Hence n≤Clog(2n)+O(1), so n is bounded. The cases x=y and a bounded larger-variable power are elementary. For each remaining exponent, the Thue theorem for a binomial form supplies finitely many pairs. The constant-power exception x=y=1, a−b=c, must be removed.

Effective norm-form height estimate

Let L be a fixed number field and let V⊂L be a fixed rational linear subspace for which the associated norm form has no unit-family degeneracy. There are effective constants A,C>0 such that every z∈V∩OL​ with nonzero field norm m satisfies
H(z)≤C∣m∣A.
(11)
To prove this, factor the principal ideal (z), write its generators as a bounded factor times powers of fundamental units, use the linear relations defining V, and apply the Baker lower bound for a homogeneous linear form in logarithms to bound the unit exponents by O(log∣m∣). Siegel's lemma

Words: 418 Articles: 5
Diophantine approximation OurBigBook.com
Let K be a number field of degree D, and let L1​,…,LM​ be linear forms in N>DM variables with coefficients in K and projective height at most H≥1. There is a nonzero x∈ZN satisfying every Lj​(x)=0 and
∥x∥∞​≤(NH)DM/(N−DM).
(12)
For K=Q, this follows by applying the pigeonhole principle to the images of the integer box {0,…,Y}N under (L1​,…,LM​). Expanding coefficients in a rational basis of K gives the factor D in the general count.

Pigeonhole proof of integer Siegel lemma (0<∥z∥∞​≤⌊(2NA)M/(N−M)⌋)

Words: 77
Siegel's lemma OurBigBook.com
For M<N homogeneous equations with integer coefficients of absolute value at most A≥1, map the box {0,…,Q}N to its M equation values. There are (Q+1)N inputs and at most (2NAQ+1)M outputs. Taking Q=⌊(2NA)M/(N−M)⌋ makes the first count larger, since 2NAQ+1≤2NA(Q+1). The difference of two inputs with the same output is the required nonzero bounded integer solution. Clearing denominators also treats rational equations; expanding a number-field basis replaces each field equation by finitely many rational equations.

Thue-Siegel auxiliary polynomial

Words: 230 Articles: 2
Siegel's lemma OurBigBook.com
For an algebraic number α of degree d and a small κ>0, Siegel lemma constructs integer polynomials P,Q of degree at most n and exponentially bounded coefficients for which P(X)+αQ(X) has a zero at X=α of order close to 2n/d. A Wronskian then prevents every specialization P(X)+yQ(X) from having a high-order zero at a rational point. This is the auxiliary-polynomial mechanism in the Thue-Siegel method.

Rational multiplicity bound for a linear auxiliary polynomial

Words: 107

Let F(X,Y)=P(X)+YQ(X) with P,Q∈Z[X] linearly independent, degX​F≤n, and H(F)≤Cn. For every δ>0, if a reduced rational p/q has sufficiently large denominator in terms of C and δ, then F(X,y) has multiplicity at most δn+1 at p/q, for every fixed real y.
Indeed, the nonzero Wronskian
W=PQ′−P′Q
(14)
has degree below 2n and height at most C0n​. A zero of multiplicity r of F(X,y) forces a zero of multiplicity at least r−1 of W. By Gauss lemma for polynomials, (qX−p)r−1 then divides W in Z[X], so qr−1 divides the leading coefficient of W and is at most C0n​.

Thue-Siegel rational approximation bound

If a real algebraic number α has degree d≥3, then for every ε>0 there are only finitely many reduced fractions p/q such that
​α−qp​​<q−d/2−1−ε.
(15)
The Thue-Siegel auxiliary polynomial proof chooses two such approximations, constructs an auxiliary polynomial nonzero at their pair, and compares its denominator lower bound with the upper bound supplied by its high-order zero at (α,α).

For x2−Dy2=1 with nonsquare positive integer D, the solution with x>1, y>0 and smallest x is the fundamental solution. It generates other positive solutions through powers of x+yD​ and can be found using continued fraction convergents of D​.

Negative Pell equation (x2−Dy2=−1)

Words: 30

The Diophantine equation x2−Dy2=−1, usually with a nonsquare positive integer D and integer unknowns. It need not have a solution. For D=2, multiplying u+v2​ by 3+22​ preserves norm −1 and gives (u,v)↦(3u+4v,2u+3v).

Unit reduction for quadratic norm equations (α↦αεn)

Words: 65
Pell equation OurBigBook.com
In a real quadratic integer ring, multiplying by a positive norm-one fundamental unit preserves a prescribed norm. Choose an exponent so that a positive representative lies in a half-open interval [A,Aε). Its conjugate is the prescribed norm divided by the representative, so both integer coefficients are bounded on that interval. Enumerating these reduced representatives gives a complete finite list of unit orbits of solutions.
