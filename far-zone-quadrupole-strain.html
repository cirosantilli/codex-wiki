Far-zone quadrupole strain (HijTT​=r2G​Λij,kl​(n)Q¨​kl(t−r))

In units c=1, a slowly moving isolated source has trace-free mass quadrupole moment Qij=∫T00(xixj−δij∣x∣2/3)d3x. The retarded linearized gravity solution and the stress-energy quadrupole identity give the displayed physical spatial strain, with n=x/r and the transverse-traceless projector Λ. If the spatial metric is written gij​=−δij​−2Eij​ for metric signature (+−−−), its trace-free Lorenz-gauge coefficient before the transverse projection is Eij​=GQ¨​ij​(t−r)/r. Thus HTT=2ETT. Far distance alone does not permit dropping source-dependent retardation: the quadrupole approximation also requires the source size to be small compared with the radiation wavelength.
