Charge-to-mass ratio (q/m)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Relativistic electromagnetism Electromagnetic four-potential Electromagnetic field tensor Lorentz force
OurBigBook.com Words: 70
The charge-to-mass ratio is a body's electric charge q divided by its mass m. Its SI unit is the coulomb per kilogram, Ckg−1. In a prescribed electric field and magnetic field, nonrelativistic acceleration depends on q/m through the Lorentz force. Two bodies with the same initial position, velocity, and charge-to-mass ratio therefore have the same trajectory in this ideal model. For an electron, write q=−e with e>0 the elementary charge: q/m=−e/m, whereas the positive number e/m is the magnitude of the charge-to-mass ratio.
