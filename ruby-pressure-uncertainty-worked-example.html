Construct a synthetic local calibration to show the calculation independently of the historical data. Choose reference pairs (Δλi​,pi​)=(1.00,2.69),(2.00,5.42),(3.00,8.09), with shifts in nm and pressures in GPa. The reference zero is already subtracted. Treat the shifts as exact for this fit and give the three reference pressures equal weights. Minimizing the ordinary least squares objective
S(a)=∑i​(pi​−aΔλi​)2
(38)
and setting its derivative to zero gives
a=∑i​(Δλi​)2∑i​Δλi​pi​​=14.0037.80​=2.70GPanm−1.
(39)
The regression residuals are (−0.01,0.02,−0.01)GPa. Their weighted moment ∑i​Δλi​(pi​−aΔλi​) vanishes, as required by the least-squares normal equations. An unknown reference offset requires fitting an intercept instead; forcing it to zero can bias the slope. If shift errors matter, this elementary fit is insufficient.
Use the local measurement model
p=aD,D=λ−λ0​−kT​(T−T0​).
(40)
Here p denotes pressure relative to the reference, a is calibrated sensitivity, and kT​ is a local wavelength sensitivity to temperature at fixed pressure. Choose a=2.70GPanm−1, kT​=0.010nmK−1 and a reading λ−λ0​=2.00nm at T=T0​. The inferred pressure is 5.40GPa. These coefficients and readings are stipulated, not measured ruby constants. With the same pressure, a 10K increase in temperature would add 0.100nm to the shift in this model; neglecting it would overestimate pressure by 0.270GPa.
Assign independent standard uncertainties ua​=0.020GPanm−1, uλ​=u0​=0.010nm and uT​=0.50K for the temperature difference. Here ua​ is an independently assigned calibration uncertainty, not an estimate from the regression residuals above; treat kT​ as exact in this example. First-order propagation of uncertainty follows by differentiating the measurement model:
dp=Dda+adλ−adλ0​−akT​d(T−T0​),
(41)
up2​=D2ua2​+a2(uλ2​+u02​+kT2​uT2​).
(42)
The four standard contributions are 0.0400,0.0270,0.0270,0.0135GPa, giving up​=0.0569GPa. This is a standard uncertainty, not automatically a 95% interval. Correlated reference and loaded wavelength readings require the corresponding covariance term; common wavelength-axis drift may cancel in their difference. Uncertain kT​, nonzero T−T0​, model inadequacy and unresolved deviatoric stress require a broader budget. Taking more optical readings reduces some random errors while leaving a common reference-scale error.
