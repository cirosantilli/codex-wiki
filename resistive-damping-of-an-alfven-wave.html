Resistive damping of an Alfvén wave (ω2+iηm​k∥2​ω−vA2​k∥2​=0)

Finite electrical conductivity σ allows magnetic diffusion, with magnetic diffusivity ηm​=1/(μ0​σ). In the same uniform, unbounded geometry as the Linear Alfvén wave equations, neglect viscosity but add ηm​∂z2​b⊥​ to induction. For real k∥​ and time dependence e−iωt, elimination gives
ω2+iηm​k∥2​ω−vA2​k∥2​=0,ω=−2iηm​k∥2​​±vA2​k∥2​−4ηm2​k∥4​​​.
(15)
Thus weak magnetic diffusion, ηm​∣k∥​∣≪vA​, gives an amplitude decay rate ηm​k∥2​/2. For ηm​∣k∥​∣>2vA​, both temporal roots are purely decaying; they no longer describe an oscillating traveling mode. This temporal calculation at fixed real wavenumber is different from a driven vessel at fixed real frequency, where a complex spatial wavenumber describes penetration and attenuation. Viscosity also dissipates kinetic energy, and walls introduce transverse gradients and additional losses.
For an axial length L, comparing the magnetic diffusion time L2/ηm​ with the transit time L/vA​ gives the ratio LvA​/ηm​. A large value favors propagation before diffusion, but a narrow vessel can introduce a shorter transverse diffusion scale. In the actual 1949 analysis, finite electrical conductivity and vessel dimensions restrict the useful frequency interval; above its upper limit the response becomes a skin wave.[ref]
