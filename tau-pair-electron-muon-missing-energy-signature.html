Tau-pair electron-muon missing-energy signature (e+e−→τ+τ−→e±μ∓+invisible recoil)

In the modern weak charged current interpretation, one allowed assignment is
e+e−→τ−τ+,τ−→e−νˉe​ντ​,τ+→μ+νμ​νˉτ​.
(43)
The electron antineutrino, tau neutrino, muon neutrino and antineutrino of the tau neutrino carry away energy and momentum. There are four undetected particles in this decay assignment. Interchanging which parent decays electronically gives the opposite electric charge assignment. Each parent conserves electric charge and, in this approximation, total lepton number; four-momentum conservation applies separately to production and to each particle decay.
If Be​ and Bμ​ denote the corresponding branching fractions, independent parent decays give an inclusive mixed-flavour fraction 2Be​Bμ​. The factor two counts the two assignments to opposite parent charges. For chosen Be​=Bμ​=1/5, the mixed fraction is 2/25, before acceptance and detection efficiency. Correlations between the parents' spins can affect angular selection even when the fully inclusive branching fractions factorize.
The neutrinos in that equation are a physical interpretation to test. Neither a deficit in visible energy nor two unusual tracks alone establishes their identities or uniquely counts them. Even a compatible invariant mass and an angular distribution require particle identification, background checks and agreement across further particle decay channels. The direct detection milestone for the tau neutrino was in 2000, separate from the charged tau lepton discovery.
