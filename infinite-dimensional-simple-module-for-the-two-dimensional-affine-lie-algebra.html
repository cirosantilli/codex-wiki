Infinite-dimensional simple module for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra (x=t,yf(t)=f(t−1))

OurBigBook.com Words: 63
The solvable Lie algebra Cx⊕Cy, with [x,y]=y, has an infinite-dimensional Irreducible Lie algebra representation on C[t]: xf(t)=tf(t) and yf(t)=f(t−1). Indeed [x,y]f=yf. An invariant subspace is an ideal p(t)C[t] because it is invariant under multiplication by t. Shift invariance gives p(t)∣p(t−1), hence p(t−1)=p(t) and p is constant. This shows why the Lie theorem needs finite-dimensional representations, even when the algebra itself is finite-dimensional.
