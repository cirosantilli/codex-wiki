The phrase equivalence principle covers several related claims. The weak equivalence principle concerns the universality of free fall for test particles of negligible self-gravity, with the same initial conditions and no relevant nongravitational forces. In a Newtonian notation,
mI​a=mG​g⟹a=mI​mG​​g.
(12)
Composition-independent motion requires the ratio of passive gravitational mass to inertial mass to be universal. Its common value can then be absorbed into the normalization of g; the empirical assertion is universality, not the numerical convention used to express the ratio.
The Einstein equivalence principle includes that universality and extends the claim to local nongravitational experiments through local Lorentz invariance and local position invariance. A sufficiently small freely falling laboratory should recover the nongravitational laws of special relativity. The strong equivalence principle extends the scope to local gravitational experiments and bodies with appreciable gravitational self-energy. An experiment establishing one part of the weak statement does not automatically establish these stronger claims.
The motivating elevator comparison makes both the power and the limitation clear. Release a small object in a freely falling cabin. The cabin and object share the local free fall, so the object initially floats relative to the cabin. In a cabin supported against gravity, a released object instead accelerates relative to the floor, as it does in a suitably accelerated cabin in flat spacetime. This suggested replacing a universal gravitational force by geometric inertial motion. It does not identify a finite planetary gravitational field with an accelerated frame everywhere: separated falling objects can have a measurable relative acceleration.
In general relativity, the precise local geometric statement is that one can choose normal coordinates at an event p with
gμν​(p)=ημν​,∂ρ​gμν​(p)=0,Γμαβ​(p)=0.
(13)
This eliminates the connection contribution to the geodesic equation at that point. It does not generally set the Riemann curvature tensor to zero. In normal coordinates the first metric corrections away from p are quadratic in the coordinate separation, and geodesic deviation measures the associated tidal forces. Equivalence is a local approximation for finite experiments whose extent and duration make these corrections negligible; it is not an assertion that curvature has been transformed away.[ref]
There are direct experimental consequences beyond the elevator thought experiment. An Eötvös experiment compares bodies of different composition on a torsion balance, using a differential signal to test the universality of gravitational acceleration. MICROSCOPE performed an analogous test in orbit using electrostatic control of titanium-alloy and platinum-alloy test masses. Its first published result in 2017 constrained their differential free fall; this was already available before the examination. It tested a specific weak-equivalence prediction, not every possible gravitational law.[ref] Comparisons of different atomic clocks can instead test local position invariance, including the expected gravitational redshift. These measurements constrain related but distinct parts of the framework.
The principle remains important as a restriction on how matter couples to gravity and as an organizing guide for experimental tests. It does not, on its own, derive the Einstein field equations. Universally coupling matter to a metric tensor still leaves choices for that metric's action; the equivalence principle does not uniquely fix field equations. For example, a metric gravitational action containing a scalar curvature squared term can retain the same minimal matter coupling while changing the gravitational dynamics. Selecting the Einstein-Hilbert action requires further dynamical assumptions and empirical success. The present role of the principle is consequently precise and limited: it supports local relativistic physics and universal matter coupling, while curvature, gravitational field equations and their quantitative predictions remain additional content.
