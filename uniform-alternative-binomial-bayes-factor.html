Uniform-alternative binomial Bayes factor (B01​(y)=(n+1)(yn​)2−n)

For a binomial distribution count, compare a point null θ=1/2 with the uniform alternative prior distribution on (0,1). The alternative Bayesian model evidence is
(yn​)∫01​θy(1−θ)n−ydθ=n+11​,
(43)
so the exact Bayes factor favouring the point null is (n+1)(yn​)2−n. With standardized deviation z=2(y−n/2)/n​, the local normal distribution approximation is B01​≈2n/π​e−z2/2. At fixed z the frequentist p-value remains approximately constant, while the factor grows like n​. This demonstrates the prior-width mechanism behind the Jeffreys-Lindley paradox for a Gaussian point null in a discrete sampling model.
For a separate original example, change the point null to H0​:p=1/2 and take HU​ to have a uniform prior p∼Beta(1,1), fixed before the ten Bernoulli trials. Here p is drawn once under HU​ and shared by all ten trials: they are independent conditional on p. For one particular ordered sequence with eight heads and two tails, the Euler beta function gives
m0​(D)=2−10,mU​(D)=∫01​p8(1−p)2dp=B(9,3)=11!8!2!​=4951​.
(44)
Thus the Bayes factor for the uniform alternative against the point null is
BFU0​=4951024​≈2.0686869,B0U​=1024495​≈0.4833984.
(45)
The binomial distribution count formulation gives m0​(K=8)=45/1024 and mU​(K=8)=45/495=1/11. Their ratio is again 1024/495; these count masses must not be combined with the ordered-sequence masses above. The general B01​ formula at the start of this article puts the point null in the numerator, so it equals B0U​ here.
In contrast, maximum likelihood estimation gives p​=0.8: differentiating the log-likelihood yields 8/p−2/(1−p)=0, and the endpoints have zero likelihood. Its peak-to-null likelihood ratio is
L(0.5)L(0.8)​=2−100.880.22​≈6.8719477.
(46)
This peak is chosen after seeing the data, so it is not the Bayes factor of the prespecified HU​. The Bayesian model evidence for HU​ averages L(p) over its entire uniform prior; it is smaller than the peak. It is also smaller than L(0.7)=0.780.32≈0.00518832. Against the same p=0.5 null, a point alternative fixed at p=0.7 would give a Bayes factor about 5.31284, greater than 2.06869. An integrated alternative need not outperform a point alternative.
For a general proper beta distribution prior distribution Beta(a,b) with a,b>0, the same ordered-sequence marginal likelihood is B(8+a,2+b)/B(a,b). This follows by substituting the normalized prior density into the integral, and shows exactly where the parameter prior distribution changes the Bayes factor. Holding the parameter prior distributions fixed while changing model prior odds leaves that Bayes factor unchanged.
