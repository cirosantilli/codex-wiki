Doppler spectroscopy

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Exoplanet Exoplanet atmosphere Exoplanet atmospheric observatory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 13
Doppler spectroscopy detects an exoplanet through the host star's changing Doppler shift. Both bodies orbit their shared astronomical barycenter: the star undergoes stellar reflex motion even when the exoplanet itself is unseen. Its velocity along the line of sight shifts its spectral lines; at speeds small compared with the speed of light, Δλ/λ≃vr​/c, with positive vr​ for recession. Repeated measurements can fit a Kepler orbit, rather than merely detect one shifted spectrum. Starspots and other changes in stellar spectral lines must be distinguished from actual orbital motion.
For one companion in an isolated two-body problem, the stellar radial-velocity semi-amplitude is
K=(P2πG​)1/3(M⋆​+Mp​)2/3Mp​sini​1−e2​1​.
(19)
Here G is the gravitational constant, P the orbital period, e the orbital eccentricity, and M⋆​,Mp​ the star and exoplanet masses. The orbital inclination i is measured relative to the sky plane: i=0 is face-on and i=90∘ is an edge-on orbit. The exact binary mass function measured from the fitted orbit is
f=2πGPK3(1−e2)3/2​=(M⋆​+Mp​)2Mp3​sin3i​.
(20)
With known M⋆​ and Mp​≪M⋆​, this is usually reported as Mp​sini. Taking sini=1 gives the minimum companion mass; a smaller orbital inclination allows a larger mass. A detected exoplanet transit constrains i close to 90∘ for a compact planet orbiting well outside the star's surface, substantially resolving this ambiguity. Combining Doppler spectroscopy and exoplanet transit photometry then supplies a mass and radius for exoplanet interior studies.
For a computed circular example, let q=Mp​/M⋆​=0.001 and choose a relative orbital speed Vrel​=100kms−1: this is the speed of the planet relative to the star, not its speed about the astronomical barycenter. Conservation of momentum gives
V⋆​=1+qq​Vrel​=99.9001ms−1,Vp​=1+q1​Vrel​=99.9001kms−1.
(21)
The barycentric velocities point in opposite directions, so their speeds add to Vrel​. Here e=0 and K=V⋆​sini: K=99.9001ms−1 at i=90∘, 49.9500ms−1 at i=30∘, and zero at i=0. The star still orbits in the face-on case, but its orbital velocity has no line-of-sight component. The plotted circular signal is vr​(t)−γ=Ksin(2πt/P) for a chosen phase origin and constant system velocity γ.
