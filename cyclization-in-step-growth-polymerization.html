Cyclization in step-growth polymerization

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Step-growth polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
In cyclization in step-growth polymerization, compatible functional groups on the same chain react and close it into a ring. They consume groups without merging two molecules. If R ring-closing events and J intermolecular joins occur in an otherwise balanced bifunctional feed, the group conversion is p=(J+R)/N0​, while N=N0​−J=N0​(1−p)+R. Thus
Xn​=N0​(1−p)+RN0​​.
(9)
For example, an AB dimer can close its remaining A and B ends: two group pairings have occurred among two starting monomer molecules, giving p=1, but the resulting single cyclic molecule has only two monomeric units. This counterexample shows exactly where the linear Carothers equation fails. Rings have no chain ends, so counting every chain-bearing molecule as two ends also fails.
