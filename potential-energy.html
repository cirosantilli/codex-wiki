Potential energy (F=−∇V)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 34
Potential energy assigns energy to a system's arrangement, so a change of arrangement accounts for the work done by a conservative force. For a time-independent potential energy V(r),
F=−∇V,Wconservative​=Vi​−Vf​=−ΔV.
(17)
The gradient points toward increasing potential energy; the force points toward decreasing potential energy. Only differences matter: adding a constant to V changes neither the force nor its work. This endpoint rule follows by substituting −∇V into the line integral for work. [ref]
Near Earth's surface, stipulate constant gravitational acceleration g=9.81m/s2 and measure height h upward from a chosen zero. Then gravitational potential energy is V=mgh. Raising a 2kg mass by 0.5m increases its gravitational potential energy by 9.81J, while gravity does −9.81J of work. These are computed model values, not measurements of a particular lift. An ideal spring obeying Hooke's law instead has V=kx2/2, with displacement x from its relaxed state and spring constant k: k=100N/m and x=0.2m give V=2J relative to x=0. [ref]
