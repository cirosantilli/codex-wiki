Proportional control (u=ke,k>0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Control theory Closed-loop control
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Proportional control chooses a control input proportional to the current tracking error. For the ideal controlled plant and constant reference signal r, its ordinary differential equation is x˙=k(r−x)+w. With w=0 and initial condition x(0)=x0​, the exact response is
x(t)=r+(x0​−r)e−kt.
(24)
For r=1, x0​=0 and k=2, this gives x(1)=1−e−2≃0.864665 and x(2)=1−e−4≃0.981684. To verify the formula, substitute it into the ordinary differential equation and check the initial condition; its decaying exponential function explains the approach to the goal.
With a constant control disturbance, setting x˙=0 gives the equilibrium point x∗​=r+w/k and steady-state tracking error e∗​=−w/k. For w=0.2, doubling the feedback gain from k=2 to k=4 reduces the offset from 0.1 to 0.05. This improvement is a statement about the ideal model: it neglects feedback delay and measurement noise, so it does not establish that arbitrarily large feedback gains are safe. [ref]
In the first diagram's switched control disturbance experiment, k=2 and w=0.2 from t=2 give, for t≥2,
x(t)=1.1+(1−e−4−1.1)e−2(t−2).
(25)
Thus x(4)≃1.097833 and x(8)≃1.099999. At the equilibrium point, the controller supplies u=−0.2 to cancel the control disturbance. Proportional control needs a nonzero tracking error to produce that persistent correction.
