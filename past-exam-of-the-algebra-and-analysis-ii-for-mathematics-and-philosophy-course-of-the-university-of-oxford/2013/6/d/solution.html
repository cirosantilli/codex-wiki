It suffices to construct an injective holomorphic map Φj​:Uj​→D for each domain. Then Φj​∘f is a bounded entire function and is constant by Liouville's theorem; injectivity of Φj​ forces f to be constant.
For the upper half-plane, use the Cayley transform between the half-plane and disk
Φ1​(z)=z+iz−i​.
(53)
Its denominator is nonzero there, and ∣z+i∣2−∣z−i∣2=4Imz>0, so its image lies in the unit disk. The determinant is nonzero, hence it is injective and conformal.
On the plane slit along the nonnegative real axis, choose the branch
S(z)=∣z∣​eiArgz/2,0<Argz<2π.
(54)
This is the holomorphic square root obtained from the corresponding logarithm branch. Its argument lies in (0,π), so it maps U2​ bijectively onto the upper half-plane, with inverse w↦w2. Its derivative is 1/(2S(z))=0. Therefore
Φ2​(z)=S(z)+iS(z)−i​
(55)
is an injective conformal map of U2​ onto the unit disk.
For the complement of the closed interval, first use
M(z)=1−zz​.
(56)
Its only finite pole is the excluded point one, and it is injective with derivative 1/(1−z)2=0. If M(z)=t≥0, solving gives z=t/(1+t)∈[0,1), which is excluded. Hence M(U3​)⊂U2​, and
Φ3​(z)=Φ2​(1−zz​)
(57)
is the required injective conformal map into the disk. More precisely, M(U3​)=U2​∖{−1} and Φ3​(U3​)=D∖{0}, since S(−1)=i. No claim that the interval complement is conformally equivalent to the whole disk is required.
Applying Liouville to the three bounded compositions gives
f:C→Uj​ holomorphic⟹f constant,j=1,2,3.​
(58)
This illustrates Liouville rigidity from a bounded holomorphic embedding: an explicit injective bounded coordinate is enough.
