Attosecond streaking

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Attosecond physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 334 Articles: 1
Attosecond streaking uses a synchronized reference electric field to change a photoelectron's outgoing momentum. Scanning relative delay produces an energy-versus-delay distribution. A short emission interval samples only a small part of the reference cycle; a longer interval samples a range of kicks and can broaden or reshape the distribution.
For an explicit classical limit, use the electric dipole approximation and choose the magnetic vector potential A(t) along the detection axis with E(t)=−dA/dt and A(∞)=0. Neglect the residual ion's Coulomb force. An electron released at ti​ with mechanical momentum p0​ obeys
pf​=p0​−eA(ti​),Kf​=2m[p0​−eA(ti​)]2​.
(42)
Indeed, integrating p˙​=−eE=eA˙ from release to the end of the field gives the stated minus sign. The useful clock variable here is A, rather than the instantaneous E itself. If A is nearly linear across a narrow emission window with temporal standard deviation σt​, and initial momentum is sharp, then
σp​≃e∣E(ti​)∣σt​.
(43)
This local mapping fails near a zero of E, where first-order timing sensitivity vanishes. A real inversion must include the incident bandwidth, initial photoelectron distribution, collection angles and target–field response.
For an original dimensionless example in atomic units, choose p0​=2, m=e=1 and A(ti​)=0.10. Then pf​=1.9 and Kf​=1.805, compared with initial kinetic energy 2. Reversing A gives Kf​=2.205: the shifts are asymmetric because of the A2/2 term. A measurement that keeps only the term linear in A would miss 0.005 in both cases.
