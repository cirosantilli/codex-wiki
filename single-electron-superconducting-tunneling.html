Single-electron superconducting tunneling

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 605 Articles: 1
Single-electron superconducting tunneling transfers individual electrons across a barrier into or out of quasiparticle states of a material with superconductivity. In a normal-metal–insulator–superconductor junction, abbreviated NIS, only one electrode has superconductivity. Such a junction can reveal a superconducting energy gap without supporting a DC Josephson effect between two superconducting reservoirs.
For an elementary interpretation, assume an opaque barrier, approximately constant normal-electrode density of states, and an energy-independent tunneling matrix element over the range probed. Write the superconducting density of states divided by its normal-state value as ns​(E), with E measured from its chemical potential. A signed voltage convention chosen for positive normal-electrode injection gives
Iqp​(V)=eRN​1​∫−∞∞​ns​(E)[f(E−eV)−f(E)]dE,
(39)
where f is the Fermi-Dirac distribution at a common temperature and RN​ is the normal-state junction electrical resistance. This current convention is local to the NIS measurement. The expression counts occupied initial and empty final states; it is a quasiparticle transport model, not a current-phase relation of a superconducting weak link.
An original analytic illustration uses the ideal fully gapped BCS theory spectrum at zero temperature, with positive gap edge Δ:
ns​(E)={0,∣E∣/E2−Δ2​,​∣E∣<Δ,∣E∣>Δ.​
(40)
For positive V, the occupation difference restricts the integral to 0<E<eV. Since dE2−Δ2​/dE=E/E2−Δ2​, integration and odd extension give
Iqp​(V)=⎩⎨⎧​0,eRN​sgn(V)​(eV)2−Δ2​,​∣eV∣≤Δ,∣eV∣>Δ.​
(41)
The threshold is Δ/e for this NIS model. In the corresponding zero-temperature single-electron process between two fully gapped superconductors, creating excitations on both sides instead requires ∣eV∣≥Δ1​+Δ2​. That quasiparticle threshold does not forbid coherent pair electric current below it. At finite temperature, thermally populated quasiparticles soften the simple threshold argument.
At zero temperature in the NIS approximation, RN​dIqp​/dV=ns​(eV) away from the singular gap edge. Thus a voltage sweep probes the excitation density of states. The ideal divergence is rounded in real measurements, and at nonzero temperature the derivative samples a thermally broadened range of energies. For ∣eV∣>Δ, the model has VIqp​>0: electrical work creates excitations and is dissipated on relaxation. This distinguishes its steady transport from the conservative Josephson coupling energy.
