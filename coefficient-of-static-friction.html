Coefficient of static friction (μs​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Force Static friction
OurBigBook.com Words: 857 Articles: 2
The coefficient of static friction is the nonnegative dimensionless quantity μs​=∣fs​∣max​/N, with N>0, in the ideal dry-contact model. It characterizes the pair of surfaces under specified conditions. It describes the largest supported tangential force, whereas the coefficient of kinetic friction describes sliding. It is an empirical approximation, not a universal material constant.[ref]
In the horizontal 2kg example, an observed state balancing a 2N pull would only establish μs​≥2/19.6≃0.10204 under the model assumptions. It would not establish μs​=0.10204: the actual static friction is below its limit. Identifying the coefficient needs an onset measurement, such as inclined-plane measurement of static friction, or the starting peak in a suitably controlled horizontal pull. The sustained sliding part of constant-speed measurement of kinetic friction measures a different coefficient.
In a simple grain model, the existing name granular friction coefficient represents resistance to downstream motion. That model excludes lift and detailed contact torques. Its coefficient should not automatically be identified with a bulk pile's angle of repose: individual contact geometry and collective rearrangement can matter.
