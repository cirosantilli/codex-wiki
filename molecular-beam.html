Molecular beam

 Home Physics Experimental apparatus
OurBigBook.com Words: 942 Articles: 7
A directed stream of molecules used to prepare samples for spectroscopy.
A molecular beam also includes a stream of chemical atoms, then called an atomic beam. An oven supplies particles; aligned slits restrict their directions, and a vacuum limits subsequent collisions. Narrowing the directions does not by itself make every particle's speed equal. This gives a useful separation of tasks: prepare a path, select or measure speed, apply an interaction, and read the resulting particle counts or deflection.
The advantage is unusually tangible. A magnetic field can separate spin components in the Stern-Gerlach experiment; even gravity can sort a beam by flight time. The effusive beam speed distribution explains which particles leave the source, while gravity deflection of an atomic beam explains how that distribution becomes a pattern on a detector.
