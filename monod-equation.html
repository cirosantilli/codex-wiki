Monod equation (K(C)=Kmax​C/(K0​+C))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology
OurBigBook.com Words: 328 Articles: 1
The Monod equation is an empirical saturating law for the specific growth rate of a culture limited by one nutrient. Write s≥0 for its residual nutrient concentration, μmax​>0 for the limiting specific growth rate, and Ks​>0 for the nutrient concentration at half that rate:
μ(s)=μmax​Ks​+ss​,μ(Ks​)=2μmax​​.
(1)
Here μ and μmax​ have units of inverse time, whereas Ks​ has the same units as s. The notation K(C) in the title denotes the same growth function, with C=s, Kmax​=μmax​ and K0​=Ks​. For s≪Ks​, μ(s)≈(μmax​/Ks​)s; for s≫Ks​, μ(s) approaches μmax​. Its derivative μ′(s)=μmax​Ks​/(Ks​+s)2 is positive: more nutrient increases the specific growth rate, with progressively smaller increments.
For example, take μmax​=1h−1 and Ks​=1gL−1. Nutrient concentrations of 0, 1 and 9gL−1 give specific growth rates 0, 0.5 and 0.9h−1. In a chemostat with dilution rate D=0.5h−1, the intersection μ(s)=D selects the residual nutrient concentration s=1gL−1, provided the feed can supply enough nutrient for a positive biomass concentration.
The Monod equation is useful because two measurable parameters describe both nutrient scarcity and saturation. It assumes the other conditions affecting growth remain controlled and does not represent inhibition at high nutrient concentration. Although its formula resembles the Michaelis-Menten equation, a whole culture's specific growth rate combines many processes; the resemblance alone does not derive it from Michaelis-Menten kinetics or identify Ks​ with a Michaelis constant.
In his 1949 review, Monod presents the hyperbolic rate law as an empirical fit in equation (2), pp. 383-384, and discusses the difficulty of identifying a single growth-controlling reaction. His reported division rates use doublings per unit time; the natural-exponential specific growth rate used here is that division rate multiplied by ln2. [ref].
