Weinberg angle (θW​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Electroweak interaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
The Weinberg angle rotates the neutral gauge fields into the photon and Z boson:
(Aμ​Zμ​​)=(sinθW​cosθW​​cosθW​−sinθW​​)(Wμ3​Bμ​​),tanθW​=gg′​,e=gsinθW​=g′cosθW​.
(25)
g belongs to SU(2)L​, g′ to U(1)Y​, and e is the positive magnitude of the electron electric charge. These definitions use Q=T3​+Y; doubling the definition of hypercharge requires halving its coefficient in the covariant derivative. The electromagnetic coupling of a weak multiplet follows by substituting this rotation.
For a fermion component, the corresponding neutral coefficient is
gcosθW​T3​−g′sinθW​Y=cosθW​g​(T3​−Qsin2θW​).
(26)
For a left-handed charged lepton, T3​=−1/2 and Q=−1, giving gL​=−1/2+sin2θW​. Its right-handed component has T3​=0, giving gR​=sin2θW​. This algebra links the neutral current to the electromagnetic charge assignment. Together with the Higgs doublet, it also gives the tree-level electroweak gauge-boson masses with mW​/mZ​=cosθW​; precise comparisons require a defined renormalization convention and radiative corrections.
