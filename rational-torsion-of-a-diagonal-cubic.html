Rational torsion of a diagonal cubic (Ed​(Q)tors​)

 ... Algebraic geometry Normalization of an algebraic curve Geometric genus Genus one curve Elliptic curve Diagonal cubic elliptic curve
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
For d∈Q× the rational torsion subgroup of X3+Y3+dZ3=0 is cyclic of order 3 if d is a cube, cyclic of order 2 if d/2 is a cube, and trivial otherwise.
To bound torsion, use the Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve y2=x3−432d2. At every good prime ℓ≡2(mod3), cubing is a bijection on Fℓ​, so the number of points is ℓ+1. Reduction injects the prime-to-ℓ torsion, since multiplication by an integer prime to ℓ is invertible in the formal group of an elliptic curve. For any prime q>3, the Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions supplies a good ℓ≡2(mod3), ℓ≡1(modq), excluding q-torsion. Good primes ℓ≡5(mod12) exclude order 4, and good primes ℓ≡11(mod18) exclude order 9. Thus every rational torsion point is killed by 6.
The flexes of a diagonal cubic give rational three-torsion exactly when d is a cube, and then it has order 3. Rational two-torsion requires x3=432d2, whose sole possible rational root exists exactly when d/2 is a cube. This equivalence follows by comparing prime valuations modulo 3, including the valuation at 2. The two conditions cannot hold together because 2 is not a rational cube. The asserted classification follows.
