Wall-induced motion of a planar potential dipole (aˉ˙=wreg′​(a))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Potential flow Complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow Planar potential dipole
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
A transported planar potential dipole follows the surrounding regular velocity, so its position satisfies the displayed equation after its own singular term is removed. The conjugation comes from the convention w′=vx​−ivy​. Reflected image parameters are held fixed when taking the spatial derivative, then evaluated at the instantaneous dipole position.
For a unit circular wall, the Milne-Thomson circle theorem gives image potential mˉz/(1−aˉz) and therefore aˉ˙=mˉ/(1−∣a∣2)2. For a horizontal straight wall the image is mˉ/(z−aˉ), giving aˉ˙=−mˉ/(a−aˉ)2. For the line y=−x, reflected conjugation gives image −imˉ/(z+iaˉ) and hence aˉ˙=imˉ/(a+iaˉ)2. In each case the image lies outside the fluid region and makes the wall a streamline; differentiation of the regular image proves the motion formula. Several compatible straight-wall reflections can be summed before the same operation is applied.
