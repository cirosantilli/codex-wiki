Spherical Helmholtz matching to a decaying harmonic exterior (xjℓ′​(x)+(ℓ+1)jℓ​(x)=xjℓ−1​(x)=0)

Words: 184
Spherical Bessel function OurBigBook.com
A scalar field solving the Helmholtz equation inside a sphere of radius R and Laplace's equation outside has a regular interior mode bjℓ​(s​r)Pℓ​(cosθ) and a decaying exterior mode ar−ℓ−1Pℓ​(cosθ). Continuity of the field and its radial derivative gives, for x=s​R>0,
xjℓ′​(x)+(ℓ+1)jℓ​(x)=0.
(2)
The Spherical Bessel function recurrence turns this into jℓ−1​(x)=0, not jℓ​(x)=0. This elimination does not require division by a possibly vanishing boundary value.
For ℓ=0, j0​(x)=sinx/x and j−1​(x)=cosx/x, so the first positive root is π/2. For ℓ=1, the matching condition is j0​(x)=0, whose first positive root is π. The distinction matters when a scalar model permits the monopole sector but a physical multipole model excludes it.
To compare angular sectors, put u=rjℓ​(s​r). Its equation and boundary conditions are −u′′+ℓ(ℓ+1)u/r2=su, u(0)=0 and u′(R)+ℓu(R)/R=0. Its Rayleigh quotient is
s=∫0R​∣u∣2dr∫0R​[∣u′∣2+ℓ(ℓ+1)∣u∣2/r2]dr+ℓ∣u(R)∣2/R​.
(3)
Both added terms are nonnegative and increase with ℓ. Thus the lowest scalar eigenvalue is π2/(4R2) in the ℓ=0 sector; if only ℓ≥1 is admitted, the lowest is π2/R2 in the ℓ=1 sector. Zeros at x=0 caused by the vanishing normalization of jℓ​ are not additional eigenvalues.

Frobenius resonance in the spherical Bessel equation (σ=ℓ,−ℓ−1)

Words: 115
Spherical Bessel function OurBigBook.com
For x2y′′+2xy′+[x2−ℓ(ℓ+1)]y=0 with integer ℓ≥0, the Frobenius method gives σ=ℓ or −ℓ−1. The general coefficient equation is [(j+σ)(j+σ+1)−ℓ(ℓ+1)]aj​=−aj−2​, taking negative-index coefficients as zero. The regular branch has a1​=0 and aj​=−aj−2​/[j(j+2ℓ+1)]. The singular branch has aj​=−aj−2​/[j(j−2ℓ−1)] except at j=2ℓ+1. That resonant equation is compatible because its earlier odd coefficient vanishes; the newly free coefficient adds a multiple of the regular solution. Setting it to zero gives a pure even series times x−ℓ−1, independent of the regular branch, with no logarithmic term. For ℓ=0 the resonance occurs already at j=1.
The normalization a0​=2ℓℓ!/(2ℓ+1)! on the regular branch gives the Spherical Bessel function
jℓ​(x)=2ℓxℓ∑s=0∞​s!(2s+2ℓ+1)!(−1)s(s+ℓ)!​x2s.
(4)

Fourier transform of the Bessel function J0

Words: 157
Bessel function of the first kind OurBigBook.com
With the Fourier transform convention f​(ω)=∫R​f(t)e−iωtdt, the regular order-zero Bessel function of the first kind has the tempered distribution transform
J0​​(ω)=1−ω2​21(−1,1)​(ω)​.​
(6)
The density is integrable despite its endpoint singularities. Fourier inversion gives the equivalent integral representation
J0​(t)=π1​∫−11​1−ω2​eitω​dω=π1​∫0π​cos(tcosθ)dθ.
(7)
The second formula follows by ω=cosθ and cancellation of the odd imaginary part. It is the standard real integral representation of J0​.[ref]
Indeed, transforming the order-zero Bessel differential equation ty′′+y′+ty=0 gives (1−ω2)Y′−ωY=0. Inside (−1,1), its solutions are C(1−ω2)−1/2. The compactly supported choice also solves the equation as a distribution: rewrite it as [(1−ω2)Y]′+ωY=0, and observe that (1−ω2)Y=C1−ω2​1(−1,1)​ is continuous and zero at the two endpoints. Its distributional derivative has no endpoint Dirac delta terms. The inverse is regular at t=0, and y(0)=1 fixes C=2.
The transformed equation alone also allows separate constants on ω<−1 and ω>1. Selecting the compactly supported density amounts to selecting the solution regular at the origin, rather than every possible singular solution of the Bessel differential equation.

Frobenius series for Bessel functions (a2m​=−a2m−2​/[4m(m+ν)])

Words: 107
Bessel differential equation OurBigBook.com
For the Bessel differential equation x2y′′+xy′+(x2−ν2)y=0, substitute y=xs∑n≥0​an​xn. The indicial equation is s2−ν2=0, and coefficients obey [(n+s)2−ν2]an​=−an−2​. For s=ν≥0, a1​=0 and all odd coefficients vanish. Iteration gives a2m​=(−1)ma0​Γ(ν+1)/[4mm!Γ(m+ν+1)]. Choosing a0​=1/[2νΓ(ν+1)] gives the Bessel function of the first kind Jν​.
For noninteger ν, choosing the even-power series with s=−ν replaces m+ν by m−ν and yields J−ν​ with its standard normalization. These solutions are independent. For integer ν≥0, the first solution has coefficients (−1)m/[22m+νm!(m+ν)!] and defines an entire function: the term ratio tends to zero at every finite complex argument. For noninteger order, the even analytic series converges everywhere, while the prefactor x±ν requires a branch off the positive real axis.

Sturm-Liouville formulation with Bessel order as eigenvalue ((xy′)′+β2xy=n2y/x)

Words: 75
Bessel differential equation OurBigBook.com
For fixed β, move the order term of the Bessel equation to the right: x2y′′+xy′+β2x2y=n2y. The integrating factor for its leading differential expression is 1/x, giving the displayed Sturm-Liouville theory form with coefficient p=x, potential q=β2x, weight w=1/x and eigenvalue n2. The origin is a singular endpoint. The integrating factor must be normalized from a positive reference point; its defining logarithmic integral cannot literally start at zero. Boundary conditions are needed before this differential identity becomes a specified self-adjoint spectral problem.
