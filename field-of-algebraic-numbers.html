Field of algebraic numbers (Q​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Algebraic number
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
The complex algebraic numbers form a field, the algebraic closure of Q inside C. To see closure under the field operations, if α,β are algebraic numbers, the tower law makes Q(α,β) a finite-dimensional field extension of Q. Every element of this extension is an algebraic element, because its successive powers are linearly dependent. In particular it contains α±β, αβ and α/β when β=0.
It is an infinite-degree algebraic extension. A primitive pth root of unity has minimal polynomial of an algebraic element 1+X+⋯+Xp−1 of degree p−1 for every prime p, so the degrees of its finite subextensions are unbounded. Finally a complex root of a polynomial whose coefficients are algebraic numbers is itself algebraic over their finite-degree coefficient field and hence over Q, again by the tower law. Thus every such polynomial splits in this field, establishing the algebraic closure assertion.
