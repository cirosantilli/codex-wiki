Centrifugation

 Home Biology
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 12
Centrifugation separates suspended material by its motion relative to a rotating liquid. A centrifuge rotor creates an outward centrifugal acceleration in the rotating reference frame; material denser than its surroundings tends to move outward, opposed by drag force. Collected material can form a centrifugation pellet, leaving a supernatant, or remain in separated zones within a density gradient. The useful question is which physical difference the separation exploits: a difference in sedimentation coefficient or a difference in buoyant density.
Centrifugation makes otherwise slow settling useful for studying biological cells, organelles, proteins and DNA. Separation and identification are different steps: a centrifugation pellet is a collected fraction, not proof that it contains only one kind of organelle. The lysozyme wall-removal experiment illustrates a complementary readout: testing material released into the separated liquid can be more informative than inspecting the retained solids.
