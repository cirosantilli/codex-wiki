Euclid-Euler theorem (n even and perfect⟺n=2p−1(2p−1), 2p−1 prime)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Integer Positive integer Perfect number
OurBigBook.com Words: 215 Articles: 1
An even integer n>0 is a perfect number if and only if
n=2p−1(2p−1)with 2p−1 prime.​
(9)
This forces p to be a prime number, as shown under Mersenne number. It does not say that every prime exponent works, nor classify possible odd integers.[ref]
For the forward direction, put q=2p−1 and use coprimality of q and 2p−1. Multiplicativity of the sum-of-divisors function and the finite geometric series give
σ(2p−1q)=σ(2p−1)σ(q)=(2p−1)(q+1)=q2p=2n.
(10)
The prime number hypothesis makes σ(q)=q+1; replacing it with a composite number changes this factor.
For a short converse outline, write n=2am with a≥1 and m an odd integer. The condition σ(n)=2n gives
σ(m)=m+t,t=2a+1−1m​.
(11)
Here t is a positive integer and a proper divisor of m. If t>1, the distinct divisors 1,t,m alone exceed the required sum m+t. Thus t=1 and σ(m)=m+1, forcing m to be prime and m=2a+1−1.
