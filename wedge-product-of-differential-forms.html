Wedge product of differential forms (∧)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential form
OurBigBook.com Words: 126 Articles: 1
The wedge product combines a degree-p differential form and a degree-q differential form into one of degree p+q. It is associative and graded-commutative:
α∧β=(−1)pqβ∧α.
(1)
In particular, dx∧dy=−dy∧dx and dx∧dx=0. For planar vectors v,w,
(dx∧dy)(v,w)=vx​wy​−vy​wx​.
(2)
This determinant is the signed area of their parallelogram. Exchanging the edges reverses the sign; parallel edges give zero. Thus antisymmetry records the geometry of oriented area rather than adding an arbitrary algebraic convention. The wedge product of differential forms requires no Riemannian metric to define it. It obeys the graded Leibniz rule under the exterior derivative.
