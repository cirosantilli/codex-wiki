Inclined-plane measurement of static friction (μs​=tanθc​)

 ... Branches of physics Classical mechanics Force Static friction Coefficient of static friction Static friction on an inclined plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 235
A university laboratory places an aluminium block on a Teflon-lined wooden board. For its inclined plane stage, the pulley and block string are removed; a rope and hook connect the board to a crank. The block is placed gently near the sheet's middle without prior sliding. Smooth cranking raises the board; a 0∘–45∘ protractor reads the smallest angle at which sliding begins. Four readings are taken without added load, then four each with 200, 400, and 800g added symmetrically at two screws. Removing the string isolates gravity-driven onset; gentle placement and smooth raising avoid premature disturbance.[ref]
Infer μs​=tanθc​ under the assumptions of static friction on an inclined plane. Repetition tests onset scatter; added loads test coefficient constancy. No measured onset values are supplied here.
For a replication, additionally check the protractor zero with a level board, retain the same contact face and surface region, and observe whether tipping or rolling precedes sliding. These are proposed additional controls. A finite angle resolution and the disturbance needed to detect first motion limit the estimate; a slow ramp does not by itself establish perfectly rigid, dry, or direction-independent contact. At the model's μs​=0.40 threshold, differentiating the tangent function gives dμs​/dθ=1+μs2​=1.16, with θ in radians. A hypothetical 0.5∘ measurement error would therefore correspond to approximately 0.0101 in the inferred coefficient at first order. This sensitivity calculation is not an uncertainty reported by the laboratory.
