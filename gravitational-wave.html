Gravitational wave

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 41
A gravitational wave is a propagating disturbance of spacetime curvature produced by changing mass-energy multipole moments. In general relativity, its two transverse gravitational wave polarizations alternately stretch and squeeze freely falling separations. The leading radiation from a slowly moving source is governed by its mass quadrupole moment, rather than by its total mass alone.
The experimental breakthrough was GW150914: observed on 14 September 2015 and published on 11 February 2016. The LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo Collaboration reported a merging pair of black holes, using the two LIGO detectors at Hanford and Livingston; Virgo was being upgraded and did not observe this event.[ref]
The 2017 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized Rainer Weiss, Barry C. Barish and Kip S. Thorne for contributions to LIGO and gravitational wave observation. Rainer Weiss developed interferometric detection and its noise analysis; Barry C. Barish led the project into construction and operation; Kip S. Thorne developed its theoretical and astrophysical foundations. The experiment depended on a large collaboration.[ref]

GW150914

Words: 1k Articles: 4
Gravitational wave OurBigBook.com
GW150914 is the first directly detected gravitational wave, interpreted as a binary black hole inspiral, merger and ringdown. Its discovery paper reports a peak projected gravitational wave strain of approximately 10−21 and a combined matched filter signal-to-noise ratio of 24. The inferred source-frame component masses were 36−4+5​ and 29−4+4​ solar masses, with 90 percent credible intervals. These are model-dependent estimates from a detector network.[ref]
The interesting measurement is a chain: curvature changes relative light travel times, wave interference turns those changes into a photodetector signal, calibrated records from separate sites constrain a common source, and statistical inference tests competing explanations. The following original models explain that chain; the plotted time series are stipulated calculations, never measured GW150914 data.

LIGO differential strain measurement (hD​=(δLx​−δLy​)/L)

Words: 671 Articles: 1
GW150914 OurBigBook.com
Each LIGO instrument is a Michelson interferometer with approximately 4km arms containing Fabry–Pérot interferometers. A stabilized 1064nm laser illuminates suspended 40kg fused-silica mirrors in vacuum. Four-stage suspensions and active isolation suppress ground motion; low-loss materials reduce thermal noise. Power recycling increases circulating power; signal recycling shapes the response to the gravitational wave.[ref]
Why measure two perpendicular arms? In the long-wavelength limit, take a gravitational wave with plus polarization normally incident on ideal perpendicular arms. Its transverse-traceless tensor has hxx​=h+​ and hyy​=−h+​. Expanding each proper length to first order gives
LδLx​​=2h+​​,LδLy​​=−2h+​​,ΔL=δLx​−δLy​=Lh+​.
(1)
Here h+​ is dimensionless, whereas ΔL is a length. A stipulated h+​=10−21 and L=4000m imply ΔL=4×10−18m, with individual arm changes of opposite sign and magnitude 2×10−18m. These are equivalent differential arm-length changes, not a photograph of mirrors moving against a rigid external ruler. The observable compares returning light phases; coordinate systems can distribute the description between mirror coordinates and light propagation.
For unit arm directions u,v, define the detector tensor Dij=(uiuj−vivj)/2. If eij+​,eij×​ are the transverse polarization tensors, then
hD​=DijhijTT​=F+​h+​+F×​h×​,FA​=DijeijA​.
(2)
The dimensionless factors FA​ encode sky direction and the polarization basis. Thus even identical sources can have different observed amplitudes and wave phases. Motion changing both arms equally cancels in this ideal differential observable, although real asymmetries can couple common disturbances into it.
Suspension supplies an inertial reference above its resonances. For example, a single ideal harmonic oscillator with support motion y obeys x¨+ω02​(x−y)=0. Its response at angular frequency ω is x/y=ω02​/(ω02​−ω2), which decreases in magnitude as (ω0​/ω)2 well above resonance. The mirror follows slow support drift but approximately free motion in the high-frequency limit. Real multistage suspensions also require damping and alignment control; resonant frequencies are not regions of good isolation.
Rainer Weiss's 1972 report proposed suspended masses, multiple-pass optical delay lines, and a servo holding an interference fringe. Its noise analysis separately treats photon-counting fluctuations, laser frequency instability, mechanical thermal noise, radiation pressure and ground motion. Those delay lines should be distinguished from the resonant arm cavities of Advanced LIGO.[ref]

LIGO displacement readout and control

Words: 311
LIGO differential strain measurement OurBigBook.com
An ideal single-round-trip Michelson interferometer converts differential length into phase difference δϕ=4πΔL/λ. If its dark-port power scale is P0​ and its static phase offset is ϕ0​, then
Pout​=P0​sin2(2ϕ0​+δϕ​),δP=2P0​​sinϕ0​δϕ+O(δϕ2).
(3)
Exactly at a dark fringe, ϕ0​=0, power is quadratic in small displacement and loses its sign. A deliberate offset supplies an optical reference field: interference with the small signal field produces a linear term. Equivalently, ∣Eref​+Esig​∣2 contains 2Re(Eref∗​Esig​). The photodetector measures power, not a signed displacement directly.
Advanced LIGO uses this DC homodyne principle with a small differential arm offset and an output mode cleaner. Its feedback control maintains optical resonance and alignment, using actuation distributed over the suspension stages.[ref] The simple formula above omits resonant storage: arm and recycling cavities give a frequency-dependent optical gain and delay, so no constant pass-count multiplier describes the entire observing band.
To see why a locked detector can still measure a disturbance, use a linear feedback control model. Let xext​ be the external differential displacement, derr​ the residual readout, dctrl​ the recorded control command, C(f) the sensing response and A(f) the actuation response. With the convention xres​=xext​−Adctrl​, reconstruction gives
xext​=C−1derr​+Adctrl​,hD​=xext​/L.
(4)
Here tildes denote Fourier transforms; C has readout units per metre and A metres per command unit. Feedback suppresses residual motion but records the compensating command. Omitting that command would suppress the reconstructed low-frequency signal too. In the actual GW150914 experiment, a modulated auxiliary laser exerted calibrated radiation pressure on a mirror to measure the response; periodic excitations monitored calibration.[ref]

Coincidence and noise controls for GW150914

Words: 248
GW150914 OurBigBook.com
The modeled matched filter search found an arrival difference of about 7ms, within the roughly 10ms intersite light travel time. Background events were obtained by shifting one detector's timestamps far beyond that time and repeating the coincidence analysis. The binary search bounded the false alarm rate below one event per 203000 years. That is an empirically estimated background rate, not a probability that the source interpretation is false.[ref]
The logic is to destroy a common astronomical arrival while retaining each instrument's real glitches. A Poisson distribution approximation would turn a background rate r into 1−e−rT for at least one equally strong noise event during live time T, only after accounting for the search's ranking and trials. Repeated time slides reuse data; their accumulated background duration is not an equally long series of independent new observations. Correlated disturbances also require separate controls because shifting timestamps can erase them along with a true signal.
The dedicated noise study inspected auxiliary channels and deliberately applied magnetic, acoustic and mechanical disturbances to measure their coupling into strain. For GW150914, checks included environmental coincidences, instrument state, and exclusion of an intentional hardware injection. It also examined potentially correlated global magnetic disturbances, which time slides alone cannot exclude.[ref] The point of an injection test is quantitative: a quiet environmental monitor is informative only if its sensitivity and coupling bound the disturbance needed to explain the candidate.

Inferring a binary from a detector network

GW150914 OurBigBook.com
For detector k, model the calibrated data as dk​(t)=hk​(t;θ)+nk​(t), with a shared source parameter vector θ, separate detector responses, propagation delays and noise nk​. Under an independent stationary Gaussian process approximation with one-sided power spectral density Sk​(f), define the noise-weighted inner product and likelihood function
(a∣b)k​=4Re∫fmin​fmax​​Sk​(f)a(f)∗b(f)​df,logL(θ)=−21​∑k​(dk​−hk​∣dk​−hk​)k​+constant.
(5)
Strain power spectral density has units Hz−1; the displayed inner product is dimensionless when the Fourier transform integrates over seconds. A matched filter rewards agreement over the full evolving wave phase, weighting frequency bands by their noise. One conspicuous peak is insufficient: a candidate should also have the right distribution of signal across frequency bands and compatible projections in the network.
The parameter paper used coherent Bayesian inference, numerical relativity-informed inspiral–merger–ringdown models and explicit calibration uncertainties. It found a strong distance–inclination correlation; source-frame masses additionally require converting the detector-frame masses by a cosmological redshift.[ref] Conceptually, multiply the likelihood function by a prior distribution to obtain a posterior distribution. Changes in waveform families, residual structure after subtraction, and checks with a generic transient reconstruction test parts of the modeling assumptions. Detection significance and parameter uncertainty answer different questions.

A Newtonian binary chirp is a leading-order model in which loss of orbital energy to gravitational radiation makes an almost circular binary system shrink and its gravitational-wave frequency rise. Here is a derivation with explicit SI units. Let the constant component masses m1​,m2​ be in kilograms, M=m1​+m2​, μ=m1​m2​/M, separation a in metres, and orbital angular frequency Ω in radians per second. Newtonian gravity, circular motion and the quadrupole formula give
Ω2=a3GM​,f=πΩ​,E=−2aGμM​,P=5c5a532G4μ2M3​,E˙=−P.
(6)
The factor π rather than 2π appears because the dominant mass quadrupole moment repeats twice per orbit. Since dE/da>0, radiative energy loss implies a˙<0, hence
a˙=−5c5a364G3μM2​,f˙​=Kf11/3,K=596​π8/3(c3GM​)5/3,M=μ3/5M2/5.
(7)
The quantity GM/c3 is a time in seconds. Thus K has units s5/3 and Kf11/3 has units s−2, as required for frequency change per second. This model assumes weak gravity, slow relative motion, negligible eccentricity of a conic, no spin corrections and adiabatic evolution over an orbit. It does not describe merger or ringdown.
Separating variables gives a finite formal coalescence time tc​ and accumulated wave phase Φ:
τ(f)=tc​−t=8K3​f−8/3,f(t)=[38K​(tc​−t)]−3/8,Φ(f)−Φ(f0​)=5K6π​(f0−5/3​−f−5/3).
(8)
Indeed dΦ/dt=2πf; the formal divergence at tc​ signals extrapolation outside the approximation. Over a finite frequency interval, the number of gravitational wave cycles is N=3(f0−5/3​−f1−5/3​)/(5K). This accumulated phase is why a chirp mass can be constrained even when distance and inclination remain entangled.

Axisymmetric radiation carries no axial angular momentum

Words: 41
Gravitational wave OurBigBook.com
An exact axial Killing vector field makes Komar angular momentum independent of the surrounding vacuum surface. Therefore gravitational waves in a configuration with exact axisymmetry carry no net angular momentum about that symmetry axis, although they can carry energy. This is specific to axial angular momentum; it is not an absence of radiation.
