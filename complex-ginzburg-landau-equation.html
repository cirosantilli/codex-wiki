Complex Ginzburg–Landau equation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Nonlinear partial differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 657 Articles: 11
The complex Ginzburg–Landau equation is a nonlinear complex-amplitude evolution equation with linear and cubic terms and generally complex coefficients. The driven-condensate form Ψt​=(α−β∣Ψ∣2)Ψ+i(∇2−g∣Ψ∣2+s)Ψ has gain/loss coefficients α,β and conservative interaction/frequency coefficients g,s. A uniform nonzero state has number density α/β when α,β>0.
A complex Ginzburg–Landau equation describes a complex number disturbance amplitude through linear amplification, advection, complex diffusion equation terms, and often cubic amplitude saturation. A constant-coefficient example is ψt​+Uψx​=μψ+(1+icd​)ψxx​−∣ψ∣2ψ. Here real U,μ,cd​ describe transport, linear growth rate and dispersive diffusion equation terms. Omitting the cubic term gives the linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation.
