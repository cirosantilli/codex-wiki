Quantum escape of a current-biased Josephson junction (Γ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity Josephson junction Current-biased Josephson phase dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 294
Quantum escape of a current-biased Josephson junction is decay of a trapped collective quantum phase state through a barrier in the tilted Josephson washboard potential. This barrier lies in the potential energy of an electrical circuit coordinate. Transfer of Cooper pairs across the physical insulating layer already supplies the Josephson coupling energy; it does not by itself demonstrate this further collective escape. Single-electron superconducting tunneling is a third, distinct process.
At higher temperature T, thermal activation instead lifts the coordinate over the barrier. In the rare-escape regime its characteristic form is the Kramers escape rate
Γth​≃ath​2πωp​​exp(−kB​TΔU​),

where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and the prefactor depends on damping and thermal equilibration. Cooling suppresses this route. For a quasistationary energy level at energy E, the leading conservative WKB approximation instead has
ΓE​ ∝ exp[−ℏ2​∫ϕ1​ϕ2​​2Mϕ​(U(ϕ)−E)​dϕ],

with the two turning points bracketing the forbidden barrier. This is an exponential estimate requiring a large action compared with ℏ, slow escape compared with intrawell motion, and weak environmental corrections. It is not a complete rate prediction near the barrier top or with substantial damping.

B=ℏ2Mϕ​ωp​​∫0a​x1−x/a​dx=15ℏ8Mϕ​ωp​a2​=5ℏωp​36ΔU​.

Substitution u=x/a reduces the dimensionless integral to ∫01​u1−u​du=4/15. This leading semiclassical action uses a cubic potential and a well-bottom reference; actual ground-state escape also needs the zero-point correction, prefactor and environment. In the chosen washboard example it gives B≃74.0 after matching the exact barrier and curvature. This matching is an illustrative approximation, not an exact action integral for the cosine potential.
Escape need not yield a sustained voltage: the coordinate can become trapped again. The running-state readout assumes sufficient underdamping and bias to avoid such retrapping. The parallel model has quality factor Q=ωp​RC; large Q makes energy loss per oscillation small. Low-temperature rate saturation alone cannot distinguish quantum tunnelling from unwanted heating or external noise.
