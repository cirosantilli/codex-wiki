Double pendulum demonstration

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Lagrangian mechanics Double pendulum
OurBigBook.com Words: 199
The University of Toronto's institutional demonstration “Chaotic Behaviour (Double Pendulum)”, activity Me-020, uses a metal stand with four leveling feet and a pendulum arm carrying a white dot. Preparation includes leveling the stand and correcting any wobble. The instructions call for holding the arm vertically upward and releasing it; the readout is the visible motion of the dot. A further comparison inserts a metal dowel in a groove on the release arm and repeats the release at the same location. Keeping the stand steady and repeating the release position provide practical controls. The page supplies no numerical angle trace or calibrated timing, and does not explain the dowel's mechanical effect. It therefore documents a qualitative release demonstration, without quantitatively testing the equal-point mass model. Its extended arms also have moments of inertia absent from massless rods. Irregular visible motion alone does not establish sensitive dependence on initial conditions.[ref]
A separate comparison with normal modes of a double pendulum would require small downward releases and measurement of both angles over time. The actual arm masses, centres of mass and moments of inertia would first have to enter the Lagrangian. The upward release in this demonstration explores a different regime from small oscillations about the downward stable equilibrium.
