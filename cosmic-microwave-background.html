Cosmic microwave background (CMB)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Cosmology
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 103
The cosmic microwave background is nearly isotropic thermal electromagnetic radiation. Its spectrum connects radio receiver measurements with the universe's thermal history: redshift preservation of a thermal photon spectrum cools a blackbody spectrum as space expands. The 1965 Princeton analysis described this relation.[ref]
A radiometer cannot label a received photon as cosmic, atmospheric or instrumental. Its measured power combines the incoming radiation with the receiver response. The useful question is which comparisons and controls can isolate a sky contribution. This makes the Penzias-Wilson microwave background experiment an example of both observational cosmology and measurement calibration.

Cosmic microwave background temperature (Tγ​(z)=Tγ,0​(1+z))

Words: 42
Cosmic microwave background OurBigBook.com
The blackbody temperature of freely propagating cosmic microwave background photons scales inversely with the scale factor: cosmological redshifting changes every frequency by the same factor and preserves the Planck spectrum shape. Its present value is approximately 2.73K. Measuring that absolute spectral temperature is distinct from measuring temperature fluctuations about it.

Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe

Words: 54
Cosmic microwave background OurBigBook.com
A space mission that mapped the temperature and polarization of the cosmic microwave background. Its nine-year analysis separates foreground emission from the microwave sky signal and fits cosmological models to the resulting maps. Combined-data fits additionally use other observations; they are not measurements from microwave maps alone.[ref]

Penzias-Wilson microwave background experiment

Words: 787 Articles: 2
Cosmic microwave background OurBigBook.com
In 1965, Arno Penzias and Robert Woodrow Wilson reported measurements with a 20-foot horn-reflector antenna at 4.080GHz. They manually switched a traveling-wave maser receiver between the horn and a helium-cooled reference. From 6.7K zenith antenna temperature, atmospheric and antenna/ground contributions left an excess 3.5±1.0K.[ref]
Elevation scans estimated the atmospheric term. Taping horn seams produced no measurable change; a nearby transmitter tested ground response. These controls addressed distinct possible contaminations.[ref]
The comparison does not make all nuisance contributions disappear. A reference can establish a response scale, but atmospheric emission and pickup through unwanted antenna directions are also real incident radiation. In his later account, Robert Woodrow Wilson describes these competing contributions as central to a radio astronomy measurement.[ref]
The Princeton group proposed primordial thermal radiation as an interpretation and called for more wavelength measurements. Their separate 3cm radiometer had not yet produced results.[ref] A single-frequency excess therefore does not by itself establish a complete Planck spectrum. Reproducibility across independent instruments, observing directions and frequencies tests the interpretation further.

Microwave radiometer calibration example (S=g(T+Tr​))

Words: 533 Articles: 1
Penzias-Wilson microwave background experiment OurBigBook.com
Consider an original idealized narrow-band model for one matched spatial mode and one polarization of waves. In the classical Rayleigh-Jeans law regime, an available thermal power can be written P=kB​TΔν, where Δν is the effective bandwidth and T the equivalent input noise temperature. Assume a linear detector with stable positive gain and absorb its bandwidth, Boltzmann constant and response factor into g. Then the output is S=g(T+Tr​), with input-referred receiver noise temperature Tr​. These are model assumptions, not a reconstruction of the historical electronics.
Two references with known noise temperatures Th​=Tc​ give
Sh​=g(Th​+Tr​),Sc​=g(Tc​+Tr​),
(1)
so the two-load radiometer calibration yields
g=Th​−Tc​Sh​−Sc​​,Tr​=Sh​−Sc​Th​Sc​−Tc​Sh​​,Ts​=Tc​+(Th​−Tc​)Sh​−Sc​Ss​−Sc​​.
(2)
Subtracting the reference equations eliminates the unknown receiver term; substituting back determines it. An arbitrary stable output offset can likewise be eliminated from the interpolation for Ts​, although separating that offset from Tr​ then requires additional information.
For deliberately chosen inputs, set Th​=300K, Tc​=4.2K, Tr​=40K and g=2mV/K. These are equivalent noise temperatures; physical load thermometry must be converted consistently at the observing frequency. The reference outputs are Sh​=680mV and Sc​=88.4mV. A sky-input output Ss​=90mV gives Ts​=5.0K. If independent estimates assign 1.4K to atmosphere, 0.5K to antenna loss and 0.1K to ground pickup in this lumped additive model, the residual is 3.0K.
The subtraction exposes what a noise budget must achieve. A 1K error in the assigned atmospheric term becomes a 1K error in the residual, even with exact detector calibration. Multiplicative attenuation requires a different correction: if an isothermal atmosphere has transmission t, its ideal contribution is tTcosmic​+(1−t)Tatm​ in the same classical equivalent-temperature convention. Recovering Tcosmic​ then requires both subtracting atmospheric emission and dividing by t. The additive toy budget applies when its terms already refer to the same receiver input.
There is also a conditioning issue. If Sh​−Sc​ is small, a fixed output error strongly changes the inferred temperature. Widely separated reference temperatures improve the scale determination only if the receiver remains linear over that range; measuring the reference repeatedly tests stability rather than proving linearity. The experiment therefore motivates a model, a calibration and a set of independent checks, each with its own assumptions.

Thermodynamic and Rayleigh-Jeans antenna temperatures (TA​=ehν/(kB​T)−1hν/kB​​)

Words: 121
Microwave radiometer calibration example OurBigBook.com
For a matched receiver accepting one spatial mode and one polarization of waves, the thermal contribution to the available spectral power of a blackbody is
dνdP​=ehν/(kB​T)−1hν​.
(3)
This expression excludes a zero-point term; it is the thermally emitted contribution. Defining the Rayleigh-Jeans law equivalent antenna temperature by dP/dν=kB​TA​ gives the displayed relation. With x=hν/(kB​T),
TTA​​=ex−1x​=1−2x​+12x2​+O(x4).
(4)
Thus TA​≃T requires x≪1. It is an approximation with a frequency-dependent error, rather than a redefinition of thermodynamic temperature.
For the stipulated T=3K and ν=4.080GHz, hν/kB​≃0.19581K and TA​≃2.90316K. Replacing TA​ by T here would differ by about 3.23%. At much higher frequencies, the approximation fails more strongly. A real instrument averages over its measured frequency response and antenna beam pattern, so using a single central frequency adds a narrow-band approximation.

COBE FIRAS blackbody spectrum experiment

Words: 334 Articles: 1
Far Infrared Absolute Spectrophotometer OurBigBook.com
FIRAS compared sky radiation with an adjustable internal blackbody using an interferometer and cold bolometers. An external blackbody could cover the sky entrance for measurement calibration. The team adjusted the internal temperature to minimize the difference signal.[ref]
Comparisons across detectors and mirror strokes tested instrumental errors; sky mapping separated Milky Way emission. John C. Mather's lecture reports a final root-mean-square spectral departure of 50 parts per million of peak brightness. This is neither the preliminary 1990 precision nor a relative error in every spectral bin. Calibration drift and nonideal reference emission required correction.[ref]
The advantage of a null measurement is that a small difference can be resolved without assigning the same absolute precision to a large detector output. Nevertheless, matching an internal reference is meaningful only after its relation to an independently calibrated blackbody has been tested. A bound on departures from a Planck spectrum constrains energy redistribution after thermal equilibrium; it does not require the cosmic microwave background to have exactly the same temperature in every direction.
Temperature perturbation of a Planck spectrum (δBν​/Bν​=[x/(1−e−x)]δT/T)
Words: 151
COBE FIRAS blackbody spectrum experiment OurBigBook.com
For a Planck function expressed as spectral radiance per frequency interval,
Bν​(T)=c22hν3​ex−11​,x=kB​Thν​,
(5)
h is the Planck constant, c the speed of light and kB​ the Boltzmann constant. Differentiating at fixed frequency gives
∂T∂Bν​​=TBν​​1−e−xx​,Bν​(T+δT)−Bν​(T)=∂T∂Bν​​δT+O(δT2).
(6)
The proof uses ∂T​x=−x/T and the chain rule. At low frequency, the multiplier tends to one, recovering the Rayleigh-Jeans law. At high frequency it grows approximately as x: the same fractional temperature change has a larger fractional brightness effect on the declining spectral tail.
For an original example choose T=2.725K and δT=1mK. At 100GHz, x=1.76119, so the first-order fractional residual is 7.80415×10−4. At 300GHz, x=5.28357, it is 1.94881×10−3. Both spectra are exact blackbodies: this residual is a temperature mismatch, not evidence of a nonthermal spectrum. Fitting temperature before assessing remaining spectral residuals therefore removes a physically distinct degree of freedom. The figure divides by a fixed peak brightness rather than by the rapidly vanishing high-frequency tail.

Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect

Words: 218 Articles: 3
Cosmic microwave background OurBigBook.com
Hot electrons in galaxy clusters scatter photons of the cosmic microwave background, producing a characteristic spectral distortion. The thermal contribution measures a line-of-sight integral of electron pressure, whereas X-ray emission depends approximately on the integral of electron density squared times a temperature-dependent emissivity. Combining the two with a geometrical model can constrain a cluster's physical size and hence its angular diameter distance. Temperature structure and departures from the assumed geometry introduce systematic uncertainty.

Cluster distance from Sunyaev-Zeldovich and X-ray signals (dA​∝(ΔT)2θ/[SX​Te3/2​(1+z)4])

For a uniform spherical cluster of depth ℓ∝θdA​, thermal bremsstrahlung gives received bolometric flux SX​∝ne2​Te1/2​ℓ3/dL2​, while the Rayleigh-Jeans law limit of the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect gives ∣ΔT∣∝ne​Te​ℓ. Eliminating ne​ and using dL​=(1+z)2dA​ yields the displayed angular diameter distance. A model for the density profile fixes its numerical coefficient. Clumping, nonsphericity and temperature structure can bias this distance because the two observables weight the gas differently.

Kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect (ΔT/T=−τv∥​/c)

Words: 49
Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect OurBigBook.com
Bulk motion of a scattering electron cloud relative to the cosmic microwave background gives a first-order Doppler temperature shift. With positive v∥​ directed away from the observer, the displayed sign applies, and τ is the Thomson scattering optical depth. In the nonrelativistic, single-scattering limit it has the spectrum of a small blackbody temperature change, unlike the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect.

Thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect (ΔT/T=y[xcoth(x/2)−4])

Words: 44
Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect OurBigBook.com
For a Planck photon distribution, the hot-electron Kompaneets equation gives Δn=yxex(ex−1)−2[xcoth(x/2)−4], where x=hν/(kB​Tγ​). The equivalent thermodynamic temperature perturbation has the displayed form, while the Rayleigh-Jeans law gives ΔT/T=−2y. The distortion changes sign at x≃3.830. This first-order expression assumes a small Compton y parameter and nonrelativistic thermal electrons.

Reionization returns some neutral cosmic gas to an ionized state after cosmological recombination. The resulting free electrons increase the cosmological optical depth through Thomson scattering and create a second contribution to the cosmological visibility function.

Photon-budget reionization threshold (fcoll​=Nreq​/Nγ​)

Words: 50
Reionization OurBigBook.com
If each collapsed baryon supplies Nγ​ effective ionizing photons and Nreq​ photons per cosmic baryon are required, the threshold collapsed fraction is fcoll​=Nreq​/Nγ​. The Press-Schechter formalism gives erfc[δsc​/(2​σ(Mmin​,z))]=fcoll​. Escape losses and recombinations must either be incorporated in the effective yield/requirement or counted separately, not twice.

Photoevaporation of a dark-matter minihalo (tevap​∼Rh​/cs,ion​)

Words: 64
Reionization OurBigBook.com
Photoionization can heat a shallow halo's gas well above its virial temperature. If the heated sound speed exceeds the escape-speed scale, the previous hydrostatic equilibrium is lost and a pressure-driven outflow removes gas. A crossing time gives a useful estimate, but photon supply, the gas profile, shielding and recombinations can delay the process. This particularly affects haloes whose virial temperatures are below the usual atomic cooling threshold and reduces their subsequent star formation.

Reionization damping of cosmic microwave background temperature anisotropy (e−τre​)

Words: 93 Articles: 1
Reionization OurBigBook.com
Ignoring velocities and evolving potentials, a thin reionization screen transmits the primary Cosmic microwave background anisotropy with amplitude e−τre​ and adds a direction-averaged source. A plane wave propagating a distance Δχ from recombination to the screen has monopole factor j0​(kΔχ)=sin(kΔχ)/(kΔχ). This suppresses the rescattered source at kΔχ≫1, while coherent large-scale modes are unchanged to leading order.

Primordial-amplitude–optical-depth degeneracy (As​e−2τre​)

Words: 39
Reionization damping of cosmic microwave background temperature anisotropy OurBigBook.com
The primary small-scale Cosmic microwave background power spectrum measures the amplitude As​e−2τre​, because reionization damps each temperature amplitude once. The unaffected large-scale temperature modes are few and limited by cosmic variance. Polarization and gravitational lensing provide additional information that partly breaks this degeneracy.
