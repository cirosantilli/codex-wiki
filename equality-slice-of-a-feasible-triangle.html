Equality slice of a feasible triangle (F∩{(x,y):x−y=1})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Feasible point Feasible region
OurBigBook.com Words: 231
Keep the feasible triangle with a squared-distance objective, but add the equation x−y=1. An equality requires an exact relation, whereas an inequality allows a range. Here the equality selects a line, and intersecting that line with the original feasible region leaves just the line segment from (1,0) to (2,1):
(x,y)=(t+1,t),0≤t≤1.
(28)
The lower bound comes from y≥0; the upper bound comes from x+y=2t+1≤3. This is a nonempty closed set even though it has empty interior in the plane. An equality can reduce the dimension of the allowed decisions without making the feasible region empty.
The old optimum (1.5,1.5) violates the added equality. On the surviving line segment, the objective function is
q(t)=(t−1)2+(t−2)2=2(t−3/2)2+1/2,q′(t)=4t−6<0(0≤t≤1).
(29)
The derivative shows that the unique global minimum is at t=1, giving (x,y)=(2,1) and value 1. The nearest point on the entire equality line would be (2.5,1.5), at t=1.5 with value 0.5, but it violates x+y≤3. Both the equality and the original inequalities must hold simultaneously.
Use g=x+y−3 and h=x−y−1 to fix the multiplier sign convention in the optimization Lagrangian L=f+λg+νh; the Lagrange multipliers of −x and −y are again zero. At (2,1), Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions hold with λ=1 and ν=−1:
∇f(2,1)+λ∇g+ν∇h=(0,−2)+(1,1)−(1,−1)=(0,0).
(30)
The Lagrange multiplier of an equality has no nonnegativity restriction. For an independent global certificate, completing the square gives f+g−h=(x−2)2+(y−1)2+1. On the feasible set, h=0 and g≤0, so f≥1, with equality exactly at (2,1).
