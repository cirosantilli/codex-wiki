Diagonal scaling for a two-dimensional linear system (κ2​(B)=51/49)

Consider the original linear system
A=(11/5​1/5100​),b=(11​),M=(10​0100​).
(9)
Completing the square proves that A is a symmetric positive-definite matrix:
eTAe=(e1​+e2​/5)2+252499​e22​>0(e=0).
(10)
The Jacobi preconditioner M has S=diag(1,10). Symmetric positive-definite preconditioning gives
B=(11/50​1/501​),c=(11/10​).
(11)
Its eigenvectors (1,1)T and (1,−1)T have eigenvalues 51/50 and 49/50. Hence
κ2​(B)=4951​≈1.040816.
(12)
The characteristic polynomial of A is λ2−101λ+2499/25. Its eigenvalues and spectral condition number of a positive-definite matrix are
λ±​=10505±245029​​,κ2​(A)=λ−​λ+​​≈100.040824.
(13)
The ellipse axis ratio drops from about 10.0020 to 1.0202. The transformation multiplies the second coordinate by ten, bringing the two energy scales together.
Both panels show the same energy level sets, expressed in error coordinates e=x−x∗​ and η=M1/2e. Their coordinate axes have equal scales. The marked displacement (1,0.1)T becomes (1,1)T; its energy remains eTAe/2=1.02. These are exact contours of the specified quadratic form.
Solving the original equations gives
x∗​=24991​(249520​),y∗​=24991​(2495200​).
(14)
Direct matrix multiplication checks both linear-system residuals:
Ax∗​=24991​(2495+4499+2000​)=b,By∗​=24991​(2495+4499/10+200​)=c.
(15)
By contrast, left preconditioning gives M−1A=(11/500​1/51​), which is visibly not a symmetric matrix, despite having the same positive eigenvalues as B.
A bad scaling can reverse the benefit: the still valid symmetric positive-definite matrix Mbad​=diag(100,1) gives Bbad​=(1/1001/50​1/50100​). Its Rayleigh quotients on the coordinate unit vectors are 0.01 and 100, so κ2​(Bbad​)≥10000. Cheapness and positive definiteness do not guarantee improved conditioning.
