Independent-moment magnetic response (M=nμtanh(μμ0​Happ​/(kB​T)))

Consider a stipulated dilute collection of independent two-level systems, each with longitudinal magnetic moment sμ, s=±1, and energy Es​=−sμB0​. The prescribed vacuum/applied magnetic flux density is B0​=μ0​Happ​. Ignore interactions, orbital magnetic response, and the small sample-field correction. This is the magnetic version of the existing independent spin-one-half two-level system, with alignment probabilities rather than an interacting Curie–Weiss model.
For temperature T>0, the canonical ensemble gives the one-moment partition function Z1​=2coshx, with x=μB0​/(kB​T). Taking the expected value over its two Boltzmann weights gives
⟨s⟩=ex+e−xex−e−x​=tanhx,M=nμtanhx,χmodel​≡(∂Happ​∂M​)T​=kB​Tμ0​nμ2​cosh2x1​.
(91)
Here n is the number density, kB​ the Boltzmann constant, and tanh the hyperbolic tangent. For ∣x∣≪1, its small-argument expansion of the hyperbolic tangent gives M≃μ0​nμ2Happ​/(kB​T), a Curie's law response in the applied-H convention. For large ∣x∣, M approaches the saturation magnetization nμ with sign set by the field, and its differential magnetic susceptibility falls towards zero.
Both plots use stipulated n=1026m−3, μ=9.2740101×10−24JT−1, kB​=1.380649×10−23JK−1, and μ0​=1.25663706×10−6TmA−1. The moment is approximately one Bohr magneton; the chosen vacuum permeability is a rounded model input. At T=2K, Ms​=927.401Am−1 and χmodel​(0)=3.91410×10−4. The linear approximation overestimates M by less than 3% for ∣x∣≤0.3, corresponding to ∣μ0​Happ​∣≤0.89324T or ∣Happ​∣≤0.71082MAm−1. These parameters keep the modeled response weak; neglected magnetic feedback is of relative order χmodel​(0), rather than an explanation of a material's ordered phase.
At B0​=0, cooling increases the zero-field slope as 1/T. At fixed B0​=0, cooling instead gives χmodel​∼4μ0​nμ2e−2μ∣B0​∣/(kB​T)/(kB​T): a saturated moment can hardly change further. The order of the zero-field and zero-temperature limits matters. This independent model has no finite-temperature collective phase transition; the diverging zero-field slope alone does not establish one.
