Write the ordinary plasma wave as
δE(r,t)=Re{[a0​+εa1​+⋯]eiS(r,t)},k=∇S,ω=−∂t​S.
(18)
The eikonal function S is dimensionless; it varies by order ε−1 across a background scale Lbg​, where ε∼λ/Lbg​≪1. The amplitudes and equilibrium plasma parameters vary on Lbg​, so ∣∇aj​∣≪k∣aj​∣ and ∣∇k∣≪k2. The equilibrium is stationary, so ω is constant along propagation. Assume infinitesimal fields, negligible collisions, kvth,s​≪ω, negligible ion response, and a fixed ordinary plasma wave polarization with B0​∥z^ and spatial variation along x. The WKB approximation is used away from wave turning points and plasma wave resonances.
Replacing derivatives on the fast factor by ik and −iω in the Maxwell equations reproduces the local magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor equation of b. In this one-dimensional ordinary plasma wave geometry, the Maxwell equations and the local cold parallel current density in fact give the scalar equation directly:
dx2d2δEz​​+k2(x)δEz​=0,k2(x)=c2ω2−ωpe2​(x)​.​
(19)
For δEz​=a(x)eiS(x), substitution gives
a′′+2ika′+ik′a+[k2(x)−(S′)2]a=0.
(20)
The leading eikonal equation is (S′)2=k2(x), and the next order gives 2ka′+k′a=0, hence the flux-normalized WKB amplitude a∝k−1/2. In particular, require ∣k′∣/∣k∣2≪1 and ∣k′′∣/∣k∣3≪1; these fail at the wave turning points.
