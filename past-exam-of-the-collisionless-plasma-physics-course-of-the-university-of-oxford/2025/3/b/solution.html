For a static equilibrium, linearization of magnetic induction gives
δB=∇×(ξ×B0​)=(B0​⋅∇)ξ−(ξ⋅∇)B0​−B0​∇⋅ξ.
(44)
The rotating local basis must be differentiated. In particular, ∂∥​x^=b0​/R and ∂∥​b0​=−x^/R. Their contributions proportional to ξ∥​ cancel in the radial component, yielding
δbx​=ik∥​ξx​,δby​=ik∥​ξy​.​
(45)
Writing h=δB/B0​, the magnitude component is
h=ik∥​ξ∥​+Rξx​​+LB​ξx​​−∇⋅ξ=LB​ξx​​−iky​ξy​,
(46)
where ∇⋅ξ=ξx​/R+iky​ξy​+ik∥​ξ∥​ when ∂x​ξx​=0.
Set M=B02​/(4π)=ρ0​vA2​ and δΠ=δp⊥​+Mh. The transverse y component of the linear kinetic magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation is
ρ0​(ω2−k∥2​vA2​)ξy​=iky​δΠ.
(47)
Since ky​≫k∥​∼ω/vA​, perpendicular magnetic pressure enforces the leading pressure-balanced plasma perturbation condition δΠ=0, giving
h=−B02​4πδp⊥​​=−2β​p0​δp⊥​​.​
(48)
In the radial component, the curvature part of the perturbed magnetic force is −2Mh/R: one contribution comes from (B0​⋅∇)δB and one from (δB⋅∇)B0​. The perturbed pressure anisotropy contributes −(δp⊥​−δp∥​)/R. After the leading total-pressure constraint has removed ∂x​δΠ, the radial equation is
−ρ0​ω2ξx​=−Mk∥2​ξx​−R2Mh​−Rδp⊥​−δp∥​​.
(49)
Substituting Mh=−δp⊥​ gives
(ω2−k∥2​vA2​)ξx​=−ρ0​Rδp⊥​+δp∥​​.​
(50)
Although h is small at low plasma beta, its contribution Mh to the force balance is of the same order as δp⊥​ and must be kept.
