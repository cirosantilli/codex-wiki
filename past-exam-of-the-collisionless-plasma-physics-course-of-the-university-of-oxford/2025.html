A plasma wave resonance has n2→∞. At finite coefficients of the magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor, its generic condition is the vanishing of the coefficient of n4:
Ssin2θ+Pcos2θ=0,tan2θ=−P/S.​
(12)
A plasma wave cutoff has n2=0, so the constant term of the dispersion relation vanishes:
PRL=0,that is P=0, R=0, or L=0.​
(13)
Both statements are branch conditions: check the undivided dispersion relation at special angles and simultaneous zero factors. In particular, the parallel plasma oscillation at P=0 is a longitudinal branch with arbitrary k in the cold limit, while the transverse parallel branches have plasma wave cutoffs at R=0 or L=0. Poles of the magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor can give additional limiting plasma wave resonances, such as the parallel cold-plasma right-handed wave at ω=Ωe​; these are not obtained by assuming finite S,P,g.

d OurBigBook.com
For parallel propagation, use the undivided dispersion relation:
P(n2−R)(n2−L)=0.
(14)
The five requested cases are:
  • Plasma oscillations: P=0 and δE∥B0​∥k. With negligible ion response, ω2=ωpe2​, independently of k in this cold approximation.
  • Cold-plasma right-handed waves: n2=R, Ey​=iEx​, Ez​=0, for propagation along +B0​. At a fixed point, the real electric field rotates from +x^ towards +y^​, in the sense of electron gyration.
  • Cold-plasma left-handed waves: n2=L, Ey​=−iEx​, Ez​=0. The real electric field rotates in the opposite sense.
  • Ordinary plasma waves: for perpendicular propagation, θ=π/2, n2=P, and δE∥B0​. Their dispersion relation is ω2=ωpe2​+c2k2.
  • Extraordinary plasma waves: for perpendicular propagation, n2=RL/S, Ez​=0, and Ey​=igEx​/(S−n2). In general the electric field has both longitudinal and transverse components.
For the perpendicular cases the undivided dispersion relation factors as (n2−P)(Sn2−RL)=0, up to an overall nonzero factor. This also identifies the extraordinary plasma wave plasma wave resonance at S=0.

e OurBigBook.com
An electrostatic plasma wave has δE=−ikϕ. In the absence of externally imposed charge density, the Gauss law for the magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor is k⋅ϵ⋅δE=0. Consequently,
k⊥2​S+k∥2​P=0.​
(15)
The antisymmetric terms of the magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor cancel in this scalar contraction. For oblique propagation this is the leading longitudinal condition in the large-refractive index approximation; a small transverse electric field is generally required by the full Maxwell equations at finite n. For parallel propagation an exactly longitudinal solution exists: P=0, hence ω2≃ωpe2​. It coincides with the θ→0 limit of the finite-coefficient plasma wave resonance condition in c. At exactly θ=0, P factors out of the transverse dispersion relation, leaving the independent plasma oscillation rather than an isolated divergence of the transverse branches.

Use Gaussian units and the kinetic magnetohydrodynamics equations
∂t​B=∇×(u×B),ρDt​u=−∇⋅P+4π(∇×B)×B​,P=p⊥​I+(p∥​−p⊥​)bb,

The specified Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution has zero mean motion and isotropic pressure, p⊥0​=p∥0​=p0​. All equilibrium scalars depend only on x, while b0​⋅∇x=0 and b0​⋅∇B0​=0. It therefore cannot erase the displacement-generated contrast between adjacent tubes. This is why the term v∥​δbx​∂x​f0α​ in c exactly cancels the apparent parallel relaxation of −ξx​∂x​f0α​. No collisional restoration of a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution is needed.

An outward-displaced flux tube carries higher pressure than its new surroundings. In the unfavorable magnetic field-line curvature of the Z-pinch, the resulting imbalance drives it farther outward. Parallel variation bends field lines, and magnetic tension supplies the stabilizing k∥2​vA2​ term. The fastest local interchange is at k∥​=0, with γ=ωI​; this endpoint is understood as the limit of the stated Alfvénic ordering. There is no leading ky​ dependence once ky​≫k∥​.

At ∣k∥​∣=kc​, the leading equation is ξ¨​x​=0, so the marginal solutions are a constant and a term linear in time; neglected corrections determine finer behavior. For a favorable reversed pressure gradient, Lp​<0, this drive is stabilizing.
The leading dispersion relation is the same in ideal magnetohydrodynamics. Its adiabatic law gives δp=−ξx​p0′​−Γp0​∇⋅ξ; the compressive part is smaller than the steep-gradient advection by O(Lp​/R) or O(β), giving the same leading δp=−ξx​p0′​. Both mechanisms can extract free energy from an equilibrium pressure gradient in unfavorable magnetic field-line curvature, but the dynamics retained here is the macroscopic electromagnetic displacement of a flux tube. It depends on the total pressure gradient, including electron and ion contributions, and it does not require an ion temperature gradient specifically. Its stable branch has ω∼k∥​vA​; with low plasma beta, this is much faster than ion parallel streaming at the same k∥​.
The familiar ion-temperature-gradient instability is a slower drift-scale instability. In its usual electrostatic ordering the Boltzmann electron response is established by rapid electron parallel motion, while ion magnetic drifts, ion temperature evolution and polarization drift provide the instability dynamics. A representative ordering is
k∥​vth,i​≲∣ω∣≪k∥​vth,e​,∣ω∣≪k∥​vA​,ω∗Ti​​∼Ωi​LTi​​ky​vth,i2​​,ωdi​∼Ωi​Rky​vth,i2​​.
(63)
The last two expressions specify scales rather than exact geometric factors; LTi​−1​=−dlnT0i​/dx. The present leading kinetic magnetohydrodynamics system drops these relative species drifts and their finite-gyroradius corrections. It also retains the perturbed field direction, which enforces the advected-distribution cancellation in c; a rapid electron streaming rate alone cannot turn that cancellation into a Boltzmann electron response on an unperturbed field.
To recover ion-temperature-gradient instability physics, take the slow, nearly electrostatic limit ∣ω∣/(k∥​vA​)≪1, retain magnetic curvature drift, gradient-B drift and polarization drift in low-flow drift kinetics or gyrokinetics, and allow a sufficiently strong destabilizing ion temperature gradient. At long perpendicular wavelengths one may still have k⊥​ρi​≪1, provided the leading nonzero ion polarization is kept; at k⊥​ρi​=O(1) full finite Larmor radius effects are needed. There is no contradiction between a drift-scale ion-temperature-gradient instability and the macroscopic interchange instability derived here: they use different asymptotic balances.
