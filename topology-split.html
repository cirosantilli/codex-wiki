Topology

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 33k Articles: 538
Topology studies topological spaces through open sets and continuous maps. Its central equivalence is a homeomorphism: a bijection that preserves continuity in both directions. This lets us distinguish spaces by connectedness or compactness even when numerical metric values are irrelevant. The open interval (0,1) and the real line have the same topology in this sense, although only the former is a bounded set with its usual metric.
The upper panels show an explicit homeomorphism and the inverse image test for a continuous map. The lower panels exhibit an obstruction to a homeomorphism: removing any point from a circle leaves a connected space, whereas removing an interior point from a closed interval gives two connected components.
Homotopy is a different notion: it varies a continuous map continuously with a parameter and may collapse points. For example, H(x,t)=(1−t)x contracts [0,1] to zero. Thus this closed interval and a singleton have the same homotopy type, but there is no homeomorphism between them because there is no bijection. Homotopy equivalence need not preserve compactness. [ref]
The abstract viewpoint already appears explicitly in Tietze's 1923 paper: he describes topology as the study of properties invariant under invertible continuous maps with continuous inverses, and discusses axioms for neighbourhoods, citing Hausdorff's 1914 book, page 213. [ref]
