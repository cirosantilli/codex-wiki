Shot noise spectral convention (SI(1)​(f)=2qI,SI(2)​(f)=qI)

Write an ideal point-transfer model using the Dirac delta function:
i(t)=q∑k​δ(t−tk​),δi(t)=i(t)−I.
(44)
For this arrival stream with independent increments, distinct arrivals contribute the product of the means, which cancels when centering. The same-arrival term leaves the autocovariance
E[δi(t)δi(t+s)]=q2λδ(s)=qIδ(s).
(45)
Its Fourier transform gives the two-sided power spectral density, while folding negative frequencies onto positive ones gives the one-sided convention:
SI(2)​(f)=qI(−∞<f<∞),SI(1)​(f)=2qI(f>0),[SI​]=A2/Hz.
(46)
These spectra describe δi, excluding the spectral line from the mean direct current. The amplitude spectral density is 2qI​ in A/Hz​; it is not a root mean square electric current until a measurement signal bandwidth is specified. Approximate white noise requires frequencies low enough that the single-transfer pulse shape and any correlations can be neglected. Neither the ideal impulses nor a flat spectrum extending to infinity describe a real detector at every timescale.
All spectra above are per hertz, with f in cycles per second. One possible angular frequency convention defines a density SI(2)​(ω) integrated using dω alone. Since ω=2πf, it has
SI(2)​(ω)=2πSI(2)​(ω/(2π))​,Var(ifiltered​)=∫−∞∞​SI(2)​(ω)∣H(ω/(2π))∣2dω.
(47)
Another common convention keeps the numerical density qI but integrates it with dω/(2π). State the integration measure as well as whether a spectrum is one-sided: a change of variable and a folding of signs are separate operations.
