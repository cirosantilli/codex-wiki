Brier score (LB​(p,y)=(p−y)2)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Scoring rule Proper scoring rule Strictly proper scoring rule
OurBigBook.com Words: 203
The binary Brier score applies squared-error loss to a reported success probability p and the observed indicator y∈{0,1}. It is a loss, so smaller is better. Its values lie in [0,1]: predicting the outcome with certainty gives zero, while assigning it zero probability gives one. Unlike the logarithmic scoring rule loss, the binary Brier score remains bounded even for a confidently wrong forecast.
For a Bernoulli distribution with true success probability q, averaging the two squared losses gives
Eq​[(p−Y)2]=q(p−1)2+(1−q)p2=(p−q)2+q(1−q).
(24)
The report controls only (p−q)2; the remaining term is the variance of the Bernoulli random variable. Consequently the Brier score is a strictly proper scoring rule. At q=0.7, the expected loss is (p−0.7)2+0.21, with unique minimum 0.21 at p=0.7. Reporting p=0.2 instead gives expected loss 0.46, despite its better loss on the particular outcome Y=0 above.
For several possible categories, the Brier score sums the squared differences between reported probabilities and observed category indicators. With two categories that sum is 2(p−y)2, so its numerical values are twice the binary convention used here, with the same optimizing report. Brier's original [ref] averages these category-sum losses over forecasts. Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation by Tilmann Gneiting and others (Section 3.1, Example 1, and Table 1) gives the category-sum and binary forms as negative rewards; negating them gives the losses described here.
