Boundary limits of telescoping series (∑k=1∞​(ak​−ak−1​)=L−a0​if an​→L)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Addition Summation Telescoping sum
OurBigBook.com Words: 194
For a real sequence (an​), the partial sum of ∑k=1∞​(ak​−ak−1​) is exactly an​−a0​. Consequently it is a convergent series if and only if an​ has a finite limit L, and its sum is L−a0​. Cancellation takes place inside each finite partial sum; taking a limit is a separate step.[ref]
The partial fraction telescoping example has SN​=N/(3N+1) and therefore limit 1/3. In contrast, let an​=(−1)n. Its first entry is a0​=1, so SN​=(−1)N−1. The even partial sums are zero and the odd partial sums are negative two. Both finite cancellations are correct, but the sequence has no single limit, giving a divergent series.
It is also essential to keep the two contributions paired. For example, ∑k=1∞​(1/k−1/(k+1)) is a convergent series with sum one, as its finite partial sum is 1−1/(N+1). Writing it instead as ∑k=1∞​1/k−∑k=1∞​1/(k+1) asks to subtract two divergent series. The permitted finite cancellation does not define that separate subtraction. Working through partial sums keeps the indices aligned and the operation meaningful.
