Weak law without an absolute first moment

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Convergence of random variables Convergence in probability Weak law of large numbers
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
Let X have a symmetric probability distribution with P(∣X∣>x)=1/(xlogx) for x≥e, mass 1−1/e at zero, and no mass with 0<∣X∣<e. Then E∣X∣=∞, but independent copies satisfy the weak law of large numbers with limit zero. Indeed nP(∣X∣>n)=1/logn→0. Their symmetric truncations Yn,j​=Xj​1{∣Xj​∣≤n}​ have mean zero and second moment O(n/logn), so Chebyshev's inequality gives n−1∑j≤n​Yn,j​→0 in probability. A union bound shows that the original and truncated sums differ with probability tending to zero. This also gives a differentiable characteristic function at zero whose derivative cannot be interpreted as an absolutely convergent expectation.
