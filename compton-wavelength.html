Compton wavelength (λC​=h/(mc))

 Home Physics Mathematical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 12
The Compton wavelength of a subatomic particle of rest mass m>0 is λC​=h/(mc), where h is the Planck constant and c the speed of light. It is the wavelength of a photon whose energy hc/λC​ equals that subatomic particle's rest energy mc2. It is a characteristic scale, rather than a measured geometric radius of the subatomic particle.
The reduced Compton wavelength is λˉC​=ℏ/(mc)=λC​/(2π), with ℏ=h/(2π). Keep that factor of 2π: the Compton scattering wavelength shift below uses h/(me​c), not ℏ/(me​c). Here we choose the rounded electron value λC​=2.426pm, where 1pm=10−12m; the maximum single-collision wavelength increase is 2λC​=4.852pm.
Why this scale? A photon of wavelength λ has momentum magnitude h/λ. At λ∼λC​ that momentum is comparable with mc, so transfer of an appreciable fraction of it can make the recoiling subatomic particle relativistic. Even for longer wavelengths, Compton scattering can resolve the small energy transferred into electron kinetic energy.
