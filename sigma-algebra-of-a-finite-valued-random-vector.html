Sigma-algebra of a finite-valued random vector (∣σ(V)∣=2∣V(Ω)∣)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Sigma-algebra Sigma-algebra generated by a random variable
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
Let a random vector V have a finite actual range of q values. Its nonempty fibres partition the sample space, and every event in σ(V) is a union of those fibres. Thus each fibre is an atom of a sigma-algebra and σ(V) has exactly 2q elements. This assertion concerns the actual range; additional values attained only on null sets must still be counted when working with an uncompleted sigma-algebra literally.
To verify the claim, the fibres belong to σ(V) as preimages of singleton Borel sets. Their unions form a finite sigma-algebra making V measurable, so minimality of the generated sigma-algebra gives the reverse inclusion. Distinct subcollections have distinct unions because the fibres are nonempty and disjoint.
Consequently a finite history of finite-valued observations generates a finite natural filtration at each fixed time. It may be strictly smaller than the filtration of its driving continuous observations: a single uniform random variable already generates infinitely many distinct events {U≤t} with different probabilities.
