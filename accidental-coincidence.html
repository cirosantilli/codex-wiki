Accidental coincidence (Racc​≃r1​r2​w)

 Home Physics Particle detection Particle detector Trigger (particle physics) Coincidence circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 236
Suppose two independent background streams follow Poisson processes with rates r1​ and r2​. Define a coincidence by ∣t2​−t1​∣<w/2: w is the full accepted time-difference width. Around each first-counter signal, the expected number of second-counter signals is r2​w. The expected pair rate is therefore r1​r2​w. A circuit producing at most one output per first signal instead gives
Racc​=r1​(1−e−r2​w)≃r1​r2​w(r2​w≪1).
(22)
The distinction matters when multiple detections occur in one window. If a different convention accepts ∣t2​−t1​∣<τ, its full width is w=2τ and the dilute expression is 2r1​r2​τ.
For a chosen numerical model, take background rates r1​=100s−1, r2​=80s−1 and w=20μs. The dilute pair rate is 0.16s−1. These are stipulated teaching inputs, not measurements of the historical chamber.
Making w smaller also rejects some genuine events. Suppose true pairs arrive at rate S0​ before the timing cut, and their time difference has a centered normal distribution with standard deviation σt​. Its accepted fraction, using the error function, is
ϵt​(w)=erf(22​σt​w​),S(w)=S0​ϵt​(w).
(23)
For chosen S0​=2.0s−1 and σt​=5μs, the 20μs window retains about 95.45% of true pairs. In the dilute background model the fraction of accepted events that are true is S/(S+Racc​)≃92.27%. A narrow window trades detection efficiency against background; it does not make either quantity equal to one. Delaying one counter's signal far beyond genuine event correlations gives a proposed background check under stationarity, without assuming that the zero-delay excess itself is accidental.
