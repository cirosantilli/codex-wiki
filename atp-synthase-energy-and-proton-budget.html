ATP synthase energy and proton budget

 Home Biology Enzyme ATP synthase
OurBigBook.com Words: 576 Articles: 1
Define side P as the higher proton electrochemical potential reservoir and side N as the synthesis-side reservoir. For transfer P→N at common temperature T, define the positive downhill proton-motive force magnitude Δpdrive​ by
Δμ​P→N​=RTlnaP​aN​​+F(ψN​−ψP​)=−FΔpdrive​.
(3)
Here a is thermodynamic activity, ψ is electric potential, R is the molar gas constant and F is the Faraday constant. Thus both a higher proton thermodynamic activity and a more positive electric potential on side P contribute to the driving magnitude. This definition fixes the sign; conventions for a signed proton-motive force differ. The calculation under chemiosmosis separates these two contributions numerically.
Assume fixed reservoirs, tight mechanical energy coupling, one transported proton per c subunit per revolution, and three ATP produced per revolution. Neglect leaks, slipping and membrane transport of the chemical reagents. Let gATP​>0 be the local molar Gibbs free energy cost of ATP formation at the stipulated composition, pH and magnesium-binding convention. It is not necessarily the standard state value. Adding changes in the state function gives, per mole of complete turns,
ΔGturn​=3gATP​−nFΔpdrive​.
(4)
For this model net synthesis is thermodynamically favoured when ΔGturn​<0. Equality is a reversible balance, not a promise of a useful reaction rate. A negative value establishes an available driving margin; it does not specify how fast the enzyme runs.
Choose n=10, gATP​=50.0kJmol−1, F=96485Cmol−1 and Δpdrive​=0.180V. These are illustrative inputs, not measurements of the preparation in the experiment below. Then
nFΔpdrive​=173.673kJmol−1,3gATP​=150.0kJmol−1,
(5)
and ΔGturn​=−23.673kJmol−1. Per ATP, the ideal input is 57.891kJmol−1, leaving a driving margin of 7.891kJmol−1. The balance point is
Δp∗​=nF3gATP​​≈0.15546V.
(6)
At 0.140V the same synthesis cycle instead has ΔGturn​=+14.921kJmol−1: the chosen reservoirs favour its reverse.
The machine is reversible: sufficient ATP hydrolysis can drive opposite rotation and pump protons uphill. Actual direction also depends on inhibition and available substrates; a weak proton-motive force need not make every intact enzyme immediately run backward. Respiratory uncoupling supplies a different failure mode: a parallel proton leak dissipates the gradient without productive shaft rotation.[ref]
The motor budget 10/3 is not the whole cost of ATP delivered outside a mitochondrion. Import of ADP and inorganic phosphate, and export of ATP, also draw on the electrochemical gradient.[ref] For a deliberately simplified system budget, assign these transports together one additional downhill-proton equivalent per delivered ATP, valued at the same FΔpdrive​. This is a chosen accounting convention, not a claim of one extra literal proton through F0​, nor a universal transport stoichiometry. The total becomes 10/3+1=13/3 equivalents and 75.258kJmol−1 of gradient expenditure per delivered ATP at 0.180V. The ratio of stored chemical free energy to that expenditure is 50/75.258≈66.4%, compared with 50/57.891≈86.4% for the motor alone. This system budget changes the resource expenditure, not the motor's balance point Δp∗​. Real transport terms require their own electrochemical potentials, and leaks add further cost.
