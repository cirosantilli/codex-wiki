Symplectic adjoint (ω(Tv,w)=ω(v,T∗w))

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Bilinear form Nondegenerate bilinear form Symplectic vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 83
For a finite-dimensional symplectic vector space, the symplectic adjoint of a linear map T is the unique linear map T∗ satisfying the displayed identity. Existence and uniqueness follow because the nondegenerate bilinear form identifies the vector space with its dual space. If ω has matrix J in a basis, then T∗=J−1TTJ. Thus (ST)∗=T∗S∗ and (T∗)∗=T, and membership in the symplectic Lie algebra is equivalent to T∗=−T. This operation is linear over the ground field; it does not involve complex conjugation. It preserves diagonalizability and the property of being a nilpotent linear map, since it is conjugate to matrix transpose.
