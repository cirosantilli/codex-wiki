Gibbs-Duhem equation (SdT−Vdp+∑i​ni​dμi​=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
The Euler theorem for homogeneous functions applied to an extensive quantity, together with dE=TdS−pdV+μdN give
E=TS−pV+μN,SdT−Vdp+Ndμ=0.
(86)
The Gibbs-Duhem equation constrains changes of the intensive quantities describing one uniform thermodynamic phase. For a mixture with k independently specified component amounts, its molar form is
SdT−Vdp+∑i=1k​ni​dμi​=0.
(87)
Here S is entropy, V is volume, T is temperature, p is pressure, ni​ is the amount of substance of component i, and μi​ is its molar chemical potential. This assumes a simple compressible system whose bulk internal energy is an extensive quantity; additional work coordinates require additional terms.[ref]
The proof exposes the source of the constraint. For internal energy E(S,V,n1​,…,nk​), replication of the whole thermodynamic phase by a factor λ gives E(λS,λV,λn1​,…,λnk​)=λE(S,V,n1​,…,nk​). The Euler theorem for homogeneous functions and the fundamental differential of internal energy therefore give
E=TS−pV+∑i​μi​ni​,dE=TdS−pdV+∑i​μi​dni​.
(88)
Taking the differential of the first equation produces all the terms in the second, plus SdT−Vdp+∑i​ni​dμi​. Subtraction proves the Gibbs-Duhem equation. Thus the constraint comes from two descriptions of the same state function, rather than an assumed microscopic interaction model.
At fixed temperature and pressure, divide by n=∑i​ni​>0 and introduce the mole fractions xi​=ni​/n:
∑i​xi​dμi​=0.
(89)
The chemical potentials cannot be varied independently. In a two-component mixture, put xA​=x and xB​=1−x. Within a smooth single-thermodynamic phase region with 0<x<1,
x(∂x∂μA​​)T,p​+(1−x)(∂x∂μB​​)T,p​=0,dμB​=−1−xx​dμA​.
(90)
One chemical potential fixes the other's change, but determining its absolute value still requires a reference value. This does not say μA​=μB​: equality at phase coexistence concerns the same chemical species in different thermodynamic phases. Nor does the constraint say dG=0 whenever temperature and pressure are fixed; changing composition still contributes ∑i​μi​dni​ to the Gibbs free energy.
