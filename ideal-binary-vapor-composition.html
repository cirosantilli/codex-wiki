For the stipulated A/B ideal solution, put x=xA​. At the fixed temperature 300K, Raoult's law reduces to
P=(20+60x)kPa,yA​=20+60x80x​=1+3x4x​.
(14)
The relative volatility is α=pA∗​/pB∗​=4. For 0<x<1,
yA​−x=1+3x3x(1−x)​>0.
(15)
Hence every interior vapor-liquid equilibrium pair in this chosen model has A-enriched vapor; neither pure endpoint is enriched. Conversely,
xA​=4−3yA​yA​​.
(16)
For example, yA​=1/2 corresponds to xA​=1/5, pA​=pB​=16kPa and P=32kPa. Equal vapor mole fractions therefore need not mean equal liquid mole fractions.
These are fixed-temperature predictions. During boiling at a specified external pressure, the temperature instead adjusts until ∑i​xi​pi∗​(T)=Pexternal​. The two stipulated vapor pressures specify only one temperature; they provide no boiling-temperature curve.
