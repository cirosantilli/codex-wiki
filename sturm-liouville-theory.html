Sturm-Liouville equation (−(py′)′+qy=λwy)

Words: 65
Sturm-Liouville theory OurBigBook.com
A second-order differential equation in the displayed divergence form. Under the usual regular spectral assumptions, p>0, w>0, the coefficients have suitable regularity, and separated self-adjoint boundary conditions specify a Sturm-Liouville problem. Multiplying by y and using zero endpoint values gives the Rayleigh quotient λ=∫(py′2+qy2)dx/∫wy2dx for a nonzero real eigenfunction. Sign conventions for the potential and eigenvalue vary; they must be read from the stated equation.

Sturm oscillation theorem

Words: 69
Sturm-Liouville theory OurBigBook.com
For a real one-dimensional Sturm-Liouville problem with separated self-adjoint boundary conditions and discrete simple spectrum, the eigenfunction for the nth eigenvalue has exactly n interior zeros, numbering from zero. For a confining real Schrödinger operator on the line, the same ordering makes the ground state nodeless and the first excited state have one node. If the potential is even, uniqueness and reflection symmetry imply that these states are respectively even and odd.

Orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions (∫ab​wym​​yn​dx=0)

Words: 109 Articles: 1
Sturm-Liouville theory OurBigBook.com
For a regular self-adjoint Sturm-Liouville problem with positive weight w and self-adjoint boundary conditions, eigenfunctions with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal in the weighted inner product ∫wuv. Integration by parts proves this by equating ∫uLv and ∫Luv.

Derivative orthogonality when the Sturm-Liouville potential vanishes (∫ab​pyn′​ym′​dx=λn​∫ab​wyn​ym​dx)

Words: 78
Orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions OurBigBook.com
For the Sturm-Liouville problem −(pyn′​)′=λn​wyn​, assume that the boundary term [pyn′​ym​]ab​ vanishes. One integration by parts gives the displayed identity. Distinct eigenvalues make the right side zero by orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions, so the derivatives are orthogonal with weight p, while the original functions are orthogonal with weight w. This conclusion uses the absence of the qy term: for a general potential the extra integral −∫qyn​ym​ need not vanish. For the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind of degree n−1, the two weights are w=1−x2​ and p=(1−x2)3/2.

Cubic-coordinate reduction of a singular Sturm-Liouville operator (L=−x−4D2+2x−5D−a ⟼ −Dz2​−a,z=x3/3)

Words: 81
Sturm-Liouville operator OurBigBook.com
For 0<x<1 and zero endpoint values, write L=w−1[−(py′)′+qy] with p=x−2, w=x2 and q=−ax2. The change of variable z=x3/3 has dz=x2dx and sends y′=x2Yz​, y′′=2xYz​+x4Yzz​. Thus it is an isometry from the weighted inner product to the ordinary integral on 0<z<1/3, and the apparent singular operator becomes −Yzz​−aY. Its Dirichlet eigenvalues are 9n2π2−a and its normalized eigenfunctions are 6​sin(nπx3), n≥1. The singular endpoint is handled through this transformed regular domain; a zero eigenvalue occurs exactly when a=9n2π2.

Flux-domain realization of a one-dimensional elliptic operator (D(L)={v∈H01​(0,1):pv′∈H1(0,1)})

Words: 74
Sturm-Liouville operator OurBigBook.com
For continuous p>0 and bounded real q, the form a(u,v)=∫(pu′v′+quv) on the zero-trace Sobolev space defines the weak operator L=−(pu′)′+qu. Its literal L2 operator domain is the displayed flux domain. If a weak solution has right-hand side f∈L2, then (pu′)′=qu−f∈L2, so it belongs to that domain. This avoids assuming that a merely differentiable coefficient has bounded derivative. With p∈C1 bounded away from zero, the domain reduces to H2∩H01​.

Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator (uL(v)−vL(u)=[p(uv′−vu′)]′)

Words: 36
Sturm-Liouville operator OurBigBook.com
For L(v)=(pv′)′+av, direct differentiation gives uL(v)−vL(u)=[p(uv′−vu′)]′. The potential terms cancel. Integrating with common homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions eliminates the endpoint term. If L(u)=0 and L(v)=f, this gives ∫uf=0, a necessary solvability condition for the inhomogeneous equation.

Exponential ground state of a linearly weighted Sturm-Liouville problem (−(xy′)′+ω2xy=λy,λ0​=ω,y0​=e−ωx)

Words: 105
Sturm-Liouville problem OurBigBook.com
For ω>0 on x>0, use the regular finite branch at zero, decay at infinity, and a finite quadratic-form energy. For trial functions with [xy2]0∞​=0, integration by parts gives
∫0∞​x(y′2+ω2y2)dx=ω∫0∞​y2dx+∫0∞​x(y′+ωy)2dx.
(4)
The Rayleigh quotient is at least ω, with equality exactly for a nonzero multiple of e−ωx. Direct substitution verifies that this is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue ω, so the lower bound is attained. The zero coefficient p(0)=0 makes this a singular-endpoint Sturm-Liouville problem; regularity replaces a prescribed zero endpoint value. Continuous piecewise differentiable trials of finite form energy are allowed in the Rayleigh-Ritz method, even if their derivative has a jump away from the endpoint.

No-log boundary condition at a linearly vanishing Sturm-Liouville endpoint (limx↓a​p(x)y′(x)=0)

Words: 125
Sturm-Liouville problem OurBigBook.com
Suppose p(x)=c(x−a)+O((x−a)2) with c>0, and the other coefficients in (py′)′+(λw−q)y=0 extend continuously to a, with w(a)>0. A bounded eigenfunction has (py′)′=O(1), hence py′=C+O(x−a). A nonzero C would give y′∼C/[c(x−a)] and an unbounded logarithmic singularity. Thus C=0, and the regular branch has bounded first derivative. The second local branch carries a logarithm. The title condition excludes that branch.
For two regular eigenfunctions, p(uv′−u′v)→0. This removes the singular-end contribution to the Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator. Together with a separated self-adjoint condition at the other endpoint, it selects the regular self-adjoint differential operator realization. The precise operator domain uses the maximal differential-operator domain and the no-log endpoint condition; boundedness of classical eigenfunctions is its convenient local expression, not a replacement for specifying an operator domain.

Dirichlet-Robin eigenvalues on a finite interval (−y′′=λy,y(0)=0,y′(ℓ)+ky(ℓ)=0)

Words: 125
Sturm-Liouville problem OurBigBook.com
For ℓ>0 and k>0, the Sturm-Liouville problem with one Dirichlet boundary condition and one Robin boundary condition has positive eigenvalues. Indeed, integration by parts gives
λ∫0ℓ​y2dx=∫0ℓ​(y′)2dx+ky(ℓ)2>0
(5)
for every nonzero eigenfunction. Let z=ℓλ​ and κ=kℓ. The eigenfunctions are proportional to sin(zx/ℓ), with
tanz=−κz​.
(6)
For each n≥1, there is exactly one root zn​ in ((n−1/2)π,nπ): on that interval tanz+z/κ increases strictly from −∞ to a positive value. There are no other positive roots. The apparent root z=0 gives only the zero sine function and is not an eigenvalue.
For fixed k,ℓ, write zn​=(n−1/2)π+εn​. The root equation becomes κcotεn​=(n−1/2)π+εn​, hence
εn​=(n−1/2)πκ​+O(n−3),λn​=ℓ2(n−1/2)2π2​+ℓ2k​+O(n−2).
(7)
An optional unit normalization uses ∫0ℓ​sin2(λn​​x)dx=ℓ/2+k/[2(k2+λn​)]. The positive sign of the Robin boundary term matters: reversing it can introduce nonpositive modes, as in nonpositive modes in a Dirichlet-Robin interval expansion.

Radial Dirichlet spectrum in three dimensions (λn​=(nπ/R)2,un​(r)=sin(nπr/R)/r)

Words: 77
Sturm-Liouville problem OurBigBook.com
For the radial Laplace operator L=−r−2(r2u′)′ on 0<r<R, require a finite value at the origin and u(R)=0. The map u↦v=ru preserves the norm from L2((0,R),r2dr) to L2(0,R) and sends L to −d2/dr2. The regular endpoint condition becomes v(0)=0. The self-adjoint realization therefore corresponds to the ordinary Dirichlet boundary conditions for the one-dimensional second derivative. Its eigenvalues and eigenfunctions are displayed above, with un​(0)=nπ/R understood by a limit. Their squared weighted norm is R/2. Completeness and orthogonality follow from the Fourier sine series on (0,R).

Nonpositive modes in a Dirichlet-Robin interval expansion (y(0)=0,y′(1)=y(1)/α,α>0)

Words: 132
Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction expansion OurBigBook.com
For −y′′=λy, positive eigenvalues have eigenfunctions sin(qn​x), where tanqn​=αqn​ and qn​>0. Their squared norms are Nn​=1/2−sin(2qn​)/(4qn​). A zero eigenvalue occurs precisely at α=1, with eigenfunction x. A negative eigenvalue occurs precisely at 0<α<1: it is −κ2 with eigenfunction sinh(κx), where tanhκ=ακ. There is exactly one such κ, since tanhκ/κ decreases strictly from 1 to 0. The integration by parts identity
⟨y,−y′′⟩=∫01​∣y′∣2dx−α∣y(1)∣2​
(10)
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality show positivity for α>1 and nonnegativity at equality. The complete expansion of a square-integrable function therefore includes the extra term 3x∫01​tf(t)dt when α=1, or sinh(κx)∫01​f(t)sinh(κt)dt/[sinh(2κ)/(4κ)−1/2] when α<1, in addition to ∑n​[∫01​f(t)sin(qn​t)dt/Nn​]sin(qn​x). Omitting that extra mode makes the system incomplete. In particular, f=x at α=1 is orthogonal to every positive sine mode and is an immediate counterexample to a sine-only expansion.

Spectral Green function of a regular Sturm-Liouville problem (Gμ​(x,ξ)=∑n​yn​(x)yn​(ξ)/(λn​−μ))

Write Ay=−(py′)′+qy and Ayn​=λn​ryn​, where r is the positive weight. Normalize the real eigenfunctions using ∫rym​yn​=δmn​. For μ outside the spectrum, applying A−μr to the displayed Green function expansion gives r(x)∑n​yn​(x)yn​(ξ)=δ(x−ξ) by weighted completeness. Normalized Sturm-Liouville spectral Green kernel (Gμ​(x,ξ)=w(ξ)∑n​λn​−μyn​(x)yn​(ξ)∗​)
Words: 89
Spectral Green function of a regular Sturm-Liouville problem OurBigBook.com
Let L=w−1[−(d/dx)(pd/dx)+q] be the regular self-adjoint operator of a Sturm-Liouville problem, with eigenfunctions orthonormal for the weighted inner product ∫wuv. For μ outside its spectrum, the displayed kernel satisfies (Lx​−μ)Gμ​=δ(x−ξ) with an ordinary, unweighted Dirac delta. Indeed weighted completeness gives w(ξ)∑n​yn​(x)yn​(ξ)∗=δ(x−ξ). Thus u(x)=∫Gμ​(x,ξ)f(ξ)dξ solves (L−μ)u=f. A kernel integrated against w(ξ)dξ instead omits the prefactor. For real coefficients, the weighted reciprocity relation is Gμ​(x,ξ)/w(ξ)=Gμ​(ξ,x)/w(x) when the spectral parameter is real and outside the spectrum. A zero eigenvalue prevents inversion at μ=0 without a forcing compatibility condition or a projected inverse.

Translation-mode solvability for a bistable front (c1​=−∫U0​′h(U0​)/∫(U0​′)2)
Words: 177 Articles: 1
Fredholm solvability condition OurBigBook.com
Suppose ut​=Duxx​+f0​(u)+εh(u) has a balanced stationary front U0​(z), where z=(x−q)/D​ and U0′′​+f0​(U0​)=0. Take q˙​/D​=εc1​+⋯ and u=U0​+εU1​+⋯. At first order, LU1​=−c1​U0′​−h(U0​) with L=d2/dz2+f0′​(U0​). Differentiating the front equation proves LU0′​=0. Integration by parts against the decaying translation mode gives
c1​=−∫−∞∞​(U0′​)2dz∫−∞∞​U0′​h(U0​)dz​.
(15)
This is the Fredholm solvability condition determining the velocity, derived from the self-adjoint differential operator rather than assumed. An orthogonality condition or a fixed midpoint removes the freedom to add a multiple of U0′​ to U1​. For the bistable cubic reaction-diffusion equation, h=−aU0​(1−U0​) gives c1​=−2​a, and its first-order forcing vanishes pointwise, so the phase-fixed shape correction is zero.
Moving-defect locking of a cubic-quintic front
Words: 77
Translation-mode solvability for a bistable front OurBigBook.com
At the Maxwell balance for a scalar reaction-diffusion front, let q=3α/4, β=3​α/2, and perturb the linear growth rate by εKδ(x+cT), T=εt. Projection onto the front's translation mode gives XT​=−Ksech2(βZ/2), where Z=X+cT+β−1logq. Hence ZT​=c−Ksech2(βZ/2). Finite locked positions exist for 0<c/K≤1. For a strict inequality, the root with KZ<0 is stable and the other unstable; they meet in a saddle-node bifurcation at c=K. This is a leading-order solvability condition, not an exact finite-perturbation solution.
