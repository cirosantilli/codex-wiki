Coupled resonant circuit model (M=kL1​L2​​)

Let two series loops have inductances L1​,L2​>0, capacitances C1​,C2​>0, total electrical resistances R1​,R2​>0, and reciprocal mutual inductance M=kL1​L2​​ with 0≤k<1. For a steady sinusoid of angular frequency ω>0, choose complex peak amplitudes with time convention eiωt. An ideal source of peak electric potential difference V drives loop 1; loop 2 has no independent source. Then
Zj​=Rj​+i(ωLj​−ωCj​1​),(Z1​iωM​iωMZ2​​)(I1​I2​​)=(V0​),VI2​​=Z1​Z2​+ω2M2−iωM​.
(92)
Eliminating I1​ proves the response formula. Reversing one coil changes the sign of M and the relative wave phase, but leaves this amplitude response unchanged. The measured sense ratio is ∣Rs​I2​/V∣, with Rs​ included in R2​, not added a second time.
For identical loops at their uncoupled resonant frequency ω0​=1/LC​,
​VI2​​​ω0​​=R2+ω02​M2ω0​M​.
(93)
Writing x=ω0​M/R makes this x/[R(1+x2)]. Its derivative has the sign of 1−x2, so response at this fixed frequency is largest when x=1, equivalently k=1/Q. Too little coupling transfers little excitation; excessive coupling reduces the response at the original centre frequency. This is a fixed-frequency optimum, not a universal optimum over every possible receiver and drive frequency.
For the chosen values, k=1/Q=0.063246 gives M=0.31623mH. At source peak V=0.100V, the secondary peak electric current is 0.500mA and the sense peak electric potential difference is 25.0mV. The primary peak electric current is also 0.500mA, so source mean power is 25.0μW: each loop dissipates 12.5μW, checking conservation of energy. Here V is the equivalent ideal source behind R1​; a generator's terminal reading must be converted using its measured Thévenin resistance.
In the lossless identical-loop limit, the normal modes have equal or opposite electric currents, making their effective inductances L+M or L−M. Thus
flow​=1+k​f0​​,fhigh​=1−k​f0​​.
(94)
At k=0.20 these are 45.944 and 56.270kHz. Finite electrical resistance broadens and shifts the driven peaks, so the exact response formula, rather than these lossless values, generates the plot. Changing a capacitor moves the uncoupled tuning through f0​∝C−1/2: increasing it by 21% lowers f0​ by the factor 1/1.1. The model explains why tuning and coupling must be adjusted together; it supplies no transatlantic propagation or detector-threshold prediction.
