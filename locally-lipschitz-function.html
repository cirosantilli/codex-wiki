Locally Lipschitz function

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lipschitz continuity
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
A function between metric spaces is locally Lipschitz if every point has a neighbourhood on which one finite Lipschitz bound works for every pair. The neighbourhood and bound may depend on the point. A continuously differentiable real function on an open interval has this property: continuity of its derivative bounds that derivative on a sufficiently small closed interval, and the mean value theorem bounds every secant slope there.
The function x2 is locally Lipschitz at every real point but has no single bound on the real line. The square root is locally Lipschitz on (0,∞), and has the bound 1/(2a​) on [a,b] for 0<a<b. Adding zero to its domain changes the conclusion at that point: the ratios in the Lipschitz continuity example become unbounded, so it is not locally Lipschitz at zero. This distinction is what permits the delayed, nonunique ODE solutions illustrated under Picard-Lindelöf theorem.
