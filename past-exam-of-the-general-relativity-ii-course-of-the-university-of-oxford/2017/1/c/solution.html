The geodesic deviation equation compares neighboring geodesics by covariant derivatives. Replacing those derivatives by ordinary derivatives of coordinate components requires a parallel transport frame along the reference geodesic. There is also an affine parameter normalization to specify: the argument of M is u, and it becomes τ only after choosing u=τ.
For the intended local tidal calculation, take the reference timelike geodesic
γ(τ)=(u,v,x,y)=(τ,−τ/2,0,0),V=∂u​−21​∂v​.
(12)
Here g(V,V)=−1, so τ is proper time. Both F and its first partial derivatives vanish on γ. All Christoffel symbols therefore vanish along this curve, and its coordinate basis is parallel transport along it. With the Riemann curvature tensor from part (b),
Riuju​=−Mij​,Rubcd​=0,Rvuju​=0on γ.
(13)
The Jacobi field equation thus becomes, in this adapted frame,
σ¨u=0,σ¨v=0,σ¨i=Mij​(τ)σj.​
(14)
A purely transverse initial Jacobi field, with σu=σv=σ˙u=σ˙v=0 initially, remains transverse. This transverse tidal equation of a Brinkmann plane wave describes the relative tidal acceleration of the freely falling bodies.
In vacuum write the symmetric matrix as
M(u)=(A(u)B(u)​B(u)−A(u)​).
(15)
Its instantaneous eigenvalues are ±A2+B2​. The tidal acceleration stretches along one principal axis and squeezes along the perpendicular axis, with the axes allowed to vary with u. This describes accelerations; it does not imply monotonically growing and shrinking separations for arbitrary initial velocities. For the simple constant profile A=ω2, B=0,
σx=Ccosh(ωτ)+Dsinh(ωτ),σy=Pcos(ωτ)+Qsin(ωτ).
(16)
Thus the geodesic deviation can be defocusing in one direction and oscillatory in the other. For a varying profile, diagonalizing at each instant does not decouple the ordinary differential equations without also including derivatives of the rotating eigenvectors.
Qualification of the coordinate claim. The displayed equation for σv is not true for arbitrary coordinate displacements along arbitrary reference geodesics. For synchronized neighboring curves with u=τ and σu=0, the exact geodesic equations are
x¨=Mx,v¨=−21​xTM′x−2x˙TMx.
(17)
Their variation gives
σ¨⊥​=Mσ⊥​,σ¨v=−xTM′σ⊥​−2x˙TMσ⊥​−2xTMσ˙⊥​.
(18)
The second expression need not vanish. A concrete vacuum counterexample is M=diag(1,−1) and the one-parameter family of timelike geodesics
u=τ,x=Aeτ,y=0,v=−2τ​−2A2​e2τ.
(19)
Each has g(V,V)=−1. Differentiating with respect to A produces a Jacobi field
σu=0,σx=eτ,σy=0,σv=−Ae2τ,σ¨v=−4Ae2τ.
(20)
This even has g(V,σ)=0, so it is a physical orthogonal separation. The covariant derivative form of geodesic deviation remains correct; the unrestricted assertion about ordinary coordinate derivatives needs the adapted reference and frame used above. If instead u˙=c is a constant affine parameter normalization, the transverse tidal coefficient is c2M(u).
