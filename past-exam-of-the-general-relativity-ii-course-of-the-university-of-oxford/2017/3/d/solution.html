The assertion about every exterior radius is false for geodesics. A circular orbit here means an equatorial null geodesic at constant r, rather than a genuinely radial trajectory. A zero of the radial effective potential alone gives a turning point, not necessarily a circular orbit. The radial geodesic equation must hold as well.

Alternatively, constant-r null directions exist throughout the exterior because the two angular-velocity roots of gtt​+2Ωgtϕ​+Ω2gϕϕ​=0 are real; almost all of their integral curves require acceleration and fail the radial geodesic equation. This distinction explains how an all-radii assertion can arise.
Finally, r=r+​ itself has the null geodesic generators of its Killing horizon, proportional to χ+​=∂v​+Ω+​∂ψ​. They are a separate horizon case. On the horizon χ+​ is normal to every tangent, so its contractions with ∂v​ and ∂ψ​ both vanish: E=L=0 for a generator tangent. Its affine parameter can be chosen by rescaling that tangent. The exterior calculation dividing by E does not include it. The presence of these generators does not supply circular null geodesics at the intervening arbitrary exterior radii.
