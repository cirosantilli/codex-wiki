Write D=ua∇a​, θ=∇a​ua for the expansion scalar, ab​=ua∇a​ub​ for the four-acceleration, and Pb​c=δb​c+ub​uc for the spatial projector of a timelike congruence. The normalization of the four-velocity gives ubab​=0 and ub∇a​ub​=0. The product rule and metric compatibility give the covariant derivative of the perfect fluid in general relativity stress-energy tensor:
Fb​:=∇aTab​=ub​[D(ρ+p)+(ρ+p)θ]+(ρ+p)ab​+∇b​p.
(1)
Its tensor contraction with the four-velocity is
ubFb​=−Dρ−(ρ+p)θ,
(2)
and its projection with the spatial projector of a timelike congruence is
Pb​cFc​=(ρ+p)ab​+Pb​c∇c​p.
(3)
Thus stress-energy conservation implies the relativistic perfect-fluid energy equation and the relativistic Euler equation:
ua∇a​ρ+(ρ+p)∇a​ua=0,(ρ+p)ua∇a​ub​+(gab​+ua​ub​)∇ap=0.​
(4)
Conversely, the two displayed equations set both projections of Fb​ to zero. Since Fb​=Pb​cFc​−ub​ucFc​, they imply Fb​=0. This establishes equivalence, including points where ρ+p=0, without dividing by that quantity.

The sign of Tab​XaXb is unchanged by a nonzero rescaling of the timelike vector X. Normalize it to a four-velocity W and, after reversing its direction if necessary, write W=γ(u+w), where u⋅w=0, w⋅w=s∈[0,1) and γ=(1−s)−1/2 is the Lorentz factor. For the perfect fluid in general relativity,
Tab​WaWb=(ρ+p)γ2−p=ρ+(ρ+p)(γ2−1).
(6)
Taking W=u requires ρ≥0. Allowing the Lorentz factor to become arbitrarily large requires ρ+p≥0; otherwise sufficiently fast observers measure a negative value. Conversely, these two inequalities make both terms in the last expression nonnegative. Therefore the weak energy condition for a perfect fluid is exactly
ρ≥0,ρ+p≥0.​
(7)
Property (1) is the weak energy condition: every physical observer measures nonnegative local energy density, namely Tab​WaWb for their normalized four-velocity W. It allows negative pressure down to p=−ρ.

For relativistic dust, p=0, the relativistic Euler equation gives ρab​=0. The nowhere-vanishing density therefore makes the four-acceleration zero: u is a timelike geodesic vector field. Let α=u♭ be the differential one-form obtained by the musical isomorphism. Its exterior derivative satisfies
(ιu​dα)b​=ua(∇a​ub​−∇b​ua​)=ab​−21​∇b​(uaua​)=0.
(8)
Here ιu​ is the interior product. By hypersurface orthogonality and the Frobenius theorem, α∧dα=0. Taking the interior product and using α(u)=−1 gives
0=ιu​(α∧dα)=−dα−α∧ιu​dα=−dα.
(9)
Thus du♭=0​. By the Poincaré lemma, locally α=−dt, and then u(t)=−α(u)=1. Choose spatial coordinate functions xi on one level set of t and extend them constantly along the integral curves of a vector field u, using its smooth flow. This constructs u=∂t​. Since dt(∂i​)=0,
gtt​=g(u,u)=−1,gti​=α(∂i​)=−dt(∂i​)=0.
(10)
The metric on the orthogonal complement of a timelike vector is positive-definite, so the synchronous coordinates for irrotational dust have
u=∂t​,g=−dt2+gij​(t,x)dxi⊗dxj.​
(11)
This construction is local; crossing geodesics can prevent these synchronous coordinates from covering a larger region.

Use the active sign convention for the linearized coordinate gauge transformation, hμν​↦hμν​+∂μ​ξν​+∂ν​ξμ​. The paper's prescribed generator has precisely this sign; using the passive convention would require its negative. Direct partial differentiation gives
∂ν​ξμ​=41​[h~κσ′′​xκxσnμ​nν​+2h~νκ′​xκnμ​−2h~μκ′​xκnν​−2h~μν​].
(16)
The middle terms cancel when the indices are symmetrized. Therefore
∂μ​ξν​+∂ν​ξμ​=21​h~κσ′′​xκxσnμ​nν​−h~μν​,h^μν​=ϕnμ​nν​,ϕ=21​h~κσ′′​xκxσ.​
(17)
To test both equations (3), put H(u)=h~(u)=ηκσh~κσ​(u), the trace of the original metric perturbation. Since n is a null vector, h^=0 and its trace-reversed metric perturbation equals h^. Differentiating the result of part (a) gives
n⋅∂ϕ=nκh~κσ′′​xσ=21​uH′′,□ϕ=H′′+2nκh~κσ′′′​xσ=H′′+uH′′′.
(18)
In the second identity, the term with n2 is zero. Thus
∂μh^μν​=21​uH′′nν​,□h^μν​=(H′′+uH′′′)nμ​nν​.​
(19)
In general h^ does not satisfy (3). On an open interval of u, both equations hold exactly when H′′=0 there: the first equation forces this for u=0, and smoothness gives it at u=0 as well. A linearized coordinate gauge transformation preserves the linearized vacuum Einstein equations, but only a residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity preserves the imposed Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity.
For an explicit failure, use the null coordinates of part (c) and take only h~uv​=h~vu​=u2/4 nonzero. Then H=u2, the original field obeys (3), but ϕ=uv/2 gives ∂μh^μu​=u and □h^uu​=2.

Put dΩ22​=dθ2+sin2θdφ2, the standard metric tensor on the unit sphere. From t=v+m/λ one has dt=dv−mλ−2dλ. The apparently divergent terms cancel:
g=−λ2dv2+2mdvdλ+m2dΩ22​.​
(32)
The (v,λ) metric tensor block has determinant −m2=0, independently of λ. Its coefficients are real analytic, so this formula defines a regular Lorentzian metric on
M1​=Rv​×Rλ​×S2.​
(33)
The polar-coordinate degeneracies at the poles are handled by ordinary two-sphere charts. Thus λ=0 is a coordinate singularity of the original chart and is crossed by this spacetime extension.

The inverse of the (v,λ) metric tensor block is
(gAB)=(01/m​1/mλ2/m2​).
(34)
The normal covector dλ has squared norm λ2/m2. Therefore
{λ=λ0​} is timelike for λ0​=0,{λ=0} is null.​
(35)
More explicitly, the induced metric tensor is −λ02​dv2+m2dΩ22​; at zero its sole zero direction is ∂v​. The coefficients are independent of v, so K=∂v​ is a Killing vector field. On λ=0,
K2=−λ2=0,K♭=−λ2dv+mdλ=mdλ.
(36)
Hence K is both tangent and normal to this null hypersurface, proving it is a Killing horizon. The definition of surface gravity is ∇K​K=κK on the Killing horizon, or equivalently d(K2)=−2κK♭. Since d(K2)=−2λdλ vanishes there while K♭=0,
κ=0.​
(37)
Thus this is a degenerate Killing horizon, and v is an affine parameter on each of its null geodesics.
For a concrete horizon frequency comparison, fix the same angular position for a narrow high-frequency light beam and two freely falling radial observers. Work in geometric optics, with the beam's affinely transported wavevector tangent to the Killing horizon, k=qK. Here q>0 is constant because ∇K​K=0. Choose the observers as translates of one timelike geodesic by the isometry generated by K, crossing at v1​ and v2​. Give them the same positive Killing energy per unit rest mass, E=−K⋅W. At their crossings their normalized four-velocities have
Wλ=−mE​,Wv=2E1​,Wθ=Wφ=0,
(38)
which satisfy g(W,W)=2mWvWλ=−1. They can be realized by local geodesics with precisely these initial data. The measured angular frequency is
ω=−k⋅W=qE,ω1​ω2​​=1.​
(39)
To connect this measurement to surface gravity, if ∇K​K=κK with constant κ, affine transport of k=q(v)K would give dq/dv=−κq. For observers related by the same Killing vector field translation, −K⋅W agrees at the crossings, giving ω2​/ω1​=e−κ(v2​−v1​). Here there is no accumulated redshift along the Killing horizon for these equally prepared observers. Arbitrary observers with different four-velocities can still measure different frequencies through their different Killing energies.

First use the original null coordinates u=t+m/λ, v=t−m/λ. They obey u−v=2m/λ, and the radial metric tensor becomes
g(2)​=−(u−v)24m2​dudv.
(40)
Apply the given substitutions u=tan(U/2) and v=−cot(V/2). The identities
u−v=cos(U/2)sin(V/2)cos((U−V)/2)​,du=2cos2(U/2)dU​,dv=2sin2(V/2)dV​
(41)
give the analytic extension of a spacetime
g=−cos2((U−V)/2)m2​dUdV+m2dΩ22​.
(42)
The poles of the individual tangent and cotangent substitutions are now ordinary coordinate lines wherever cos((U−V)/2)=0. Set T=(U+V)/2 and X=(U−V)/2. Allowing these coordinate functions to continue across every such line gives
M=RT​×(−π/2,π/2)X​×S2,g​=m2sec2X(−dT2+dX2)+m2dΩ22​.​
(43)
This is the universal cover of two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime of radius m, times a two-sphere of radius m: the simply connected Bertotti-Robinson spacetime. In defining this maximal analytic extension, T ranges over the whole real line without periodic identification. A periodic identification would instead create closed timelike curves and would be an additional global choice.
For clarity, the original patch is embedded by the branches −π<U<π and 0<V<2π. The inequality u>v is equivalent on these branches to ∣U−V∣<π, so the original patch occupies
−2π​<X<2π​,X<T<π−X.
(44)
The first spacetime extension in part (a) occupies the larger band 0<T−X<2π, with ∣X∣<π/2. Its finite-v Killing horizon is the interior line T+X=π. The edges T−X=0,2π of that band are further regular Poincaré horizons; continuing through them produces the full conformal strip of two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime.
The original coordinate λ has a smooth continuation throughout the strip:
λ=cosX2mcos(U/2)sin(V/2)​=mcosXsinT−sinX​.​
(45)
Consequently the complete set of zero-level Killing horizons is
T=X+2πjorT=π−X+2πj,j∈Z.​
(46)
These are two alternating families of parallel null lines; different families meet only at the excluded conformal boundaries, never inside the spacetime. The continuation of the original Killing vector field is
K=(1−sinTsinX)∂T​+cosTcosX∂X​,K2=−λ2.
(47)
The field never vanishes in the interior, including on the Killing horizons.
To obtain the Penrose diagram, suppress the two-sphere and multiply by the conformal factor cos2X/m2. The resulting radial metric tensor is −dT2+dX2, so the diagram is an infinitely tall strip of width π, with null lines at 45 degrees and timelike edges X=±π/2. Generically,
X→−π/2: λ→+∞,X→+π/2: λ→−∞.​
(48)
At the special edge points where the two families of Killing horizons end, the displayed formula is a 0/0 limit and λ has no unique limiting value. Thus the infinity labels on the diagram refer to the open edge segments, not those vertices. Each interior point of the diagram represents a two-sphere of fixed radius m; neither edge is a centre where that two-sphere shrinks.
Finally, these timelike edges are conformal boundaries, not omitted regular points of a further spacetime extension. One way to establish maximality is geodesic completeness. The two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime factor embeds locally as Z⋅Z=−m2 in a flat space with metric signature (−,−,+). Along an affinely parametrized geodesic with constant ϵ=Z˙⋅Z˙, the ambient equation is
Z¨=m2ϵ​Z.
(49)
Its oscillatory, linear or hyperbolic solutions for negative, zero or positive ϵ exist for all real affine parameters and lift to the universal cover. The two-sphere is also geodesically complete, and geodesics of the product split into geodesics of its factors. Hence g​ is geodesically complete. A proper regular spacetime extension would furnish a geodesic reaching its boundary in finite affine parameter, contradicting that completeness. This proves that the displayed simply connected analytic spacetime is maximal.
