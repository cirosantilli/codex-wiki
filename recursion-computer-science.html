Recursion (computer science)

 Home Computer science Algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
Recursion solves a problem by invoking the same procedure on smaller instances, with base cases answered directly. For merge sort, an empty or one-entry array is already sorted, and every larger array is split into two shorter parts. The decreasing lengths justify termination; a self-call without a progress rule would not suffice.
A recursive procedure refers to itself, directly or through another procedure. A recursive call starts another invocation, with its own arguments and local variables; the caller can wait for its returned output. A base case supplies an answer without further recursive calls. To establish algorithm termination, also show that every recursive call progresses in a well-founded relation. “Smaller” means smaller in that chosen relation: argument values need not all decrease, and the arguments need not even be numbers.
Recursion is especially expressive when a data structure already decomposes into smaller structures. A binary tree has two child subtrees, so structural recursion can follow its shape. A short rule for each case can describe an entire traversal or computation. The distinction between a recursive procedure and the recursive process it generates helps connect that compact description to its resource use.[ref]
