Multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol ((pab​)=(pa​)(pb​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Quadratic residue Legendre symbol
OurBigBook.com Words: 255
For an odd prime p and any integers a,b, the Legendre symbol obeys
(pab​)=(pa​)(pb​).
(7)
In particular, if p∤a, then (a−1/p)=(a/p), where a−1 is a modular multiplicative inverse. Multiplication by a nonzero square also leaves the Legendre symbol unchanged: (ar2/p)=(a/p) for p∤r.[ref]
The subgroup and coset arguments under quadratic residue and quadratic nonresidue prove the sign rule without computing a large power: square times square is square, square times nonsquare is nonsquare, and nonsquare times nonsquare is square. If either factor is zero modulo p, both sides of the identity vanish. On Fp×​, the Legendre symbol is thus a group homomorphism to {1,−1} whose kernel of a group homomorphism is the quadratic residue subgroup. Viewed on all integers, it is a completely multiplicative function and a Dirichlet character modulo p.
For an original calculation, factor the numerator 30=2⋅3⋅5. Modulo seven, (30/7)=(2/7)(3/7)(5/7)=1⋅(−1)⋅(−1)=1, agreeing with 30≡2(mod7). Modulo eleven, the signs are instead −1,1,1, giving (30/11)=−1, agreeing with 30≡8(mod11). The same factorization can therefore certify different square behavior at different prime numbers.
Alternatively, Euler's criterion gives (ab)(p−1)/2≡a(p−1)/2b(p−1)/2 modulo p. For nonzero factors both sides correspond to signs in {1,−1}. Since p is odd, these signs are distinct modulo p, so the modular congruence proves the asserted equality of integers, not merely an equality of representatives.
