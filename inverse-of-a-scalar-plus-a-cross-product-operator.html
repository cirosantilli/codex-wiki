Inverse of a scalar plus a cross-product operator (αx+βn×x=b)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Vector Cross product
OurBigBook.com Words: 173 Articles: 1
For a unit vector n, real α=0, and real β, the displayed linear equation has the unique vector solution
x=α2+β2αb−βn×b+(β2/α)n(n⋅b)​.
(19)
To prove it, split vectors into components parallel and orthogonal to n. On the parallel line, the linear map is multiplication by α. On the perpendicular plane, the operator Nv=n×v obeys N2=−I, by the vector triple product. Therefore (αI−βN)(αI+βN)=(α2+β2)I on that plane. Combining its inverse with the parallel solution gives the formula. This avoids component calculations in equations with an axial cross product term.
