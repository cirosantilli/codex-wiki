Gaussian-damped martingale density construction (dQ/dP∝e−∣X∣2−h∗​⋅X)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Fundamental theorem of asset pricing
OurBigBook.com Words: 100
For a finite-dimensional discounted gain random vector X, absence of arbitrage means h⋅X≥0 almost surely only when h⋅X=0 almost surely. Let N={h:h⋅X=0 almost surely} and minimize F(h)=Ee−∣X∣2−h⋅X on the orthogonal complement N⊥. The Gaussian damping makes F finite and differentiable without physical integrability assumptions on X. Every nonzero direction in N⊥ has negative gains on an event of positive probability; compactness of directions proves coercivity. At a minimizer, differentiation gives E[Xe−∣X∣2−h∗​⋅X]=0. Normalizing the positive weight produces an equivalent martingale measure with integrable gains. This is a constructive one-period proof of the fundamental theorem of asset pricing even on an infinite probability space.
