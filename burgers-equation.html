Burgers' equation (ut​+uux​=νuxx​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 15
Burgers' equation is the one-dimensional partial differential equation
ut​+uux​=νuxx​,ν≥0.
(3)
The term uux​ supplies nonlinear transport in a transport equation: the transported value u is also its own characteristic speed when ν=0. Larger values catch up with smaller values ahead of them, steepening a decreasing profile. The diffusion term νuxx​ opposes this steepening. Positive ν gives the viscous Burgers equation; setting ν=0 gives the Inviscid Burgers equation.
This small equation makes three different behaviors accessible in one model: Burgers characteristic breaking from continuous decreasing data, an entropy shock for a downward step, and a rarefaction wave for an upward step. The Burgers Riemann problem with positive flux and the Viscous Burgers travelling shock below give exact examples. These are mathematical calculations with chosen data; the figures contain no experimental measurements.
Unless stated otherwise, take x∈R, t≥0, constant ν, no PDE source term, and an initial condition u(x,0)=u0​(x). There are no finite endpoints requiring boundary conditions. Where a profile has different limiting states at x=±∞, those states are specified explicitly. On a finite interval, boundary conditions become part of the problem: positive diffusion normally requires a condition at each endpoint, whereas the Inviscid Burgers equation requires appropriate inflow transport boundary conditions. With dimensional variables, u has units of velocity and ν of kinematic viscosity; all numerical examples below use dimensionless quantities.
