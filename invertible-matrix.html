Invertible matrix (rankA=n⟺detA=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 572 Articles: 1
An n-by-n square matrix A over a field is invertible when it has a two-sided matrix inverse. Equivalently, its linear map x↦Ax is a bijection: every output comes from exactly one input. For a square matrix, the following conditions are equivalent:
A is invertible⟺rankA=n⟺kerA={0}⟺detA=0⟺Ax=b has exactly one solution for every b.
(85)
Here matrix rank n means both full column rank and full row rank. The square hypothesis matters: a rectangular matrix can have full column rank without reaching every output, or full row rank without determining every input.[ref]
The equivalences explain why invertibility is a question about retaining information. A trivial kernel makes the linear map injective; the rank-nullity theorem then gives matrix rank n, so its columns form a basis and the linear map is also surjective. Assigning to each output its unique input gives a linear map: the preimage of αb+βc is αA−1b+βA−1c. The determinant is nonzero precisely when the columns are linearly independent. Thus algebraic solvability and independent directions describe the same phenomenon; over the real numbers, nonzero oriented volume supplies its geometric interpretation.
