Rotational symmetry group of a regular dodecahedron (Rot(D)≅A5​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Rotation matrix Rotational symmetry
OurBigBook.com Words: 179
The rotations in three dimensions preserving a dodecahedron form a group of order 60. There are 24 nonidentity rotations about the six axes through opposite face centres, 20 about the ten axes through opposite vertices, and 15 half-turns about the fifteen axes through opposite edge midpoints. Its nonidentity conjugacy classes have sizes 12,12,20,15: the centralizer of a rotation of order five or three consists of rotations about its oriented axis and has order five or three, respectively, while all edge half-turns are conjugate. A normal subgroup is a union of whole conjugacy classes containing the identity. No proper nontrivial sum of these class sizes plus one divides 60, proving that it is a simple group. The centralizers of the fifteen involutions are five distinct Klein four-groups, each containing three involutions. Conjugation permutes these five subgroups nontrivially. The kernel is a normal subgroup, so simplicity makes this nontrivial action injective. Its sign map to a group of order two must be trivial, since simplicity would otherwise force an impossible injection of sixty elements into two. Thus its image lies in the alternating group A5​, and equality of orders makes the image all of A5​.
