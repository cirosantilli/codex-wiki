Buoyancy (Fb​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
Buoyancy is the resultant upward force exerted by a resting fluid on an immersed body in a downward gravitational field. The fluid pushes inward, perpendicular to each part of the body's surface. Because hydrostatic pressure increases with depth, the upward push from below exceeds the downward push from above. Opposite horizontal forces cancel; buoyancy is the remaining vertical force. A sinking body still receives this upward support. [ref]
More precisely, pressure p on a small surface of area dA with outward unit normal vector n exerts
dF=−pndA.
(37)
The minus sign says the force points into the body. On a cube, the top force points down, the bottom force points up, and the side forces cancel in pairs because opposite sides have the same pressure at each depth. This is a surface traction; gravity on the body itself is a separate body force. [ref]
For a computed example, stipulate resting water with constant mass density ρf​=1000 kgm−3 and uniform gravitational acceleration g=9.81 ms−2. A fully immersed cube has side L=0.10 m, top depth ht​=0.20 m and bottom depth hb​=0.30 m. Each horizontal face has area A=L2=0.010 m2, and its volume is V=L3=0.001 m3. Its top and bottom gauge pressures are
pg,t​=ρf​ght​=1962 Pa,pg,b​=ρf​ghb​=2943 Pa.
(38)
The corresponding forces relative to the common surface pressure are pg,t​A=19.62 N downward and pg,b​A=29.43 N upward. Therefore the net buoyancy is
Fb​=(pg,b​−pg,t​)A=9.81 N.
(39)
Using absolute pressures adds the same psurface​A to both face forces, leaving their difference unchanged. Zero gauge pressure at the surface does not mean zero absolute pressure. Increasing both depths by the same amount also leaves buoyancy unchanged in this constant-mass density model.
Archimedes' principle turns this calculation into a rule for bodies of any shape. It explains floating equilibrium and lets us infer an immersed body's volume from the difference between its force from gravity and the force needed to support it. In particular, buoyancy depends on the displaced volume and the surrounding mass density, whereas the body's own mass density decides how its force from gravity compares with that support.
