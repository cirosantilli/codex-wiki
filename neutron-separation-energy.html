Neutron separation energy (Sn​(Z,N)=B(Z,N)−B(Z,N−1))

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Nuclear shell model Nuclear separation energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Writing positive nuclear binding energy as B(Z,N) and nuclear mass as M(Z,N),
Sn​(Z,N)=[M(Z,N−1)+mn​−M(Z,N)]c2=B(Z,N)−B(Z,N−1).
(21)
The two-neutron nuclear separation energy is S2n​(Z,N)=B(Z,N)−B(Z,N−2). In an independent-particle example, let the last occupied energy level have ϵ−​=−8u and the next empty level ϵ+​=−3u, for a chosen positive energy unit u. With an unchanged core and no residual interactions, removal from the filled level costs 8u, whereas removing the first particle added above it costs 3u: the drop 5u equals the one-particle gap. These are invented model inputs, not measured nuclear values. Nuclear pairing, rearrangement of the nuclear mean field, and correlations complicate that identification in real masses; a finite difference of nuclear binding energies is not automatically a bare one-particle gap.
