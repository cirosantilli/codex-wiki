Hopfield recall from a corrupted cue (E(d)=−((M−2d)2−M)/(2M))

OurBigBook.com Words: 180
Store only ξ=(1,−1,1,−1,1) with M=5, zero thresholds, and Hebbian memory weights for a Hopfield network. If d bits differ from ξ, their Hamming distance is d and ∑i​ξi​xi​=M−2d. Squaring this sum and subtracting the M diagonal terms yields the displayed energy. Thus d=0,1,2,3,4,5 gives E=−2,−2/5,2/5,2/5,−2/5,−2.
For the cue x=(−1,1,1,−1,1), the first unit has input h1​=2/5 and flips to 1. The second then has h2​=−4/5 and flips to −1, completing recall. The energy drops 2/5→−2/5→−2. Every cue with at most two wrong bits is corrected under a fair asynchronous schedule: a wrong bit sees the correct majority among the other four, while a correct bit either sees agreement or retains its value at a tie. The opposite pattern −ξ is also stored implicitly, so cues with at least three errors retrieve it. This is a limitation of this symmetric one-pattern construction.
