Compton scattering experiment of 1923

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Compton wavelength Compton scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 157
In Arthur Holly Compton's A Quantum Theory of the Scattering of X-rays by Light Elements (1923), pp. 494–495 and Figure 4 compare X-rays from a molybdenum target with rays scattered by graphite at right angles. Slits select the rays; a calcite crystal analyzes their wavelengths by Bragg's law. The plotted readout is scattered wave intensity against the crystal's glancing angle, distinct from the fixed 90∘ photon deflection. The primary-beam comparison uses the same slits, crystal, and tube potential. First-order diffraction lines on both sides establish angular zero. The displaced spectral line supports a longer outgoing wavelength and hence photon energy transfer. The two curves use different vertical scales, so their heights cannot directly compare absolute wave intensities. The paper postpones a fuller apparatus description; it also states on p. 496 that recoil electrons from ordinary X-rays had not been observed. Thus this spectral test infers recoil through the conservation model rather than directly tracking an electron.[ref]
