Quadric surface (xTAx+bTx+c=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 536 Articles: 7
A quadric surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space is the zero set of a real polynomial of degree two. Its quadratic part can be written with a symmetric matrix A, since the antisymmetric matrix part contributes zero to xTAx. An orthogonal diagonalization and a translation expose its geometry: examples include an ellipsoid, an elliptic cone, and a hyperboloid. This affine geometric description allows singular surfaces such as a cone's vertex.
