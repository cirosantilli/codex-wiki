Zero vector (0)

Words: 42
Vector space OurBigBook.com
The zero vector of a vector space is its additive identity: v+0=0+v=v for every vector v. It is unique, because if 0′ is another such identity then 0=0+0′=0′. Distributivity gives α0=α(0+0)=α0+α0, so α0=0 after cancellation. In a coordinate vector space it has every coordinate zero.

Quaternionic structure on a complex vector space (J2=−1)

Words: 67
Antilinear map OurBigBook.com
An antilinear endomorphism J with square −1 equips a complex vector space with a quaternionic structure: complex multiplication supplies the quaternionic generator i and J supplies j, satisfying Ji=−iJ. Such a space has even complex dimension. There is no invariant complex line, because Jv=cv would imply −v=∣c∣2v. In dimension four the invariant complex two-planes are quaternionic lines, forming HP1≅S4. This is the Euclidean reality structure on twistor space used in twistor theory.

Majorization (x≺y)

Words: 203 Articles: 2
Real vector space OurBigBook.com
For real vectors of equal length and equal sum, write x≺y when ∑j=1k​xj↓​≤∑j=1k​yj↓​ for every proper initial segment; the downward arrow means decreasing rearrangement. Majorization compares how unevenly a fixed total is distributed. The uniform probability vector is majorized by every probability vector, while (1,0,…,0) majorizes every probability vector. The order controls exact pure-state entanglement conversion in Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem.

Majorization of summable sequences (x≺y)

Words: 142 Articles: 1
Majorization OurBigBook.com
For decreasing positive sequences in ℓ1, write x≺y if every initial partial sum of x is at most the corresponding partial sum of y and the finite total sums agree. The finiteness requirement matters: equality of two divergent totals interpreted as infinity is too weak to guarantee a doubly stochastic realization of summable-sequence majorization.
Doubly stochastic realization of summable-sequence majorization (xi​=∑j​pij​yj​)
Words: 95
Majorization of summable sequences OurBigBook.com
Decreasing strictly positive summable sequences with equal totals and x≺y admit an infinite doubly stochastic matrix realizing the displayed relation. Choose k with yk​≥x1​>yk+1​ and average the pair to obtain x1​ and yk​+yk+1​−x1​. The remaining decreasing sequence majorizes the remaining target sequence: short initial sums contain values at least x1​, and longer sums are the old sums minus x1​. Repeat on the tail, freezing one normalized finite-support row at each stage. Limit column sums are at most one; Tonelli's theorem, equal finite totals and strict positivity of every yj​ force every column sum to equal one.

Tangent-line finite-field Kakeya construction (K={(x,y):x2−y is a square}∪{x=0})

Words: 62
Finite-field Kakeya set OurBigBook.com
For odd characteristic, the union of the lines y=mx−m2/4 contains a line of every finite slope. For fixed x, completing the square shows that it comprises the (p+1)/2 values of y for which x2−y is a square, including zero. Adding the vertical line x=0 completes the directions and gives cardinality (p2+2p−1)/2=(1/2+o(1))p2. This is a finite-field Kakeya set whose density tends to one half.

Finite-field Kakeya polynomial bound (∣A∣≥(nq+n−1​)≥qn/n!)

Words: 75
Finite-field Kakeya set OurBigBook.com
For a finite-field Kakeya set A⊆Fqn​, a smaller cardinality would give a nonzero polynomial of total degree of a polynomial at most q−1 vanishing on A, by the dimension of a bounded-total-degree polynomial space. Its restriction to each complete affine line in a vector space has q roots of a polynomial and degree less than q, hence vanishes identically. The top homogeneous polynomial part then vanishes in every direction. A polynomial of degree less than q in each variable cannot vanish everywhere on Fqn​ unless it is zero, giving a contradiction. For the prime field, this is the polynomial nonvanishing below the field size.
