Power (P=dW/dt)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Work
OurBigBook.com Words: 255 Articles: 2
Power describes how quickly energy is transferred. For mechanical work, the instantaneous power supplied by a force is
P(t)=dtdW​=F(t)⋅v(t),W=∫ti​tf​​P(t)dt.
(107)
The SI unit is W=J/s=Nm/s, distinct from the velocity unit m/s. The italic symbol W for work and the upright unit W for power mean different things. Two processes can transfer the same energy while requiring different powers: doing 12J of work in 2s requires an average 6W, whereas doing it in 4s requires an average 3W. [ref]
