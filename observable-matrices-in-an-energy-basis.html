Observable matrices in an energy basis (Amn​=⟨m∣A∣n⟩)

Choose normalized energy eigenstates ∣n⟩ of a time-independent Hamiltonian operator, with H∣n⟩=En​∣n⟩, and assume first that they form a complete orthonormal basis. For a pure state and an observable A,
∣ψ⟩=∑n​cn​∣n⟩,Amn​=⟨m∣A∣n⟩,(Aψ)m​=∑n​Amn​cn​,⟨A⟩=∑m,n​cm∗​Amn​cn​.
(4)
The column index n labels the input basis vector, and row m labels the output component. Self-adjointness gives Anm​=Amn∗​, so a finite example is a Hermitian matrix. A matrix element can be negative or complex; by itself it is not generally a probability. The Born rule assigns measurement probabilities using the eigenstates of A, which need not be energy eigenstates. For an unbounded observable, these expressions require a suitable operator domain; a system with continuous spectrum also needs the corresponding spectral integrals.
