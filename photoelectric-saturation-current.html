Photoelectric saturation current (Isat​=eηP/(hν))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Photon Photon absorption Photoelectric effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 197
The photoelectric saturation current is the photocurrent reached when increasing the collecting electric potential difference no longer increases the fraction of available emitted electrons collected. It is saturation with collecting voltage at fixed illumination. It does not mean that increasing incident light intensity cannot increase emission.
If P is incident radiant flux on the emitting area and η is the external quantum efficiency for producing escaping electrons, then the incident photon arrival rate is P/(hν) and complete collection gives Isat​=eηP/(hν). For the example's ν=8.00×1014Hz, choose η=0.10 and P=1.00μW. The calculated photon arrival rate is 1.886488×1012s−1 and Isat​=30.224866nA. Doubling P doubles this current to 60.449731nA while leaving Vs​ fixed. At fixed illuminated area this doubles the irradiance. Changing frequency at fixed P changes the arrival rate as well as the photon energy; η can also change, so there is no general rule that a higher frequency gives a larger photocurrent.
A missing plateau can indicate incomplete collection or an electric field altered by the emitted electric charge. Heating, surface changes and additional emission channels can invalidate a constant η. Even a small amount of unwanted higher-frequency radiation can move an apparent endpoint. A stopping potential inferred from zero net current additionally needs background subtraction and a check for emission from the collector itself.
