Minimum roof length at fixed wall height and cross-sectional area (2tan2θ=w/h,w4=16A(A−wh))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Lagrange multiplier
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
For A,h>0, a rectangular cross section of width w and height h, capped by a symmetric triangular roof at angle 0<θ<π/2, has area A=wh+w2tanθ/4. Its total sloping roof length is wsecθ. A cost 2αh+βwsecθ with β>0 has the same minimizing shape as this roof length, since the fixed wall term is constant.
The Lagrange multiplier equations for wsecθ under the area constraint give w(1−tan2θ)=4htanθ. This is the first displayed condition; it also implies θ<π/4. Eliminating tanθ=4(A−wh)/w2 gives the second condition.
For a global check, the squared roof length is Q(w)=w2+16(A−wh)2/w2 for 0<w<A/h. Its derivative has the sign of w4+16Ahw−16A2, a strictly increasing expression with one root in that interval. Thus this root is the unique minimum. The shape is independent of the positive roof-cost coefficient and the constant wall cost. If β=0, every feasible shape has the same cost and these conditions are no longer necessary.
