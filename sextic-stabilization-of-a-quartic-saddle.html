Sextic stabilization of a quartic saddle (Hκ​(x,y)=x6+y6−κx2y2,κ>0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Critical point Saddle point of a scalar function
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
For every κ>0, the polynomial Hκ​ has exactly five stationary points: the origin and the four points with x2=y2=κ/3. Its gradient factors as
∇Hκ​=2(x(3x4−κy2), y(3y4−κx2)).
(59)
If a coordinate vanishes at a stationary point, both do. Otherwise put u=x2, v=y2 to obtain u2=(κ/3)v and v2=(κ/3)u. Dividing gives u=v and then u=v=κ/3.
At the origin, the Hessian matrix vanishes. Axis restrictions give Hκ​(t,0)=t6>0, while diagonal restrictions give Hκ​(t,±t)=2t6−κt4<0 for small nonzero t. Thus the origin is a degenerate saddle point of a scalar function. Its leading quartic term is nonpositive, showing why the sign of the first nonzero homogeneous term alone need not settle the problem when that term vanishes along some directions.
At each nonzero stationary point, the Hessian matrix has diagonal entries 8κ2/3 and off-diagonal entries ±4κ2/3. Its positive eigenvalues 4κ2/3 and 4κ2 prove strict local minima. They are also global minima of value −κ3/27: the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives x6+y6≥2∣xy∣3, and the minimum of 2u3−κu2 for u≥0 is −κ3/27 at u=κ/3. Equality requires ∣x∣=∣y∣.
