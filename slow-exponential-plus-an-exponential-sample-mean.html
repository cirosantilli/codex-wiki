Let S be a fixed sum of independent exponential random variables with smallest rate λ>0, and let ZN​ be the sum of N−k independent rate-r exponential random variables, independent of S, with fixed k and r>λ. At large-deviation speed N, S/N has rate function λy for y≥0; the sample mean has rate function Kr​(z)=rz−1−log(rz) for z>0. The product large-deviation principle and contraction principle for large deviations give
I(x)=inf0<z≤x​{λ(x−z)+rz−1−log(rz)}=⎩⎨⎧​rx−1−log(rx),λx+log((r−λ)/r),∞,​0<x≤(r−λ)−1,x≥(r−λ)−1,x≤0.​

Indeed the derivative of the quantity minimized with respect to z is r−λ−1/z. Its minimizer is min(x,(r−λ)−1). Beyond that transition, a single slow exponential random variable carries the extra excursion; the rate function becomes affine.
