Positive operator-valued measure (POVM)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum measurement Generalized measurement postulate
OurBigBook.com Words: 542 Articles: 7
A positive operator-valued measure is a family of positive operators Ei​ satisfying ∑i​Ei​=I. Each Ei​ is called an effect and determines a probability pi​=Tr(Ei​ρ) for input density operator ρ. The effects need not be orthogonal projections or commute. This permits measurements with more outcomes than the dimension of the Hilbert space, such as the trine qubit POVM. Coupling to a quantum ancilla and measuring the larger system supplies a physical route to generalized measurements. [ref].
For an elementary unsharp Z example on a qubit, take
E±​=21​(I±ηZ),0≤η≤1.
(11)
On the eigenstate ∣0⟩, p±​=(1±η)/2; at η=1/2 they are 3/4 and 1/4. At η=0 the outcome is a fair coin independent of the input, and at η=1 these effects specify a sharp Z projective measurement. Choosing K±​=E±​​ specifies one quantum instrument, but U±​E±​​ for outcome-dependent unitary operators has the same effects and different state updates. Thus POVM does not determine the post-measurement state.
