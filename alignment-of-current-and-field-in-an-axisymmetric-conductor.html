Alignment of current and field in an axisymmetric conductor (σ=a(I−nnT)+dnnT+h[n×])

 ... Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Tensor Cartesian tensor Cartesian second-rank tensor Oriented-axis invariant Cartesian tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
For a real conductivity tensor invariant under every rotation about a unit vector n, the electric current density is J=aE⊥​+dE∥​+hn×E⊥​. Its collinearity with a nonzero real electric field E has three cases. Axial fields are always mapped to an axial current. Purely transverse fields give a collinear current exactly when h=0, equivalently when this tensor is symmetric. Fields with nonzero axial and transverse parts require both h=0 and a=d, making the tensor isotropic. The zero vector is included as a scalar multiple; requiring a nonzero current also requires the relevant scalar conductivity to be nonzero.
To prove necessity, take the dot product of J=λE with n×E⊥​. If E⊥​=0 this gives h=0; the transverse and axial equations then respectively give λ=a and, when E∥​=0, λ=d. Reversing these steps proves sufficiency. Rotations about the axis alone permit h=0; symmetry under a transverse half-turn would eliminate this antisymmetric term.
