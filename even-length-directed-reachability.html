Even-length directed reachability

 ... Complexity class Space complexity Logarithmic space NL (complexity) NL-complete ST-connectivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
Decide whether a finite directed graph has an even-length directed walk from a specified source s to a specified target t. Vertices may repeat and a zero-length walk is allowed. The NL-completeness proof uses the graph of pairs (v,b) with b∈{0,1} tracking integer parity: every original edge flips b, and acceptance means reaching (t,0) from (s,0). At most 2∣V∣−1 steps are needed in the parity graph. For hardness, subdivide every edge of an ordinary reachability instance into two directed edges. Both constructions are computable by logspace transducers. Requiring a simple path in a graph in the original graph changes the question; deleting an odd closed portion need not preserve integer parity.
