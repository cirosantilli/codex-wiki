Semisimple Banach algebra (J(A)={0})

Words: 131 Articles: 1
Banach algebra OurBigBook.com
A Banach algebra is semisimple when its Jacobson radical is zero. This does not assert that every quasinilpotent element is zero. For example, the algebra of bounded operators on a Banach space of dimension greater than one is semisimple, but contains nonzero square-zero rank-one operators. This notion also does not require the finite-dimensional direct-sum decomposition associated with a semisimple algebra.

Automatic continuity onto a semisimple Banach algebra (T:A↠B⟹T is continuous)

An algebra homomorphism onto a semisimple Banach algebra is continuous. To see the spectral mechanism, suppose an​→0 and T(an​)→b. For any c∈B, choose u,v∈A with T(u)=b and T(v)=c, and set d=cb, dn​=T(van​). Apply the spectral-radius three-circle inequality to T((1−z)van​+zvu). On the outer circle its norm tends uniformly to ∥d∥; on the circle of radius 1/R its spectral radius is bounded by (1+1/R)∥van​∥+∥vu∥/R. Hence r(d)2≤∥d∥∥vu∥/R for every R>1, so r(cb)=0. The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical gives b∈J(B)=0, and the closed graph theorem finishes the proof.

Hermitian Banach algebra

Words: 127 Articles: 1
Banach algebra OurBigBook.com
A Hermitian Banach algebra is a Banach algebra with an algebra involution for which every Hermitian element of a star algebra has real spectrum of an element. A closed unital star-subalgebra has spectral permanence for Hermitian Banach algebras. The assumption is weaker than being a C-star algebra and does not require the C-star norm identity.

Spectral permanence for Hermitian Banach algebras (σB​(b)=σA​(b))

Words: 97

Let B be a closed star-subalgebra of a unital Hermitian Banach algebra A, containing the same identity. For Hermitian h∈B, the complement of σA​(h)⊆R is connected. The resolvent belongs to B at large modulus by the Neumann series; its membership in B is both open and closed in that complement, so σB​(h)=σA​(h). If b is invertible in A, both bb∗ and b∗b are invertible there and Hermitian algebra elements, hence invertible in B. Their inverses supply respectively a right and a left inverse for b in B, proving invertibility there. Applying this to b−λ1 proves the spectrum equality.

Character space of an Abelian L1 group algebra (Δ(L1(G))≅G)

Words: 69

For a locally compact Hausdorff Abelian group, each continuous unitary character defines an algebra character by f↦∫f(x)χ(x)dx. Conversely, if Φ is a nonzero algebra character and Φ(g)=0, the ratio Φ(Tx​g)/Φ(g) is independent of g, continuous and multiplicative. Translation isometries bound all its integer powers, so its modulus is one. The convolution formula f∗g=∫f(x)Tx​gdx recovers Φ(f)=∫fχ. The Gelfand topology corresponds to the compact-open topology on the Pontryagin dual group; conjugating the characters gives the alternative Fourier sign convention.

Closed subalgebra of a Banach algebra

Words: 48
Banach algebra OurBigBook.com
A norm-closed subalgebra of a Banach algebra is itself a Banach algebra with the inherited norm: completeness follows from closedness, and the submultiplicative norm bound restricts to it. A unital inclusion uses the same identity as the ambient algebra. Inverse membership may still differ between the two algebras; spectrum in a closed unital subalgebra describes that difference.

Continuously differentiable functions on a compact interval form a Banach algebra (C1[0,1],∥f∥=∥f∥∞​+∥f′∥∞​)

With complex scalars and pointwise multiplication, the product rule gives a submultiplicative norm. Character space of C1 on a compact interval (ΦC1[0,1]​={δt​:t∈[0,1]})

Words: 62

The coordinate function has spectrum [0,1]: outside the interval its difference from a scalar has a continuously differentiable reciprocal, and on the interval it has a zero. Every character of an algebra therefore takes the coordinate to some t∈[0,1]. Polynomial density in the norm containing both function and derivative, together with automatic continuity of characters, forces evaluation at t on every function. The maximal ideals are exactly {f:f(t)=0}.

Uniform algebra (A⊆C(K))

Words: 468 Articles: 4
Banach algebra OurBigBook.com
A uniform algebra on a compact Hausdorff space K is a closed complex subalgebra of C(K), with the supremum norm, containing the constants and separating points. Evaluation embeds K continuously and injectively in the character space of an algebra. The embedding is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset because its domain is compact and its target is Hausdorff. Extra characters need not be point evaluations on the specified K.

Boundary-analytic disk algebra with doubled character space (ΦC​≅D∪∂D​D≅S2)

Words: 168
Uniform algebra OurBigBook.com
Let C consist of continuous functions on the closed unit disk whose boundary values extend to a member of the disk algebra. The extension is unique by the maximum modulus principle; the extension map T:C→A(D) is a surjective unital algebra homomorphism of norm at most one. Its kernel I is the ideal of functions vanishing on the boundary. A character vanishing on I factors through T, so it is f↦T(f)(λ) for some closed-disk point λ. For a character χ not vanishing on I, choose h∈I with χ(h)=0. The rule F↦χ(hF)/χ(h) extends χ to a character of all continuous functions on the disk: multiplicativity follows from χ(hF)χ(hG)=χ(h)χ(hFG). It is therefore evaluation at an interior point, since its value at h is nonzero. These two closed-disk families agree exactly on their boundaries. Their glued compact space maps continuously and bijectively to the Hausdorff character space of an algebra, giving the stated homeomorphism. The algebra is closed because uniform convergence of boundary data gives uniform convergence of their analytic extensions.

Disk algebra (A(D))

Words: 74
Uniform algebra OurBigBook.com
The disk algebra consists of functions continuous on the closed unit disk and holomorphic in its interior, with the supremum norm. Polynomials are dense: dilations fr​(z)=f(rz) converge uniformly to f as r↑1, and each fr​ is uniformly approximated on the closed disk by its Taylor polynomials. A character of an algebra is therefore determined by its value λ at the coordinate function; boundedness gives ∣λ∣≤1, and polynomial approximation yields φ(f)=f(λ). Consequently the disk algebra is a natural uniform algebra on the closed disk.

Words: 117

A uniform algebra is natural exactly when functions with no common zero generate the unit ideal. If a generated ideal is proper, it is contained in a maximal ideal. That maximal ideal is closed: the closure of a proper ideal stays proper, since an element within distance less than one of the identity is invertible by the Neumann series. The Gelfand-Mazur theorem identifies the maximal-ideal quotient with the complex numbers, yielding a character of an algebra. Naturality would make that character a common-zero evaluation, a contradiction. Conversely, the asserted unit-ideal property makes the zero sets of every finite collection in a character's kernel intersect. Compactness gives a point common to all these zero sets; applying this to f−φ(f)1 proves φ(f)=f(x). For C(K), one can directly take gj​=fj​​/∑k​∣fk​∣2.

Identity component of Banach-algebra invertibles (G0​(A)=⟨ea:a∈A⟩)

Words: 71

Finite products of Banach algebra exponentials form a subgroup: inverses are e−a and conjugation sends ea to egag−1. Each product is joined to the identity by t↦∏etai​. The logarithm of an element near the identity puts a norm neighborhood of 1 in this subgroup, so its cosets are open and its complement is open.
