Shot noise departures from Poisson statistics (FT​=Var(NT​)/E[NT​])

The Fano factor FT​ compares count variance with its mean over a specified gate. For a stationary unidirectional stream with fixed charge q, finite count second moments, and a generalized autocovariance with finite integral so that the long-gate limit exists,
F∞​=limT→∞​E[NT​]Var(NT​)​,SI(1)​(0+)=2qIF∞​.
(56)
The zero-frequency relation concerns the continuous fluctuation spectrum, excluding the mean spectral line. It follows by writing Var(QT​) as the double integral of the current autocovariance; for long T, its leading term is TSI(2)​(0). A finite-gate FT​, a frequency-dependent ratio SI(1)​(f)/(2qI), and a factor inferred from one filtered variance are not interchangeable. The last is weighted by the instrument's ∣K(f)∣2.
Two original counting counterexamples show why electric charge discreteness is insufficient. In n independent time slots, transfer one charge with probability p in each slot: the binomial distribution gives mean np, variance np(1−p) and Fano factor 1−p. A random common origin for the slots removes a preferred time origin; the long-gate factor remains 1−p. Alternatively, if independent Poisson process bursts each transfer exactly two charges, then NT​=2MT​ gives Var(NT​)=2E[NT​] and factor 2. Both count individual charges; only the first model allows at most one per slot, and the second groups them in pairs.
Consequently 2qI is a reference for independent unidirectional arrivals, not a universal noise law for all electrical conductors. Correlations, charge multiplication, and bidirectional transfer require a more specific model. For example, independent opposite streams with positive current magnitudes I+​ and I−​ have net mean I=I+​−I−​ but one-sided fluctuation spectrum 2q(I+​+I−​). Using 2q∣I∣ would miss their fluctuations when the net current is small. At thermal equilibrium, the full transport model must also respect thermal electrical noise; adding unrelated phenomenological formulas can double count the same underlying transfers.
