Hahn decomposition (X=P⊔N)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Measure Signed measure
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
A Hahn decomposition of a signed measure partitions its space into measurable sets P,N such that every measurable subset of P has nonnegative measure and every measurable subset of N has nonpositive measure. For finite total variation measure, set μ+=(∣μ∣+μ)/2 and choose a μ+-null set N maximizing ∣μ∣(N): a countable union of approximating null sets attains the supremum. If some subset of its complement has negative signed mass, successively remove disjoint subsets with positive mass at least half the supremum still available. Their masses tend to zero because their sum is bounded by total variation. The remainder has no positive-measure subset, has negative mass, and is μ+-null, contradicting maximality after adjoining it to N. On P one has ∣μ∣=μ and on N one has ∣μ∣=−μ. Consequently dμ+/d∣μ∣=1P​, an explicit Radon-Nikodym derivative.
