For every real parameter a,
F(a)=∫0∞​1+x2aln(a2+x2)​dx=πaln(1+∣a∣),​
(83)
where F(0)=0. For a>0, differentiation under the integral sign and a partial fraction decomposition give
F′(a)−F(a)/a=2a2∫0∞​(x2+a2)(1+x2)dx​=1+aπa​.
(84)
Solving this first-order linear differential equation yields F(a)=a[πln(1+a)+C]. To fix the constant, dominated convergence gives lima→0+​F(a)/a=∫0∞​ln(x2)/(1+x2)dx=0; the last absolutely convergent integral changes sign under x=1/t. Thus C=0. Oddness in a supplies the result for negative parameters. The initial value F(0)=0 alone does not determine C, since every solution a[πln(1+a)+C] tends to zero as a→0+.
