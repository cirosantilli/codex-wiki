Delayed-pumping model of Sisyphus friction (F=−U0​k2vγp​/[2(γp2​+k2v2)])

Consider an original reduced rate equation model using the same potentials. Write s(z)=cos(2kz) and stipulate positive optical pumping rates R+→−​=γp​(1−s), R−→+​=γp​(1+s), with γp​>0. They pump towards + at z=0 and towards − at z=λ/4, consistently with the diagram. These rates retain the relevant symmetry and delay, while omitting excited-state dynamics, quantum coherence and recoil diffusion.
For populations p+​+p−​=1, put q=p+​−p−​. The rate equations conserve population and give q˙​=2γp​(s−q). The stationary atom has q=s; a moving atom cannot follow it instantaneously. On a prescribed trajectory z=vt, the periodic solution is
q(z)=γp2​+k2v2γp2​cos(2kz)+γp​kvsin(2kz)​.
(57)
Substitution into vq′=2γp​(s−q) verifies both coefficients. The mean mechanical force is F=−p+​U+′​−p−​U−′​=−U0​kqsin(2kz). Averaging over a spatial period, with mean squared sine 1/2 and mean sine times cosine zero, gives
F=−2(γp2​+k2v2)U0​k2vγp​​,F≃−2γp​U0​k2​v(∣kv∣≪γp​).
(58)
Thus the force opposes either sign of velocity and vanishes at rest. A small pumping rate makes the small-velocity friction slope large while narrowing its useful velocity range. Holding v fixed while sending γp​ to zero makes the force vanish; the apparently divergent zero-velocity slope is not unlimited cooling. Because this model has no momentum diffusion, it predicts no equilibrium temperature. It demonstrates the lag mechanism rather than replacing a multilevel quantum calculation.
