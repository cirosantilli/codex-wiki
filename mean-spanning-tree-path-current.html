Mean spanning-tree path current (J(u,v)=E[1{u→v∈γT​}​−1{v→u∈γT​}​])

In a finite unweighted connected graph, take distinct graph vertices a=z and orient the unique graph path from a to z in a uniform spanning tree and assign signed unit current to its edges. Its mean over trees is the electrical unit flow from a to z. The Kirchhoff node law follows by averaging graph path divergences. For the graph cycle law, let Faz​ be the two-component spanning forests separating the terminals and AF​ the component containing a. Deleting a tree-path edge and conversely adjoining an edge across a forest cut are inverse operations. Hence the mean signed current on an edge u→v is
J(u,v)=∣T(G)∣1​∑F∈Faz​​(1AF​​(u)−1AF​​(v)).
(5)
This is the gradient of h(u)=∣T(G)∣−1∑F​1AF​​(u), proving the graph cycle law. In particular Reff​(a,z)=∣Faz​∣/∣T(G)∣. For an oriented existing edge e=(a,z), that edge is used by the tree graph path exactly when it belongs to the tree, and then has positive orientation. Thus its mean current is P(e∈T), giving the edge-inclusion formula for a uniform spanning tree by Ohm's law.
