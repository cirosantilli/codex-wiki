Manometer height-to-pressure calibration (d(Δp)/d(Δh)=ρg)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Manometer U-tube differential manometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 298
The ideal measurement calibration is a straight line through the equal-pressure zero. For the stipulated water mass density and gravitational acceleration above, its slope is 9.81Pa per vertical millimetre. A lower liquid mass density gives a larger length change for the same pressure, improving the height-reading contribution to measurement resolution but requiring more vertical space for a given pressure range. The pressure scale depends on the actual mass density, which depends on temperature; 1000kgm−3 here is a chosen model value, not an exact property of all water samples.
Suppose each liquid meniscus height is rounded to the nearest scale division q=1mm. Each height measurement error lies within q/2, so the error in their difference lies within q. The resulting pressure error is bounded by ρgq=9.81Pa. To quantify a standard uncertainty, make the additional model assumption that the two rounding errors are independent random variables, each having a continuous uniform distribution on [−q/2,q/2]. Each then has variance q2/12; adding the two variances gives
u(Δh)=6​q​,u(Δp)=6​ρgq​=4.0049Pa.
(7)
Here u denotes standard uncertainty. The 9.81Pa worst-case bound and 4.0049Pa standard uncertainty answer different questions. At 588.6Pa they are respectively 1.67% and 0.680% of the reading. Shared reading bias or a different reading method can violate the assumed independence.
If the mass density, gravitational acceleration and height difference also have small independent standard uncertainties, a first-order estimate follows from the partial derivatives of Δp=ρgΔh:
u2(Δp)≃(gΔh)2u2(ρ)+(ρΔh)2u2(g)+(ρg)2u2(Δh).
(8)
This displays why a finer scale does not remove uncertainty in mass density or gravitational acceleration. A pressure transducer compared against the column needs its own differential-pressure calibration to establish an electrical response.
