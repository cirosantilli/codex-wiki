Photon-recoil lag correlation (ℓ=tγ​−te​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation X-ray Bothe-Geiger coincidence experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 426
A photon-recoil lag correlation asks how often a photon record occurs at a specified time displacement from an electron record. Let re​,rγ​ be observed separate-channel rates, S the rate of detected genuine pairs before a timing cut, and f(ℓ) their normalized timing probability density function. For an original dilute, stationary model with observation time T, neglecting end effects and detector dead time, the expected pair density is
dℓdE[N]​=T[re​rγ​+Sf(ℓ)],∫−∞∞​f(ℓ)dℓ=1.
(8)
This counts all pairs of records, rather than at most one detector trigger per electron. It uses independent unrelated detections for the flat term and an added same-event contribution. A normal distribution for timing spread is a chosen approximation, not a claim about the historical instrument. Intrinsic emission delay, counter response and readout uncertainty can all affect the observed probability density function.
Define the accepted lag interval by ∣ℓ∣<w/2: w is its full width. Integrating the flat baseline gives the existing accidental coincidence expectation Tre​rγ​w. The additional area is TS∫−w/2w/2​f(ℓ)dℓ. Moving a window of the same full width away from the peak tests the baseline under the model's stationarity assumption. It does not remove correlated instrumental backgrounds or changing rates automatically.
For the lag model, choose T=1000s, re​=2s−1, rγ​=0.5s−1, S=0.1s−1 and timing standard deviation σ=1ms. A full width w=2ms gives 2 expected accidental coincidences and 100erf(1/2​)≃68.27 accepted genuine pairs, using the error function. The total is approximately 70.27, whereas the displaced interval from 4 to 6ms has approximately 2.003 expected pairs. A finite sample fluctuates around these expectations; these values are not measured counts or a significance calculation.
The right panel isolates the information lost by retaining singles totals alone. In a separate idealized model, stipulate M=106 independent 1ms bins, with Bernoulli distribution indicators X,Y for electron and photon detection in a bin. Set pe​=Pr(X=1)=0.002 and pγ​=Pr(Y=1)=0.0005. Independence predicts Pr(X=1,Y=1)=pe​pγ​=10−6, hence one expected double detection. Alternatively choose
Pr(X=1,Y=1)=0.000101,Pr(X=1,Y=0)=0.001899,Pr(X=0,Y=1)=0.000399,Pr(X=0,Y=0)=0.997601.
(9)
These nonnegative probabilities sum to one and preserve both marginal probabilities, yet predict 101 double detections in expectation. Their covariance is 0.000101−pe​pγ​=0.000100. This is an exact joint probability distribution illustrating pairing; it does not model the timing spread in the left panel or reconstruct either historical hypothesis quantitatively. Its lesson is why two singles totals cannot decide whether the observed events share a cause.
