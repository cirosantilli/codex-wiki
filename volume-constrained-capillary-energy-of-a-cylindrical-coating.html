Volume-constrained capillary energy of a cylindrical coating (E=21​∫(hz2​−h2)dz)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Lubrication theory Capillary instability of a thin film on a rigid cylinder
OurBigBook.com Words: 95
Expand the area of an axisymmetric cylindrical free surface to second order in film thickness and eliminate the first-order area term using conservation of the exact liquid volume. Up to positive factors and constants this gives the displayed surface energy. Its variation is δE/δh=−h−hzz​. The thin-film equation is the conserved gradient flow ht​=∂z​[h3∂z​(δE/δh)]. For periodic boundaries, or boundaries with vanishing energy flux,
dtdE​=−∫h3(hz​+hzzz​)2dz≤0.
(23)
The decrease in capillary surface energy supplies viscous dissipation. On an infinite coating with unequal far films, use a local energy balance including boundary fluxes rather than an unsubtracted infinite energy integral.
