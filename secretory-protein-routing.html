Secretory protein routing

 Home Biology Biological cell Organelle Endoplasmic reticulum
OurBigBook.com Words: 507 Articles: 1
Secretory protein routing connects entry into the endoplasmic reticulum, protein folding and sorting, transfer to the Golgi apparatus, and delivery onward. In the conventional soluble-cargo route, transport vesicles carry lumenal contents between compartments; exocytosis releases cargo outside the cell membrane. Resident endoplasmic reticulum proteins can be retained or retrieved, and defective cargo can be held back. Many cytosol proteins never enter this route.[ref]
The useful geometric rule is preservation of membrane sides: during budding and fusion, a lumen-facing region of a membrane protein stays lumen-facing, then faces the exterior after exocytosis; its cytosol-facing region stays cytosolic.[ref]
Work through the schematic using two distinguishable labels A and B on a stipulated membrane protein. Start with A facing the ER lumen and B facing the cytosol. At each budding or fusion step, carry the surrounding membrane together with the labels; do not turn the protein over. The resulting pair is A facing the Golgi apparatus interior and B facing the cytosol, then A facing a transport vesicle interior and B facing the cytosol, and finally A facing the exterior and B facing the cytosol. This is a stepwise argument about the depicted operations, not a claim that the compartments have identical chemical contents. Likewise, once soluble cargo is inside the ER lumen, this schematic reaches the exterior with zero further crossings through a lipid bilayer: enclosed spaces join when membranes fuse. Delivery therefore need not mean repeatedly pushing a folded protein through a membrane.
