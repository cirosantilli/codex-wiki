Taphonomy

Words: 570 Articles: 26
Paleontology OurBigBook.com
Study of processes between an organism's death and observation of its remains, including transport, decay, burial and preservation. These processes bias the fossil and archaeological record.

Fossilization

Words: 20
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Processes through which biological remains or traces enter the fossil record. Complete replacement by new minerals is only one possible pathway.

Exceptional preservation

Words: 23
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Preservation retaining anatomical information normally removed by decay or burial alteration. It reflects particular preservational pathways, not an unbiased sample of all life.

Reworking of fossils

Words: 17
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Erosion and redeposition of previously buried remains into a younger deposit, potentially confusing environmental and chronological interpretation.

Taphonomic transport

Words: 41 Articles: 1
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Movement of remains by currents, waves, gravity or organisms. Sorting depends on size, density, shape and articulation rather than solely on taxon abundance.

Hydraulic sorting

Words: 18
Taphonomic transport OurBigBook.com
Differential movement or deposition of remains by flowing water, dependent on their shape, density, size, orientation and articulation.

Disarticulation

Words: 22
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Separation of connected skeletal elements as supporting tissues fail or external forces act. An articulated skeleton need not have been buried instantly.

Post-mortem decay

Words: 34 Articles: 1
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Transformation and loss of biological tissues after death through autolysis, microbial activity and associated processes. Decay rates differ among anatomical characters.

Autolysis

Words: 13
Post-mortem decay OurBigBook.com
Enzymatic breakdown of cells by their own components after loss of normal regulation.

Biostratinomy

Words: 21
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Processes affecting remains before final burial, including decay, disarticulation, transport and surface modification. These mechanisms can continue or recur during reworking.

Diagenesis

Words: 78 Articles: 4
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Physical and chemical alteration after burial, potentially modifying fossil tissues and isotopic compositions.

Fossil recrystallization

Words: 16
Diagenesis OurBigBook.com
Change in the crystal organization of fossil material during alteration, potentially obscuring original histological or chemical information.

Diagenetic mineralization

Words: 23
Diagenesis OurBigBook.com
precipitation or replacement by minerals during burial alteration. Early mineral formation can retain fine biological detail, while later alteration can destroy or obscure it.

Sedimentary compaction

Words: 13
Diagenesis OurBigBook.com
Reduction of sediment volume during burial that can flatten, distort or fracture enclosed remains.

Phosphatization

Words: 14
Diagenesis OurBigBook.com
Preservation or replication by calcium phosphate minerals, sometimes retaining soft-tissue detail when precipitation occurs before its destruction.

Exceptional fossil preservation

Words: 44 Articles: 2
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Preservation retaining anatomical information normally lost, especially soft tissues. Such deposits reveal otherwise hidden diversity but still select particular environments and tissue types.

Chengjiang

Words: 10
Exceptional fossil preservation OurBigBook.com
An exceptionally preserved early Cambrian biota from China, including Haikouichthys and Myllokunmingia.

Burgess Shale

Words: 11
Exceptional fossil preservation OurBigBook.com
A Cambrian fossil-bearing succession in Canada preserving diverse animal anatomy, including Metaspriggina.

Carcass decay

Words: 27
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Postmortem loss and alteration of tissues. Replicated observations show that loss can be anatomically selective, so a decayed specimen may look systematically less derived.[ref]

Time averaging

Words: 21
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Mixing remains produced at different times in one sampled assemblage. It can occur locally without long-distance transport, blurring short ecological events.

Fossil reworking

Words: 22
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Erosion of an older deposit and incorporation of its fossil material into a younger one. It can create a misleading apparent last occurrence.

Transport of fossil remains

Words: 24
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Movement before final burial can sort remains by size, shape and density. Articulation alone cannot prove local origin, and disarticulation alone cannot prove transport.

Fossil assemblage

Words: 76 Articles: 2
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
A collection of fossil remains associated in a specified deposit or sample. Its members may have different original habitats or ages; transport of fossil remains and time averaging affect its interpretation.

Allochthonous fossil assemblage

Words: 26
Fossil assemblage OurBigBook.com
An assemblage containing remains transported from their original living sites. Hydraulic sorting can enrich durable parts or mix habitats; transport does not invariably disarticulate a carcass.

Autochthonous fossil assemblage

Words: 26
Fossil assemblage OurBigBook.com
An assemblage whose organisms are preserved where they lived. Local origin does not eliminate taphonomic bias: soft tissues can disappear and resistant parts can accumulate through time.

Taphonomic bias

Words: 74 Articles: 1
Taphonomy OurBigBook.com
Systematic distortion of biological information through differential preservation and recovery. teeth and delicate soft tissues need not have the same probability of entering the fossil record; even a locally buried assemblage can therefore give a biased account of its living community.

Stemward slippage

Words: 35
Taphonomic bias OurBigBook.com
Erroneous placement toward the base of a lineage when loss of diagnostic characters through carcass decay is interpreted as an ancestral biological absence. Coding unobservable characters as unknown and comparing preservation across specimens can reduce the problem.

Transitional fossil

Words: 50 Articles: 1
Fossil OurBigBook.com
A fossil with a combination of ancestral and derived characters informative about an evolutionary transition. It need not be the direct ancestor of a named living group.

Missing link

Words: 24
Transitional fossil OurBigBook.com
A historical metaphor for a predicted but unsampled evolutionary connection. Modern phylogenetic hypotheses concern branching relationships and character changes rather than one obligatory intermediate.
