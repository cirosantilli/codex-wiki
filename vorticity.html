Vorticity (ω=∇×u)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 39
Use vorticity to distinguish the local spin of a fluid element from the motion of its center along a pathline. Circular streamlines can occur both with and without vorticity: the two velocity fields below even have the same circulation around the unit circle.
For a continuously differentiable velocity field u=(ux​,uy​,uz​), vorticity is the curl vector
ω=∇×u=(∂y∂uz​​−∂z∂uy​​,∂z∂ux​​−∂x∂uz​​,∂x∂uy​​−∂y∂ux​​).
(1)
Each partial derivative divides velocity by distance, so vorticity has units s−1. In a planar velocity field independent of z, only ωz​=∂x​uy​−∂y​ux​ survives. Positive ωz​ points along +ez​: the right-hand rule associates it with counterclockwise spin viewed from positive z toward the plane. [ref]
The local rotational angular velocity is half the vorticity, Ωspin​=ω/2. To see the factor, split the velocity gradient Lij​=∂j​ui​ into its symmetric rate-of-strain tensor E=(L+LT)/2 and antisymmetric spin tensor W=(L−LT)/2. A nearby displacement δx has relative velocity Lδx; its rotational part satisfies Wδx=(ω/2)×δx. This is a proof outline of the local spin interpretation. The rate-of-strain tensor can also change the angle of a particular material line, so that line's angular velocity need not equal ω/2.
For solid-body rotation, u=(−Ωy,Ωx,0) gives ω=(0,0,2Ω) and Ωspin​=(0,0,Ω). Taking Ω=1 s−1, the vorticity is 2ez​ s−1, although the angular velocity is only 1ez​ s−1. For the potential line vortex, u=Keθ​/r, the planar curl is r−1∂r​(ruθ​)=r−1∂r​K=0 wherever r>0. Its circular pathlines therefore coexist with zero local spin.
This distinction makes vorticity useful for recognizing shear flow as well as swirling motion; taking the curl of the Euler equations also removes a pressure gradient and leads to the vorticity equation. A historical landmark is Hermann von Helmholtz's 1858 paper on fluid vortex motion, an early study devoted to vortex dynamics. [ref]

Vorticity of a linear shear flow (ωz​=−γ,Ωspin,z​=−γ/2)

Words: 216
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
Use a linear shear flow to see why straight pathlines can still carry local spin. Faster layers pull past slower ones, and a small symmetric paddle can turn clockwise while its center travels straight ahead.
For the planar simple shear flow u=(γy,0,0) with constant signed shear rate γ, direct differentiation gives
ωz​=∂x​uy​−∂y​ux​=−γ,Ωspin,z​=−2γ​.
(2)
With γ=1 s−1, these are −1 s−1 and −0.5 s−1: both signs indicate clockwise orientation under the right-hand rule. Yet a particle initially at (x0​,y0​) follows the straight pathline x(t)=x0​+γy0​t, y(t)=y0​. The curl measures differences of velocity between neighboring particles, rather than bending of one particle's pathline.
The velocity gradient has Lxy​=γ as its only nonzero entry. Its spin tensor supplies clockwise rotation, while its rate-of-strain tensor supplies simultaneous deformation. For a material line making angle α to the horizontal, the equation δx˙=Lδx gives α˙=−γsin2α. Initially horizontal and vertical material lines therefore have angular velocities 0 and −γ; their mean is −γ/2. At t=0.5 s with γ=1 s−1, an initially vertical displacement becomes (0.5,1) times its original length and has angle arctan2≈63.435∘, a clockwise change of 26.565∘. The plotted cross deforms; an actual paddle's response also depends on its shape and interaction with the fluid. The exact local spin statement concerns the spin tensor, rather than every tracer's orientation.

Vortex line

Words: 52
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
A vortex line is an integral curve of the vorticity vector field: its tangent is parallel to ω=∇×u. In a two-dimensional incompressible flow, these lines are perpendicular to the flow plane and the vorticity equation gives Dω/Dt=0. Since material cross-sectional area is preserved, the flux of vorticity through a material vortex tube, equal to its circulation, is constant.

Vortex (fluid mechanics)

Words: 571 Articles: 9
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
A fluid vortex is a region in which velocity field circulates about an axis or center. Vorticity measures local rotation, while circulation measures the integral of velocity around a closed path. A phase vortex is instead a topological defect defined by winding of an order parameter; superfluid examples connect the two notions.

The evolution of a vorticity distribution together with the velocity field it induces. A transport equation advances vorticity; diagnostic inversion recovers velocity from it and the specified boundary conditions.

Slow-drain angular-momentum amplification (r2ω≃ω0​R2)

Words: 80
Vortex dynamics OurBigBook.com
Axisymmetric inviscid flow has no azimuthal pressure torque, so each parcel conserves specific axial angular momentum j=ruθ​=r2ω. If late draining fluid originally lay near the rim R of a uniformly rotating pool, then j≃ω0​R2 and ω≃ω0​R2/r2. The late-origin assumption is essential; torque conservation alone gives j=ω0​r02​ for each parcel's own initial radius r0​. Quasistatic radial and vertical pressure balance then gives free-surface depression h(r)=ω02​R4(r−2−R−2)/(2g). The singular profile applies outside a finite drain or vortex core, not at the rotation axis.

Nonrotating layerwise-two-dimensional vortex dynamics (∂t​ζ+J(ψ,ζ)=0,∇h2​ψ=ζ)

Words: 68
Vortex dynamics OurBigBook.com
In the limiting model of infinitely strong stable density stratification, small Froude number and fixed vertical scales suppress vertical displacement. Each horizontal level evolves by two-dimensional vortex dynamics; z labels independent copies of the horizontal Poisson equation. The leading normalized Ertel potential vorticity is Q/N2=ζ. This is a constrained asymptotic model: finite buoyancy frequency restores vertical motion and coupling, and arbitrarily short vertical scales invalidate the independent-layer limit. McIntyre's discussion of potential-vorticity inversion explains the limiting independent-layer picture.

Two-dimensional vortex dynamics (ζt​+J(ψ,ζ)=0,∇2ψ=ζ)

Words: 105 Articles: 1
Vortex dynamics OurBigBook.com
For inviscid planar incompressible flow, write velocity as (−ψy​,ψx​) and vorticity as ζ=∇2ψ. Taking curl of the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid removes pressure and gives the displayed transport equation, with J(ψ,ζ)=ψx​ζy​−ψy​ζx​. The Poisson equation then recovers the streamfunction at each instant. A velocity tangent to the plane cannot stretch its perpendicular vorticity vector.
Steady planar vorticity as a local function of stream function (Δψ=F(ψ)locally where ∇ψ=0)
Words: 63
Two-dimensional vortex dynamics OurBigBook.com
In a steady inviscid planar incompressible flow, vorticity is constant along streamlines. A regular stream function supplies a local transverse coordinate, so locally Δψ depends only on ψ. A global single-valued function needs the same value on every component of each level set. This can fail: ψ=x3−x gives the steady Euler shear (u,v)=(0,1−3x2) with constant pressure, but ψ(0)=ψ(1)=0 while Δψ is respectively 0 and 6.

Line vortex (u=2πΓ​∣r−r0​∣2ez​×(r−r0​)​)

Words: 250 Articles: 3
Vortex (fluid mechanics) OurBigBook.com
An ideal straight line vortex in a two-dimensional incompressible flow has circulation Γ and induces velocity
u(r)=2πΓ​∣r−r0​∣2ez​×(r−r0​)​.
(3)
Its stream function, with u=(ψy​,−ψx​), is ψ=−Γlog∣r−r0​∣/(2π). Positive Γ means counterclockwise circulation. In an ideal point-vortex description, the vortex position is advected by the regular velocity produced by the other vortices and the boundaries; its own singular velocity is excluded.

Motion of two point vortices (d˙=(κ1​+κ2​)Jd/(2π∣d∣2))

Words: 64
Line vortex OurBigBook.com
Take vortex strength to mean circulation and J(x,y)=(−y,x). Each point vortex moves with the velocity induced by the other, so for d=x1​−x2​ the displayed equation holds. Since d⋅Jd=0, separation is constant; weighting the two velocities also gives d(κ1​x1​+κ2​x2​)/dt=0. Equal strengths at opposite points of a circle of radius a rotate with angular speed κ/(4πa2).

Image vortex at a plane wall (U=4πbΓ​)

Words: 123 Articles: 1
Line vortex OurBigBook.com
The method of images enforces impermeability at a plane wall by reflecting a line vortex across it and reversing its circulation. The two logarithmic stream functions cancel on the wall, which is consequently a streamline. For a vortex of circulation Γ at (0,b) above y=0, the image of circulation −Γ at (0,−b) induces velocity (Γ/(4πb),0) at the real vortex. The signed drift parallel to the wall is therefore Γ/(4πb).
Line-vortex trajectory in a quarter-plane (rsin2θ=constant)
Words: 59
Image vortex at a plane wall OurBigBook.com
In the quadrant x,y>0, a line vortex of circulation Γ at (x,y) has three images: circulations −Γ at (−x,y) and (x,−y), and +Γ at (−x,−y). Their induced velocity is
x˙=4πy(x2+y2)Γx2​,y˙​=−4πx(x2+y2)Γy2​.
(4)
In polar coordinates, these equations give r˙=Γcot(2θ)/(2πr) and θ˙=−Γ/(4πr2). Direct differentiation yields dtd​(rsin2θ)=0, so the trajectory obeys rsin2θ=constant.

In an unbounded incompressible fluid with localized vorticity and no added harmonic background, velocity is reconstructed by u(x)=(4π)−1∫ω(y)×(x−y)/∣x−y∣3d3y. Its multipole expansion relates hydrodynamic impulse to a dipolar far-field velocity.

Hydrodynamic impulse (I=21​∫x×ωdV)

Words: 79 Articles: 1
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
Hydrodynamic impulse per unit density is I=21​∫x×ωdV for localized vorticity. In unforced all-space incompressible flow its derivative vanishes when far-field fluxes vanish. For a spherical all-space limit, I=(3/2)limR​∫VR​​udV; a dipolar velocity tail makes the distinction from a bare momentum integral important.

Dipolar velocity field of a localized eddy (ufar​=[3n(I⋅n)−I]/(4πr3))

Words: 38

A localized vorticity distribution with nonzero hydrodynamic impulse induces u∼[3n(I⋅n)−I]/(4πr3). This curl-free exterior velocity can remain long-ranged even when the vorticity is localized. In a random ensemble it produces dipolar longitudinal and transverse correlation tails.

Vortex tube

Words: 270 Articles: 3
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
A vortex tube is a bundle of vortex lines bounded by a surface to which vorticity is tangent. Circulation measures its vorticity flux. Stretching a nearly incompressible tube reduces its cross-sectional area and increases its vorticity. The Burgers vortex is a local model in which this concentration balances viscous diffusion.

Vortex filament

Words: 54
Vortex tube OurBigBook.com
A vortex filament is the ideal thin-core limit of a vortex tube, concentrating a finite circulation on a curve. In an unbounded incompressible fluid with velocity tending to zero at infinity, its induced velocity field follows the vorticity version of the Biot-Savart law. The ideal field is singular on the filament; finite-core models are needed there. A closed circular filament is a thin vortex ring.

Vortex ring

Words: 173 Articles: 1
Vortex tube OurBigBook.com
A vortex ring is a vortex tube bent into a closed loop, often approximately a circle. Its vorticity runs around the loop, while the induced velocity field passes through the centre and returns outside. For a thin ring of radius r and circulation Γ, the Biot-Savart law for incompressible vorticity gives an axial velocity uz​(z)=Γr2/[2(r2+z2)3/2], measured relative to the ring centre, with its sign set by the circulation orientation. A vortex ring is not a loop of fluid whose velocity everywhere merely follows the circular vortex line.

Velocity field of a dense coaxial vortex-ring array (uinside​≃Γ/l)

Words: 101
Vortex ring OurBigBook.com
A long array of equal co-oriented vortex rings, spaced by l≪r, approximates an azimuthal vortex sheet on a cylinder of radius r. The circulation per unit axial length is Γ/l. The conditions ∇⋅u=0 and ∇×u=ω are mathematically analogous to the magnetostatic equations for a solenoid. Away from its ends, an array long compared with r produces nearly uniform axial flow Γ/l inside and weak flow outside. A finite array has return flow outside connecting its two ends. The uniform-core approximation fails close to individual vortex cores and near the ends, and the field requires a stated circulation orientation and background flow.

Enstrophy (Z=⟨ω2⟩/2)

Words: 71 Articles: 1
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
Enstrophy measures squared vorticity: a local density is ω2/2, and its spatial integral or ensemble mean gives a global measure. In homogeneous incompressible flow, viscous dissipation satisfies ϵ=ν⟨ω2⟩. Vortex stretching can produce enstrophy in three dimensions; kinematic viscosity destroys it.

Mean enstrophy balance (Z˙=⟨ωi​ωj​Sij​⟩−ν⟨∣∇×ω∣2⟩)

Words: 38
Enstrophy OurBigBook.com
For unforced homogeneous incompressible flow, the vorticity equation gives Z˙=⟨ωi​ωj​Sij​⟩−ν⟨∣∇×ω∣2⟩. Curl and full-gradient squared averages agree because the vorticity is divergence free and boundary divergences average to zero. In established high-Reynolds-number turbulence, slow integral-time evolution makes production and destruction nearly balance.

Planar vorticity velocity kernel (K(z)=±z⊥/(2π∣z∣2))

Words: 50
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
The velocity induced by planar vorticity is a convolution with this kernel, whose sign depends on the stream function and vorticity conventions. Its magnitude is (2π∣z∣)−1 and its derivative is bounded by C∣z∣−2. Splitting the convolution at radius R gives ∥K∗ω∥∞​≤R∥ω∥∞​+∥ω∥1​/(2πR). The same singularity yields a log-Lipschitz modulus, enough for a Yudovich characteristic flow.

Kinetic helicity conservation law

Words: 84 Articles: 1
Kinetic helicity density OurBigBook.com
For smooth inviscid barotropic fluid motion without gravity or magnetic forces, let w be the specific enthalpy with ∇w=ρ−1∇p, and set q=u⋅ω. Then
∂t​q+∇⋅[qu+(w−2u2​)ω]=0.
(7)
This follows by dotting the barotropic vorticity transport equation with u and using the divergence of a cross product. A volume integral is constant when the resulting boundary flux vanishes.

The kinetic helicity conservation law implies
DtDq​=ω⋅∇(2u2​−w)−q∇⋅u.
(8)
Therefore q is materially constant exactly when the right side vanishes. Incompressible flow together with constancy of w−u2/2 along vortex lines is sufficient. In compressible flow, the density-normalized quantity satisfies D(q/ρ)/Dt=−(ω/ρ)⋅∇(w−u2/2).

Circulation (physics, ΓC​=∮C​u⋅dl)

Words: 476 Articles: 2
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
Use circulation to summarize how much a velocity field runs along an oriented closed curve C, such as a circle around a fluid vortex. Its definition is the line integral
ΓC​=∮C​u⋅dl,
(9)
where dl is tangent to C in the chosen direction. Reversing that direction reverses the sign of circulation. Its units are m2s−1, because velocity is multiplied by a length. Counterclockwise circulation in the horizontal plane is positive when the chosen unit normal vector is +ez​, according to the right-hand rule.
For solid-body rotation u=(−Ωy,Ωx,0), a counterclockwise circle of radius R has constant tangential velocity ΩR. Multiplying by its circumference gives
ΓC​=2πΩR2.
(10)
For the potential line vortex u=Keθ​/r, the same circle instead gives ΓC​=(K/R)(2πR)=2πK, independent of R. Stipulate Ω=1 s−1, R=1 m and K=1 m2s−1: both velocity fields have ΓC​=2π m2s−1≈6.283185 m2s−1, although their vorticities in the fluid are 2ez​ s−1 and zero respectively. On a circle of radius 2 m, their circulations are 8π m2s−1 and 2π m2s−1. The comparison plot in vorticity shows this difference in radius dependence.
The bridge between circulation and vorticity is Stokes theorem:
Γ∂S​=∫S​ω⋅ndS.
(11)
Here the velocity field must be continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of the entire spanning surface S inside the fluid, and its boundary orientation must match the unit normal vector n. For solid-body rotation, the disk is admissible and its surface integral is 2ΩπR2. For the potential line vortex, the origin is singular and excluded from the fluid: a disk crossing it is not admissible. On an annulus, Stokes theorem instead gives ΓRout​​−ΓRin​​=0, with both circulations here measured counterclockwise; the induced inner boundary direction is clockwise. Nonzero circulation therefore does not imply nonzero local curl in the available fluid. [ref]
This is useful because Stokes theorem lets a local vorticity measurement determine a loop's circulation when a suitable surface exists, while Kelvin's circulation theorem tracks circulation along a moving material curve. The beauty is the precise link between a local derivative and a global line integral, with the domain deciding which conclusions are valid.

Kelvin's circulation theorem (dΓ/dt=0)

Words: 152 Articles: 1
Circulation (physics) OurBigBook.com
In an inviscid barotropic fluid subject to conservative body force, the circulation of a material loop is constant. For constant density, parameterize that loop by r(s,t) with rt​=u. Differentiating Γ=∫u⋅rs​ds gives an integral of (Du/Dt)⋅dr plus ∫∂s​(∣u∣2/2)ds. The latter vanishes on a closed loop, and the Euler equations make the former the integral of a gradient, also zero. For a barotropic density, ∇p/ρ is still a gradient of specific enthalpy. Constant circulation is consistent with changing local vorticity when a vortex tube changes its cross-sectional area.

Irrotational circulation in an exterior domain

Words: 58
Kelvin's circulation theorem OurBigBook.com
In smooth barotropic inviscid flow with conservative body force, Kelvin's circulation theorem preserves circulation of material curves. Initially zero vorticity therefore gives zero circulation on contractible loops for all times. A loop winding around an excluded obstacle can have a nonzero constant circulation: the planar field u=Γeθ​/(2πr) is irrotational outside the origin but has circulation Γ on a positively oriented enclosing loop.

Irrotational flow (∇×u=0)

Words: 166
Vorticity OurBigBook.com
Use irrotational flow to identify where a velocity potential can describe the velocity field locally. An irrotational flow has ω=∇×u=0 throughout the fluid region. For a continuously differentiable velocity field, the Poincaré lemma supplies a velocity potential on sufficiently small neighborhoods. A global single-valued velocity potential requires zero circulation around every closed curve; a simply connected domain is a sufficient domain assumption.
The potential line vortex u=Keθ​/r is an irrotational flow on the punctured plane, or on an annulus a<r<b with a>0, even though its pathlines are circular. Every sufficiently small loop bounding a disk in that fluid has zero circulation, by Stokes theorem. A loop winding once counterclockwise around the excluded origin has circulation 2πK. Thus local irrotational flow and nonzero circulation around a hole are compatible: the enclosing disk passes outside the domain of the velocity field.
An irrotational flow is a kinematic condition on a velocity field, independent of whether a particular model neglects viscosity or assumes incompressible flow. Adding incompressible flow makes the local velocity potential satisfy the Laplace equation. This reduces the number of unknowns and makes potential flow a powerful starting point for flow around bodies and for gravity waves. [ref]
